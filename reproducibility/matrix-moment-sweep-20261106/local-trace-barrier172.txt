STRONG INVERSE-TRACE RECTANGLE CERTIFICATES LOSE THE TARGET ORDER

1. Actual rule being tested, not every capacity/partition construction
Fix D>=3, target c>0 and n>=3. For an actual axis-aligned integer rectangle
R in the unit-spacing torus, retain its actual internal positive-axis edges,
and let L_R=mu*d_R* d_R for uniform coefficient mu>0. A proper coordinate
interval gives a path; a full periodic coordinate may retain its cycle.
The graph is connected, m=|R|, and its only zero mode is the constant.
All norms/traces below are ordinary COUNTING norms/traces, not volume-normalized.
For s>0 define
 T_s(R)=Tr((L_R^dagger)^s)=sum_(lambda>0)lambda^(-s).
A singleton has T_s=0. Consider the concrete sufficient stopping rule

                  c^s T_s(R)<=1 on EVERY terminal rectangle.          (A)

It certifies c*Var_R(u)<=E_R(u): the largest inverse eigenvalue is at most
T_s^(1/s), so(A) forces the local spectral gap to be at least c. Summing
the local inequalities and discarding positive crossing-edge energy then
gives the global normalized-mean inequality with quadratic penalty c.
This rule is sufficient, not necessary. It is a restriction on a proof
certificate, not on the original observation class or permitted penalty.

For s=1 it is exactly the total-pair-effective-resistance certificate:
 T_1(R)=(1/m) sum_(x<y) R_eff(x,y).
Indeed Var_R(u)=(1/m)sum_(x<y)(u(x)-u(y))^2 and each squared difference
is at most R_eff(x,y)E_R(u). Any path or resistance-diameter upper bound
on this pair sum is at least as restrictive. In particular the diameter
coefficient is(m-1)R_diam/2, not a volume-normalized trace.

4. The critical strong trace s=p=D/2 STILL necessarily loses log n
The previous norm bound is insufficient at the endpoint; here is a full
rectangle-shape-uniform proof of the logarithmic obstruction.
Write L_R=mu L_R^0 and A_D=(2*pi*e)^(-D). On a coordinate interval of
length l, path eigenvalues are4sin^2(pi*j/(2l)),0<=j<l; a full cycle

In BOTH cases lambda_j<=4pi^2 j^2/l^2 in this enumeration. For t>=1,
there are at least l/(2pi sqrt(t)) indices j with lambda_j*t<=1:
take0<=j<=floor(l/(2pi sqrt(t))); the upper index is always<l.

The Cartesian product spectrum gives
          Tr exp(-t L_R^0)>=A_D*m*t^(-D/2) (t>=1).
Subtract the single constant mode. If
                1<=t<=T=(A_D*m/2)^(2/D),

          Tr exp(-t L_R^0)-1 >=(A_D/2)*m*t^(-p).

 T_p(R)>=[A_D/(D Gamma(p))]*(m/mu^p)*log(A_D*m/2).                  (C)

This bound includes highly anisotropic rectangles and full periodic axes;
it makes no bounded-aspect-ratio or supplied-cube assumption.

1. Actual rank-one family

Let D>=2,n>=2,V=n^D, h>=1 and use the unit-spacing forward gradient d from158.
For ONE selected direction/component row e of d write v=d* e, a vector in
the hV-dimensional field space. All coefficient channels equal m>0 except
this one, whose coefficient is -B, B>=0. Thus

 H=mL0-(m+B)vv*,      L0=d*d.

All constant fields are annihilated. On their orthogonal complement let
G=L0^-1, and put rho=v*Gv. The actual value is

 rho=(V-1)/(D V).                                      (1)

Proof. The scalar torus translations and coordinate permutations act
transitively on its DV forward rows and preserve L0 and its pseudoinverse.
Their leverage values are equal. Their sum is
Tr(d G d*)=Tr(G L0)=V-1 in one component. For h components they decouple,
so each of the DVh rows has the same value as in the scalar case. Parallel
reverse rows at n=2 are still counted individually, exactly as in d.

2. Exact phase boundary, not an assumed certificate

On the mean-zero space, congruence by G^(1/2) turns H into
m I-(m+B)ww*, where w=G^(1/2)v and ||w||^2=rho>0. Sylvester inertia
therefore gives the sharp threshold

 Bcrit=m*(D V/(V-1)-1).

For B<Bcrit, H is positive definite on mean-zero fields. At B=Bcrit it
has exactly one extra zero direction there; for B>Bcrit it has exactly one
negative direction there. Add the h constant zero modes in each case.
The equality witness is Gv: H Gv=[m-(m+B)rho]v. No simultaneous spectral
diagonalization with L0 is asserted. This is also the classical one-negative-
edge effective-resistance threshold, expressed via the full positive reference
graph rather than via the graph with the changed edge removed.

For every mean-zero u, Cauchy-Schwarz in the L0 energy gives
|v*u|^2<=rho<u,L0u>. Hence

 <u,Hu> >= [m-(m+B)rho]<u,L0u>.                          (2)

The constant in this comparison is attained at u=Gv, so the energy-relative
comparison threshold itself is sharp. This does not say that the following
Euclidean gap lower bound is the exact first positive eigenvalue.

3. A uniform finite gap despite negative coefficient depth growing to infinity

Set m=n^2, B=theta*m, with a fixed 0<=theta<D-1. Since rho<1/D,
m-(m+B)rho >= m*(D-1-theta)/D. The actual torus Fourier eigenvalues of L0
are4 sum_i sin^2(pi*k_i/n). On mean-zero fields their minimum is
4sin^2(pi/n)>=16/n^2, by sin(t)>=2t/pi for0<=t<=pi/2. Equation(2) gives

 <u,Hu> >= g ||u||^2,  g=16*(D-1-theta)/D>0, u mean-zero.

Define the actual normalized observation C(u)=V^(-1/2)sum_x u(x) in R^h.
For any0<c<=g and Phi(y)=c||y||^2,

 Q_A(u)+Phi(Cu)>=c||u||^2,       rank C=h, ||C||=1.

Its rank is optimal because the h-dimensional constant kernel must be seen
by any differentiable-at-zero observation with locally quadratically bounded
penalty, or simply by any linear observation. The proof uses the constructed
gap, not a supplied spectral/nonlinear certificate.

For theta>0, the negative coefficient depth b=theta*n^2 diverges as n grows,
yet this observation has the UNIFORM penalty c. Thus a uniform depth bound
is sufficient for159's generic estimate, not necessary for every signed
family. This does not contradict161's large-B counterexample with FIXED m:
the admissible relative depth B/m here stays strictly below the exact
threshold, while that counterexample sends it to infinity. The signed
coefficient matrix A is indefinite, but the actual gradient form H is
nonnegative through graph redundancy. One must distinguish those statements.

The whole one-channel family also obeys N_<c(H)<=h+1 whenever
0<c<=16m/n^2: apply rank-one interlacing to mL0, whose nonconstant spectrum
starts at least16m/n^2. At/above Bcrit this bound is attained because the

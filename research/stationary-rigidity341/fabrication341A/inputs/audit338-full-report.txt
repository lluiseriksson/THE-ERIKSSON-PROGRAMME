8. CHEEGER IMPLICATION: CORRECT DIRECTION AND CONSTANT
For nonnegative f with support of nu-mass <=1/2, each positive superlevel set is eligible for Phi. Coarea for f^2 and the symmetric double sum counts each boundary twice, yielding sum W|f(x)^2-f(y)^2|>=2Phi||f||_(2,nu)^2. Cauchy-Schwarz factors this into a first factor squared 2E_h(f,f) and a second factor squared at most 4||f||_(2,nu)^2 by (a+b)^2<=2a^2+2b^2 and the stationary W marginals. The result is E_h(f,f)>=Phi^2||f||^2/2; zero f is trivial.
For arbitrary f subtract a stationary median m. The strict positive and negative supports each have mass <=1/2 even if there is an atom at the median. Pointwise the squared differences of the two truncations sum to at most (f(x)-f(y))^2. Their squared norms sum to E_nu(f-m)^2>=Var_nu(f). This proves gap>=Phi^2/2 with the stated Dirichlet normalization.
Therefore gap->0 implies Phi<=sqrt(2gap)->0, not a claimed converse from absence of a chosen bottleneck. Each finite state space has a minimizing nonempty cut with mass <=1/2. If those masses did not tend to zero, some subsequence would have u>=epsilon, contradicting (5.3). Thus actual gap collapse would force rare stationary minimizing cuts. This does not show such cuts exist, nor that rare mass alone produces collapse.

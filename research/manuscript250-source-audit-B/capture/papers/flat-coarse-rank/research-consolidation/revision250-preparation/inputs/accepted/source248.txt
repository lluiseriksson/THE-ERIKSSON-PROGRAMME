2. A finite COUNTING two-dimensional point-Poincare estimate.
Let L>=1 be an integer and Q={0,...,ell_1-1}x{0,...,ell_2-1}, where each
ell_i is an integer in [L/2,L]. Put N=ell_1 ell_2 and
 E_Q(f)=sum_(i=1,2) sum_(y,y+e_i in Q) |f(y+e_i)-f(y)|^2,
with each internal path edge counted once, no periodic edge. At ANY b in Q,
 f(b)=0 implies ||f||_Q^2<=13 L^2(1+ln L) E_Q(f).                 (248.4)
Here ||f||_Q^2 is the counting sum, not the probability average.

We derive (248.4), including its constant mode. The counting path matrix on
ell vertices has endpoint degree1, interior degree2 and off-diagonal -1;
at ell=1 it is the zero matrix. Its counting-orthonormal cosine vectors are
 phi_0(t)=1/sqrt(ell),
 phi_j(t)=sqrt(2/ell) cos(pi j(t+1/2)/ell), 1<=j<ell,
with eigenvalues lambda_j=4sin^2(pi j/(2ell)). To check the formula, the
interior second difference multiplies cos(theta(t+1/2)) by
2(1-cos theta); the endpoint follows from the same identity with virtual
values f(-1)=f(0), f(ell)=f(ell-1) when theta=pi j/ell. Orthogonality follows
by product-to-sum and the finite cosine identity
 sum_(t=0)^(ell-1) cos(pi r(t+1/2)/ell)=0 for 1<=r<2ell,
whose value is sin(pi r)/(2sin(pi r/(2ell))); for r=0 the sum is ell.
Thus every asserted normalization and eigenvalue follows from finite sums.
For ell=1 only phi_0 exists. Every squared point value is <=2/ell.

Tensor products give a counting-orthonormal basis phi_kappa on Q, with
 |phi_kappa(b)|^2<=4/N,
 lambda_kappa=4sum_(i=1,2) sin^2(pi kappa_i/(2ell_i)),

For kappa!=0, concavity of sine on [0,pi/2] gives sin t>=2t/pi, hence
 lambda_kappa>=4sum_i kappa_i^2/ell_i^2>=4|kappa|^2/L^2.            (248.5)
A singleton factor merely forces its index to zero. If N=1, f(b)=0 forces
f=0 and (248.4) is immediate; no nonexistent positive gap is invoked.
Assume N>1 below, so the smallest positive eigenvalue is >=4/L^2.

For L>=2, on the nonnegative-index shell r=max(kappa_1,kappa_2)>=1

The harmonic bound follows by comparison of 1/r, r>=2, with its integral.

3. Actual two-dimensional paired-layer strong graph.
Channel1 is strong at odd sources and channel2 at even sources. Fix one
original channel alpha, one spatial component k, and let A be its strong
source parity. For every s in Z/nZ of parity A put, for y in (Z/nZ)^2,

map is a bijection onto layer s+1. Residue parity agrees with original site
parity because n is even. The n/2 layer pairs {s,s+1} are disjoint and cover
all residues for the chosen A, including {n-1,0} for odd A. Hence summing
||u_s||^2+||v_s||^2 over these s gives EXACTLY the original scalar norm.

The strong +e_3 edge is u_s(y)--v_s(y); for i=1,2 the identity
 x_s(y+e_i)+e_3=x_s(y)+e_i mod n
shows that the strong +e_i edge is u_s(y)--v_s(y+e_i). These are ALL three
actual outgoing strong labels, and they stay in this pair. Their exact
unweighted energy is
 E_s=sum_y|u_s(y)-v_s(y)|^2

Each pair graph is connected: a cross edge and a rung connect u_s(y) with
u_s(y+e_i), the two translations generate the source torus, and every v
has its rung. Connectivity is derived, not a supplied gap certificate.
The three heads at a source are distinct even at n=2. Induced source-torus
edges may then repeat an undirected neighbor pair, but we retain their
positive FORWARD labels and never deduplicate them. All weak-source terms
are nonnegative. Thus (248.1) is at least H times the sum of (248.7) over
all strong layer pairs, both channels and all three spatial components.

For any such u_s define the labelled source-torus energy

Insert v_s(y+e_i) between the two source values and square with
|a+b|^2<=2|a|^2+2|b|^2. Translations y->y+e_i are bijections, so summing

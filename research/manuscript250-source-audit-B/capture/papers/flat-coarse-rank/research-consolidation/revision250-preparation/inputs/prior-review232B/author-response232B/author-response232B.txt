ADDITIONAL PRESENTATION AND BIBLIOGRAPHIC COMMENTS
The report's Appendix A request concerns the compressed finite projection/folding steps. The following expands what the existing argument uses; it is an explanatory companion, not replacement source or a newly reviewed proof.
For a finite set T in a D-dimensional product, the finite projection inequality is |T|^(D-1)<=product_i |pi_i T|. One way to make the iterative-Holder step explicit is the nonnegative product inequality
 sum_x product_i g_i(pi_i x)^(1/(D-1)) <= product_i (sum g_i)^(1/(D-1)).
With g_i the indicators of pi_i T, every x in T contributes one on the left. Raising the resulting bound to D-1 gives the displayed finite projection inequality. This is the classical finite Loomis-Whitney/generalized-Holder step, not a new novelty claim.
For folding an aspect-at-most-two integer rectangle into a cube of its longest side M, the one-coordinate map first lists 0,...,m_i-1, repeats the final endpoint, then reflects until M sites have been assigned. Since M<=2m_i, a vertex has at most two preimages and an actual edge at most two; repeated endpoints contribute zero difference. In the product, every target edge has at most 2^D preimages. The initial rectangle block maps identically, so restriction cannot increase the shifted L^(D/(D-1)) norm, while the pulled-back variation is at most 2^D times the rectangle variation. Applying the cube bound 2/c_iso yields the existing safe rectangle constant 2^(D+1)/c_iso. This identifies the multiplicity and restriction steps whose compression the reviewer noted. It supplies no new executable or formal certificate, and the frozen Appendix A has not been expanded in place. Any desired revised presentation remains a later editorial change with its own identity.

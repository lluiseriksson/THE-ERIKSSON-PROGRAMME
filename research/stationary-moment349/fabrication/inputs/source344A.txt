1. SETTING AND RESULT
Let mu_L be uniform probability on {-1,1}^L. For fixed 0<rho<1 let P=P_rho be the Boolean noise Markov operator: conditional on x, the coordinates of Y are independent, with P(Y_i=x_i)=(1+rho)/2. Its Walsh multiplier on chi_S is rho^|S|. Set s=(1+rho)/(1-rho). Fix C>0 and 0<b<1. Let integers 0<=k_L<=L satisfy k_L/sqrt(L)->C. All expectations and unqualified norms below use mu_L.

There exist positive h_L with exact energy normalization E[h_L P h_L]=1 and stationary densities pi_L=h_L P h_L satisfying, for every L,
 E pi_L=1, pi_L>=b, E pi_L^s<=2,
 E[pi_L chi_S]=0 whenever 1<=|S|<=k_L.
They can moreover be chosen with a fixed upper bound pi_L<=1+t and lower bound pi_L>=1-t, where
 t=min{(1-b)/2, (2^(1/s)-1)/2, 1/4}>0.                         (1.1)
Nevertheless,
 liminf_(L->infinity) ||P h_L-1||_infinity >= t/[2(3+2t)]>0.    (1.2)
The prescribed density pi_L is explicit. The positive potential h_L is defined uniquely by a finite-dimensional strictly convex minimization, whose existence and uniqueness are proved below. No numerical scaling algorithm is assumed to converge or executed.

The boundary cases of the original floor parameter are different: b>1 is incompatible with E pi=1; b=1 forces pi=1 and, by the uniqueness lemma below, the energy-normalized h=1. The counterexample concerns precisely the nontrivial range 0<b<1.

2. INTEGER BLOCKS AND EXACT MARGINAL MATCHING
For every L with k_L+1<=L put m=k_L+1 and let n be the largest positive odd integer at most L/m. Then
 L/m-2<n<=L/m,  mn<=L.
For all sufficiently large L these definitions apply, and
 m/sqrt(L)->C,  n/sqrt(L)->1/C.
Partition the first mn coordinates into m disjoint blocks B_1,...,B_m of size n, ignoring the remaining coordinates. Define
 phi_i(x)=sign(sum_(j in B_i) x_j),   F_L(x)=product_(i=1)^m phi_i(x).
Odd n eliminates ties. Each phi_i has mean zero by the sign-reversal symmetry of the uniform block measure, and takes values in {-1,1}. Its Walsh expansion therefore has no constant term. In the product expansion of F_L, disjointness of the blocks means every resulting character uses a nonempty coordinate subset from each block. Consequently F_L has no Fourier coefficient of degree below m=k_L+1. In particular E F_L=0, F_L^2=1, and F_L takes each sign with probability 1/2. This reasoning does not require any claim about the signs of majority Fourier coefficients.

Set pi_L=1+t F_L. Then E pi_L=1 and pi_L lies in [1-t,1+t]. Since t<1-b and t<2^(1/s)-1, we have pi_L>=b and
 E pi_L^s=((1+t)^s+(1-t)^s)/2 <=(1+t)^s<2.                 (2.1)
The strict inequalities follow directly from the halves in (1.1), with all entries of the minimum positive. For 1<=|S|<=k_L, E pi_L chi_S=0. This is exact matching of the actual stationary density, not an assumption about the weighting density r.

Equivalently, every labelled marginal on at most k_L coordinates is uniform: the probability of a prescribed sign pattern on J is the expectation of 2^(-|J|) product_(j in J)(1+a_j x_j); all its nonconstant characters have zero pi_L-expectation.

For the finitely many L for which k_L+1>L, set pi_L=1. Thus the construction is defined for every positive integer L; finite exceptional dimensions do not affect (1.2).

3. NOISE AT THE ALL-PLUS POINT
Let x_+=(1,...,1). In one block, the noised bits Y_j are independent signs with E Y_j=rho. Write S_n=sum_(j=1)^n Y_j and q_n=P(S_n<0). For tau=atanh(rho)>0, Markov's inequality gives
 q_n<=P(S_n<=0)<=E exp(-tau S_n)
     =(cosh(tau)-rho sinh(tau))^n=(sqrt(1-rho^2))^n
     <=exp(-rho^2 n/2).                                      (3.1)
The last inequality is log(1-z)<=-z for z=rho^2. Since n is odd, S_n is never zero and E sign(S_n)=1-2q_n. Independence of noised blocks yields the exact identity
 (P F_L)(x_+)=(1-2q_n)^m.                                   (3.2)
As m is of order sqrt(L) while n is of order sqrt(L), m exp(-rho^2 n/2)->0. For all sufficiently large L, 0<=2q_n<=1, and Bernoulli's inequality gives
 1>= (1-2q_n)^m >=1-2m q_n ->1.
It follows that
 (P pi_L)(x_+)-1=t(P F_L)(x_+)->t.                           (3.3)
In particular this expression is at least t/2 for every sufficiently large L. This is compatible with the high Fourier support: noise suppresses high-degree coefficients in L^2 but need not suppress the value at one exceptionally biased input uniformly in dimension.

4. POSITIVE SYMMETRIC SCALING: COMPLETE EXISTENCE AND UNIQUENESS
Lemma. On any finite set with a strictly positive probability mu, let P be a reversible Markov transition matrix with nonnegative entries and P(x,x)>0 for each x. For every strictly positive density pi with E_mu pi=1, there exists a unique positive vector h satisfying h(x)(Ph)(x)=pi(x) for every x.

Proof. Write R_xy=mu(x)P(x,y)=R_yx and w_x=mu(x)pi(x)>0, so sum_x w_x=1. For real z indexed by the finite set, define
 J(z)=(1/2)sum_(x,y) R_xy exp(z_x+z_y)-sum_x w_x z_x.         (4.1)
Writing h_x=exp(z_x), direct differentiation, using symmetry, gives
 partial_x J=mu(x)[h_x(Ph)(x)-pi(x)].                        (4.2)
For any real direction v its Hessian quadratic form is
 v^T Hess(J) v=(1/2)sum_(x,y) R_xy exp(z_x+z_y)(v_x+v_y)^2.   (4.3)
If v is nonzero, the term x=y at any nonzero coordinate is strictly positive because R_xx>0. Hence J is strictly convex on the full real vector space; there is no additive-constant degeneracy.

For completeness, its sublevel sets are bounded, with dimension-dependent constants permitted. Let M=max_x z_x, m0=min_x z_x, c=(1/2)min_x R_xx>0, and w_*=min_x w_x>0. A diagonal term at a maximizing coordinate, together with sum w=1, gives
 J(z)>=c exp(2M)-M.                                         (4.4)
Also, if j is a minimizing coordinate,
 sum_x w_x z_x<=w_j m0+(1-w_j)M,
 so J(z)>=w_j(M-m0)-M>=w_*(M-m0)-M.                         (4.5)
Fix a real sublevel height A. If J(z)<=A, (4.4) implies M>=-A (since c exp(2M)>=0) and M<=M_A, where a finite M_A>=max{0,-A} can be chosen so that c exp(2M)-M>A for every M>M_A. Such a choice exists because this expression tends to infinity as M->infinity. Equation (4.5) now implies
 m0>=M-(A+M)/w_* >=-A-(A+M_A)/w_*.                          (4.6)
Thus every nonempty sublevel set is bounded. It is also closed by continuity. A minimizing sequence can be restricted to the nonempty sublevel J<=J(0), a compact set; consequently J attains a minimum. Strict convexity makes the minimum unique. Its gradient vanishes, so (4.2) produces the desired positive h. Conversely every positive solution corresponds to a critical point of J and is this unique minimizer. Finally E hPh=E pi=1. This proves the lemma. No bound uniform in dimension on c, w_* or the minimizer is asserted.

The Boolean P_rho has strictly positive entries and a positive diagonal for 0<rho<1, and is reversible for uniform mu. Apply the lemma to each pi_L from section2. For exceptional dimensions with pi_L=1 the solution is h_L=1. Define g_L=P h_L. The associated actual Doob chain is
 K_L(x,y)=P(x,y)h_L(y)/g_L(x).
Its rows sum to 1, and
 mu(x)pi_L(x)K_L(x,y)=mu(x)P(x,y)h_L(x)h_L(y)
 is symmetric in x,y. Thus pi_L is indeed its normalized stationary density. The family satisfies the original hPh relation and is not merely a pair of abstract nearby probability densities.

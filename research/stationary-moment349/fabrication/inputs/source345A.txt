1. QUESTION, CONVENTIONS AND QUANTIFIED RESULT
Let mu be uniform probability on {-1,1}^L, P=P_rho, Q=P_sqrt(rho), 0<rho<1 fixed. Noise uses Walsh multiplier rho^|S|, so Q^2=P. Let h>0 and normalize E hPh=1. Put
 g=Ph, pi=hg, s=(1+rho)/(1-rho), B=2^(1/s), s'=s/(s-1).
Assume pi>=b>0, E pi^s<=2, and E pi chi_S=0 for 1<=|S|<=k, with integer 0<=k<=L. All unqualified norms and expectations use mu. The nontrivial case is 0<b<1; b=1 forces pi=h=1 by the positive scaling uniqueness in344, and b>1 is infeasible. The actual Doob chain and its stationary probability are
 K(x,y)=P(x,y)h(y)/g(x),    nu(x)=mu(x)pi(x).
The energy normalization makes nu a probability. The symmetric edge measure is
 W(x,y)=nu(x)K(x,y)=mu(x)P(x,y)h(x)h(y).
For a set A define
 q=nu(A), J(A)=sum_(x in A,y notin A) W(x,y).
If 0<q<=1/2, its conductance is J(A)/q. The normalized actual transfer gap equals gamma=1-lambda_2(K). Indeed, with u=h/g and psi0=sqrt(pi), diagonal similarity by psi0 carries M_sqrt(u) P M_sqrt(u) to K; this matrix has Perron root1, whereas T_r for r=u/E u is its scalar multiple by1/E u. No weighting-law marginal assumption is imposed on r.

Fix C0>0. From accepted source334 let alpha_rho>0 be its inherited, dimension-independent global endpoint constant and set
 G=G_rho(C0)=min{1-rho,1-exp(-alpha_rho C0^2/2)}>0.
Set
 eta^2=max{rho,(1-rho)/2}<1, epsilon=B eta^(k+1),
 c=G/4, M0=64 B/c^2=1024 B/G^2.                              (1.1)
The name M0 denotes a scalar threshold constant, not an operator or an amplitude truncation.

Theorem (candidate). There is L0 depending only on rho,C0 such that, for every L>=L0, every integer C0 sqrt(L)<=k<=L, and every h satisfying the stationary hypotheses above, every A with 0<q<=1/2 obeys
 J(A)>=c q-2 sqrt(Bq) epsilon-B epsilon^2.                    (1.2)
Consequently,
 M0 epsilon^2<=q<=1/2  ==>  J(A)/q>=G/8.                    (1.3)
Equivalently, every cut of conductance strictly below G/8 has
 nu(A)<M0 epsilon^2=(1024 B^3/G^2)eta^(2(k+1)).              (1.4)
The same cut has mu(A)<M0 epsilon^2/b. These are upper bounds on the mass of a possible bottleneck, not assertions of its existence.

If gamma<G^2/128, there exists a cut with 0<q<=1/2 satisfying (1.4). In particular, along any admissible sequence with k_L/sqrt(L)->C>0 and gamma_L->0, for every fixed 0<C0<C, all sufficiently large L possess such exponentially small bottleneck cuts. The finite Cheeger sweep needed for this implication is proved in section6, with its convention and constants. The theorem does not bound gamma below when these much smaller sets are allowed. Since exp(-constant*k) at k of order sqrt(L) is much larger than the smallest cube atom of order exp(-constant*L), the remaining range is nonempty in scale.

3. NEW LOCALIZED ENERGY COMPARISON FROM THE ACTUAL hPh RELATION
First, for every real function f,
 ||Q(hf)||_2^2=<hf,P(hf)>
 =E pi f^2-(1/2)sum_(x,y) W(x,y)(f(x)-f(y))^2
 <=E pi f^2.                                               (3.1)
The equality follows by expanding the square, symmetry of W and its row sums nu. P is positive semidefinite, so the left side is exactly the square of the indicated norm. This ground-state form inequality uses the actual hPh identity, not a guessed reciprocal-moment estimate.

Since g-1=Q(Qh-1), (2.1)-(2.3) give
 ||g-1||_(2s')<=epsilon,
 E pi(g-1)^2<=||pi||_s ||g-1||_(2s')^2<=B epsilon^2.         (3.2)
The first bound is a direct critical-endpoint consequence of341's L2 potential estimate; it is stronger than merely its displayed L^(s') estimate but uses no additional assumption. Its exponent is finite for each fixed rho>0. It is fully compatible with344's failure of supremum convergence.

The identity h-pi=h(1-g) and (3.1), with f=(1-g)1_A, imply for EVERY subset A
 ||Q((h-pi)1_A)||_2^2
 <=E pi(1-g)^2 1_A<=B epsilon^2.                            (3.3)
More generally the same proof gives ||Q((h-pi)f)||_2<=sqrt(B)epsilon ||f||_infinity for every bounded real f. It does not give an L2(nu)-to-L2(mu) operator bound, because no replacement of ||f||_infinity by ||f||_(L2(nu)) is justified uniformly. This distinction is the residual rare-set obstruction.

4. AUXILIARY PERRON PROJECTOR AND CUT COERCIVITY
Write lambda_1>=lambda_2 for the top two eigenvalues of T_pi, and D=lambda_1-lambda_2>0. Positivity of pi and of the noise entries gives a simple positive Perron vector w of L2(mu) norm1; choose w>0. Put psi=sqrt(pi), also of norm1. The operator T_pi is positive semidefinite. In addition,
 1<=<psi,T_pi psi>=<pi,P pi>,                               (4.1)
because E pi=1 and the Walsh eigenvalues of P are nonnegative: <pi,Ppi>=1+sum_(S nonempty)rho^|S| pi_hat(S)^2. In particular lambda_1>=1.

The ordinary endpoint estimate also gives lambda_1<=B. To verify this for the correct operator, let U=Q M_sqrt(pi). Then U*U=T_pi and UU*=Q M_pi Q have the same finite spectra. Holder and (2.3) bound the latter's norm by ||pi||_s<=B.

Let z=<w,psi>>0 and d1=lambda_1-1>=0. The spectral decomposition and (4.1) give
 1<=<psi,T_pi psi><=lambda_1 z^2+lambda_2(1-z^2),
 so 1-z^2<=d1/D.                                           (4.2)
The rank-one projector identity
 ||w w* - psi psi*||=sqrt(1-z^2)                             (4.3)
holds for real unit vectors with this positive overlap. For example, in the basis psi,e where w=z psi+sqrt(1-z^2)e, the difference of projectors has trace0 and eigenvalues plus/minus sqrt(1-z^2); it vanishes on the orthogonal complement. This supplies a complete finite proof of the identity rather than a claim that psi is already a Perron vector.

By the spectral theorem,
 T_pi<=lambda_2 I+D w w*
      <=lambda_2 I+D psi psi*+sqrt(D d1) I.                  (4.4)
For v=sqrt(pi)1_A we have ||v||_2^2=q and <psi,v>=q. Therefore
 I_pi(A):=||Q(pi 1_A)||_2^2=<v,T_pi v>
 <=lambda_2 q+D q^2+sqrt(Dd1)q
 =lambda_1 q^2+lambda_2 q(1-q)+sqrt(Dd1)q.                 (4.5)
This estimate is valid for every A and has no hidden dependence on its size. In particular D<=lambda_1<=B since lambda_2>=0.

Use the uniform conclusions (2.2) to choose L0 so that, for all permitted k,pi in every L>=L0,
 lambda_2<=1-3G/4,
 d1+sqrt(B d1)<=G/8.                                       (4.6)
Such a single cutoff exists: G>0 is fixed, d1 tends to0 uniformly, and the limsup bound for lambda_2 is 1-G. Enlarge it if necessary so L>=1 and k is in its stated integer range. The cutoff depends only on rho,C0 via334, not on h,pi,A or b. No explicit numerical value of this existential cutoff is claimed.

For 0<q<=1/2, (4.5)-(4.6) now give
 I_pi(A)<=q-(3G/4)q(1-q)+(d1+sqrt(Bd1))q
          <=(1-G/4)q=(1-c)q.                              (4.7)
Here q^2<=q and 1-q>=1/2 were used. This is auxiliary cut coercivity. It is not yet a bound for the actual chain.

5. ACTUAL CUT CAPACITY AND THE EXPONENTIAL THRESHOLD
The actual internal energy is I_h(A)=||Q(h1_A)||_2^2. Its relation to the actual flux is exact:
 q=E h1_A Ph=I_h(A)+<h1_A,P(h1_Ac)>,
 J(A)=q-I_h(A).                                             (5.1)
By (3.3), the triangle inequality and (4.7),
 sqrt(I_h(A))<=sqrt(I_pi(A))+sqrt(B)epsilon,
 I_h(A)<=I_pi(A)+2 sqrt(B I_pi(A))epsilon+B epsilon^2
         <=(1-c)q+2 sqrt(Bq)epsilon+B epsilon^2.             (5.2)
In the last step I_pi(A)<=q from (4.7). Inserting (5.2) into (5.1) proves (1.2).

If q>=64B epsilon^2/c^2, then
 2 sqrt(B)epsilon/sqrt(q)<=c/4,
 B epsilon^2/q<=c^2/64<=c/64,
because 0<c<1. Thus J(A)/q>=c-c/4-c/64=47c/64>=c/2=G/8. This proves (1.3), with a deliberately nonoptimal threshold constant. Taking the contrapositive gives (1.4). If M0 epsilon^2>1/2 in a finite dimension, (1.3) has no eligible sets; this does not assert otherwise. Eventually the threshold tends to0 uniformly when k>=C0 sqrt(L).

The floor implies q=E pi1_A>=b mu(A), yielding the stated base-mass bound. For larger cuts, replace A by its complement; J is symmetric and the smaller stationary mass is used. Neither the stationary cap nor matching has been transferred to r in this reasoning.

6. SELF-CONTAINED FINITE CHEEGER SWEEP AND GAP CONSEQUENCE
Define Phi=min_{0<nu(A)<=1/2} J(A)/nu(A). The minimum is attained because the state space is finite and has at least two points. We show
 Phi<=sqrt(2 gamma), gamma=1-lambda_2(K).                    (6.1)
This is the usual conductance direction of Cheeger's inequality, proved here to fix the one-sided flux and constants. K is reversible under nu and positive, so its nonconstant second eigenfunction f can be chosen real, with E_nu f=0 and (I-K)f=gamma f. Its positive and negative parts are nonzero; at least one has support of nu-mass at most1/2. After changing sign if needed take v=f_+ with that property.

Let E_K(a,b)=<a,(I-K)b>_nu=(1/2)sum W(x,y)(a(x)-a(y))(b(x)-b(y)). For nonnegative disjointly supported f_+,f_-,
 E_K(f_+,f_-)=-sum W(x,y)f_+(x)f_-(y)<=0.
Therefore E_K(v,v)<=E_K(v,f)=gamma ||v||_(L2(nu))^2.         (6.2)
For thresholds t>0 put A_t={x:v(x)^2>t}. Every nonempty A_t has stationary mass at most1/2. Finite layer-cake integration gives
 integral_0^infinity nu(A_t)dt=||v||_(L2(nu))^2,
 integral_0^infinity J(A_t)dt
 =(1/2)sum W(x,y)|v(x)^2-v(y)^2|.
By Cauchy--Schwarz this last expression is at most
 [E_K(v,v)]^(1/2) [(1/2)sum W(x,y)(v(x)+v(y))^2]^(1/2)
 <=[2 E_K(v,v)||v||_(L2(nu))^2]^(1/2),                     (6.3)
where (a+b)^2<=2(a^2+b^2) and the stationary row sums of W were used. The definition of Phi bounds the same integral below by Phi ||v||_(L2(nu))^2. Dividing by the positive norm and using (6.2) proves (6.1). In particular a minimizing cut has conductance at most sqrt(2 gamma); the proof can equivalently select a threshold with at most this ratio.

If gamma<G^2/128, (6.1) gives Phi<G/8. Applying (1.4) to a minimizing cut proves the gap consequence in section1. This does not claim that every low-gap eigenfunction itself is supported on an exponentially small set, or that the upper bound on bottleneck mass supplies a lower bound on gamma. It only supplies an actual small cut when the gap is sufficiently small.

7. SCOPE, FAILED APPROACHES AND THE REMAINING MECHANISM
The useful no-go statement is quantitative: an almost-resonant rare localized obstruction, if it makes the actual gap small, cannot be witnessed by a cut of stationary mass above a constant times eta^(2k). Prior338 only excluded macroscopic cuts;345 excludes the much larger class of cuts down to an exponentially small matching-dependent scale. Uniform potential convergence was already falsified by344 and is neither assumed nor retried.

The key substitute is a bounded-test energy comparison from hPh, not an L^s bound on r or an assertion that T_pi and K share spectra. The exact auxiliary-projector argument is needed: sqrt(pi) need not be a Perron vector of T_pi. Estimates based only on the auxiliary eigenvalue gap without this projector control would not establish (4.7).

The lower scale q of order epsilon^2 or smaller remains uncontrolled because the absolute energy error in (3.3) becomes order-one relative to q. No admissible collapsing family at that scale has been constructed. No critical r-moment/tail theorem, full operator perturbation, second-eigenvalue bound for K, physical gap or continuum conclusion is proved. The original single question remains open. A future unit must exploit additional structure inside these exponentially small sets, strengthen the local error relative to their mass, or construct an actual near-resonant family with all stationary constraints. Relabeling (3.3) as a full operator bound is forbidden.

The bounded attempts and failures are preserved separately. The candidate produces a substantive capacity reduction, so the three-unproductive-unit stop rule is not triggered by this successful unit. It does not license indefinitely repeating the same bound or making a paper out of a small consequence. No new active mathematical question or publication is opened.

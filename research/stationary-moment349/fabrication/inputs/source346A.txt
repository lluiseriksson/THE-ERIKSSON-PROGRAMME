1. ACTUAL MODEL AND PRECISE CLAIM
Let mu be uniform probability on {-1,1}^L, L>=1, and let P=P_rho with fixed0<rho<1. Thus each bit is retained with probability a=(1+rho)/2, and the Walsh multiplier is rho^|S|. Let Q=P_sqrt(rho), so Q^2=P. Take h>0, normalized by E hPh=1, and write
 g=Ph, pi=hg, s=(1+rho)/(1-rho), B=2^(1/s).
Assume only
 pi>=b>0, E pi^s<=2.                                        (1.1)
In particular E pi=1. Define the actual Doob kernel and its stationary probability
 K(x,y)=P(x,y)h(y)/g(x), nu(x)=mu(x)pi(x).
K is stochastic and reversible because nu(x)K(x,y)=mu(x)P(x,y)h(x)h(y) is symmetric. Exact stationary marginal matching is NOT needed for this theorem; therefore every family in the original k~C sqrt(L) question satisfying (1.1) is included. There is no hypothesis about the weighting density r=(h/g)/E(h/g).

Put
 d=a^L=P(x,x), alpha=2^(-L),
 theta=1-1/(2s),
 kappa=2^(1/s) a (1+rho^3).                                  (1.2)
The letter alpha here is the mass of one cube atom and is unrelated to source334's endpoint constant. The quantities d and alpha are independent of x.

Theorem (candidate). For any nonempty subset A of cardinality m with m d<1, define
 R_L(m)=(sqrt(B)/b) m^theta kappa^(L/2)/(1-m d)^2.             (1.3)
Then, uniformly over every h satisfying (1.1) and every x in the cube,
 K(x,A)<=R_L(m)/(1+R_L(m)).                                  (1.4)
Consequently, if K_A is the kernel killed upon leaving A, its spectral radius is at most the right side of (1.4). Moreover its actual one-sided cut flux satisfies
 J(A)/nu(A)>=1/(1+R_L(m)),
 J(A)=sum_(x in A,y notin A)nu(x)K(x,y).                     (1.5)
The formula applies even if nu(A)>1/2; in the usual conductance definition one uses sets of stationary mass at most1/2, or their complements.

For every fixed rho with kappa<1, and every fixed
 0<tau<min{-log(a), -log(kappa)/(2 theta)},                    (1.6)
all sets m<=exp(tau L) have R_L(m)->0 uniformly as L->infinity. Thus no almost-unit killed eigenvalue can be supported on those geometrically arbitrary sets, and their escape probability tends to1. Logs here and below are natural.

A completely explicit subrange is
 0<rho<=1/4,   m<=exp(rho L/1920).
There, for all L>=1 and all such integer m,
 R_L(m)<=(9 sqrt(B)/b)exp(-rho L/640).                        (1.7)
In particular the killed spectral radii tend to0, not merely remain bounded below1. The exponent and constants are deliberately nonoptimal.

At kappa=1, (1.3) still gives a dimension-independent positive escape bound for singletons. For kappa>1 the displayed estimate need not give any nontrivial asymptotic exclusion even for singletons; specifically kappa>1 at rho=1/2. No result for all0<rho<1, or for larger support sizes, is asserted. The original full stationary-gap question remains open.

2. DIAGONAL CAP ON h AND THE INTERNAL TRANSITION CONTRIBUTION
Positivity of P gives, for every y,
 pi(y)=h(y)(Ph)(y)>=P(y,y)h(y)^2=d h(y)^2.
Therefore h(y)<=sqrt(pi(y)/d). The maximum matrix entry of P is d, because on each coordinate a>(1-rho)/2. It follows that for every x and A,
 I_x(A):=sum_(y in A)P(x,y)h(y)
 <=sqrt(d)sum_(y in A)sqrt(pi(y)).                           (2.1)
The critical cap and uniform atom mass give sum_y pi(y)^s<=2/alpha. Ordinary finite-sum Holder with exponents2s and2s/(2s-1) yields
 sum_(y in A)sqrt(pi(y))
 <=m^(1-1/(2s)) [sum_(y in A)pi(y)^s]^(1/(2s))
 <=sqrt(B) m^theta alpha^(-1/(2s)).                         (2.2)
Thus
 I_x(A)<=sqrt(B d) m^theta alpha^(-1/(2s)).                  (2.3)
For a singleton this is the exact elementary diagonal-cap bound I_x({x})<=sqrt(d pi(x)), followed by pi(x)<=B alpha^(-1/s). A small upper bound on this internal contribution alone does NOT control K(x,A), since its denominator g(x) might also be small. The next section proves a lower bound for the outside contribution instead of assuming a denominator floor.

3. STATIONARY FLOOR, RECIPROCAL CAUCHY AND A SECOND-POTENTIAL BOUND
Write
 O_x(A)=sum_(y notin A)P(x,y)h(y),  p_x=P(x,A^c).
Since pi(y)=h(y)g(y)>=b, we have h(y)>=b/g(y). Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives
 p_x^2
 <=[sum_(y notin A)P(x,y)/g(y)]
   [sum_(y notin A)P(x,y)g(y)].
All terms are positive, and p_x>=1-m d>0 under the theorem's size condition. Also the second bracket is at most (Pg)(x). Therefore
 O_x(A)>=b p_x^2/(Pg)(x)
         >=b(1-m d)^2/(P^2h)(x).                            (3.1)
The direction of this denominator replacement is important: enlarging the positive denominator weakens a lower bound and is legitimate here.

We next bound P^2h pointwise using the energy normalization, without a pointwise claim about Ph. Since
 P^2h=P_(rho^(3/2))(Qh),   ||Qh||_2^2=E hPh=1,
point evaluation and Cauchy in L2(mu) imply
 (P^2h)(x)<= [sum_y P_(rho^(3/2))(x,y)^2/mu(y)]^(1/2).
For the uniform cube, symmetry and the semigroup identity show the quantity under this square root is
 P_(rho^3)(x,x)/alpha
 =[((1+rho^3)/2)^L]/2^(-L)=(1+rho^3)^L.
Equivalently, its Walsh kernel coefficients have squared sum sum_S rho^(3|S|)=(1+rho^3)^L. Thus, for every x,
 (P^2h)(x)<=(1+rho^3)^(L/2),
 O_x(A)>=b(1-m d)^2(1+rho^3)^(-L/2).                       (3.2)
This bound is allowed to be exponentially small in dimension. It is an actual consequence of the floor and hPh relation, not an assumed lower bound for h, g or the weighting density. No exact marginal information or source345 estimate is used.

4. COMBINING THE CONTRIBUTIONS, CAPACITY AND KILLED MODES
Divide (2.3) by the strictly positive lower bound (3.2). Since alpha^(-1/s)=2^(L/s) and d=a^L,
 I_x(A)/O_x(A)
 <=(sqrt(B)/b) m^theta [2^(1/s)a(1+rho^3)]^(L/2)/(1-m d)^2
 =R_L(m).                                                   (4.1)
The actual transition probability is I_x(A)/(I_x(A)+O_x(A)). The scalar function t/(1+t) is increasing for t>=0, so (4.1) proves (1.4). No normalization of an auxiliary operator is substituted.

The restriction K_A has nonnegative entries and every row sum at most r_A:=R_L(m)/(1+R_L(m)). Hence its operator norm on finite coordinate supremum norm is at most r_A, and every eigenvalue has modulus at most r_A. This proves the killed spectral-radius assertion without an assumption of invariant support. Because K is reversible under nu, K_A is also self-adjoint in L2(nu|_A); in fact its eigenvalues are nonnegative by similarity to the corresponding principal submatrix of M_sqrt(h/g) P M_sqrt(h/g), which is positive semidefinite. Thus every real f supported on A satisfies
 <f,Kf>_nu<=r_A ||f||_(L2(nu))^2.                            (4.2)
This is a genuine exclusion of a near-unit localized Rayleigh quotient on that support. It does not assert that a general second eigenfunction must have such support.

For x in A, (1.4) also gives K(x,A^c)>=1-r_A. Integrating against nu over A proves J(A)>=(1-r_A)nu(A), namely (1.5). No factor1/2 is missing because J is the one-sided flux. For a singleton, the statement is exactly an upper bound on K(x,x) and a lower bound on its escape probability.

5. PARAMETER REGION AND EXPLICIT EXPONENTIAL-CARDINALITY RANGE
Suppose kappa<1 and tau satisfies (1.6). If m<=exp(tau L), then
 m d<=exp[(tau+log a)L]->0,
 m^theta kappa^(L/2)<=exp[(theta tau+(1/2)log kappa)L]->0.

For the explicit range0<rho<=1/4, compute

 <=rho[1+rho^2-2log2/(1+rho)]
 <=rho[17/16-(8/5)log2]
 <-rho/240.                                                 (5.1)
Here log(1+x)<=x, rho^2<=1/16 and (1+rho)^(-1)>=4/5 were used. To verify the last strict numerical comparison without a decimal approximation, log2>2/3: for example log2=2 integral_0^(1/3)(1-t^2)^(-1)dt>2/3. Hence (8/5)log2>16/15, and16/15-17/16=1/240.

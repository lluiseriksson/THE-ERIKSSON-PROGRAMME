Question: can the actual low residual lie in the lift image S=JZ of290?
Candidate stronger answer: for the quadratic dyadic cut and the fixed
polarization v=(0,1,-1)/sqrt(2), no nonzero l in the low sector L_v has
r(l) in S. Consequently the residual modulo S is injective on L_v.
The tentative real dimension of L_v is n/2-1 (must check complex-to-real
count explicitly against the paid spectrum). This would rule out using
the290 lift alone to cancel even this whole low polarization sector.
It does NOT estimate residual energy or refute the quadratic recovery cut.

Let G(u)_i=sum_(j,l)u(i,j,l) be full coordinate1-sectional sums.
For a field with T=0 and P-value in S, G(u)=0: sum the actual cell sums
recovered by multiplying each original row by sqrt(n^3 |cell|). The four
entries of J give plane a,-a and long-third -a,a, other entries zero.
Every diagonal low field also has G(l)=0 because its scalar profile has
zero mean. Thus r(l) in S forces G(f_T(l))=0.

Write alpha=M_Tdagger T_L l, f=B^-1 T_F* alpha, so f_T=-f.
Paid range gives M_T alpha=T_L l. If T_L l is nonzero, then
 <alpha,T_L l>=||B^-1/2 T_F*alpha||^2>0.
For l in L_v, T_L l=n^-3/2 phi(i+tau_i) v, where phi is mean-zero.
For the dyadic quadratic cut f(i)=i+tau_i is a permutation, so
sum_i phi(i+tau_i)=0 and T_L l is nonzero for nonzero l.

Candidate key lemma: G(B^-1 T_F* alpha)=0 forces the scalar sequence
alpha_v(i)=<v,alpha_i> to be constant, when all tau_i are even.
It suffices to inspect frequency pairs (t,0,0) and (t+N,N,N), N=n/2.
For t nonzero, both are off diagonal and are entirely in O subset F.
The vector v is orthogonal to g at both frequencies, so this common
polarization reduces the actual restricted two-by-two Hermitian block.
Let theta=2pi t/n, D_t=4sin^2(theta/2), D'_t=12-D_t,
d_t=A0 D_t-c, e_t=A0 D'_t-c, gamma_t=2i sin(theta).
The B block on v is
       [ d_t, epsilon B0 gamma_t ;
         epsilon B0 conjugate(gamma_t), e_t ].
Here A0=(H+a)/2, B0=(H-a)/2 as in290. Positivity follows from O subsetF.

Let p_t be the unitary one-dimensional Fourier coefficient of alpha_v.
The T* input to these two modes is a common positive normalization
n^-5/2 times (p_t,p_(t+N)): tau_i even removes the factor(-1)^tau_i
in the partner sample phase. Projection to U does not change the v
component, because v belongs to both actual Fourier coexact fibers.
No low projection contributes because both frequencies are in O.
The first inverse-block component is proportional to
        e_t p_t - epsilon B0 gamma_t p_(t+N).
G(f)=0 makes this zero for each nonzero t.

If t and t+N are both nonzero, apply the same equation to t+N:
 e_t p_t = epsilon B0 gamma_t p_(t+N),
 e_(t+N) p_(t+N) = -epsilon B0 gamma_t p_t.
Because D'_t>=8, e_t>=8A0-c>2B0>=|B0 gamma_t| for n>=8,c>0,
the two strict contraction inequalities force both coefficients to zero.
The t=N exception is separate: gamma_N=0 and e_N>0, so p_N=0.
No t=0 equation is needed (constant coexact fields are absent).
Thus p_t=0 for every nonzero t and alpha_v is constant.

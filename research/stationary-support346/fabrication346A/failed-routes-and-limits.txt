One original stationary-gap question; analytical reasoning only. No345 conclusion is used while that source is under separate audit. No new active question or paper.

1. Parent-supplied critical Holder identity: valid but insufficient.
For u=h/(Ph), h^2=pi u and p=2s/(s+1)=1+rho,
 E h^p1_A=E pi^(p/2)u^(p/2)1_A
 <=(E pi^s1_A)^(p/(2s))(E u1_A)^(p/2),
so ||h1_A||_p^2<=(E pi^s1_A)^(1/s) E u1_A. Accepted341 supplies E u<=1. Hypercontractivity controls the internal energy by the same expression. This is elementary and was not counted as an advance. The missing step is a relative comparison between E u1_A and q=E pi1_A on exponentially small sets. Global normalization and small total Hellinger discrepancy do not supply it. No critical r-moment hypothesis is introduced to bridge the gap.

The previously proposed nonlinear inequality
 E(fPf)<=E(f) ||fPf||_s^(1/2)
remains unproved and is not a premise or result here. No valid general proof or counterexample was obtained. It was preserved, not silently accepted, rebranded or rerun as an established route.

2. Small diagonal contribution alone: insufficient.
For a point x, positivity and the critical cap give
 P(x,x)h(x)<=sqrt(B)[2^(1/s)(1+rho)/2]^(L/2).
Even if this quantity is small, K(x,x) divides it by g(x), which may also be small. Assuming g(x)>=sqrt(b), or a uniform g->1 upgrade, would be unjustified; the latter was already refuted by accepted344. The point calculation alone is therefore not a resonance exclusion. The successful proof supplies a lower bound for the outside contribution using the actual stationary floor, rather than making such a denominator assumption.

An additional endpoint reverse-Holder comparison was considered. With r=1+1/rho, t=r/(r+1) and k_x(y)=P(x,y)/mu(y), ||g||_r<=1 follows from Q:L2->Lr and ||Qh||2=1. Holder implies E[k_x1_{y!=x}/g]>=(E[k_x^t1_{y!=x}])^(1/t), where the bracket is exactly c_t^L-((1+rho)^t/2)^L and c_t=((1+rho)^t+(1-rho)^t)/2. This supplies another finite lower bound on the outside contribution after multiplication by b. A uniform comparison covering every rho was not obtained from it. It is not used in source346A, is not a claimed full-gap repair, and no numerical optimization of its exponents occurred.

3. Stationary floor plus second-potential energy: successful restricted theorem.
Weighted reciprocal Cauchy outside an arbitrary set A gives O_x(A)>=b P(x,A^c)^2/(P^2h)(x). The exact cube kernel and energy normalization give P^2h(x)<=(1+rho^3)^(L/2). The critical cap controls the sum of sqrt(pi) over A, yielding the finite ratio R_L(m) in source346A. This excludes killed near-unit modes on small-cardinality supports when kappa=2^(1/s)((1+rho)/2)(1+rho^3)<1.
The proof pays every parameter explicitly for0<rho<=1/4 and m<=exp(rho L/1920). It does not simply specialize an abstract two-block matrix; the inequality holds for every actual admissible hPh family. It does not require stationary matching and therefore applies to all matching families in that noise region.

Boundary falsifier and scope.
At rho=1/2, kappa=27*2^(1/3)/32>1 by the exact integer cube comparison39366>32768. Thus the present sufficient criterion does not extend uniformly to all rho. This is a failure of that attempted extension, not an admissible counterexample, proof of a resonant family, or evidence of a sharp phase boundary. Large supports remain uncontrolled even where kappa<1. A small stationary mass need not mean small cardinality: q>=b |A|2^(-L) permits exponentially many vertices at q of order exp(-constant*sqrt(L)).
The original question therefore remains open.346 narrows which localized mechanisms are possible in a stated region; it does not claim an original-gap solution. The next substantive task must improve outside control in the remaining parameter/support regime or construct a valid collective well with all stationary conditions. Repeating the same one-point cap calculation without a denominator bound is abandoned. This successful bounded theorem does not constitute three unsuccessful units, but no further paper-sized claims should be manufactured from its elementary consequences.

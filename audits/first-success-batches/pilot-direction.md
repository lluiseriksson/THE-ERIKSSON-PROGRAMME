# A diagnostic observation before selecting an inspection order

Under two specified target regimes with identical external proxy rankings, let $A>0$ be the excess cost of the harder-first order under the first regime, and $B>0$ the excess cost of the easier-first order under the second. For a pilot observation with laws $P_1,P_2$, let $e_i$ be the probability of choosing the easier-first order in regime $i$. The increases over the regime-specific best of those two orders are $A(1-e_1)$ and $Be_2$, before paying for the pilot.

Because an action based on the pilot is a bounded randomized statistic of its observation, $e_1-e_2\le \lVert P_1-P_2\rVert_{\rm TV}$. If both excesses are at most $v$, then $e_1-e_2\ge 1-v(1/A+1/B)$. Thus $v\ge AB(1-\lVert P_1-P_2\rVert_{\rm TV})/(A+B)$. A pilot independent of regime cannot beat the no-pilot minimax loss. Two disjoint pilot laws identify the regime and can remove all of the directional loss. In either case the pilot itself must be charged.

For one diagnostic label on a selected task, let $p_1,p_2$ be its passing probabilities, and $x,y$ the chances of selecting the easier-first order following a failed or passing label. Then $e_i=(1-p_i)x+p_i y$. The worst loss is the maximum of two affine functions on the unit square. At least one minimizer is a corner or an intersection of the equal-loss line with an edge: each half of the square divided by that line has a linear objective. The stored program checks these vertices with exact fractions for all 118 tasks. It treats the pilot and the subsequent evaluation panel as independently drawn conditional on the regime; reusing the same pilot slot inside the panel would change the conditional costs.

The underlying target regimes and task profiles are known to this two-point design calculation. It asks how well one can distinguish those regimes at a stated cost. It cannot by itself establish that a pilot selected this way generalizes to a previously unseen model.


**Result for the stated two-regime experiment.** Of the 118 tasks, HumanEval/74 is the sole minimizing diagnostic. It has 98/100 passes for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and 8/100 for Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct. After a failed pilot choose harder-first. After a passing pilot choose easier-first with probability 0.973850. The two excess costs agree at 2.863910 checks before charging the extra diagnostic. Including that check gives 3.863910, versus 23.182959 for the optimal target-blind mixture. The pilot selection used both historical profiles: this is a two-regime information calculation on fixed banks. The exact risks for every task appear in [the result file](pilot-direction.json).

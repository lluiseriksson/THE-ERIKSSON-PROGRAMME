# Finite negative bias with two potential trials per task

Let there be \(m\ge2\) independent tasks. On task \(i\), two independent potential trials have the same success probability \(p_i\). Write \(S\) for the number of successes in all \(2m\) trials. Let \(Z\) be the manuscript's canonical score calculated from logs stopped on the first success. Its weight on an all-hit record with \(s\) preceding failures is \(\binom{m+s-1}{s}/\binom ms\); otherwise it is zero.

**Theorem.** For every finite \(m\) and every probability vector in the closed cube,
\[
 \Pr(S\ge m)-E Z\ \ge\
 \sum_{i<j}(p_i-p_j)^2\prod_{\ell\ne i,j}2p_\ell(1-p_\ell).
\]
In the interior, equality of the score expectation with the target holds exactly when all probabilities coincide. In fact \(E[Z\,1_{\{S=k\}}]\le\Pr(S=k)\) for each \(k\ge m\). No limit, minimum variance across tasks, or rare-event assumption is needed. This controls the expectation; a realized score can exceed one.

**Proof.** First take interior probabilities, let \(q_i=1-p_i,\theta_i=p_i/q_i\), and remove the common factor \(D=\prod q_i^2\) from all probabilities. A particular labelled trial outcome of count vector \(r\) then has weight \(\prod\theta_i^{r_i}\). For a vector \(r\), let \(M_r\) be this monomial averaged over permutations of its exponents. Group the equally numerous permutations of the labelled outcomes on a fixed stratum \(S=k\). Each orbit of count vectors contributes \(N_r M_r\) to the true mass after removal of \(D\), where \(N_r\) is the number of labelled outcomes with its unordered count profile. In particular \(\sum_r N_r=\binom{2m}k\).

For \(k\ge m\), a nonzero stopped score is supported on the single unordered profile \(\beta\) with \(k-m\) twos and \(2m-k\) ones. Task relabelling preserves the score. Consequently the entire score-weighted mass on this stratum, with \(D\) removed, is \(b_k M_\beta\). To obtain \(b_k\), put every odds equal to \(t>0\). Homogeneous unbiasedness for every success probability gives a polynomial identity after removing \(D\). Equating the coefficients of \(t^k\) gives \(b_k=\binom{2m}k\). For \(k<m\) the score vanishes identically.

If \(r\ne\beta\), its zeros can be eliminated by pairing a two with a zero and replacing both by ones. Each replacement decreases the symmetric monomial mean: fixing the other slots and averaging the two orientations gives \((x^2+y^2)/2-xy=(x-y)^2/2\) times a nonnegative remaining product. Repeating ends at \(\beta\). Hence the stratum deficit divided by \(D\) is \(\sum_r N_r(M_r-M_\beta)\ge0\). This elementary balancing step is a special case of [Muirhead's symmetric-mean inequality](https://doi.org/10.1017/S001309150003460X).

At \(k=m\), retain just the profile with one double, one zero, and all other counts one. For a doubled slot \(i\) and an empty slot \(j\) there are \(2^{m-2}\) labelled outcomes. Pairing its two orientations and subtracting the balanced monomials gives \(2^{m-2}\sum_{i<j}(\theta_i-\theta_j)^2\prod_{\ell\ne i,j}\theta_\ell\). Multiplying back by \(D\) gives the claimed quadratic lower bound. All discarded contributions are nonnegative. Continuity extends the result to boundary probabilities. With two tasks this bound is an equality, recovering the previously derived squared difference. The characterization of equality of the target and expected score in the interior follows from the bound and homogeneous unbiasedness.

**Extension to any common cap.** The stratum proof works for \(k=m\) and \(k=m+1\) for every common cap \(n\ge2\): there is again just one all-positive profile \(\beta\), respectively all ones or one two with the rest ones. Every admissible count vector balances down to it. For exponent entries \(a\ge b+2\), the drop in the symmetrized monomial under \((a,b)\mapsto(a-1,b+1)\) is a nonnegative multiple of \((xy)^b(x-y)(x^{a-b-1}-y^{a-b-1})\). The calibration coefficient is now \(\binom{mn}k\). Also, for \(mn-k<n\), all tasks hit and every possible wait sum \(s<n\) has weight one since truncation leaves the coefficient at \(s\) unchanged. These statements carry no general sign conclusion for the intervening strata at larger caps.

**Implication for evaluation.** In a two-attempt design a downward discrepancy of the calibrated mean from the full pooled-success target has a definite direction for every finite sample size. This target counts repeat successes on the same task. Applying the result to dependent or changing per-attempt probabilities would require a different argument.

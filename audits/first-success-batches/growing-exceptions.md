# Growing heterogeneity at a fixed pooled success count

Consider a bank of m independent tasks with n independent attempts per task, a common probability within each task, and fixed n at least two. Write S for the number of hits in all mn potential attempts. Stop each task at the first hit. The score Z is zero if any task exhausts its attempts. Otherwise, when the total number of misses before those hits is s, the score is the ratio of the unrestricted and capped counts of compositions of s into m nonnegative parts, with each capped part less than n.

The original [fixed-group comparison](fixed-group-all-strata.md) and the [supercritical sparse-group analysis](sparse-supercritical.md) address a fixed group across strata and a growing group after averaging over the count, respectively. Here a growing group is evaluated **within each prescribed interior count stratum**, with a uniform logarithmic error. This matters when two synthetic evaluation panels are matched on their exact observed total.

## Statement

Let the background task probability be interior. Let r named replacements have individual failure odds equal to t_i times the background failure odds, with 0 <= t_i < infinity. Set a=k/(mn) in a fixed compact interval I inside (1/n,1) and take 1 <= r=o(m). Define F_a(t)=E[t^(n-B)] for B binomial with parameters (n,a). For G_a(t)=E[t^(n-W)], choose U on {0,...,n-1} with probabilities proportional to z^u, choosing z>0 so that E U=(1-a)/a. Given U, take W=1+Bin(n-1-U,a). Set R_a=G_a/F_a and use 0^0=1.

Uniformly in k and in the individual odds, the conditional expected score obeys
\[
 \left|\log E_{\rm het}[Z\mid S=k]-\sum_{i=1}^r\log R_a(t_i)\right|
 \le C_{n,I}\,r\sqrt{(r+\log m)/m}
\]
for sufficiently large m along every such sequence. An infinite failure-odds replacement has zero score exactly. For large m the interior stratum remains attainable from the background tasks alone even with deterministic replacements (account for any forced successes). Endpoints of I may be chosen arbitrarily inside the given open interval.

## Proof

Write P_k for the homogeneous full law conditional on S=k and Q_k=Z P_k, a probability by homogeneous calibration. The change to heterogeneous odds gives the conditional score as the ratio of the Q_k and P_k expectations of the product of t_i raised to the failure counts of the designated tasks. This follows by multiplying the task likelihoods on full labelled strings and cancelling constants; at t=0 take the polynomial limit.

Under each law the histogram of site failure counts concentrates around its respective one-site limiting probability vector, denoted by pi, on {0,...,n} for P_k and {0,...,n-1} for Q_k. We first show that for small e with e >= C/m the probability of a maximum coordinate error greater than e is at most C m^C exp(-c m e^2), for constants uniform on I. Under P_k start from m independent binomial sites of success probability a and condition on exactly k successes. This costs at most C sqrt(m) by Stirling at the binomial mean. Before conditioning each category frequency is the mean of independent indicators.

Under Q_k the total wait s is the position of the m-th hit minus m in a uniform string with k hits. The count follows by multiplying the number of wait compositions by the score and by the number of completions, giving mass binom(m+s-1,s) binom(m(n-1)-s,k-m)/binom(mn,k). Counts of hits in prefixes of lengths near m/a plus or minus m e bracket that position. Their means differ from m by at least c m e once the rounding error is absorbed. Hoeffding's bound for sampling without replacement gives probability at most C exp(-c m e^2) outside the bracket. All central prefixes fit inside the string uniformly on I. [Hoeffding, 1963](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830).

For central s, generate m independent tilted waits with mean s/m. After each forced hit fill the remaining n-1-U slots independently with success probability a_s=(k-m)/(m(n-1)-s). Condition on the sum of waits being s and on the total suffix successes being k-m. This is exactly the Q_k law given s. Indeed all capped compositions of s have equal tilted probability, the total number of free suffix slots is then fixed, and every qualifying suffix configuration has equal probability. The first conditioning event has probability at least c/sqrt(m) by the bounded-lattice Fourier estimate in the [one-site proof](interior-count-order.md). The tilt lies in a compact positive interval. Given that event, the second count is binomial at its integer mean with order m slots and parameter in a compact interior interval, so its probability is at least c/sqrt(m). Before either conditioning the completed site counts are independent with the same law. The inverse tilt is Lipschitz on the central interval because the derivative of its mean with respect to log z is its variance, uniformly positive. The function a_s is also Lipschitz in s/m there and equals a at s/m=(1-a)/a. Each ideal one-site category probability therefore changes by at most C times the wait-mean error. Apply the independent-indicator bound, divide by the conditioning probability, and use a sufficiently small constant multiple of e for the wait bracket. This proves the stated concentration estimate for Q_k.

On the correct support, the ideal category masses for each law have a common positive lower bound over I. Given a histogram within e of pi, each ordered r-site pattern has probability within multiplicative factors exp(plus or minus C(r e+r^2/m)) of its independent pi probability. Exchangeability gives sampling without replacement. At draw j the difference between its conditional category probability and the ideal probability is bounded by C(e+j/m). Ratios and logarithms satisfy the same bound once r/m and e are small.

For each site divide t^y by max(1,t)^d where d=n for P_k and d=n-1 for Q_k. The factors lie in [0,1]. Their independent expectations are at least b>0 uniformly in t, by the probabilities of y=0 and y=d. The ideal product expectation is thus at least b^r. Summing the pattern comparison gives the same multiplicative bounds on the good event. The bad event contributes at most its probability, hence at most its probability divided by b^r in relative units. Choose e=K sqrt((r+log m)/m) with K large enough that this latter error is at most m^-2. The lower bound includes the good-event probability. Taking logarithms yields an error O(r e+r^2/m+m^-2) under each law. The deterministic normalizations cancel in the comparison to the respective ideal expectations. Subtract the logarithms and absorb the smaller errors to obtain the claim.

## Consequences and a check with matched means

Uniformly on I one has log R_a(t) <= -c ((t-1)/(t+1))^2. The [one-site convex order](interior-count-order.md) gives strict inequality G_a<F_a for finite t other than one, including zero by its endpoint hinge. At one the first derivatives agree and the strictly positive full-minus-weighted variance gives strictly negative curvature for log R_a, uniformly by compactness. At infinity the degree of the numerator is one smaller than that of the denominator, with leading coefficients uniformly bounded away from zero. Compactness covers the remaining t. In particular a positive fraction of odds bounded away from one in this compactified coordinate gives exponential suppression in r on **every** stratum in I when r diverges. Smaller contrasts also suffice if their mean square dominates the error per site.

For n=3, background probability 1/2, even m and k=3m/2, change r/2 pairs to probabilities 2/3 and 1/3. The expected total count is exactly k before and after the change. Here z=1, F(t)=(1+t)^3/8 and G(t)=(1+4t+7t^2)/12. The one-site ratios at 1/2 and 2 are 76/81 and 74/81. Consequently for every diverging even r=o(m),
\[
 \lim (E[Z\mid S=3m/2])^{2/r}=5624/6561 .
\]
Even after matching the total at its mean, the conditional expected score tends to zero. The conclusion concerns this stopped score under independent synthetic trials. Dependence among retries, the endpoint strata, the general finite sign and external priority remain open here. This supplement postdates the assessed PDF.

In the homogeneous bank its value is exactly one on every eligible stratum.

# How approximate homogeneity changes first-success scoring

Research note for PR334, subsequent to its assessed PDF. An exact impossibility and a quantitative approximation have different implications for benchmark design.

**Grouped probabilities.** Let m≥2 batches be divided into g≥2 nonempty groups, each with its own unknown within-group success probability. Suppose at least one batch permits more than one attempt. There is no nonnegative score unbiased for at least m total full-budget successes throughout any nonempty open subset of the g-dimensional probability cube, even a narrow neighborhood of equal probabilities.

Every finite record has positive probability in the interior. Unbiasedness with a finite target implies finite conditional means. The expected score and the full target are polynomials; equality on an open set extends to the entire cube. Restricting to equal probabilities then forces zero means on records missing a success in any batch. Choose a group not containing a particular batch with extra capacity. Write r for its number of batches. Hold other probabilities in (0,1) and let the chosen group probability x approach zero. Expected score is O(x^r) because successes must occur in all r of its batches. The outside groups have at least m−r+1 trials. Therefore the full target has a positive term of order at most x^(r−1): require successful trials outside and exactly max(0,m−T_out) successful trials inside. This contradicts the order bound. With one common probability the constructive estimator from the paper applies; with every cap one the fully observed threshold indicator applies.

**The two-batch bias law.** Give each of two batches two attempts; let their probabilities be p₁,p₂ and qᵢ=1−pᵢ. The homogeneous optimum Z scores zero if either batch fails, one if both hit, and an extra two if both hits are late. The full-budget probability B of at least two successes equals the probability both hit plus p₁²q₂²+p₂²q₁². Thus E[Z]−B = −(p₁q₂−p₂q₁)² = −(p₁−p₂)². For any 0≤δ≤1 the sharp worst absolute bias on |p₁−p₂|≤δ equals δ² among all nonnegative scores exactly unbiased for every homogeneous probability. To prove minimality, symmetrize any such score by uniformly exchanging batch labels. On the equal-probability model, nonnegativity and coefficient matching force this symmetrized conditional mean to equal Z at every log: two immediate hits have weight one, one early plus one late has mean one, and two late have weight three. Label symmetrization cannot increase worst absolute bias on the symmetric domain. The supremum therefore equals δ² with the explicit rule.

**A signed-score tradeoff on the full cube.** Even allowing negative conditional means as low as −b for 0≤b≤1 leaves minimum worst absolute bias 1−b, when exact unbiasedness on the homogeneous diagonal is required. After label symmetrization the all-failure score is zero by letting p→0. Equating first-order coefficients on the diagonal gives score(early hit, failure)+score(late hit, failure)=0. The latter is ≥−b, hence the former is ≤b. At (p₁,p₂)=(1,0) the early-hit/failure log is certain and B=1. The lower bound also holds as an interior supremum by continuity. For attainability mix Z with weight 1−b and the exactly heterogeneous-unbiased signed score with weight b. The latter has minimum −1 and takes value +1 on any two-hit record, +1 on one early hit with one failure, −1 on one late hit with one failure, and zero on two failures. The mixture has floor at least −b and bias −(1−b)(p₁−p₂)².

All results concern the specified independent Bernoulli experiment. In particular a small departure from equality can rule out exact unbiased nonnegative scoring while causing only a quadratically small bias in its canonical symmetric two-batch score.

# Uniform sign near certain success for arbitrary numbers of batches and attempts

In each of $m\ge2$ independent batches, $n\ge2$ potential independent trials have common success probability $p_i$ within batch $i$. The shared cap is $n$. The target $P$ is the chance of at least $m$ successes in the full $T=mn$ trials. In a batch we observe its first hit or exhaustion. The canonical homogeneous score $Z$ is zero unless every batch has a hit. For waits $u_i$ (failures preceding that hit), $s=\sum u_i$, it then equals $\binom{m+s-1}{m-1}/A_s$, where $A_s$ counts the vectors in $\{0,\dots,n-1\}^m$ summing to $s$.

**Theorem.** Set $q_i=1-p_i$, $t=\max_i q_i$, $A=\binom{m+n-1}{m-1}-m$, $B=3^T+2^m\binom{T}{m}$ and $\epsilon=m/(2A T(T-1) B)$. For $0<t\le\epsilon$,
$$
P-\mathbb E Z \ \ge\ \frac{m}{4A}\,t^{n-2}(\max_i q_i-\min_i q_i)^2 .
$$
In particular the score strictly underestimates the target whenever the probabilities differ in this region, including cases where some batches succeed with certainty. The bound is uniform over the degree and direction of heterogeneity at fixed $m,n$. The deliberately conservative $\epsilon$ is an existence bound. The theorem does not identify the largest region of validity.

**First nonzero order.** Let $D=P-\mathbb E Z$. Removing from each side the probability that every batch has a hit gives the useful expansion. The latter probability is $\prod_i(1-q_i^n)$. The full target differs from one only in degree at least $mn-m+1>n$, since at least this many failures are needed. The score exceeds one on its support only from total wait $s=n$ onward. At $s=n$ the unlimited compositions excluded by the cap are exactly the $m$ vectors with one coordinate equal to $n$. The excess weight per surviving vector is $m/A$. Since the probability of a wait vector is $\prod_i(1-q_i)q_i^{u_i}$, the degree-$n$ term of $D$ is exactly
$$
L(x)=\sum_i x_i^n-\frac mA\sum_{\sum u_i=n,\ 0\le u_i<n}x^u .
$$
There are no lower terms. On equal probabilities $D=0$ identically: on the stopped side summation over waits gives the probability that the $m$th success arrives by time $T$ in a sequence with the same Bernoulli parameter.

**Quantitative gap.** Normalize $x=q/t$, relabel so $\max x_i=1$, and write $y=\min x_i$. By symmetry the sum of $u_i$ over the $A$ vectors is $nA/m$ for each coordinate. Consequently $L$ is $m/A$ times the sum, over these vectors, of the weighted arithmetic-geometric mean gaps $\sum_i(u_i/n)x_i^n-x^u$. Each term is nonnegative. Keep the vectors assigning exponents $n-1,1$ in the two orders to a maximum and a minimum. For $n>2$ the sum of the retained gaps is $(1-y)(1-y^{n-1})\ge(1-y)^2$. For $n=2$ there is only one such vector and its gap is $(1-y)^2/2$. Thus $L(x)\ge m(1-y)^2/(2A)$ for every design.

**Remainder uniform up to the diagonal.** The sum of absolute monomial coefficients of $D$ is at most $B$. For the target, expand the full independent Bernoulli outcomes in the $q_i$. The total bound obtained by giving a success factor norm two and a failure factor norm one is $3^T$. For the score the bound is $2^m\sum_{s=0}^{m(n-1)}\binom{m+s-1}{m-1}=2^m\binom{T}{m}$. Write $D(tx)=t^n[L(x)+tR_t(x)]$. For $0<t\le1$, $R_t$ has coefficient norm at most $B$ and degree at most $T$. Every homogeneous component of $D$ vanishes at the all-ones vector since $D$ vanishes on the diagonal. Its first partial derivatives there coincide by symmetry and sum to zero by Euler's identity. This holds also for $R_t$. Along the segment from the all-ones vector to $x$ the absolute second directional derivative is at most $T(T-1)B(1-y)^2$, by differentiating monomials on the unit cube. Taylor's integral formula now gives $|R_t(x)|\le T(T-1)B(1-y)^2/2$. Substituting the definition of $\epsilon$ gives the stated bound, even for arbitrarily close unequal probabilities.

**Scope and literature.** This is an analytic supplement to the first-success manuscript. Hoeffding's original comparison of full Poisson-binomial sums under an average constraint concerns a different comparison distribution; here both laws use the same heterogeneous potential trials and the second expectation scores censored observations. The stopped score is defined by the manuscript. No empirical model reliability, priority claim, or new exact-PDF assessment is inferred here.

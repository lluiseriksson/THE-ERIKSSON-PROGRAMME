# The first stratum with two all-hit profiles, in every dimension

**Theorem.** In the independent common-cap experiment defined in profile-transport.md, if \(m,n\ge2\) then
\[
 E[Z\,1_{\{S=m+2\}}]\le \Pr(S=m+2)
\]
for every \(p_1,\ldots,p_m\in[0,1]\). Equal probabilities give equality. For cap two the earlier theorem already gives every stratum. The following proof covers every common cap at least three, including arbitrarily large caps and numbers of tasks. It treats a single stratum; other strata with several all-hit profiles remain open in unrestricted dimensions.

**Reduction to one scalar.** Put \(k=m+2\). There are two all-hit count profiles: \(A=(3,1,\ldots,1)\) and \(B=(2,2,1,\ldots,1)\), with the entries permuted. \(B\) is the most balanced profile of total \(k\). Every profile with a triple or more in some task majorizes \(A\): successive transfers can retain a chosen triple while filling each other slot to one, because the total equals \(3+(m-1)\). Define the full probability measure on the labelled \(k\)-success stratum by uniform counting. Define a second measure by weighting each labelled outcome by \(Z\). It has the same total mass by homogeneous calibration. Write \(u\) for the uniform probability of some task having at least three successes, and \(v\) for the weighted probability of \(A\). If \(u\ge v\), match mass \(v\) from the triple-or-higher group to \(A\), and the complement to \(B\). The majorization comparison in profile-transport.md gives the required result for arbitrary task odds. For \(m=2,3\) an even simpler match works: every profile with an empty task must have a count at least three since \(2(m-1)<m+2\). Leave the all-hit full mass in place, because \(w_s\ge1\); every surplus then majorizes both demands. Below suppose \(m\ge4\).

**A collision upper bound.** Conditional on the total wait \(s\) under the weighted measure, the first-hit waits are uniform over capped compositions of \(s\). Indeed each wait vector has the same weight \(w_s\) and the same number of possible completions, \(\binom R2\), where \(R=m(n-1)-s\). The remaining two successes are a uniform pair of unseen positions. Thus \(v\) is the probability of a collision in one task. For any \(R\ge2\) its conditional probability is at most \(2/(m+1)\).

To see this, the unseen counts are a uniform weak composition of \(R\) into \(m\) parts conditioned on all parts at most \(N=n-1\). For an unconditioned uniform composition, the expected number of pairs sharing a part is \(2\binom R2/(m+1)\); for example, use \(\sum_{t\ge0}\binom t2 z^t=z^2/(1-z)^3\) and symmetry. Conditioning on the cap can only lower the second factorial moment of one part. To verify this, for a first part \(t\le N\) the bounded-to-unbounded marginal mass ratio is proportional to the probability that every part in an unrestricted composition of \(R-t\) into \(m-1\) parts is at most \(N\). That probability increases weakly with \(t\). A coupling in the other direction adds balls to parts with probabilities proportional to their current size plus one. It preserves uniformity: the total incoming predecessor weights at a vector of sum \(d+1\) are proportional to the sum of its entries, hence are constant. Above \(N\) the ratio is zero. The difference of the two marginal laws is therefore nonpositive in the tails and nonnegative on one middle interval, with identical mass and mean \(R/m\). Subtract from the convex function \(\binom t2\) the affine chord through the ends of the middle interval. Its product with the difference of the laws is everywhere nonpositive (use a supporting line if the interval is a singleton). This proves the collision bound.

**Full-stratum lower bound.** Let \(X\) be the number of tasks having at least three successes under uniform sampling of \(k=m+2\) of \(mn\) slots. The marginal probability of exactly three successes is at least \(1/81\). Prespecify three slots of one task and require them all to be selected. Conditional on that event, the probability the remaining \(m-1\) selections avoid its other \(n-3\) slots is at least \((1-1/m)^{m-1}\ge1/3\): at every selection the forbidden fraction is at most \((n-3)/(mn-k+1)\le1/m\). Sum over the \(\binom n3\) disjoint exact-three choices and use \((k)_3/(mn)_3\ge1/n^3\) and \(\binom n3/n^3\ge1/27\), where subscripts denote falling factorials. Hence \(E X\ge m/81\). Threshold indicators on different tasks have nonpositive covariance: conditional on the first task's count, the second task is sampled with fewer draws as this count rises, making its threshold probability nonincreasing. Therefore \(\operatorname{Var}X\le E X\), and Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(u=\Pr(X>0)\ge m/(m+81)\). For \(m\ge14\) this is at least \(2/(m+1)\).

**Small task counts, all caps.** The number of \(k\)-subsets without a triple is
\[
 H_m(n)=\sum_{j=2}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}
 \binom mj\binom{m-j}{k-2j}\binom n2^j n^{k-2j}.
\]
To prove \(u\ge2/(m+1)\) it suffices that \(G_m(n)=(m-1)\binom{mn}{k}-(m+1)H_m(n)\ge0\). Its degree is at most \(m+2\). In uniform.json the complete integer Newton coefficients of \(G_m\) at 3 for \(5\le m\le13\), and at 4 for \(m=4\), are nonnegative. Each coefficient is computed by forward differences of the displayed polynomial; the next difference vanishes. The basis \(\binom{n-n_0}{i}\) is nonnegative at every integer \(n\ge n_0\). This proves the assertion for *every* such cap, rather than just the interpolation points. The remaining design \(m=4,n=3\) is verified directly by enumerating labelled outcomes at \(k=6\) and comparing their two total masses on triple profiles. Substitution in the scalar reduction and polynomial continuity complete the proof.

**Earlier attempt and evidence.** Bounding the conditional collision by \((n-2)/(R-1)\) leads to the coarser upper bound \((n-2)\binom{mn}{k-1}/(2\binom{mn}k)\), by summing over waits. The Newton coefficient certificate for that estimate succeeds for \(6\le m\le84\), but fails to certify \(m=2,3,4,5\). Its retained file two-extra.json describes failures of the sufficient test. The bounded-composition comparison resolves the gap with the small-case checks above. The finite code also compares bounded composition moments by enumeration; the all-dimension moment bound is analytic. No assertion of bibliographic priority or empirical model performance is intended.

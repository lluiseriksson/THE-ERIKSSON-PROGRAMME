# A fixed group of changed tasks: every count stratum at sufficiently large bank size

Fix an attempt cap \(n\ge2\) and a group size \(r\ge1\). There are \(m>r\) independent tasks and \(n\) independent potential trials per task. The complete count \(S\) credits repeat successes on each task. The score \(Z\) from the [first-success experiment](interior-count-order.md) is zero on exhaustion of any task and otherwise uses the unrestricted-to-capped composition ratio at the total wait. On \(S=k\ge m\) let \(P_k\) be the uniform law and let \(Q_k=ZP_k\). Both are probability measures by homogeneous calibration. Write \(Y\) for the number of failures among the \(nr\) trials of the designated group, \(f=mn-k\) and \(T=mn\).

**Theorem.** There exists \(M(n,r)\) such that for \(m\ge M(n,r)\), on **every** stratum \(m\le k\le mn\), \(Y\) under \(P_k\) dominates \(Y\) under \(Q_k\) in convex order. Consequently, if the \(r\) designated tasks share any one success probability and the remaining tasks share any other, then
\[
 E[Z\,1_{\{S=k\}}]\le\Pr(S=k),\qquad EZ\le\Pr(S\ge m).
\]
The second inequality is strict for unequal interior probabilities. This covers \(r=1\) at every fixed cap. The cutoff \(M(n,r)\) is not evaluated here, and the theorem does not cover a growing minority or several distinct replacement probabilities. No numerical sign limit is used in its proof.

Indeed the two measures give mean \(rf/m\) to \(Y\). It suffices to compare the hinges \(H_h(y)=(y-h)_+\), \(1\le h<nr\). For two interior probabilities the likelihood on a fixed stratum is a positive constant times \(t^Y\) for the ratio \(t\) of group to background failure odds. This function is strictly convex for \(t\ne1\); the interior strata below give strict gaps. Continuity gives weak comparison on the closed probability square.

**Upper endpoint estimate.** Put \(\tau=f/m\), and let \(E\) be the event of exhausting at least one of the designated tasks. Uniformly for sufficiently large \(m\), all \(n\le f\le\delta m\) for sufficiently small fixed \(\delta=\delta(n,r)>0\), and every \(h\), we have
\[
 E_{P_k}H_h(Y)-E_{Q_k}H_h(Y)
 =E_{P_k}[H_h(Y)1_E]+O_{n,r}(\tau^{\,n+h}).
\]
If \(f\le h\), the hinge vanishes. Otherwise the leading term is bounded below by \(c_{n,r}\tau^{\max(n,h+1)}\). The relative error is \(O_{n,r}(\tau)\) or better since \(n,h+1\ge2\). When \(f<n\), every path hits every task and the score is identically one. In particular for one designated task the leading term is exactly \((n-h)(f)_n/(mn)_n\), with falling factorials. This endpoint estimate resolves the region left open by the interior law.

**Local changes in the composition weight.** Write \(w_b(s)\) for the reciprocal cap-compliance probability in a uniform weak composition of \(s\) into \(b\) parts, with maximum allowed part \(n-1\). We only use \(b\in[m-r,m]\), \(0\le s\le f\) in the sparse endpoint region, where the capped counts are positive. The following uniform estimates are used:
\[
 1\le w_b(s+1)/w_b(s)\le 1+C\tau^{n-1},\qquad
 1-C\tau^n\le w_{b+1}(s)/w_b(s)\le1 .
\]
In the first inequality use \(s+1\le f\). To prove them, first consider one or two specified parts of a **capped** uniform composition. A specified pattern of total \(d\ge1\) on these parts has probability at most \((C s/b)^d\) when \(s/b\) is sufficiently small. Indeed clear the specified parts and distribute their units among \(d\) previously empty, distinct other parts. Every source has at least \(\binom{b-2-s}{d}\) images, while each image allows at most \(\binom{s}{d}\) inverse choices of singleton parts. This proves the bound, including impossible patterns when \(d>s\).

Insert a uniformly placed star in a uniform stars-and-bars word. Its part is chosen proportionally to its size plus one, and the resulting word is uniform since deletion of a marked star has constant multiplicity. A compliant word becomes noncompliant precisely when the chosen part is already saturated. The conditional probability of this event is \(nb\,\Pr(\text{specified part}=n-1)/(b+s)\le C\tau^{n-1}\). A noncompliant word stays noncompliant. This gives the first ratio bound. To get the second, delete a uniformly chosen bar from a uniform \((b+1)\)-part word. The result is uniform, and a compliant word fails precisely when the two merged adjacent parts sum to at least \(n\). By exchangeability and the two-part bound this conditional probability is at most \(C\tau^n\), summing a fixed finite number of patterns. A noncompliant word cannot become compliant after merging. This gives both sides of the second inequality. The comparisons concern conditional changes in compliance and remain useful even when the absolute compliance probability is small.

**Removing the named group without losing precision.** Fix the full labelled outcome of the group, with \(y\) failures. If any designated task exhausts, the score is zero. Otherwise the group adds \(u\le r(n-1)\) waits. The remaining tasks are homogeneous conditional on the remaining success count. For all sufficiently large \(m\) with \(f\le\delta m\), this count is at least \(m-r\), since even \(n(m-r)-f\ge m-r\). Weighting this remaining conditional law by its own score therefore gives a probability measure. On its all-hit logs the ratio of the enlarged to the reduced score is \(w_m(s+u)/w_{m-r}(s)\), where \(s+u\le f\). Apply \(r\) bar comparisons and at most \(r(n-1)\) star comparisons. The ratio is \(1+O_{n,r}(\tau^{n-1})\) uniformly on these logs; both scores vanish on the other logs. Thus for every nonexhausted fixed group record the conditional mean enlarged score differs from one by at most this error.

Decomposing the hinge expectation by the group record proves the endpoint estimate, since \(H_h(Y)\le nr\,1_{\{Y\ge h+1\}}\) and sampling \(nr\) slots without replacement gives \(\Pr(Y\ge h+1)\le C_{n,r}\tau^{h+1}\) by a union bound. For its lower bound prescribe exactly \(d=\max(n,h+1)\) failures in the group, including all \(n\) slots of its first task. The probability of this one pattern is \((f)_d(T-f)_{nr-d}/(T)_{nr}\ge c_{n,r}\tau^d\) when \(f\ge d\). Here \(d\) is bounded and \((f)_d\ge d!f^d/d^d\). This proves the asserted sign uniformly for small enough \(\delta\).

**Completing the other strata.** On any sequence \(k/T\to a\in(1/n,1)\), the full success counts on the \(r\) named tasks converge jointly to independent \(\mathrm{Bin}(n,a)\) variables. Under \(Q_k\) they converge jointly to independent copies of the limiting count \(W\) in the [interior proof](interior-count-order.md). To verify the multivariate step, conditional on total wait \(s\) the probability of prescribed waits on \(r\) sites is their independent tilted probability times the local sum probability on \(m-r\) remaining sites divided by that on \(m\) sites. Their displacements from their respective means differ by a bounded amount. The local estimate in the interior proof gives a ratio tending to one for fixed \(r\). Conditional on the waits, a bounded set of suffix sites samples without replacement from a linearly growing population, giving joint independence in the limit.

The full one-site limit strictly dominates the weighted one for all interior integer hinges. The same holds for failures instead of successes by reflection and equality of means. Replace the summands in a sum of \(r\) independent full failure counts by weighted ones, one at a time. No replacement increases a hinge expectation by convexity. The first decreases it strictly: the other \(r-1\) full variables have positive probability at each integer between zero and \((r-1)n\). For \(1\le h<rn\) some such integer shifts the hinge threshold into \(1,\ldots,n-1\). Hence every group hinge has strictly positive limiting gap.

At the lower endpoint \(k/T\to1/n\) the weighted group **success** count is at least \(r\) and has mean \(rk/m\to r\); thus it converges to \(r\). The full group count converges to \(\mathrm{Bin}(rn,1/n)\), which has mean \(r\) and positive mass at both endpoints. Every interior hinge has strictly larger expectation for this full limit than at the constant \(r\), by the two linear pieces of the hinge. Reflection gives the same strict inequalities for failures. Finally a sequence of violations outside the sparse endpoint region would contain a subsequence with fixed \(h\) and \(k/T\) converging to a point in \([1/n,1-\delta/n]\), contradicting the strict limiting gaps. The uniform endpoint estimate handles all remaining strata.

**Reproduction and scope.** The [finite audit](group-endpoint.py) evaluates the marked-group joint failure counts by integer counts and rational composition weights. Its endpoint ratios compare the hinge deficit to the exhausted-group contribution, with very small designs also checked against enumeration of labelled outcomes. These checks concern the finite independent-trial model and do not measure a deployed model or establish priority. Random weak compositions and their classical geometric representation are studied in [Bevan and Threlfall](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06287). This supplement postdates the reviewed PDF.

# Growing exceptional sets at a pooled success threshold

Consider the [first-success score and pooled target](exceptional-tasks.md). Each of the \(m\) tasks has \(n\ge2\) independent attempts and different tasks are independent. The full target \(P\) is the probability of at least \(m\) successes among all \(mn\) attempts, counting repeated successes on a task. Logging stops each task at its first success. The score \(Z\) is zero unless every task succeeds; otherwise it assigns the ratio of the unrestricted composition count to the capped composition count at the observed total wait. The homogeneous reference probability is \(p_m\), with \(q_m=1-p_m\). Replace the probabilities of \(r\) named tasks by \(1-x_i\), where \(r\to\infty\) and \(r/m\to0\).

**Result.** Put \(t=r/m\) when \(0<p_m=p<1/n\) is fixed, and \(t=\sqrt{r/m}\) when \(|p_m-1/n|\le K/\sqrt m\) for fixed \(K\). Then, uniformly in \(0\le x_i<1\),
\[
\log(EZ/P)=\sum_{i=1}^r\log(1-x_i)+(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^r\log(x_i+t)+O(r).
\]
Constants depend only on the fixed cap and the background or window bound. A task with \(x_i=1\) gives a zero numerator exactly. The error bound determines the leading logarithmic rate for nearly certain replacements; it need not resolve the sign of an error of order \(r\) or less. The result allows arbitrarily slow divergence of \(r\).

For certain replacements, the leading losses are \(-(n-1)r\log(m/r)\) below threshold and half that in the critical window. At the exact threshold, take \(n=3\) and \(r=\lfloor m^{2/3}\rfloor\). The full target tends to one because the number of successes has distribution \(3r+\operatorname{Bin}(3(m-r),1/3)\), with mean \(m+2r\) and variance \(2(m-r)/3\). Yet the log of the mean stopped score is \(-m^{2/3}\log(m)/3+O(m^{2/3})\). If instead every changed task has failure probability \((r/m)^\beta\) for fixed \(\beta>0\), the coefficient of \(-(n-1)r\log(m/r)\) is \(\min(\beta,1)\) below threshold and \(\min(\beta,1/2)\) in the critical window.

**Pattern probability under the score.** Call the homogeneous full target \(P_0\). Under weighting the homogeneous log law by \(Z/P_0\), the total wait has the distribution of \(m\) summed geometric waits conditioned on being at most \(L=m(n-1)\). This follows by canceling the capped count in the weight. Conditional on a deficit \(D=L-s=d\), the \(m\) coordinate deficits are uniform among capped compositions of \(d\). For a prescribed pattern of the \(r\) named waits \(u_i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), write \(j=\sum_i(n-1-u_i)\) and \(h=(n-1)r\). We claim that each such pattern has unconditional probability under the weighted law between \(e^{-Cr}t^j\) and \(e^{Cr}t^j\).

For the upper bound, start from any composition with the prescribed named deficits. Move all \(j\) units out of those coordinates and place one in each of \(j\) previously empty unnamed coordinates. For \(d\le m/4\) there are at least \(\binom{m-r-d}{j}\) choices. For any resulting composition there are at most \(\binom{d}{j}\) preimages with choices, since one picks \(j\) singleton coordinates and restores the given pattern. Double counting bounds the conditional probability by \((C d/m)^j\) for \(j>0\). A pattern with \(j>d\) is impossible. For \(j=0\) the trivial upper bound suffices.

Below threshold the ratios of consecutive conditional masses of \(D\) are at most a constant \(\rho<1\) for all sufficiently large \(m\), by the negative-binomial formula in the linked note. Thus \(E[D^j]\le (Cj)^j\) for \(j\ge1\). In the critical window the conditioning probability is bounded below by the central limit theorem for sums of geometric waits (uniform bounded third moments). Their centered exponential moments give \(\Pr(D\ge d)\le C\exp(-c d^2/m)\) up to \(L\): apply the exponential Markov inequality with parameter a sufficiently small multiple of \(d/m\) for \(d\) larger than a constant times \(\sqrt m\); enlarge the prefactor below this range. Integrating this tail gives \(E[D^j]\le(C\sqrt{mj})^j\). The contribution from \(d>m/4\) is exponentially small in \(m\) in each regime. Since \(r\log(m/r)=o(m)\), this is negligible even compared with \(t^h\). These estimates establish the uniform upper bound.

For the lower bound let \(A_m(d)=[z^d](1+\cdots+z^{n-1})^m\). The pattern probability conditional on \(d\) is \(A_{m-r}(d-j)/A_m(d)\). For \(h\le d=o(m)\) bound the numerator below by using only zeros and ones outside the named sites, and the denominator above by dropping the cap. The ratio is at least
\[
 \frac{\binom{m-r}{d-j}}{\binom{m+d-1}{d}}
 \ \ge\ \left(\frac{d-j+1}{m}\right)^j
 (1-(r+d)/m)^{d-j}(1+d/m)^{-d}.
\]
Below threshold take \(d=2h\). Its conditional mass is at least \(e^{-Cr}\), because the mass at zero stays bounded below and all successive mass ratios through \(2h=o(m)\) are bounded below by a positive constant. In the critical window take every integer \(d\) between \(\sqrt{mh}\) and \(2\sqrt{mh}\). The unconditioned negative-binomial mass at \(L\) is at least \(c_K/\sqrt m\) by Stirling's formula (the displacement from its mean is \(O_K(\sqrt m)\)). Moving from \(L-i\) to \(L-i-1\) multiplies that mass by \((L-i)/(q_m(L+m-1-i))\), at least \(\exp[-C(m^{-1/2}+(i+1)/m)]\) in this range and on the way to it. Thus the whole interval has probability at least \(e^{-Cr}\), even before conditioning. On the selected sets \(d-j+1\ge cmt\), and \((rd+d^2)/m=O(r)\). Substitution proves the claimed lower bound for all patterns, including \(j=0\).

**Full experiment and likelihoods.** In the homogeneous full experiment conditioned on success, the overall success fraction is in a fixed closed interval interior to \((0,1)\) with probability at least \(1/2\) for all large \(m\). Below threshold this follows from geometric domination of the binomial excess above \(m\), and in the critical window it follows from concentration and the lower bound on \(P_0\). Given the total, the \(nr\) named trials are drawn without replacement, so every specified binary pattern has probability at least \(c^{nr}\) on this interval. For each named task select either all successes or all failures according as its success or failure likelihood ratio is larger. That larger ratio is at least one because their reference-weighted mean is one. The full likelihood is also bounded above by \(\max(p_m^{-1},q_m^{-1})^{nr}\). Consequently \(|\log(P/P_0)|\le Cr\), uniformly.

The likelihood ratio of a named all-hit wait pattern in the stopped law is \(\prod_i[(1-x_i)/p_m](x_i/q_m)^{u_i}\). Summing the pattern bounds against these nonnegative weights introduces a total multiplicative error between \(e^{-Cr}\) and \(e^{Cr}\), independent of the number of terms. For each task the polynomial \(\sum_{u=0}^{n-1}t^{n-1-u}(x_i/q_m)^u\) is within fixed positive factors of \((t+x_i)^{n-1}\) by coefficient comparison. This proves the assertion.

**Check at cap two.** Conditional on \(d\), the deficient coordinates form a uniform \(d\)-subset. The probability of prescribed \(j\) ones and \(r-j\) zeros among named sites is exactly \((d)_j(m-d)_{r-j}/(m)_r\), where parentheses denote falling factorials. This gives the same powers in both selected deficit ranges and independently checks the pattern indexing. For unchanged tasks \(x_i=q_m\), the exact ratio is one and the logarithmic estimate permits this, as it must.

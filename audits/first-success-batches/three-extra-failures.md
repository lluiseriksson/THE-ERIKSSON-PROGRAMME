# The next upper stratum also has the downward sign

Under the same independent, common-cap assumptions, for all \(m,n\ge2\), if \(k=mn-(n+3)\ge m\), then
\(E[Z1_{\{S=k\}}]\le\Pr(S=k)\). Combined with near-full-strata.md this covers every eligible stratum with at most \(n+3\) failures. Cap two is known; take \(n\ge3\). Failure-odds profiles and score excesses refer to the normalization used in that supplement.

At \(f=n+3\) failures, every supplying profile has form \((n,3)\), \((n,2,1)\), or \((n,1,1,1)\) with zero padding. For \(n=3\) count the profile \((3,3)\) only once per pair. On a fixed pair of nonempty failure slots, demand does not exceed the supply of the first type. For \(n\ge5\), restrict to the two slots: all outcomes there have \(2n-f=n-3\ge2\) successes. Every outcome uses both failure slots, so equality of total supply and excess demand holds by homogeneous calibration. Padding with perfect tasks can only decrease the weights, as proved by deleting one bar from a uniform composition. For \(n=3,4\) there is no two-slot all-hit demand. The first supply type can dominate any two-slot demand.

The supply profile \((n,2,1)\) majorizes every demand with exactly three nonempty failure slots. On any fixed triple, its multiplicity is \(6n\binom n2\). If \(m=3\), after filling the two-slot demands, all remaining sources dominate the three-slot demands, and total balances agree. For \(m=2\) the pair argument suffices. For \(m\ge4\) it is enough to show that three-slot demand is at most its own supply on each triple. By the same monotonicity of the weights, the hardest comparison here is at \(m=4\).

For \(n\ge4\), distributing \(f=n+3\) failures on the three ordered slots, all positive and at most \(n-1\), gives \(B=\binom{n+2}2-9\) admissible vectors. Given such a vector \(a\) and \(e\) failures after first hits, the associated excess is \(w_{n+3-e}^{(4)}-1\); only \(0\le e\le3\) matter. For a split \(e_i\) of those trailing failures, there are \(\prod_i\binom{n-a_i+e_i-1}{e_i}\) arrangements when \(e_i\le a_i\). Dropping these upper restrictions and using the negative-binomial series gives at most \(\binom{2n-4+e}e\) arrangements. Consequently it suffices that
\[
 B\sum_{e=0}^3\binom{2n-4+e}{e}\big(w_{n+3-e}^{(4)}-1\big)\le6n\binom n2 .
\]
For \(s=n+3-e<2n\) the positive denominator is \(A_s=\binom{s+3}3-4\binom{s-n+3}3\). Multiply the supply minus the upper bound by \(\prod_{e=0}^3 A_{n+3-e}\). The result is a polynomial of degree at most 15 in \(n\). Its forward differences of orders 0 through 15 at \(n=4\) are all nonnegative (exact list in three-failures.json; three-extra-failures.py reconstructs them from the formula). Newton's identity proves nonnegativity for every integer \(n\ge4\). For \(n=3\), direct enumeration on a fixed triple at \(m=4\) gives demand \(8247/380<54\). This settles all three-slot demands.

Any demand with at least four nonempty slots has largest one, two and three failure counts summing to at most \(n-1,n+1,n+2\), respectively. Thus even the least concentrated source \((n,1,1,1)\) majorizes it. All remaining supplies can cover those demands by equality of total masses. Muirhead's comparison and continuity complete the proof for heterogeneous probabilities. The supplementary computation checks finite polynomial coefficients, not a grid approximation to the probability cube.

# Direction of misspecification bias for two-attempt batches

Let all m batch caps be two. Each batch has its own success probability pᵢ in (0,1), and trials are conditionally independent given these fixed probabilities. Apply the symmetric minimum-variance nonnegative rule Z derived for homogeneous batches. It always underestimates the full-budget probability of at least m successes in expectation, with strict inequality for m≥2 unless all pᵢ coincide.

To prove this, condition on the total K of successes across the 2m potential draws. For K<m, the score is zero. For K=k≥m let H be the event that every batch contains at least one success. Off H the score is zero. On H, exactly k−m batches contain two successes and 2m−k contain a single success. In each single-success batch the success is equally likely to be first or second, independently across such batches conditional on their counts. Double successes are recorded as immediate hits. Thus the conditional mean on H depends only on k, under either homogeneous or heterogeneous probabilities. In the homogeneous experiment E[Z|K=k]=1 for k≥m, by comparing coefficients in the full binomial basis. Consequently the heterogeneous conditional mean equals the ratio Pr_heterogeneous(H|K=k)/Pr_homogeneous(H|K=k).

For the coverage probability, write θᵢ=pᵢ/(1−pᵢ). At fixed K=k the mass of a vector t of batch counts is proportional to ∏ binom(2,tᵢ) θᵢ^{tᵢ}. On H the exponents consist of k−m twos and 2m−k ones. Every other orbit of the exponents has some zero entries and more twos. Its symmetric monomial average across permutations is at least the corresponding average on H: repeatedly replace (2,0) by (1,1) in the exponent vector. Pair permutations interchanging the two chosen positions. The decrease in their mean is a nonnegative factor times (θᵢ−θⱼ)²/2. Multiplicity ∏ binom(2,tᵢ) is constant within each orbit. The odds-weighted denominator per full path therefore averages to at least the odds weight per full path on H. This proves the coverage bound. For unequal odds the permutation inequality is strict when a (2,0) replacement is needed. At k=m this case has positive probability whenever m≥2, establishing strict bias.

For m=2 the exact deficit is (p₁−p₂)². More generally for two batches, if the reporting rule is unbiased for equal probabilities and may take values as low as −b, 0≤b≤1, then the minimum worst absolute bias on |p₁−p₂|≤δ is (1−b)δ². After symmetrization let g be the conditional score on the two late hits. In the order failure, early, late, the six upper-triangular conditional scores forced by homogeneous unbiasedness are 0,(3−g)/2,(g−3)/2,1,1,g. This identity follows by equating the five coefficients in p; it is checked symbolically in the supplement. The lower bound forces g≥3−2b. The signed unbiased score corresponds to g=1, the homogeneous nonnegative optimum to g=3, and the symmetric bias at g is −(g−1)(p₁−p₂)²/2. Taking g=3−2b gives the claimed sharp value. Symmetrization cannot increase a symmetric worst-case loss.

# Whole-design versus staged simulation

Follow-up research note to the observed Pareto-cover manuscript. The following example changes its evaluation rule. It concerns hypothetical designs and does not assert a property of SWMM or its users.

There are m equal-construction-cost candidates and a free fully evaluated reference of loss (2,1). A candidate has a screen x_i in {0,2} costing a_i>0 and a second loss z_i in [0,1] costing b_i>0. Both coordinates must be measured to return the candidate as a witness. The tolerance is (0,1). All combinations are feasible. The reference covers every high screen. Any fully evaluated candidate with a zero screen covers every design, including the reference. If a unique zero occurs, that candidate must be returned.

**Worst-case cost.** A fixed nonadaptive set of measurements must buy both coordinates for each candidate, costing sum_i(a_i+b_i). The optimal adaptive cost is

    min over orders π of max over j of [sum_{l≤j} a_{π(l)} + b_{π(j)}].

For the upper bound follow the order, complete the first zero, and otherwise return the reference after all screens. On the all-high world, any correct strategy must screen every candidate, since an unread coordinate could hide a unique zero. Fix all secondary coordinates arbitrarily, and let π be the actual order of screening on this path. For the world with a unique zero at position j, every answer before that screen agrees with the all-high world. The strategy must have purchased the prefix of screens and must eventually pay the secondary cost for the unique zero, even if that cost was prepaid. This gives the displayed lower bound for any interleaving strategy. The optimum sorts b_i in nonincreasing order. For an adjacent inversion, moving the larger tail earlier cannot increase either completion-plus-tail maximum. This is Jackson's scheduling interchange argument (1955), applied after the reduction. https://ntrl.ntis.gov/NTRL/dashboard/searchResults/titleDetail/AD152722.xhtml

For constant costs a,b, these two totals are m(a+b) and ma+b. Complete product uncertainty therefore does not give the whole-vector no-adaptivity conclusion when queries are staged and returned witnesses must be fully evaluated. This example uses the second measurement as an actual evaluation requirement: if one can return a partially evaluated witness, the saving attributed to that requirement disappears.

**Expected cost without a deadline.** Suppose the screens are independent with known 0<p_i<1 probabilities of a zero and secondary outcomes reveal nothing about any screen. The secondary costs remain deterministic. Query by increasing r_i=b_i+a_i/p_i and complete the first zero. This minimizes the expected cost even among strategies that continue searching after success. Indeed the decision to screen an unseen candidate is independent of its success indicator X_i. Write A_i for whether it is screened. Then E[a_i A_i]=E[(a_i/p_i)A_i X_i]. On every world with a zero, if the returned winner is h, the sum of the reweighted screen costs and actual secondary costs is at least r_h, hence at least the minimum r among all zero candidates. On the all-failure event the lower bound is zero. Scanning in the stated order attains this reweighted bound pointwise. This reasoning explains why zero in the lower bound on the all-failure event does not mean those screens cost nothing in that event. The all-fail cost is incorporated in the expectation identity. Equal tails give the classical ratio order; see the testing literature in https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221721002642.

**Hard deadline: an unbounded penalty for immediate completion.** With just three candidates A,B,C take all screen costs equal to 1, finishing costs (M,M,1), M an integer at least 2, and worst-case deadline D=M+2. Screen probabilities are independent and each equals 1-1/M. Any correct fixed screen order that completes the first success immediately must place A and B in the first two positions. If one costly candidate were third, its unique-success world would miss the deadline by one. The expected cost of every such order is at least (1-1/M)(M+1) from the first success alone. This lower bound also applies to immediate completion with contingent ordering after failures in this binary screening model: before a first success, the all-failure path prescribes the order. Noninformative secondary outcomes cannot improve a minimum by randomizing between orders.

Instead screen A. If A succeeds, screen C. Finish C if successful and otherwise finish A. If A fails, screen B and finish it when successful, or screen C last and finish only if necessary. The policy is feasible on every world. Outside two events it costs at most 4. The two events (A succeeds and C fails, or A fails and B succeeds) each have probability at most 1/M and cost M+2. Thus its expectation is at most 4+2(M-2)/M ≤ 6, giving an unbounded ratio as M increases. The gain arises with independent outcomes. The policy keeps one expensive but feasible return option while testing a cheaper one. This is a hard-deadline example about acquisition and mandatory observed witnesses; deferring acceptance is known more broadly in sequential search.

For a small exact example use M=3 and probabilities (1/2,1/2,3/4). The same policy has expected cost 63/16 and either feasible immediate-completion order costs 67/16. The supplement computes the unrestricted optimum by backward induction over outcomes and remaining budget. It may complete any observed success or continue testing. Secondary measurements before choosing a winner can be postponed until selection: their values are independent and all lie within tolerance, and removing an otherwise unused measurement only lowers elapsed cost. One need not purchase a second qualification after already completing a valid winner.

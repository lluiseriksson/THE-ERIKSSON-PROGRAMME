# Correlation of paired anomalies from pixelwise variability maps

Figure 11 of Tchonang's figure-code deposit (version DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24407734) displays a spatial correlation between forecast and SWOT standard deviations. It also prints a separately stored anomaly-correlation attribute. Those are different statistics. The following concerns the sensitivity of a *within-pixel centered* pooled anomaly correlation to coverage. The published figure code does not establish the common repeat masks, weights or centering of the attribute.

Let Y and F be temporally centered observations and forecasts in each pixel j, and let s, f, d be the standard deviations of Y, F and F-Y. All three use the same finite paired repeat sample, denominator and weights at that pixel. Restrict to positive s and f. The sample covariance is (s²+f²-d²)/2. The local correlation is therefore determined. If |s-f| ≤ d ≤ s+f fails, the three inputs cannot represent the stated common sample at their stated precision. Passing the test does not prove common masks. This identity is classical (Taylor, 2001, DOI 10.1029/2000JD900719).

For arbitrary nonnegative pixel weights w, not all zero, define A_j=f_j²/s_j² and B_j=(s_j²+f_j²-d_j²)/(2s_j²). Absorb the within-pixel observation sum of squares into the weights: p_j=w_j s_j² / sum w_i s_i². The centered pooled correlation is (sum p_j B_j)/sqrt(sum p_j A_j). Conversely all p in the simplex are obtained. Thus the feasible (A,B) pairs fill the convex hull of the pixel points. The extrema of B/sqrt(A) are both attained on its boundary: at fixed A the objective increases strictly with B. Each endpoint is attained using at most two pixels, including for a collinear hull. On an edge B=mA+c, evaluate its endpoints and, if m is nonzero and c/m lies on the edge, the stationary point A=c/m. On a vertical edge endpoints suffice. Continuity gives every intermediate correlation. If positive weights on every pixel are required, the same bounds describe the closure. Uniform repeat weights or known sample sizes give a specific pooled estimand instead of this interval.

Example: pixel 1 has (s,f,d)=(1,1,0); pixel 2 has (2,4,2). Both have temporal correlation one and the spatial correlation of the two variability pairs is one. Their transformed points are (1,1) and (4,2). The pooled correlation reaches its minimum at A=2, B=4/3, yielding 2 sqrt(2)/3. With weights w1:w2=8:1, the weighted covariance sum is 16 and the two variance sums are 12 and 24, giving exactly 16/sqrt(288). A pooled comparison therefore need not retain even perfect local correlation when amplitudes vary across space.

The example is constructed. No value for a real ocean region has been inferred. The deposit contains notebooks and no NetCDF observations. The remaining empirical test is to identify a public companion archive, verify masks and conventions against its attributes, and compare the bound with a correctly defined regional quantity. If those inputs cannot be verified, preserve the conditional result and move on to another dataset. Do not publish a standalone short corollary on this basis.

# How many distinct flagged cases contained a confirmed failure?

Rohlfs et al., *When Evaluators Cry Wolf* ([EMNLP Industry Track, October 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-industry.193/)), evaluate 21 judges on 2,482 synthetic educational examples. The presentation registration is [10.48448/3hat-t238](https://doi.org/10.48448/3hat-t238). We analyze the reported benchmark adjudications. A confirmed case has at least one flag judged true. A clean review queue entry here means all flags on that case were judged true. Neither quantity measures recall on unflagged examples.

The baseline has 4,002 flags on 2,086 cases with 24 confirmed flags. After calibration, 43 of 88 flags on 72 cases were confirmed. Given 45 false flags after calibration, at most 45 distinct cases contain even one false flag. Consequently at least 27 cases have exclusively confirmed flags, and at most 43 have any confirmed flag. Baseline has at most 24 cases with any confirmed flag. The observed-label case coverage therefore rose by at least three distinct cases, and the fraction of reviewed cases containing any confirmed flag rose from at most 24/2086 to at least 27/72. Even if all baseline confirmations occur on different cases, the case-level yield increases at least 32.59-fold. These are counts on the benchmark the authors used for tuning.

## Why the bounds are attainable given the disclosed margins

Table 2 reports two-decimal activation percentages on a common denominator. Reconstructing each integer count gives totals of 4,002 and 88, matching the text. After calibration 65 output-quality flags cover 50 cases. Of these, 49 were flagged only at the output-quality tier according to Appendix A. All 23 higher-tier flags must occupy different cases because exactly 72−49=23 cases have a higher-tier flag. Only one case spans both groups. Distinct singleton higher-tier flags may be assigned their individual evaluator identities in any order consistent with their counts; any such assignment gives the published higher-tier severity-bin sizes provided the category totals match the figure.

Following the explicit manual assessment in Section 5, the 20 completeness flags are false and the 16 well-formedness and 14 truncation flags are true. The Table 2 caption describes completeness differently; treating all three groups as confirmed would already give 50 confirmations, exceeding the reported 43. Our label-conditioned calculation states which interpretation it uses. The 27-case counting bound itself needs only the aggregate totals.

We grouped the 72 cases by their post-calibration flags and their adjudicated labels. A nonnegative integer gives the multiplicity of each pattern. The supplied solver and its exact integer witnesses reach both 27 and 43 cases containing a confirmed flag, including when five of ten mathematics flags are confirmed (a sensitivity scenario for the paper's approximate description). For cases with exclusively confirmed flags, the attainable range is 27 to 43 with the mathematics count unspecified, and 27 to 38 with exactly five confirmed mathematics flags. The files describe synthetic assignments compatible with the stated constraints. They contain no original student records. Baseline's upper bound of 24 distinct confirmed cases is attainable by labeling one flag in each of 24 different flagged cases, conditional on the overall baseline counts and absence of a disclosed tighter label allocation.

## Two robustness thresholds have different operational meanings

Consider re-adjudication edits to binary flag labels, keeping all case identifiers, flags and reviewed denominators fixed. Count edits per flag. Three well-placed edits can erase the guaranteed increase in the absolute number of cases with any confirmed flag. At the post lower endpoint, 43 confirmed flags spread over 27 cases leave at least 11 of those cases with exactly one confirmed flag. Demote three such flags and place the original 24 baseline confirmations on distinct cases. Two edits cannot erase the difference because each changes the gap by at most one.

Erasing the guaranteed improvement in case-level yield needs at least 43 edits under the Section 5 interpretation. Suppose q post-calibration cases have at least one confirmed flag after edits. At most five originally confirmed flags could survive on any one such case: at most one higher-tier flag and at most four potentially confirmed output evaluators. Thus at least max(0,43−5q) post labels must change. Baseline must then have at least ceil(2086q/72) confirmed cases, requiring at least max(0,ceil(2086q/72)−24) baseline edits. For q=0 or 1 the combined lower bound is 43. For q≥2 the lower bound is larger (at q=2 it is 67; before truncation the second term grows faster than the first falls). Demoting all 43 post confirmations attains 43. If auditing only the headline margins and omitting the output-label and overlap information, this particular five-per-case argument is unavailable.

The protocols differed: baseline flags used fixed rules plus human review, while all final flags were manually reviewed. These tipping counts apply to changes from those reported adjudications. They are not estimates of error rates, independent review actions or a like-for-like gold standard. The study withholds unflagged ground truth and reports no held-out benchmark split. A common-protocol audit and a case-by-evaluator incidence export would resolve questions these aggregates leave open.

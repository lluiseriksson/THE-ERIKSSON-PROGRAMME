# When a sharp risk factor is attained only at infinity

Supplement to *Prediction risk under intersecting economic restrictions*. The comparison uses the same raw fit for both quadratic estimators. It concerns squared excess prediction error relative to a feasible coefficient. The result below is deterministic.

## Fixed restrictions, finite error budgets

Fix a slope s>0 and a gap h>0. Use Euclidean fitting and prediction metrics. In coordinates (z1,z2), let B={z2<=h} and C={z2<=0, z2<=s z1}. For raw fit y let u=P_B(y) and e=P_C(y). For any L>0 define F(L) as the supremum of ||e-t||²/||u-t||² over t in C and 0<||u-t||<=L. Write K=1+1/s². Then

    F(L) = max{1, K [max(0,1-h/L)]²}.

Every F(L) is attained. The unrestricted supremum is K. No finite pair attains K. Requiring a uniform positive gap between the truth set and the ordinary gate therefore does not guarantee attainment.

**Upper bound.** When y lies in B, u=y and projection on C can only decrease the distance to t. Otherwise y=(p,R), R>h, and u=(p,h). If p>=0 then e=(p,0); all truths have t2<=0, so projection improves or preserves error. If p=-a<0, as R runs from h to infinity e moves along a segment from P_C(u) to the origin, possibly a singleton. To check this, project to the inclined line. The foot is (-a+sR)(1,s)/(1+s²) when sR<=a. If R>=a/s then y is a nonnegative combination of normals (-s,1) and (0,1) at the origin. At P_C(u) the ratio is at most one. Since squared distance is convex along the segment it suffices to bound the origin endpoint.

If t1>=0, then ||t-u||²-||t||²=a²+2at1+h²-2ht2>0. Otherwise write b=-t2, so b>=s|t1| and ||t||²<=K b². Put l=||u-t||. Then l>=b+h and at this endpoint

    ||t||²/l² <= K(b/l)² <= K(1-h/l)² <= K(1-h/L)².

This case is possible only for l>=h. These estimates cover all raw fits and truths, including points on the boundaries.

**Attainment and rate.** A ratio of one is attained by any two distinct points in C within distance L, using the first point as the raw fit. If the other term is greater than one, set b=L-h, a=b/s, t=(-a,-b), u=(-a,h), and take y=(-a,R) with R>h and R>=a/s. Then e=0, l=L and the ratio is exactly K(1-h/L)². For all finite L this is strictly smaller than K. For large L the gap K-F(L) equals 2Kh/L-Kh²/L². At s=1/2,h=1 the unrestricted factor is five. Under baseline error budgets of two and ten, its sharp values are 5/4 and 81/20. A budget below h/(1-1/sqrt(K)) precludes deterioration; equality at the threshold also precludes strict deterioration.

## What is special to the example?

For fixed nested nonempty polyhedra C subset B and positive definite fitting and prediction metrics H and M, a finite supremum can always be approximated to order 1/L under a bound L on the M-norm of the ordinary error. Moreover, every positive-budget supremum is attained if its domain is nonempty. The worst constant and the onset of this approximation depend on the polyhedra and metrics.

Here are details. Partition (y,t), t in C, by the finitely many closed projection cells of B and C. On each cell the maps U=P_B^H(y)-t and E=P_C^H(y)-t are affine. Their joint image Q in the pair (U,E) is a closed polyhedron. Its recession cone is the image of the cell recession cone by the linear parts. This follows by writing each nonempty cell as the sum of a finite polytope and a finitely generated cone, allowing opposite generators for lineality. In Q, U=0 implies E=0 by nestedness. The analogous statement holds in rec Q. Indeed, along a recession direction (j,k), where k belongs to rec C, the two slopes are the projections of j onto rec B and rec C in H, minus k. To obtain this slope, first translate the sets by a point of C. The scaled sets A/q decrease to rec A for q increasing to infinity. Projections of j+y0/q onto A/q are bounded by comparison with zero and every subsequential limit minimizes the limiting distance by comparison with every point of rec A. If the first slope is zero then k is already the projection on rec B and belongs to rec C, so the second slope is zero.

For a fixed cell, take the closed cone generated by (U,E,1) for (U,E) in Q. At height zero its points are (v,w,0) with (v,w) in rec Q. Its projection to the first two components is still a polyhedral cone, hence closed, with no pair (0,w) for w nonzero. Its unit section ||v||_M=1 is compact: otherwise normalize an unbounded sequence by ||w||_M to obtain a forbidden vertical direction. Maximizing squared ||w||_M on these sections proves finiteness of the unrestricted factor and either gives a finite attaining pair or a recession pair (v,w) with ||v||_M=1 and ||w||_M² equal to that factor. Height-zero pairs are genuine limits by adding positive multiples of any height-one lift.

At finite budget, a maximizing sequence has bounded U and therefore bounded E by finiteness. In a single cell subsequence, (U,E) converges in Q. If the limit has U nonzero it attains the maximum. Otherwise the limit is the origin. Take a convergent subsequence of (U,E)/||U||_M. Its limit is in the closed tangent cone of Q at zero, and its first component has unit norm. In a polyhedron every tangent direction at a point is feasible for a sufficiently small positive step. Such a step belongs to Q, respects L, and attains precisely the limiting ratio. This proof uses compactness of displacement pairs, without imposing compactness on raw fits or truths.

Finally, if the unrestricted maximum has only a recession lift, choose any base pair (u0,e0) in the same Q. The ray (u0+qv,e0+qw) lies in Q. For L sufficiently large take q=L-||u0||_M. Its baseline norm is at most L and nonzero. Dividing its numerator and denominator by q² gives K+O(1/L), with a fixed constant on this ray. Consequently 0<=K-F(L)=O(1/L). If the unrestricted maximum is attained, F(L) is constant for all sufficiently large L. The wedge family proves that o(1/L) is impossible in general.

## Prior work and scope

Niu, Su and Yang, [Structural Fine-Tuning under Economic Restrictions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25420v1), motivate the nested-fit comparison and give a finite example with a binding gate. The restricted Lipschitz notion is established in [Traonmilin, Aujol and Guennec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06607). Related theory includes [Peña, Vera and Zuluaga on relative Hoffman bounds](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-020-01473-6). Polyhedral image and recession arguments are standard. The specific claims here concern the finite-budget envelope and its matching rate in this prediction-error problem. The checks do not establish bibliographic priority or estimate a credit policy's performance. The earlier manuscript's assessment applies to its own PDF; this supplement has a separate source identity.

## Why polyhedrality matters even under a fixed budget

Set H=M=I, B={z2<=0} and C={z2<=-z1²}, a fixed closed convex hypograph. For q>0 set delta=3q+2q³, y=(delta,1) and t=(delta,-delta²). Then u=(delta,0) and e=(q,-q²). Indeed y-e=(1+q²)(2q,1) is outward normal at e. The ordinary error has norm delta². The new squared error is at least (delta-q)², and q<=delta/3. Thus the squared ratio is at least 4/(9 delta²) and tends to infinity as q decreases to zero. Both estimators, the truths and even the raw fits stay in a bounded region along this sequence. For every budget L>0 the supremum is already infinite. Intersecting C with the box [-1,1] x [-1,0] preserves the exact witness for sufficiently small q: t and e still belong to the smaller set and e still minimizes the distance. Consequently even compactness of a smooth-contact truth set near the witness cannot replace the polyhedral geometry. The compact truncation has corners away from the witness.

The contrast is in the baseline error. In the offset polyhedral example any sequence approaching the sharp constant must have baseline error tending to infinity. In the tangent convex example the ordinary errors tend to zero while the error ratio diverges. No stochastic frequency is inferred.

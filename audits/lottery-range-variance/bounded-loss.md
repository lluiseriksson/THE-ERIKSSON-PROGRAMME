# How a cap on adverse contrasts changes paired lottery trials

Research supplement to the four-draw uniform model in [PR 332](https://github.com/lluiseriksson/THE-ERIKSSON-PROGRAMME/pull/332). This supplement has its own source revision. The assessment of the earlier PDF applies to that PDF alone.

For two four-draw samples from the same uniform distribution on 0,…,n−1, preserve each arm's iid marginal and match both extrema and the sum on every trial. Write Q for the sum of squared prizes. Let Pₙ(D) be the largest probability that Q increases from the first arm to the second, subject to the adverse cap Q_A−Q_B ≤ D on **every** trial. At common sum this cap corresponds to D/3 in sample variance with divisor three. All results below concern the pairing design, without participant data.

## The first nonzero cap

For n ≥ 2, the sharp count n⁴Pₙ(2) is 12 binom(n,3) − 6(n−2). For 0 ≤ D < 2 the count is zero. At n=16 the two-unit cap allows 6,636 strict increases among 65,536 pairs. The optimum with unrestricted losses was 39,942.

Fix a stratum of minimum, maximum and sum. Write the two middle prizes as x≤y, with separation h=y−x. Increasing their separation by two while fixing their sum increases Q by 2(h+1). If a gap between consecutive score levels exceeds D, no flow can cross it downwards. Equal marginal masses on the two sides force zero crossing upwards as well. At D=2 the only possible two-level component is (t,t) and (t−1,t+1). Swapping the smaller of their counts in each direction is optimal. For a span d, there are d−1 eligible choices of t if d≥2. The balanced level has twelve permutations. At d=2 the other level has six. At d≥3 it has at least twelve. Weighting by the n−d choices of endpoints gives the formula. The parity of Q at fixed sum gives the zero result.

## A sharp transition as the number of prizes grows

For Dₙ/n → c with 0<c<∞,
    
    lim Pₙ(Dₙ) = ∫₀¹ 4(1−s)³ k(s)/(k(s)+1) ds,
    k(s) = floor(c/(2s)).
    
Equivalently this is the convergent sum over integers j≥1 of
[1−max(0,1−c/(2j))⁴]/[j(j+1)]. It is strictly between zero and one for every finite positive c. The limit is zero if Dₙ/n→0 and one if Dₙ/n→∞. The latter conclusions also follow by monotonicity from the finite-c formula and dominated convergence. These limits are in units of the unscaled prize grid; rescaling prizes by 1/(n−1) rescales the cap in Q by 1/(n−1)².

**Local estimate.** Within a stratum, h runs up a lattice of step two. A downward jump of j lattice steps from separation h loses exactly 2j(h−j) in Q, where j≤h/2. In particular it loses at least jh. Fix a closed interval I inside (0,1) on which c/(2s) is strictly between the same consecutive integers k and k+1. For h/n in I and all large n, a feasible downward jump takes at most k steps, and any available downward jump of k steps is allowed. Indeed j≤Dₙ/h is uniformly bounded and, for each bounded integer j, 2j(h−j)/n = 2j(h/n)+O(1/n). The inequalities are uniform because I stays away from the thresholds. When k=0 the statement means no downward jump is possible there.

Let M be the count of ordered quadruples in a particular stratum with h/n in I, using trial weights scaled by n⁴. Each separation level has mass at most 24. Across either endpoint cut of I, downward crossings can originate only within K lattice steps of that cut for some K depending on I and c: their source h is at least the cut separation and j≤Dₙ/h. Upward and downward crossing masses at each cut are equal. Both are O(1), uniformly in the stratum and in the coupling.

Clip the integer level index to the first and last levels in I (empty or single-level intersections contribute at most 24 to the error). The mean change of this clipped index is zero. Each positive internal comparison increases it by at least one; every negative internal comparison reduces it by at most k. The total reduction due to crossings is O(1), since the crossing mass and each crossing's downward displacement inside I are bounded. With W,L the internal positive and negative masses, W≤kL+O(1) and W+L≤M. Including the O(1) outward crossing mass, the wins from I are at most Mk/(k+1)+O(1).

Conversely, except for endpoint coincidences and balance, each level represents 24 distinct orders. For fixed endpoints and sum, coincidence with an endpoint can occur only at the largest feasible separation; balance occurs only at the smallest separation. Remove these levels and partition the remaining levels inside I into consecutive blocks of k+1. Shift each level up by one in its block and send the top level to the bottom. This preserves both marginals and the three controls, and all adverse jumps satisfy the cap. The unused levels can be paired identically. The construction gives at least Mk/(k+1)−O(1) wins. Constants may depend on I and c, but not on the stratum or n.

**From blocks to a limit.** There are O(n³) strata (two endpoints and the sum). Thus the accumulated error over any fixed finite collection of disjoint such intervals, divided by n⁴, vanishes. To compute the weight in each interval, generate iid grid prizes by taking the floors of n times four independent uniforms on [0,1). The normalized middle spacing converges almost surely to that of the continuous sample. For four uniform points the five spacings, including the two exterior gaps, are uniform on the unit simplex (the ordered joint density is 24, with unit change-of-variables determinant). Hence any one spacing has density 4(1−s)³. For any desired error exclude a short interval at zero, then short neighborhoods of the finitely many thresholds remaining and of the endpoints. Their total limiting probability can be made arbitrarily small. Apply the upper bound and the disjoint block construction elsewhere, pairing the omitted trials identically for the lower bound. This proves the integral. Summing the identity k/(k+1)=∑_{j=1}^k 1/[j(j+1)] gives the series. Small-s and large-s examples are both part of the same limit; the weights are ordered-trial probabilities.

**Design consequence.** A bound on the magnitude of a reverse comparison changes the interpretation of an asymmetric sign frequency. At fixed absolute cap, the achievable fraction of positive signs vanishes on increasingly fine *index counts with unit prize spacing* (a growing prize interval). A cap growing in proportion to n yields a nontrivial asymptotic fraction. A design seeking an almost unanimous sign needs Dₙ/n→∞: if the ratio has a bounded subsequence, take a further convergent subsequence and apply the corresponding limit. Symmetric random labeling separately bounds either strict direction by one half when the adverse constraint is not itself imposed asymmetrically after labeling.

**Prior work and scope.** Liu and Scheibehenne study perceived variability from samples. Mroz, Fernández-Sánchez, Fuchs and Trutschnig study extremal order probabilities under fixed marginals. The stratum-specific loss constraint and the growth regimes above address a more restricted design. Priority of this exact limit has not been established.

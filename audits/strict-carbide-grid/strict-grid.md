# Full support and unequal metal fractions in an eighth-grid carbide screen

In arXiv:2611.25127v1, section III.4, the six available metals are sampled with fractions in eighths over all nominal subsets with at least two metals. Filtering for all declared metals to be present gives 1,281 composition vectors, including all full-support equimolar vectors on this grid. Choosing k metal positions and splitting eight positive integer units among them gives C(6,k) C(7,k-1) vectors. Equal fractions require k to divide eight. Thus 15 binary and 15 quaternary equimolar vectors survive this filter, leaving 1,251 strictly non-equimolar vectors. A composition with k active metals is represented in 2^(6-k) nominal subsets before filtering. Weighting the counts by this multiplicity reproduces the published raw total of 7,581.

Supplementary Table S1 has five equimolar rows among its top 100, at ranks 1, 19, 20, 31 and 61. The first strictly unequal composition is nominal rank 2, Ta 3/8 and Zr 5/8 on the metal sublattice, T*=-801.049 K. Rank 1 is the 1:1 composition. This concerns the table label and the class of mixtures, not experimental synthesis.

Fix the entropies and independently perturb each predicted mixing energy per atom by at most epsilon. For winner w and competitor j the energy error radius at which a tie is possible is (T_j-T_w)/(1/S_w+1/S_j), because the w and j errors can take opposite signs. Here S=-(k_B/2) sum x log(x) for stoichiometric rocksalt MC. Use the temperatures reported to 0.001 K; allow 0.001 K total difference uncertainty from independent nearest rounding. Rank 3, the reversed Ta/Zr ratio, is the limiting opponent. Its tie radius is enclosed by 0.78661 to 0.78665 meV/atom. Every unlisted candidate has T* at least the rounded 100th-row lower endpoint. The minimum entropy over the grid is attained by the 7/8:1/8 binary, which majorizes every admitted vector. These two facts bound the radius for any unlisted strictly unequal composition below by 4.8709 meV/atom. The argument assumes the published table is ordered and exhaustive through rank 100. An energy MAE on a test set is not a uniform bound on composition-level mixing-energy errors.

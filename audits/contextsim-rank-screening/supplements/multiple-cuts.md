# What changes when two cutoffs belong to the same ranking?

For a single permutation, write D(k) for the number of selected ranks outside the first k, and E for half its squared displacement. Suppose several cutoffs k require D(k) ≥ d. Ignore zero demands. Around each cutoff form the coordinate interval [k−d+1,k+d], using one-based ranks and positions. For disjoint rank sets the minimum E equals the sum of the individual cubes. For intervals meeting at an endpoint, the lower bound below still holds; our attainment claim requires disjoint rank sets.

For the lower bound clip both endpoints of every item's movement to each coordinate interval. Along a movement these disjoint interiors contribute sublengths whose sum is at most the original displacement in absolute value. The sum of their squares is therefore at most its squared displacement. At a given cutoff take d distinct crossings in each direction. Even after clipping, the sum of the d clipped starting coordinates on the left is at most the sum of the d left coordinates nearest the cut. Indeed a rank farther away is clipped to the smallest of those d coordinates. The d clipped endpoints on the right have sum at least that of the nearest d right coordinates, by the same ordering argument. Thus in each direction the displacement sum is at least d² and the squared displacement sum at least d³ by Cauchy–Schwarz. Summing both directions over cuts proves the bound. On disjoint rank sets, simultaneous swaps of adjacent blocks of the requested sizes attain it.

Overlapping rank sets can instead change the cost in either direction. At cuts 1 and 2 with both demands 1 on three items, the two three-cycles cost 3, and reversal costs 4. These are the only feasible permutations; the sum of cubes was 2. At cuts 2 and 3 with demands 2 and 1 on four items, the order (3,4,1,2) attains energy 8, already the minimum for the first demand alone; the sum of cubes was 9. Appending untouched items preserves the examples (the lower bound of 3 in the first example also follows because energy at most 2 permits only disjoint adjacent swaps or the identity).

This supplement concerns the dependence of multiple operating points within one strategy. A general formula for overlapping intervals remains open in this investigation. These are synthetic rank examples.

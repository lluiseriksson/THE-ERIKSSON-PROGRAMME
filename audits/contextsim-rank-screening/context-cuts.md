# What correlations rounded to 0.73 and 0.70 imply for screening 55 ranked items

Bougie et al. describe a retrospective evaluation across 55 A/B tests, for each strategy, in §4.6. Figure 3 of the [publisher PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-industry.2.pdf) prints 0.73 and 0.70 for the two ContextSim variants and shows multiple points per method. The per-strategy ranks and their tie patterns are unavailable here. We examine a precisely defined question suggested by those numbers: suppose one simulated ranking and one observed ranking of 55 items have no ties and their Spearman correlation rounds to one of those labels. What overlap is guaranteed if the first k items are retained? Interpreting an aggregate plotted point as one particular pair of ranks would require further evidence. The result does not evaluate selections actually made by ContextSim.

Let d items cross out of the first k in either direction. For the d displaced ranks from the top, the sum of the downward displacements is at least d squared: the cheapest available original ranks are the d largest in the top set and the cheapest destination ranks are the d smallest in the bottom set. Cauchy–Schwarz then bounds the sum of their squared displacements below by d cubed. The same holds in the other direction. If E is half the sum of squared displacements, E is at least d cubed. Swapping the two adjacent blocks of d items attains equality. The tie-free coefficient is 1 - E / 13860.

At the displayed correlations the sharp overlap bounds, for every cutoff from 1 through 55, are

* for 0.73: from k - min(k,55-k,15) to k;
* for 0.70: from k - min(k,55-k,16) to k.

The rounding cell supplies the necessary bound on E. For sharpness inside that cell (and not merely a one-sided correlation threshold), we give two permutations for every cutoff and each printed value. They share a common energy for that printed value, including between the minimum-overlap and maximum-overlap constructions. The extrema at different cutoffs need not be realized by the same permutation. All 220 vectors are returned by the verifier, labelled as synthetic.

Start with the minimum-energy adjacent block swap. Across four consecutive blocks of lengths k-d, d, d, and 55-k-d, independently permuting each block keeps the overlap fixed. Its original displacement is constant within each block, so the cross terms sum to zero. Extra energy is therefore the sum of the chosen internal permutation energies. For a block of m items, the maximum half-energy is m(m²-1)/6 by reversal and the rearrangement inequality. For m=4 direct enumeration attains every integer up to this maximum. For m greater than 4, append the largest element or put it first and permute the remainder. The second option increases the remainder's half-energy by m(m-1)/2. The two attainable intervals overlap or touch because the maximum for m-1 plus one is at least this shift when m is at least 5. This proves completeness for these block sizes. Direct enumeration handles smaller blocks. The verifier constructs the vectors, then recomputes the correlation and overlap directly from every vector.

In particular a reported value of 0.73 for a single tie-free ranking of 55 tests is compatible with none of its top 15 being in the observed top 15, and also with all 15. At k=16, at least one must coincide. This is a statement about choosing from a ranking across tests; it does not give the correctness of choosing a treatment arm within a particular test.

Prior work studies distance measures on incomplete top-k rankings. [Fagin, Kumar and Sivakumar (2003)](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~vagelis/classes/CS242/publications/Fagin%20sidma03.pdf). We claim verified conditional bounds and explicit witnesses for this numerical setting. Priority for the general inequality has not been established.

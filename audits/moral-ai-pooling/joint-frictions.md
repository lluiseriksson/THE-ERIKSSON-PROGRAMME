# A local test of pooling under two frictions in the moral-AI game

Working derivation, confined to a defensive pooling equilibrium. The model is the joint extension suggested in Sections 4.1 and 4.2 of [Moral AI and the Erosion of Its Own Demand](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.25213v1). Its two individual frictions are taken directly from those sections. Independence between all visibility and bypass draws is an additional assumption for the joint game. All agents in the candidate pool choose unrestricted AI A. There is random matching, no refusal, and one simultaneous action by each agent. An observer sees the opponent's AI with probability ρ. Commitment M forces cooperation with probability 1−ε. An M-user privately learns whether the constraint binds. The population share of conditional cooperators is x. Material payoffs obey T>R>P>S, and conditional cooperators receive b in addition to R in mutual cooperation, with R+b>T. Write κ=(P−S)/(R+b−T+P−S).

Suppose 0<x<κ, 0<ρ≤1 and 1−ε>κ. Then defensive all-A pooling is sustainable exactly when

    ρ x [(1−ε)(R+b−S)+ε(T−P)] ≤ (1−ε)(P−S).

To see this, first consider the continuation on the equilibrium path. A selfish agent always defects. Even if all conditional agents cooperated, an A user observing A (or missing the label) could expect cooperation with probability at most x<κ. Thus everybody defects and receives P. An A user who sees an unexpected M cooperates if conditional: the probability of a forced cooperative action alone exceeds κ, independently of the off-path type belief. A selfish observer defects. If the counterparty misses the label, its action is on-path defection. A unilateral M deviation therefore elicits cooperation with probability q=ρx. Visibility of the other agent's label, the user's own bypass and the other agent's observation of the user are independent. Upon bypass, q<κ makes defection optimal for the deviating conditional type as well. Its gain over P is (1−ε)[S−P+q(R+b−S)]+εq(T−P). The selfish type's gain is smaller by (1−ε)qb. The displayed inequality therefore prevents either deviation, including at equality. For a continuation at unreached histories, a free conditional agent seeing M cooperates. An M deviator seeing A or no label can place probability one on an A opponent when the label is missing and use the on-path prior over types; these beliefs and choices arise in the limit of symmetric type-independent rare adoption. The actions at a double-M observation are optimal by the same forced-cooperation bound. If the inequality fails the conditional type has a strictly profitable deviation under every compatible off-path belief.

As ε tends to zero the local cutoff agrees with the visibility expression in Proposition 3 in the intersection of their domains. At ρ=1 it agrees with the small-bypass expression in Proposition 4 in their common domain. For T=5,R=3,P=1,S=0,b=3 and ε=1/10, the baseline cutoff 1/6 is exactly restored at ρ=27/29: at x=1/6 the opponent cooperates with probability 9/58 after a deviation. Substitution gives zero conditional gain and strictly negative selfish gain. This is a model prediction about a fixed pooling equilibrium. Classifying other equilibria and the evolutionary differential requires additional arguments.

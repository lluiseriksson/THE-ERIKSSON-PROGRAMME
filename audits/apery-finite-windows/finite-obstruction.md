# A lower bound on the degree of a finite Stieltjes obstruction
Research note, pending comparison with the companion materials and the literature on the least eigenvalue of Hankel matrices.

Write A_k(x) = sum over 0 <= j <= k of binom(k,j)^2 binom(k+j,j)^2 x^j, c = 17+12 sqrt(2), m_k=A_k/c^k. For s=0,1 write H_n^s(x)=[m_{i+j+s}(x)] for i,j from 0 through n. Diagonal congruence and a positive scalar account for normalization, so sign tests for the corresponding unscaled moments are equivalent.

Fix a=1/4, b=3/4, and let h be the minimum of c phi(cy) for a<=y<=b, where phi is Edgar's probability density of the Apéry numbers. This minimum is strictly positive: the interval c[a,b] is a compact subset of (1/c,c), where the density is positive and analytic. Let K=6+sqrt(35). Both matrices are strictly positive definite for x=1-delta whenever 0<=delta<=1 and
delta < a h (b-a)/((n+1)^2(2n+1)^2 K^(2n)).

Proof. By the nonnegative coefficients and 1-(1-delta)^j <= j delta, each moment changes by at most k delta A_k(1)/c^k <= k delta. The latter uses total mass 1 on [0,c]. The maximum absolute row sum bounds both perturbation norms by (n+1)(2n+1)delta.
For a polynomial P with coefficient vector v and degree at most n, the two original forms dominate a h times the squared L2 norm of P on [a,b]. Expand P in the orthonormal shifted Legendre basis L_j. Since every zero of L_j lies in (a,b) and a>0, its coefficient signs alternate. Its coefficient 2-norm is bounded by the coefficient 1-norm, equal to |L_j(-1)|. The affine argument at -1 equals -6. The classical Laplace integral for the Legendre polynomial at 6 bounds its absolute value by K^j. Hence the squared coefficient norm of L_j is at most (2j+1)K^(2j)/(b-a). Cauchy-Schwarz in the basis expansion bounds ||v||^2 by (n+1)(2n+1)K^(2n)/(b-a) times the squared L2 norm. Subtract the perturbation bound to conclude strict positivity.

Consequently if N(delta) is the least polynomial degree yielding a negative quadratic form for one of the two matrices, with infinity if there is no certificate, then liminf N(delta)/log(1/delta) >= 1/(2 log K). For any positive alpha below the right-hand side, substitute floor(alpha log(1/delta)) in the strict bound. The exponential factor dominates the polynomial prefactor, so all degrees through that order remain positive for small delta. This is a one-sided lower bound. It supplies neither a matching upper bound nor a certificate of any particular degree. The result concerns initial moment matrices of bounded degree. It does not settle coefficientwise total positivity. A finite numerical comparison would also require an explicit lower estimate for h.

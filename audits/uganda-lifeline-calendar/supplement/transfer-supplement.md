# How many kWh of chronological error can change a quarter of lifeline eligibility?

Keep the billed loads and total prior consumption fixed. For each historical month i specify valid bounds l_i ≤ x_i ≤ u_i. Allow at most B kWh to be moved across months (half the L1 change). A trailing window for a current bill consists of a historical suffix S plus certain fixed billed months. The largest possible increase in the window total is min(B, sum_{i in S}(u_i-x_i), sum_{i before S}(x_i-l_i)); the largest decrease exchanges headroom and removable mass. The two capacities and budget are necessary by conservation. They are attained simultaneously for every suffix by sending mass from the earliest remaining donor to the latest remaining receiver, until the budget is exhausted or their indices meet. Across a given suffix boundary the moves proceed from outside to inside until its donor capacity, its receiving capacity, or the total budget is exhausted. If donors and receivers have met before spending B, that cut has already exhausted one capacity. Reverse chronological order for the opposite extremum. Months on or after the first bill are fixed.

Thus to make every Q4 month eligible, compute each excess of its six-month sum over 600 kWh. If any positive excess exceeds its decrease capacity, full eligibility is impossible with the stated bounds. Otherwise the exact minimum shifted energy is the largest positive excess (zero if none). Making all months ineligible uses strict inequalities, so a threshold can be an infimum. On a finite meter grid use the first attainable total above the cutoff. The energy reallocation describes an uncertainty set for monthly accounting, not a recommendation to physically move a household's electricity demand.

The integer oracle checked 14,548 reachable combinations against the simultaneous witnesses. At a resolution of 0.0001 kWh, reassigning 13.9816 kWh is sufficient and necessary for the August-high case to qualify in all three Q4 months. For the January-high case, 51.0184 kWh is sufficient and necessary to lose all three discounts. Six-high histories in the retained example cannot reach three eligible Q4 months under the same bounded transfer model. These are conditional sensitivity results.

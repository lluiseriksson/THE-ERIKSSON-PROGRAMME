# How much of the recent majority in the coffee value-chain bibliography survives screening uncertainty?

Alan Obando Carlos reports that 360 of the 576 retained Scopus records on coffee, value-chain organization and sustainability fall in 2021–2026. The search was frozen on 18 November 2026. The question is whether the majority depends on the treatment of abstract-only coffee records, and whether the pilot adjudications support a majority among truly relevant records in the original search. All counts concern a bibliographic query, not the amount of activity in the coffee sector.

**Restoring excluded records.** Hold all retained records fixed, continue excluding one duplicate and two records without identified authors, and allow any subset of the 134 C3 exclusions back in. The late and early retained counts are 360 and 216. The C3 ledger has 77 late and 57 early records. Thus the late fraction can vary from 360/(576+57) to (360+77)/(576+77), or about 56.87% to 66.92%. Both endpoints are attainable. A late majority survives every restoration. Restricting to complete publication years through 2025 leaves 256 late retained and 216 early retained, with 59 late and 57 early C3 exclusions. The bounds become 256/529 and 35/59, roughly 48.39% to 59.32%. Exactly 40 extra earlier records can produce a tie and 41 a reversal in that complete-year window when no later records are added. Of the 104 retained 2026 records, 18 suffice to guarantee a strict majority even under the least favorable restoration, since 256+18 exceeds 216+57. Seventeen leave a possible tie. The robustness claim conditions on retaining all the 2000–2025 records that the original screen accepted.

**Targeting substantive relevance in the entire search.** The source reports a random pilot of 30 of 713 records, adjudicated by the author after an initial assisted reading. Conditional on uniform sampling without replacement, stable labels and the realized numbers drawn in each year stratum, the two stratum samples are independent simple random samples. The dated search frame has 438 recent and 275 earlier records. Of 14 recent records in the pilot, eight were judged relevant; of 16 earlier records, thirteen were judged relevant. A proportional expansion estimates 438(8/14)−275(13/16)=3007/112 additional recent relevant records, with substantial uncertainty. These estimates concern the original query including records later excluded. The inferential analysis is retrospective and conditions on the source's sampling description; we did not reproduce the draw or independently adjudicate the labels.

For recent and earlier population sizes N,M, sample sizes n,m, hits X,Y and fixed unknown totals A,B, use the one-sided statistic T=NX/n−MY/m. For testing A−B≤d, enumerate A=0,…,N and set B=max(0,A−d), ignoring infeasible B>M. Indeed, replacing an earlier irrelevant record by a relevant one can only decrease T for any fixed sample. The supremum of the exact product-hypergeometric tail Pr(T≥T_observed) is therefore attained on that lower boundary in B. Inverting the tests gives a one-sided 95% lower bound for the excess count A−B. The full-frame test of a tie or reversal has p=0.36715, attained at A=B=211; the lower bound is −79. Excluding 2026 leaves 26 pilot records, with 6/10 recent and 13/16 earlier judged relevant. The lower bound becomes −128. The pilot does not establish that most substantively relevant query results are recent. Nor does it establish an early majority. Conditioning on year sizes exploits the known vintage distribution, which is different from the pilot's vintage mix.

The auxiliary unconditional test uses the unweighted difference of true recent and earlier sample counts under multivariate hypergeometric sampling. Its one-sided p-value for the full frame is one: some admissible tied populations could never produce a score below the observed −5. This gives a concrete reason to distinguish calendar-composition uncertainty from relevance uncertainty. The package includes exact numerator/denominator output and 252 comparisons to exhaustive enumeration for small populations. The two date windows are sensitivity analyses; their bounds apply individually.

**Sources and reproducibility.** Primary source: [Obando Carlos, v08 dataset and supplement](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24406553), downloaded from its version record, including the author's adjudication and screening tables. The deterministic endpoints follow from year counts alone. Run coffee_bounds.py with the three named CSV files from the source's datos folder on the import path as local files (working directory); save its stdout as coffee.json, then run coffee_stratified.py. The public analysis contains aggregate results and scripts. The rights-limited full Scopus export is unnecessary. Exact finite-population inference has extensive prior art; our contribution here is the application to two distinct targets and the sharp cutoff of 18 partial-year records.

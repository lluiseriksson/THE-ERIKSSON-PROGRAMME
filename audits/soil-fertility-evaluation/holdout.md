# What the reported agricultural classifier accuracies identify

Behera and Padhy list 880 soil samples, an 80:20 stratified split and fivefold cross-validation. Table 2 describes a held-out test set for final evaluation. Table 4 reports two resampling accuracies to nine decimals. Treat the reported values as rounded, unweighted counts of correct predictions on one common test set. This identifies a consequence that can be checked without the fitted models. It does not identify the actual split used.

**Denominator.** The two accuracies are the nine-decimal roundings of 112/122 and 111/122. For any denominator N≤880, every fraction k/N unequal to 111/122 is separated from it by at least 1/(122N), greater than 0.000009. Fractions rounding to the same nine-decimal number differ by at most 0.000000001. Thus the second accuracy is exactly 111/122 under these assumptions. Since gcd(111,122)=1, N is a multiple of 122. The same separation argument determines the first accuracy as 112/122. Restricting N to at most the nominal holdout of 176 gives the unique value 122. With that denominator the two highest distinct accuracies in Table 3 are also compatible with 115 and 114 correct cases. No pair of integer success counts on N=176 yields the Table 4 accuracies.

**Paired interpretation.** On 122 common cases, let b count cases correct only for SMOTE and d count cases correct only for BL-SMOTE. The margins imply b=d+1 with d=0,…,10. Every such table exists: the count correct under both is 111−d and the count incorrect under both is 10−d. Conditional on b+d, the two-sided exact McNemar test with equal null probabilities gives p=1 for all eleven tables, because the observed discordant counts are the central two outcomes of an odd-sized fair binomial. This applies to Table 4 accuracy. The McNemar test the authors actually report compares a different pair of classifiers in Table 5. The analyses address different questions. The published weighted F1 and MCC also favor the first method descriptively; their uncertainty needs additional information.

**Sensitivity to the denominator cap.** For N=122m≤880 the net discordance is m. The smallest attainable two-sided exact p is 2^(1−m), attained when all m discordances favor one side. Adding one discordance in each direction weakly increases the two-sided tail probability: for X~Bin(t,1/2), compare Pr(X≤d) and Pr(X+Y≤d+1) with Y~Bin(2,1/2). The difference is [Pr(X=d+1)−Pr(X=d)]/4≥0 when t=2d+m, m≥1. Even the best possible pairing cannot put p below 0.05 until m≥6, or N≥732, under the same margins. The inference about 122 cases is conditional on individual test-set predictions. Fold averages, weights, exclusions or another cohort require a different reconstruction. The result makes no claim of misconduct or model failure.

**Implication.** Archive the effective test-case count and the two correctness indicators per case before treating accuracy alone as evidence for preferring one resampling method. The discrepancy can also be resolved by documenting a different aggregation protocol. Generalization to farms, minority-class performance and gains in weighted F1 do not follow from the sign test on accuracy.

Primary report, Tables 2–5, and sections 4.1–4.7. [Behera and Padhy, Engineering Proceedings](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4591/163/1/114). Exact paired inference is classical. The contribution here is the conditional denominator identification and its implication for this comparison.

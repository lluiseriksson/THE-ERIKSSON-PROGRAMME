# Is an article-to-role linkage necessary?

Condition on the number of recorded positions and documented comparisons in each article, and the total number of editor positions with a comparison. Before inspecting the policy results, compute the sharp interval for the advantage of always taking the editor over taking two positions uniformly. Use only these published-data margins; every allocation described is compatible with those margins alone. Check the interval extremes by a separate integer-count dynamic program. Vary the editor-positive total to locate the smallest value admitting an editor advantage and the smallest value forcing it. The calculation concerns existing review histories and recorded statements, without an intervention or attribution of actual editorial behavior.

For a nontrivial article profile (n,y), 0<y<n, attributing one of its y positive flags to the editor instead of assigning them all to reviewers raises the editor-included detection probability by 1-y/(n-1). The uniform-pair baseline depends only on (n,y). Profiles with y=0 or y=n force the editor flag. Under the total constraint each free positive editor flag must go to a different mutable article. Largest and smallest attainable sums therefore choose the largest and smallest gains. The extremal assignments are attainable by filling the remaining y-e reviewer positions in each article.

# Which two forecasts survive matters in a six-forecaster panel

**Decision.** When a forecast aggregator can retain two outputs from a completed panel of six, does it matter whether both forecasters received the same evidence? This is a descriptive reanalysis of 609 strict pre-end contracts from [Aluguvelly, FinNLP 2026, Tables 3 and 10](https://aclanthology.org/2026.finnlp-1.30/). The workshop took place in October. A November discovery/index record is not a November first-publication date.

**Experiment.** Condition C1 supplies the same two articles to six calls. Condition C3 assigns two calls to each of the three unordered pairs of three articles. Within each contract and condition fix all forecasts and the outcome. Retain exactly two outputs. With probability q choose both members of one uniformly selected evidence group. Otherwise choose two distinct groups uniformly and one member independently and uniformly from each. In C1 average over the 15 partitions into three pseudo-pairs as in the primary study. No retention decision uses the forecast or outcome. C1 then selects a uniform unordered pair for every q. This post-hoc thinning policy is different from running adaptively selected new queries.

**Identity.** For group means g1, g2, g3 and overall mean u, write B for the average of (g-u)^2 across groups, W for the average squared deviation from each member's own group mean across all six members, and E for squared error of u against the common outcome. Both selection plans have conditional expected mean u. With the same group, both members average to g, producing expected loss E+B. With distinct groups, the mean of the two group means has variance B/4 since their sum is 3u minus the uniformly omitted group mean. Conditional on the choice of groups, the two within-group deviations are independent, centered, and contribute W/2 after averaging over the group choice. Thus expected loss is E+B/4+W/2. Subtracting C1 from C3 and averaging over markets commutes with each operation. The point-estimate contrast is affine in q.

**Results.** Negative Brier contrast favors C3. Rounded published means imply the following conservative enclosures for expected contrasts and zero crossings. These intervals cover transcription precision only. The two component contrasts come from the source's between-pair and within-pair *changes*, not the absolute variance of C3.

| Endpoint | Both from same group | From distinct groups | Same-group probability at sign reversal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GPT-4.1-mini | +0.004185 to +0.004295 | −0.002528 to −0.002442 | 0.3625 to 0.3766 |
| Qwen3-30B | −0.004665 to −0.004555 | −0.009453 to −0.009367 | No crossing on [0,1] |
| OLMo-3.1-32B | +0.003065 to +0.003175 | −0.002348 to −0.002262 | 0.4160 to 0.4338 |

A simple random pair has q=3/15. The expected point contrast then favors routing for all three endpoints. Selecting both from the same evidence group reverses it for GPT and OLMo. Across all mixtures Qwen retains a favorable point contrast. The source's multiplicity correction for complete six-member panels does not test any of these two-forecast policies. Contract-level data and paired uncertainty estimates are needed for population claims. Real service outages can preferentially remove particular labels or contracts, which our mechanism does not identify.

**Verification and prior work.** The base ensemble squared-error decomposition is classical ([Krogh and Vedelsby, 1994](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/1994/hash/b8c37e33defde51cf91e1e03e51657da-Abstract.html)). Our application distinguishes two ways of retaining forecasts and bounds their reversal probabilities from the published aggregates. The script checks both identities against direct pair enumeration on all 729 ordered panels with values 0, 1/2 and 1 and both binary outcomes. The proof above covers arbitrary real forecasts. The zero crossing is a linear-fractional function of three inputs with a positive denominator on their rounding box. Its extrema over that box occur at vertices, since on each coordinate segment it is monotone or constant. The script verifies denominator positivity at all vertices. No new LLM calls, market-price downloads or claims about investment returns are involved.

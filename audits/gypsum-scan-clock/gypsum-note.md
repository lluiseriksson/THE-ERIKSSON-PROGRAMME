# How accurately can scan number reconstruct the acquisition clock of VA_dirS_08-LR?

We tested the public ESRF collection for the gypsum fault-gouge experiment reported by Harpers and colleagues. The DOI manifest links all 54 acquisitions to sample VA_dirS_08-LR, proposal ES-1548, beamline ID19, investigation 1917568640. Number the acquisitions from 0 through 53. Use the recorded startDate and endDate fields with their time zone offsets. These are start and end times for the archived acquisition records. Neither field locates the instant of a physical event inside a scan.

**Result.** For any fixed interval between consecutive acquisitions, with any choice of time origin, the worst error in assigning a recorded start time is at least 18,024,607/88 milliseconds, about 204.825 seconds. This bound is attained. From the first start (14:27:10.695 CET, 17 November 2024), a minimax fit in milliseconds is (12,835,839/88) + (11,076,409/44) times scan number. Scans 9, 48 and 53 certify optimality. For any linear clock, its fitted time at scan 48 is 5/44 of its time at scan 9 plus 39/44 of its time at scan 53. If all three residuals are bounded by E, the observed deviation from this interpolation is bounded by 2E. For these three timestamps half the deviation equals the stated lower bound. The checker evaluates the attained maximum over all 54 points using rational arithmetic.

**Acquisition gaps.** Fourteen of the 53 end-to-next-start intervals run from 372.466 to 410.704 seconds. They follow indices 0, 4, 8, ..., 52. Every other interval is at most 0.068 seconds. The largest gap separates the end of scan 36 at 17:00:44.606 and the start of scan 37 at 17:07:35.310 CET. The cadence concerns this public collection. Other acquisitions or instruments might cover these intervals.

**What can be inferred about an event?** Even ideal instantaneous observations just before and after an unsampled gap cannot locate a persistent transition within it. For example, place a hypothetical stress peak at the midpoint of gap 36 and suppose the localization indicator is zero before the gap and one afterwards. A monotone step at one quarter of the gap and a monotone step at three quarters give identical indicator values over every archived acquisition interval, with opposite order relative to that same hypothetical peak. This counterexample uses neither measurement noise nor uncertainty in clock synchronization. The real stress peak could occur elsewhere. To order the actual events requires a matched mechanical trace plus a localization measure and timing uncertainty. If the intervals for those events are disjoint on a common clock, their order is identifiable. The public metadata analyzed here establish the cadence result without identifying the physical order. We have not found evidence that the original authors assumed a constant cadence.

**Reproduction.** Run `python3 scan_clock.py` in a directory containing `scans.json`. Compare the JSON with `clock-result.json`. The retained rows contain public IDs, names, sample and timestamps.

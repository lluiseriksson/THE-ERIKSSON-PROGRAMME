# When can a top-up align water-compensation payments with downstream benefits?

The Nanpan River replication package models the net transfer induced by an upstream point-source project and the reduction in pollution load received by each prefecture. Its policy instrument P5 adds payments proportional to modeled downstream benefits. We ask whether that rule can make the *total* payer shares coincide with the benefit shares, and which additional budget would suffice if each prefecture could pay a different surcharge. These are model outputs and counterfactual transfers. No change in actual water quality or government payments has been observed in this analysis.

Consider only Kunming, Yuxi, Honghe and Wenshan. Normalize their modeled benefits to shares s_i with sum one, and write d_i for each prefecture's initial signed incremental payment. A negative d_i means a net receipt. Let R=sum d_i>0 be Qujing's incremental return, using the common annual contract base as unit. Suppose these prefectures pay additional nonnegative amounts z_i with sum x to Qujing. As in the original policy comparison, shares are calculated after clipping signed payments to their positive part. Suppose every s_i>0. Exact alignment requires every final payment to be positive. Hence exact alignment is equivalent to d_i+z_i=(R+x)s_i for every i. Since transfers sum correctly, the necessary and sufficient threshold and the only aligning schedule at that budget are

    x_min = max_i d_i/s_i - R
    z_i = (R+x)s_i - d_i.

The threshold is nonnegative because the share-weighted average of d_i/s_i is R. For a uniform benefit-proportional surcharge z_i=x s_i, the residual d_i−R s_i stays constant. An initially nonproportional signed schedule therefore cannot align exactly at any finite uniform surcharge. Before all net payments become positive, any clipped-to-zero prefecture with positive benefits independently rules out equality. Once all are positive the total-variation discrepancy equals sum_i |d_i−R s_i| / [2(R+x)]. This distinguishes an asymptotically small mismatch from exact alignment at a finite budget.

The script reads the 24 site, retention and calibration combinations in the released spatial-incidence table. The build_ring.py generator divides each signed transfer by years and the common base, and aggregates benefit shares over shared boundary reaches. The four downstream shares are renormalized after omitting Qujing and outside reaches, following the scope of the payer comparison in ring_policy.py. The threshold in currency units also applies when R is nonpositive provided the largest d_i/s_i is positive. We report ratios to the initial return only for R>0. For the lower project, the released table assigns all modeled load benefit outside the four-prefecture group, so its normalized benefit distribution is undefined. Input decimals are taken as reported; the certificates are exact relative to that table. Budget balance is cross-checked to rounding against the Qujing row.

The identified schedule offers a way of testing the incentive target in this model and does not establish its political feasibility. Equalizing these particular modeled shares does not measure avoided exceedance months, fiscal capacity, or a society-wide optimum. A common surcharge schedule that must be set before the calibration and retention scenario is known is a different problem.

## Results across the released spatial scenarios

For the primary upper-project central-retention scenario the minimum differentiated top-up is 111.693% of the original incremental return. The binding prefecture is Wenshan. A uniform top-up doubling the original return leaves a total-variation mismatch of 0.1390. Across the 11 upper-project scenarios with positive original return, the least differentiated top-ups range from 33.603% to 472.100% of the incremental return. Doubling the return permits exact alignment with differentiated payments in 3 of those 11. All 12 lower-project distributions are undefined for this downstream group. These spatial scenarios come from the spatial-incidence table; the separate policy table uses its own project assumptions.

In the March-2024 class-II recalibration at high retention, the upper project has negative incremental return. For that case a multiple of positive returns is undefined. The unique threshold in annual base units is 0.03082280, computed from max_i d_i/s_i minus the signed total. The largest d_i/s_i is positive, so the same proof applies.

Benefit-based allocation has substantial prior art. For example, the US Army Corps of Engineers describes the separable-costs/remaining-benefits method for multipurpose water projects in Appendix E of [ER 1105-2-100](https://www.publications.usace.army.mil/portals/76/publications/engineerregulations/er_1105-2-100.pdf). The present threshold concerns additional contributions to a fixed signed schedule and a specific published load-benefit model.

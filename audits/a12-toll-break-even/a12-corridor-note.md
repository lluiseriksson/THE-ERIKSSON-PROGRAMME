# Can a single effective length reproduce all published Kufstein–Innsbruck-Amras truck totals?

ASFINAG lists the A12 corridor as 75 km and gives a total in euro cents for each of 24 truck classes in its 2026 section-toll leaflet. Compare each total P_i with L times the class-specific model rate R_i, where R_i is the ordinary statutory rate plus one quarter of the infrastructure component. The published daily-distance table corroborates all ordinary rates. Prices and model inputs exclude VAT. This is a test of a continuous single-length calculation against a published tariff illustration, not an invoice reconciliation. [Official corridor leaflet, A12, first page](https://www.go-maut.at/media/zglel2dk/go-maut-tarife-2026_streckenmaut_en.pdf) · [Regulation §3](https://ris.bka.gv.at/eli/bgbl/ii/2025/298/P3/NOR40273503)

**Result.** None of the 24 displayed corridor prices lies within half a cent of 75 times its rate. The largest absolute difference is EUR 0.226875. Even allowing any common effective length, the smallest maximum discrepancy is EUR 0.024447, achieved at about 75.278113 km. A class-by-class table rounded only once to the nearest cent cannot therefore be reconstructed this way. The calculation does not identify where or how often the actual operator rounds charges.

**Certificate.** For tolerance e in euros, each class admits the length interval [(P_i−e)/R_i, (P_i+e)/R_i]. The intersection is nonempty precisely if each lower bound is at most every upper bound. Taking the maximum over ordered pairs gives e*=max_{i,j}(P_i R_j−P_j R_i)/(R_i+R_j). The exact verifier checks a length attaining the lower bound for every cell. A binding pair consists of the two-axle Euro IV, CO₂ class 1 truck (published EUR 27.58, modeled EUR 0.36605/km) and the three-axle Euro VI, CO₂ class 4 truck (EUR 31.63, EUR 0.42050/km). Those two cells alone contradict a single-length, final-cent-rounding explanation. Intermediate section rounding, effective charge lengths and other discrete tariff conventions require separate evidence.

The JSON contains the rational certificate and the Python script reads the previously verified threshold.json. The source of the displayed line totals is ASFINAG's truck row in each class, separately from its bus row.

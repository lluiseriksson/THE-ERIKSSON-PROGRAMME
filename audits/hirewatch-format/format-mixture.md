# How format compliance changes a selection-rate comparison in HireWatch

This audit uses Table 17 of [Nghiem, Kostylew and Kolt](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.24970v1) and the authors' [format-check notebook](https://github.com/hnghiem-nlp/hirewatch_repo/blob/3a230c4d1ef492d7398eb7cf6217ea362eac80e9/notebooks/qa/format_qa_check.ipynb). The observation is a trial; the binary format flag indicates that exactly the requested number of questions was returned. The response is the authors' recorded illegal-selection rate (ISR), expressed in percentage points. It uses their benchmark labels. The notebook pools no-instruction trials at temperature zero over the seven tiers and uses unweighted trial means, assigning a missing ISR field its default of zero.

Let A be a model's all-trial mean, F its mean among the m trials meeting the format, and n the total trials. For m<n, the excluded-trial mean is B=(nA-mF)/(n-m). To compare models at the same hypothetical share z of excluded-format trials use (1-z)F+zB. This calculation preserves each model's within-format means. It describes reweighting recorded outcomes; it does not establish that changing a prompt or enforcing format would cause the standardized rate.

Use the closed rounding intervals of radius 0.005 percentage points for A, F and the reported difference F-A. The checker intersects these three strips exactly and optimizes each linear score at their vertices. Comparison thresholds are ratios of affine functions on pairs of compatible vertices. When the order is strictly opposite at both stratum endpoints for every choice of inputs, there is one crossing and the denominator has fixed sign; extrema over the product polytope occur at vertices. Bounds cover printing precision. They do not quantify uncertainty from new occupations, seeds or model runs.

**Result.** Thirteen of eighteen models had recorded trials in both strata. For 31 of their 78 unordered pairs the direction of the recorded-rate comparison reverses between the two pure strata even after propagating rounding. Five models had no excluded-format trials in Table 17 and were omitted from this comparison. This result is compatible with the authors' high rank correlation between their own two summaries. It answers a different question about a common mixture.

For Sonnet 4.6, the 25 excluded-format trials out of 700 have a reconstructed mean between 66.32 and 66.60 percentage points. For Sonnet 4.6 and Mistral Small the crossover is bounded by 362/13097 and 366/13097 as fractions of excluded trials, i.e. about 2.764% to 2.795%. At a common share of 2%, Sonnet is between 1.4222 and 1.4322 points, below Mistral Small's 1.9325 to 1.9425. At 4%, Sonnet is between 2.7488 and 2.7600, above Mistral Small's 1.9500 to 1.9600. These are descriptive contrasts of the published estimates. They do not separate pressure tiers or occupations, and small excluded strata can give unstable means.

**Reproduction.** On Linux run format_mix.py alongside table17.json. bounds.json gives all certified comparisons. The full-grid table is separately transcribed from the authors' saved notebook output for the follow-up on baseline versus explicit instructions. The two regimes overlap; subtract the no-instruction component before interpreting the pooled explicit-instruction stratum. The exact binary trial archive was not needed or used for the Table 17 result.

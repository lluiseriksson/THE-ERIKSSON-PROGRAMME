# Choosing before the number of future batches is known

In the setting of the companion timing audit, define regret as the total seconds a policy uses beyond the minimum attainable in hindsight over all legal activation times and the option to stay with the larger baseline throughout. Each episode serves one configuration on the reported hardware; the cache remains valid. The size of a batch is known when it arrives. The number and sizes of subsequent batches are unknown. Once the system selects views it uses them for the rest of the episode. The comparator observes the realized batch sizes and service times. All claims are conditional on the benchmark service-cost model.

**Sharp deterministic result.** For Chronos-2 xhigh, activating on the first batch minimizes worst-case additive regret among deterministic policies. The value is 4.357 seconds at the printed central values and 4.3625 seconds uniformly over the jointly compatible display intervals. These values concern maximum total excess time per episode. They are not relative competitive ratios or estimates of measurement uncertainty.

**Upper bound.** Write D(f)=C(f)−T(f) for the cold premium and S(f)=T(f)−W(f) for the warm saving. Every S(f) is positive and moving any delayed activation one step earlier reduces cost under the measured bounds. The offline optimum is therefore the better of immediate activation and always using the baseline. Against this optimum, immediate activation incurs at most max D: subsequent warm batches reduce its excess relative to the baseline.

**Matching lower bound.** Consider repeated batches of a size maximizing D. If a deterministic policy never activates on this path, its regret relative to immediate activation grows without bound because S is positive. Otherwise end the episode at the first batch on which the policy activates. On that prefix it has accumulated exactly D more seconds than the always-baseline option, so its regret is at least D. Once the policy is fixed the terminal prefix is fixed, so even a horizon chosen before execution attains this lower bound. For the jointly rounded model take one-forecast batches, cold time 4.495 s, comparator time 0.1325 s, and small-model base time 0.01434 s. Their cold/base ratio is inside [312.5,313.5]. If a reporting tie convention excludes an endpoint, times arbitrarily close to it give the same supremum. All remaining rows admit compatible choices independently.

This decision model is related to the classical rent-or-buy problem. The objective here is additive latency regret, with the option to activate at different measured batch sizes. The argument does not analyze randomization against a workload selected independently of the policy's random draws.

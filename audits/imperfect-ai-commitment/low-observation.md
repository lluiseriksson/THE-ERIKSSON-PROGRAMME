# Limiting preference shares with imperfectly observed AI commitment

Research note on Ao and Wu, *Moral AI and the Erosion of Its Own Demand*, arXiv:2611.25213v1, especially Definition 1, Section 4.1 and Appendix A.4. Scope: random matching, independent observation of the other player's device with probability rho, binding cooperation under M, and the paper's symmetric type-homogeneous PBE and continuous payoff-monotone dynamics. This is an analytical model of incentives, without an empirical claim about adoption.

Let p=P-S, h=R+b-T, L=R+b-S, k=p/(h+p) and c=p/(rho L). Assume 0<rho<=(h+p)/L, so c>=k and rho<1. For an interior starting share x0, the set of limits over all dynamics allowed by Definition 1 is

    [min(x0,k), min(x0,c)].

This identifies an evolutionary outcome without solving every mixed continuation in the low-observation region. It describes the latitude in Definition 1, which does not fix a replicator or equilibrium-selection rule.

1. A selfish player can copy the conditional type's random device choice and defect whenever discretion remains. The opponent receives the same signal law, and defection strictly dominates cooperation in material payoffs against either action. Every equilibrium fitness gap is therefore nonpositive, strictly so whenever the conditional type voluntarily cooperates with positive probability.

2. Selfish players never adopt M in equilibrium. Write q_M and q_A for the opponent's cooperation probabilities conditional on one's device. With independent observation, q_M>=q_A: upon seeing M a discretionary conditional opponent cooperates and a selfish opponent defects, and an uninformed opponent's action does not change with one's device. For a conditional type, the extra utility over a selfish type's optimal expected payoff on M is b q_M. With A it is at most b q_A and is strictly below this bound if q_A>0: either the extra mutual-cooperation benefit is forgone on some positive-probability cooperating encounter or own cooperation incurs a strictly positive material cost. Consequently if any selfish type adopts M, an opponent is forced to cooperate with positive probability even against A. The conditional type then strictly prefers M whenever the selfish type weakly prefers M. With every conditional type using M, the opponent is either constrained or selfish; no opponent changes actions in response to one's device. A selfish player strictly prefers A. This contradiction works at every positive rho in our regime.

3. A zero fitness gap requires no adoption. If the conditional type sometimes uses A and any M users exist, there is positive probability of observing M while discretionary, which requires cooperation and makes imitation strictly profitable. If all conditional players use M, against each opponent a selfish player can defect using A, giving expected material payoff xT+(1-x)P rather than xR+(1-x)S. When nobody adopts M, equality requires defection by all conditional types on path. With all defecting, an M deviation by a conditional type has gain rho x L-p: an informed conditional opponent responds to M by cooperating, and everyone else defects. The selfish type's gain is weakly smaller. Thus a zero-gap pooling equilibrium exists exactly when x<=c. In particular every equilibrium gives a strictly negative gap when x>c.

4. Below k only the zero-gap outcome is possible in this parameter regime. A conditional A user cannot cooperate when seeing A or no label: the probability of opponent cooperation is at most x (the posterior conditional-type probability given A is at most x by step 2). When seeing M she must cooperate. If m is the conditional type's adoption probability, the resulting gain from M instead of A is affine in m, with endpoint gains rho x L-p and x(1-rho)h-(1-x)p. The first is strictly negative since x<k<=c. The second is negative since x<k implies xh<(1-x)p. Thus m=0. By Definition 1 the velocity vanishes on (0,k), and continuity gives V(k)=0.

5. For every x>=k there is a second pooling equilibrium, with both types using A and all conditional players cooperating on either on-path signal. They best respond since the perceived cooperation probability is x, including at equality. Seeing the off-path committed device also calls for cooperation. Changing devices does not change opponents' actions. Conditional types are indifferent, and selfish types strictly prefer discretion. The on-path material gap x(R-T)+(1-x)(S-P) is strictly negative. Hence throughout [k,min(c,1)] at interior shares there are both a zero and a negative equilibrium gap, permitting any nonpositive velocity subject to the global continuity and endpoint conditions.

6. Every trajectory is nonincreasing, cannot traverse the interval on which V vanishes below k and cannot converge from above to any interior share greater than c at which V is strictly negative. Together with x(t)<=x0 this proves the stated bounds. Attainment needs no discontinuous selection. For any ell in [k,min(c,1)] take V_ell(x)=-x(1-x) max(x-ell,0). It is continuous and Lipschitz, respects the signs in every region and yields limit min(x0,ell). If c=k the range collapses continuously to the paper's high-observation formula. For c>=1 some permissible dynamics leave every starting state fixed. This does not assert that all equilibrium selections yield stasis or that the true velocity equals this illustrative family.

For T=5,R=3,P=1,S=0,b=3 and rho=1/4, k=1/2, c=2/3. Starting at x0=4/5, every limit from 1/2 through 2/3 is attainable. At ell=3/5 the two no-adoption equilibria yield a zero and a strictly negative gap. Observation is exogenous; experimentally estimating a selection rule is a separate task.

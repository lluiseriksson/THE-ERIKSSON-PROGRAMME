# Positive adoption at a permissible rest point

Use Ao and Wu's illustrative payoffs T=5,R=3,P=1,S=0,b=3 with observation probability 1/4 and conditional-cooperator frequency x=3/5. The low-observation limit interval includes 3/5 for every initial share at or above it. Consider a defensive continuation: every selfish player chooses A and defects. A conditional player chooses M with probability 2/3; when using A she cooperates if and only if she sees M. The population adoption rate y is 2/5.

A conditional discretionary player who sees A faces defection, while observing M guarantees cooperation. Without a signal the cooperation probability against her A is y=2/5, strictly below k=1/2. These are sequential best responses at all three information sets. For general x with this continuation, the conditional utility difference between M and A is rho x L-p+y[(1-rho)(h+p)-rho(R+b-P)]. Substituting the example gives (3/2)x-1+y/4. Thus y=4-6x gives indifference, feasible with strict continuation inequalities throughout 7/12<x<2/3. The selfish type strictly prefers A (the incentive comparison in the main argument is strict). A positive-adoption equilibrium therefore exists on an open interval, alongside the zero-adoption pooling equilibria.

The field V(x)=-x(1-x) max(x-3/5,0) satisfies Definition 1 and has limit 3/5 from any interior x0>=3/5. For initial x0 in (3/5,2/3), choose the defensive mixed equilibrium on this entire trajectory; adoption then converges to 2/5. The definition constrains the velocity by the signs across *all* equilibria, so it permits stasis or vanishing speed even though the selected mixed equilibrium has strictly negative material fitness gap. A law requiring strict response to the realized equilibrium's gap at every state would be stronger. This construction refutes extension of universal zero limiting adoption to low observability under the original definition. It makes no assertion about actual platform users.

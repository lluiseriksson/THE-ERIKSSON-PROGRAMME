# What one missing issuer-day leaves unidentified in the REDATA event comparison

This supplement concerns portfolios that renormalize issuer weights on the available stocks each day. The manuscript's fixed-weight calculation on published issuer totals remains valid for that different estimand. The original replication script computes its equal-weight headline by taking each day's mean over the available residuals. We use the versioned [REDATA replication deposit](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24405076). The analysis below is deterministic and conditional on the stated observation pattern.

## Can the reported returns establish event-day coverage?

Let the eight printed issuer CARs have arithmetic mean m and let p denote the printed three-day equal-weight portfolio CAR in the same row. In each of the four event/model rows, |p-m| is at most 0.005 percentage points. Set the latent issuer CARs to their printed values plus (p-m)/2. Each is strictly inside its printed cell, and their mean is strictly inside the portfolio cell. Let s be the printed event-day equal-weight return. Construct a three-day panel with zero for every issuer on the first day, s for each on the event day, and issuer total minus s on the last day. Delete just one zero on the first day. The complete and incomplete panels give exactly the same issuer CARs, three daily equal-weight averages, and central-day and three-day headlines. Thus these printed disclosures, even supplemented with the three exact equal-weight daily returns of the construction, do not establish completeness. These panels are examples compatible with the specified disclosures in each row separately. We have not fit actual prices or a joint return-generating model across specifications.

## A sharp disclosure result for portfolios that renormalize daily

Assume exactly issuer k is missing on one day and all issuers are present on the other days. Let c_i be each issuer's observed CAR, let x_i be its residual on the incomplete day for i different from k, and fix nonnegative portfolio weights summing to one with w_k < 1. Summing the full-day contributions and the incomplete-day contribution gives the exact identity

    renormalized CAR = sum_i w_i c_i + [w_k/(1-w_k)] sum_(i != k) w_i x_i.

Suppose exact issuer CARs, the mask and each daily equal-weight return are known. For one specified portfolio there is no remaining ambiguity if w_k=0 or the seven observed issuers have identical weights. Otherwise exactly one additional real linear summary suffices, namely the incomplete day's weighted sum in the identity. To see necessity, choose two present issuers i,j of different weights. Add h to i and subtract h from j on the incomplete day, and undo both changes on a complete day. Every issuer sum and each day's equal-weight mean is unchanged. The renormalized CAR changes by h(w_i-w_j)w_k/(1-w_k). Without any bound on the signed residuals the feasible set for this one portfolio is the whole real line. This is an identification statement, not an estimate of plausible realized volatility.

For **every** portfolio whose weights lie between 5 and 20 percent, six additional independent linear summaries are necessary and sufficient to resolve this ambiguity with eight issuers and a known single missing cell. The seven present residuals on the incomplete day have known sum, leaving six free directions. Reporting any six of them suffices. For the lower bound, realize every zero-sum perturbation d of those seven residuals by offsetting it on a full day. Any family of fewer than six real linear disclosures on the panel has a nonzero kernel on this six-dimensional subspace. Keep the missing issuer's weight at 1/8 and choose the seven remaining weights as 1/8 plus a sufficiently small positive multiple of d. The weights remain strictly between the caps and the change in the incomplete-day weighted sum is a positive multiple of the squared norm of d. Therefore the disclosed summaries leave some admissible portfolio unresolved. This dimension count concerns real linear disclosures and exact reconstruction; it does not apply to arbitrary encodings or to approximate estimates.

In an explicit diversified example, four issuers receive 20 percent and four receive 5 percent. With one of the 20 percent issuers missing, a one-percentage-point offset between another 20 percent issuer and a 5 percent issuer changes the three-day portfolio CAR by exactly 3/80 of a percentage point. Equal-weight daily returns and all issuer CARs are identical. A corresponding move appears in classical fixed-margin table methods; the application here is the effect of the daily normalization and the amount of disclosure required to reconstruct a portfolio. See [Diaconis and Sturmfels](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Diaconis/Diaconis8.pdf).

A mask alone can certify complete coverage. In an incomplete window it locates missing observations, but the required extra linear information depends on whether one portfolio or a whole class is to be reconstructed. The price-file fingerprints in the deposit can authenticate a recovered vintage. A different fingerprint alone neither proves historical revision of the relevant sessions nor establishes missingness.

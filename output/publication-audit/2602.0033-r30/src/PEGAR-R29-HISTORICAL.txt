ERRATUM (v3). Neither of the two pillars of this paper is established: neither the gap at the terminal scale (Theorem 3.1) nor the doubling cascade that transports it (Theorem 4.1). Consequently Theorem 1.1 is not established at several distinct points audited below. What the counterexamples below refute are general implications used by the proofs, one level below the numbered results: both abstract extraction implications are false. There are two of them, with different premises and different conclusions, and each family satisfies the abstract hypotheses relevant to the implication it targets, not the union of both sets. The exact-trace family targets the exact/additive extraction, with zero admitted error and the printed window of the displayed estimate; its gap is a fixed positive number, so the product of gap and window tends to infinity and the one-sided cap of part (i) is not among its hypotheses, nor need it be. The finite-error family targets the finite-window relative extraction and does satisfy that cap for every kappa' >= 1. An earlier version of this erratum said both families met every printed hypothesis; they do not, and they need not. Since none is shown to arise from the Balaban effective actions or to coincide with the transfer operators of Step 1, the conclusion for the intended Yang-Mills transfer operators is not established, not refuted here; that no such family can so arise is not established either. The additive estimate displayed in Proposition 4.4 is not established by the printed proof, and it is not a survivor either, because the exact-trace family also contradicts the displayed additive estimate read as a general implication; Proposition 4.4(ii) is not reached by the two families in its own volume regime but is likewise not established by the printed proof, since it rests on the same additive extraction, and its separate volume-cap algebra is not audited here; Lemma 4.3(a) is a conditional algebraic identity; and Lemma 4.3(b) is an invalid inference that uses a lower bound as an upper bound. Nothing here claims the physical gap differs from what the paper expected: what is refuted are the general implications that would extract it from those data. Classified rather than counted, what is false here is the exact/additive extraction implication, the finite-window relative extraction implication, and the printed dimension of the orbit space; none of the three is a conclusion of a numbered result about the intended operators, and this note does not claim the list is closed. This audit does not refute (H-BAL) or (H-FACT), but those hypotheses do not suffice for the printed conclusions without the additional operator and identification inputs recorded below. For Theorems 1.1, 3.1, 4.1 and Proposition 4.4, what is withdrawn here is the printed derivation; no conclusion of theirs about the intended operators is refuted. An earlier version of this erratum declared the conclusion of Proposition 4.4(i) refuted outright; that went one level too far and is withdrawn, and the families themselves stand.

The printed derivation of Theorem 3.1 breaks down for three separate reasons. First, the printed Euclidean measure is not identified with the ground-state measure: Step 2 introduces the Gibbs measure of the EFFECTIVE ACTION, dmu_print = e^{-S_eff} dVol_B / Z, applies Holley-Stroock, and then writes the terminal gap as half the squared coupling times the first non-zero eigenvalue, which is the Hamiltonian identity and requires the measure to carry minus the logarithm of the squared ground state. The Gibbs measure of a Euclidean effective action is a priori a distinct object and its weighted Dirichlet-form gap a priori a distinct spectral quantity; no identification of dmu_print with psi_0^2 dnu_push has been proved, and in general the passage requires a transfer-operator or Osterwalder-Seiler identification theorem. The programme already knew this: the v2 of ai.viXra:2602.0035 corrected the same substitution in its own v1, recording the action-based measure as dimensionally inconsistent and numerically failing by more than fifty per cent. That numerical check concerns the literal potential-based measure used in that paper's v1, not the epsilon-corrected effective-action measure printed here; it supplies strong numerical counterevidence to a universal action-measure substitution and reinforces that no identification may be made by notation, but it is neither a proof that no general identification exists nor an analytic counterexample to Theorem 3.1 of the present paper; the reason to withdraw the printed derivation is that no identification theorem has been proved, not that a number came out wrong. Second, the geometric input is no longer available: the pointwise Ricci bound cited from ai.viXra:2602.0036 is stated at REGULAR points while Bakry-Emery is applied globally, and that theorem is itself not established by its printed proof, its O'Neill trace omitting mixed sectional terms that subtract. No pointwise Ricci bound on the orbit space may presently be cited, at regular points or anywhere else. What is available instead is the synthetic global statement of ai.viXra:2602.0046 v3 for the metric-measure quotient. That statement is for the pushforward measure nu_push, and it does NOT repair Step 3 by a change of reference measure. What Step 2 prints is a regular-stratum formula against Vol_B, dmu_print = Z_print^{-1} e^{-S_eff} dVol_B on B_reg, with no global extension and no closed Dirichlet form specified. On B_reg one has dnu_push = w dVol_B with w the orbit-volume density, so dmu_print = Z_print^{-1} e^{-(S_eff + log w)} dnu_push, an exact rewriting of one and the same measure with the same normaliser. Relative to nu_push the printed measure therefore carries the exponent S_eff + log w, whereas the candidate nu_push-based measure dmutilde = Ztilde^{-1} e^{-S_eff} dnu_push carries S_eff; they coincide only under an additional constancy or identification statement for w, none of which is established here, so replacing dVol_B by dnu_push under a fixed exponent is not a free rewriting. Nor is the converse asserted: the two measures are not claimed to be proved distinct, which would need w non-constant. Two routes are open and they are different theorems. Route A keeps the printed measure and must extend the regular-stratum formula globally and bound the oscillation of S_eff + log w, for which the bound on the oscillation of S_eff does not suffice, since w may degenerate at strata of enlarged stabiliser; no control of log w is supplied anywhere in this chain. Route B reformulates over (B,nu_push) with the candidate measure, where the synthetic result does supply the base inequality, but that is not the measure the paper printed and a theorem is then owed showing it is the correct Euclidean object. Third, a four-dimensional Euclidean effective action is used as the exponent of a measure on a three-dimensional spatial orbit space, with no transfer-matrix construction connecting them. Step 1 also prints the dimension of that orbit space as three L cubed times N_c squared minus one, which is the dimension of the spatial configuration manifold and not of the quotient: the site gauge group acts with discrete generic stabiliser, so the principal-stratum dimension is two L cubed times N_c squared minus one, and at reducible configurations the quotient is stratified. The order L cubed against order L to the fourth mismatch is unchanged by that correction. Three further defects. First, Lemma 4.3 uses an inequality backwards: Theorem 3.1 supplies only that the terminal gap is at least c_0, and Lemma 4.3 writes m_{nmax} = c0 = (gamma N_c/8) exp(-K/gamma), replacing that lower bound by an equality, and then uses the smallness of that quantity as an upper bound, so the claim that the doubling window is automatic is not established and would not be established even if Theorem 3.1 were repaired; what the window needs is an upper bound on the terminal gap, or the window condition assumed outright. Second, the printed control on the accumulated factorisation error ceases to be small as the coupling tends to zero: the proof carries a factor one plus order of the terminal scale times e to the minus kappa over two, replaces it by one plus little-o of one, and folds the smallness condition into the upper bound on the coupling; but the terminal scale grows like one over g squared, so that BOUND tends to infinity as the coupling tends to zero and the condition is a LOWER bound on the coupling, which cannot be absorbed into a hypothesis of the form coupling at most g_0 squared. Stated exactly: with R(g) the relative remainder, the printed estimate is an upper bound |R(g)| <= A_rel(g) n_max(g) exp(-kappa_tilde(g)/2); the printed upper bound ceases to be small and becomes unbounded, but this does not prove that the actual error grows, since R identically zero satisfies any such bound. What follows is only that the printed estimate can no longer justify the replacement by one plus little-o of one: information insufficient, not conclusion false. Earlier versions of this erratum said the accumulated error itself grows and diverges, which reads an upper bound as a value. The printed hypothesis does not specify coupling uniformity: (H-FACT) says only that kappa_prime is independent of L_n, not that it or C_fact is independent of g or uniform on the admitted coupling range. The printed hypothesis does not suffice under either reading. The printed hypothesis is insufficient under either reading. If C_fact, kappa_prime and a fixed explicit bound A_rel are uniform in g, the printed upper bound becomes unbounded as g tends to zero and therefore ceases to justify R(g) = o(1); this does not prove that the actual accumulated error diverges. It cannot be folded into an upper bound on the coupling either; repairing that branch means restricting the theorem to a coupling window chosen so that A_rel times n_max times exp(-kappa_tilde/2) stays below some eta less than one, since a compact window alone only makes n_max finite and not small against the error floor, and in any case that changes the statement rather than repairing its printed quantifier. If they may depend on g, the hypothesis is under-specified: with kappa_tilde(g) = kappa_prime(g) - log C_fact(g), choose an explicit A_rel(g) >= 1 with |R(g)| <= A_rel(g) n_max(g) exp(-kappa_tilde(g)/2) - a specified function, not the implicit constant of an O, which is not unique and can be scaled up at will; the accumulated factor is then that bound, so the conclusion needs kappa_tilde(g) - 2 log n_max(g) - 2 log A_rel(g) to tend to infinity, that is kappa_tilde(g) = 2 log n_max(g) + omega(1) once the implicit relative-error constant must be uniform in g is granted, which since n_max grows like one over g squared means kappa_tilde(g) at least 4 log (1/g) + omega(1). It is NOT an O(1) statement: a bounded difference keeps the factor below a constant, it does not send it to zero. That growth condition is an additional missing hypothesis. This propagates to Theorem 1.1's own constant: the paper declares c(N_c) to depend only on N_c and Balaban's universal constants, which cannot be maintained unless C_fact, kappa_prime and the implicit O-constants are controlled uniformly over the admitted data. Third, a debt of domain rather than of proof: the blocking sequence requires admissible volumes, two to the terminal scale plus one at most L at most C_0 times two to the terminal scale, so a multiple of two to the terminal scale is not enough by itself, and the lower endpoint moves with the coupling; Theorem 1.1 quantifies over every L in an interval and no rounding, interpolation or irregular-blocking argument is given for the rest, so it is not literally established with its printed quantifier over all L.

Theorem 4.1 is independently not established, and this is the defect that removes the last standing pillar. Step 1 defines the transfer operator on the spatial orbit space of a lattice with equal spatial and temporal extent, so both the Hilbert space and the operator depend on that extent. Step 2 then sets the exponent equal to that same extent and justifies the limit by varying the original isotropic lattice size: the transfer operator changes when the isotropic volume is varied, so the limit is not taken over powers of one fixed operator. The asymptotic expansion of Step 3 extracts eigenvalues only for a fixed operator raised to a growing power, yet it treats the eigenvalues and the gap as constants in the exponent. That the family of trace identities does not imply the doubling or the displayed additive estimate is shown by an abstract example, which is not the stronger claim that the identities carry no spectral information at all: for fixed a,b>0 with b different from twice a, take a two-by-two diagonal operator with gap a at one scale and, at the next, a positive multiple of a diagonal operator with gap b; the multiple can be chosen so that the two traces agree for every even exponent while b ranges over all positive values other than twice a. Positivity of a and b is what makes the top eigenvalue the first entry, so the two gaps are exactly a and b. A three-by-three version removes any objection that the remainder term has no third eigenvalue to refer to: with fixed a, b, r, s positive, b different from twice a, r above a and s above b, take diag(1, e^{-a}, e^{-r}) at one scale and, at the next, the same normalising multiple times diag(1, e^{-b}, e^{-s}); the traces then agree exactly for every even exponent with zero admitted error, the gaps are a and b and the next distinct gaps are r and s, and the displayed additive estimate would force b = 2a + O(e^{-(r-a)M}/M), whose right-hand side tends to twice a, contradicting the fixed choice. So the exact-trace family also contradicts the displayed additive estimate. Verified at a = 1/3, b = 1, r = 2, s = 5 for M = 4, 8, 20, 60, 200 and 1000. A second and purely local defect of Step 3 is recorded as well: the printed expansion sets the coefficient of the leading correction to one and takes the remainder exponent strictly larger, but a degenerate second eigenvalue makes the remainder the same order as the term it is supposed to dominate, and positive self-adjoint trace class does not make the top eigenvalue simple; the correct form groups distinct eigenvalues with their multiplicities. This does not change the verdict. The one-dimensional clock-chain miniature of the verification suite cannot detect the main defect, because in one dimension varying the length varies only the temporal direction and the operator stays fixed. A fixed spatial slice by itself is not sufficient: rectangular lattices with the slice held fixed remove the dependence of the operator on the exponent, but the extraction still needs an identity to feed it, and the printed one is obtained by varying the isotropic size. Repair would therefore require a fixed-spatial-slice family TOGETHER WITH an identity valid for all temporal extents over that fixed space; or an operator relation such as conjugacy of one transfer operator to the square of the previous, which gives the doubling directly; or a quantitative, normalised spectral-convergence theorem for the volume-dependent family, carrying the multiplicities. Ordinary spectral convergence is insufficient, and the exact-trace family above already refutes it: the normalising multiple tends to one, so both operators converge in operator norm and every eigenvalue converges, the limiting gaps being a and b with b different from twice a; the correction to the leading eigenvalue is of the same exponential order as the first excited contribution and absorbs it exactly. What a repair needs is leading-eigenvalue normalisation below the first-excited scale, carrying the multiplicity of the top eigenvalue, since the leading trace term is that multiplicity times the leading eigenvalue to the power of the exponent: the ratio of multiplicity times leading eigenvalue at the next scale, evaluated at half the exponent and raised to half the exponent, to the same quantity at the present scale, must equal one plus little-o of exp(-mu M), with mu the minimum of the LIMITING gap m_n and half the limiting gap m_(n+1), together with convergence of the ratios of distinct eigenvalues and control of the multiplicities; and subexponential control of the excited multiplicities, that is, one over M times the logarithm of the ratio of the first-excited multiplicity to the top multiplicity tending to zero at both scales, AND uniform control of the total excited trace tail, that is, the sum over levels j at least two of the ratio of the multiplicity of level j to that of the first excited level, times the exponential of minus the difference of their gaps times the exponent, tending to zero at both scales. Level-by-level multiplicity control is not sufficient: it bounds each row of an enumeration, not how many rows there are. Positivity improving removes only the top-eigenvalue multiplicity: it says nothing about the excited ones, nor about the spectral counting function. A positive counterexample shows the difference: with D_M the integer part of exp(M), take at one scale the matrix q times the identity of size D_M + 1 plus one minus q times the rank-one averaging projector, with q = exp(-2), and at the next the same construction in size two times a normalising multiple. Both have strictly positive entries, hence are positivity improving; the traces agree exactly; both top eigenvalues are simple; the distinct-eigenvalue ratios are constant; the normalisation quotient is one plus little-o of exp(-mu M); and both gaps equal two, so the doubling fails. The mechanism is the excited multiplicity: the trace sees the effective exponent two minus one over M times log D_M, which tends to one, not the spectral gap two. Checked at M = 4, 8, 16, 30 and 60. A second family forces the tail condition and is not caught by the first: keep D_M and take at one scale the diagonal operator with entries one and, for j up to D_M, e to the minus two times the M-th root of one plus epsilon_j, with the epsilon_j distinct in minus a quarter to a quarter and summing to zero; at the next scale the same normalising multiple times diag(1, e^-2). The traces agree exactly, EVERY multiplicity is one so the multiplicity condition holds trivially, the excited ratios converge uniformly, both limiting gaps are two, the normalisation quotient is again one plus little-o, and the doubling still fails. What fails is the tail: each of its D_M - 1 terms lies between three fifths and five thirds, so the sum grows like e^M. Checked at M = 4, 8, 16 and 24. Neither condition alone excludes both families.

\title{The Measure the Spectral Results Were About:\\
a Machine-Checked Transfer Bridge for the Spatial $\Z_2$ Slice\\
\large the dressing identity, and the modulus\\
that a strict gap does not supply}
\author{Lluis Eriksson}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Four companion papers studied an \emph{operator}: a strictly positive kernel on
the spatial configuration space of a $\Z_2$ slice, its Perron vacuum, and the
strict separation of its spectrum \cite{ErikssonSpatial,ErikssonBirkhoff,%
ErikssonPerron,ErikssonGap}.  None of them exhibited a \emph{measure}.  That
omission is the kind this programme is built to notice: a transfer operator is
a matrix until something says which Boltzmann weights it transfers, and until
then a vacuum is an eigenvector and a gap is a statement about eigenvalues.
Neither is yet a statement about a statistical-mechanical system.

\textbf{Proved.}  We define the two-dimensional Gibbs weight of the spatial
system from Boltzmann factors alone --- a spatial factor at every time slice, a
time-bond factor between consecutive slices --- and prove the \emph{dressing
identity}: that weight equals the path weight of the \emph{symmetrised} kernel
multiplied by a factor supported entirely on the two boundary slices.  Hence
every Gibbs two-point function of the spatial system is a matrix element of an
iterated \emph{self-adjoint} transfer operator between boundary-dressed
observables.  The generic half holds for an arbitrary symmetric kernel on an
arbitrary finite type; no positivity and no structure of the configuration
space enter it.  We further show that the operator the bridge lands on is the
one the companion papers analysed, that the fluctuation sector is invariant,
and that under an explicit contraction hypothesis the connected two-point
function decays geometrically in the time separation.

\textbf{Not proved, and this is the point of the last section.}  The
contraction hypothesis is carried as a theorem hypothesis and is \emph{not}
discharged.  The companion papers prove a \emph{strict} gap with no modulus,
and a strict inequality among finitely many eigenvalues does not by itself
produce the operator-norm bound a decay rate requires.  Converting one into the
other needs the spectral maximum over the fluctuation sector, which is not
constructed here.  In particular \textbf{nothing uniform in the spatial extent}
is obtained, and none is suggested.

Reflection positivity is not addressed.  Nothing in this paper is a claim about
$\SU(N)$, the continuum limit, or the Yang--Mills mass gap.
\end{abstract}

\section{What was missing}\label{sec:missing}

The one-dimensional $\Z_2$ chain in this repository already has the bridge this
paper builds.  There the path weight is written from Boltzmann factors, with no
operator occurring in its definition or in any definition it depends on, and a
separate theorem identifies path sums with iterates of the transfer operator.
That is what makes the chain's spectral statements statements about a measure.

The spatial development took a different route.  Its object is a kernel on
$(\Z_2)^L$ --- the configurations of a single time slice --- and it proceeded
directly to the vacuum and the spectrum.  The results are real, but each of
them is a statement about a matrix.  The following is the honest inventory of
what the four papers established and what they did not:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Object & Established & By \\
\midrule
Kernel on $(\Z_2)^L$ & yes & \cite{ErikssonSpatial} \\
Vacuum (Perron vector) & yes, every $L$ & \cite{ErikssonPerron} \\
Strict spectral separation & yes, every $L$ & \cite{ErikssonGap} \\
Gibbs measure & \textbf{no} & --- \\
Partition function & \textbf{no} & --- \\
Correlations as matrix elements & \textbf{no} & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The last three rows are what this paper supplies.  They are not a refinement of
the earlier work; they are the reason the earlier work is about anything.

\section{The generic bridge}\label{sec:generic}

Nothing in this section uses positivity, the two-element site, or the product
structure of the configuration space.  Let $\iota$ be a finite type and
$K:\iota\times\iota\to\R$ any kernel.

\begin{definition}
For $X:\{0,\dots,N\}\to\iota$ the \emph{path weight} is
$W_K(X)=\prod_{t=0}^{N-1}K(X_t,X_{t+1})$, and for observables $A,B:\iota\to\R$
the \emph{path sum} is
\[
  S_K(N;A,B)\;=\;\sum_{X}A(X_0)\,B(X_N)\,W_K(X).
\]
\end{definition}

\osline{SpatialGibbs}{73}{pathWeight},
\osline{SpatialGibbs}{100}{pathSum}.
No operator occurs in either definition.

\begin{theorem}[the bridge, generic form]\label{thm:generic}
If $K$ is symmetric then
$S_K(N;A,B)=\sum_i \bigl(T_K^{\,N}A\bigr)(i)\,B(i)$, where
$(T_KA)(i)=\sum_j K(i,j)A(j)$.
\end{theorem}

\osline{SpatialGibbs}{130}{pathSum\_eq\_iterate}.

The proof is an induction that absorbs one bond into the left observable at
each step; symmetry of $K$ is exactly what allows the bond to be read in either
direction.  The base case is the empty product.

\section{The spatial Gibbs measure}\label{sec:measure}

Now the physics.  A configuration of the two-dimensional system is a map
$X:\{0,\dots,N\}\to(\Z_2)^L$: $N+1$ time slices, each a spatial configuration.

\begin{definition}\label{def:gibbs}
With $w$ a strictly positive spatial weight and $K_\beta$ the decoupled
time-bond kernel of \cite{ErikssonSpatial},
\[
  \mathcal{W}(X)\;=\;\Bigl(\prod_{t=0}^{N}w(X_t)\Bigr)
    \Bigl(\prod_{t=0}^{N-1}K_\beta(X_t,X_{t+1})\Bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\osline{SpatialGibbs}{160}{gibbsWeight}.
The spatial factor appears at \emph{every} slice and the time-bond factor
between consecutive slices; this is the ordinary two-dimensional Boltzmann
weight, written without reference to any operator.

The obstacle is that the natural transfer kernel $w(\sigma)K_\beta(\sigma,\tau)$
is \emph{not} symmetric, so it is not self-adjoint and no spectral theory
applies to it directly.  The companion paper introduced the symmetrised form
$\widetilde K(\sigma,\tau)=\sqrt{w(\sigma)}\,K_\beta(\sigma,\tau)\sqrt{w(\tau)}$
and proved it symmetric.  The following identity is what that symmetrisation
costs.

\begin{theorem}[the dressing identity]\label{thm:dress}
For every space-time configuration $X$,
\[
  \mathcal{W}(X)\;=\;\sqrt{w(X_0)}\;\sqrt{w(X_N)}\;\;W_{\widetilde K}(X).
\]
\end{theorem}

\osline{SpatialGibbs}{205}{gibbsWeight\_eq\_dressed}.

\begin{remark}
The entire discrepancy between the honest Gibbs weight and the path weight of
the self-adjoint kernel is two factors, each supported on one boundary slice.
Nothing is changed in the bulk.  The proof is the observation that the
truncated products $\prod_t\sqrt{w(X_t)}$ over the first $N$ and over the last
$N$ slices are the full product with one endpoint removed, so their product
with the two boundary factors is the full product squared.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[correlations are matrix elements]\label{cor:bridge}
For all observables $A,B$,
\[
  \sum_X A(X_0)B(X_N)\,\mathcal{W}(X)
  \;=\;\sum_\sigma\bigl(T_{\widetilde K}^{\,N}(\sqrt{w}A)\bigr)(\sigma)\,
        \bigl(\sqrt{w}B\bigr)(\sigma),
\]
and the partition function is the same expression at $A=B=1$.
\end{corollary}

\osline{SpatialGibbs}{253}{gibbsPathSum\_eq\_iterate},
\osline{SpatialGibbs}{262}{gibbsPartition\_eq\_iterate}.

This is the statement the four companion papers were missing.  Their spectral
results are now results about the correlations of a measure.

\section{The operator is the one that was analysed}\label{sec:vacuum}

The bridge lands on $\widetilde K$, which is strictly positive whenever $w$ is,
so the existence theory of \cite{ErikssonPerron} applies verbatim: it has a
strictly positive Perron vacuum, and after normalising by its eigenvalue the
vacuum is a genuine fixed point of unit Euclidean norm.

\osline{SpatialGibbs}{280}{symVacuum\_exists}.

That this needs saying at all is the point.  A bridge that landed on a
\emph{different} operator would have proved nothing about the earlier papers.

\section{The fluctuation sector, and what decay would need}\label{sec:decay}

\begin{theorem}[invariance]\label{thm:perp}
If $K$ is symmetric and $T_K\Om=\Om$, then $\langle\Om,u\rangle=0$ implies
$\langle\Om,T_Ku\rangle=0$, and hence $\langle\Om,T_K^{\,n}u\rangle=0$ for all
$n$.
\end{theorem}

\osline{SpatialGibbs}{346}{perp\_invariant},
\osline{SpatialGibbs}{352}{iterate\_perp}.

\begin{theorem}[geometric decay under an explicit hypothesis]\label{thm:decay}
Suppose in addition that for some $r\ge0$,
$\|T_Ku\|\le r\|u\|$ for every $u$ orthogonal to $\Om$.  Then for every such
$u$ and every $n$,
\[
  \bigl|\langle u,\;T_K^{\,n}u\rangle\bigr|\;\le\;r^{\,n}\,\|u\|^{2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\osline{SpatialGibbs}{382}{connected\_decay}.
Specialised through the dressing identity, this bounds the Gibbs two-point
function of the spatial system itself:
\osline{SpatialGibbs}{505}{gibbs\_connected\_decay}.

\begin{remark}[the hypothesis is not vacuous, and not trivial]\label{rem:nonvac}
A hypothesis-carrying theorem is worth nothing if nothing satisfies the
hypothesis.  For any unit vector $\Om$ and any $r\ge0$ the kernel
$\Om_i\Om_j+r(\delta_{ij}-\Om_i\Om_j)$ is symmetric, fixes $\Om$, and contracts
the fluctuation sector by exactly $r$ --- so the hypotheses are jointly
satisfiable at every rate, including $0<r<1$ where the conclusion is
non-trivial.  Separately, whenever there are two distinct states and the vacuum
is strictly positive, the fluctuation sector contains a nonzero vector, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:decay} is not quantified over an empty set.
\end{remark}

\osline{SpatialGibbs}{457}{gapWitness\_contracts},
\osline{SpatialGibbs}{469}{exists\_nonzero\_perp}.

\section{Scope: what this paper does not give}\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{The contraction hypothesis is not discharged.}  This is the
substantive limitation and it is not a formality.  The companion
paper \cite{ErikssonGap} proves that every eigenvalue other than the Perron
eigenvalue is \emph{strictly} smaller in modulus.  That is a statement about
finitely many strict inequalities.  The hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:decay}
is an \emph{operator-norm} bound on the fluctuation sector.  In finite
dimension the first does imply the second --- take the maximum of
$|\mu|/\lambda$ over the finitely many non-Perron eigenvalues --- but that
maximum has to be constructed, together with the enumeration of the spectrum
and the spectral decomposition of the symmetric kernel that makes the norm
equal to it.  None of that exists in this development, so no rate is derived
here from the earlier papers.

\paragraph{Nothing uniform in the spatial extent.}  Even granting the previous
paragraph, the constructed $r$ would depend on $L$.  The measured evidence
in \cite{ErikssonGap} is that it approaches $1$ with $L$ outside the disordered
region.  This paper adds nothing to that question in either direction, and the
reader should not read the geometric decay of Theorem~\ref{thm:decay} as a
mass gap of any kind: with $r=r(L)\to1$ the bound is empty in the limit.

\paragraph{Reflection positivity is untouched.}  The bridge gives a
self-adjoint transfer operator and a positive measure; it does not give the
Osterwalder--Schrader positivity that a reconstruction theorem would need, and
no claim in that direction is made.

\paragraph{No claim about the physical target.}  Nothing here concerns
$\SU(N)$, the continuum limit, or the Clay problem \cite{Clay}.  The distance
to that problem is unchanged by this paper, and remains effectively total.

\section{What is verified and not proved}\label{sec:verified}

A machine-checked proof guarantees that the definitions are internally
consistent.  It does not guarantee that they model the object the prose claims
they model.  For that reason both load-bearing identities --- the dressing
identity and the bridge corollary --- were recomputed by brute force from the
Boltzmann weights, by an independent script that has no access to the
formalisation, over all space-time configurations for $L\le3$ and $N\le3$ with
a genuinely configuration-dependent strictly positive spatial weight.  The
worst relative discrepancy over all cases was $9.1\times10^{-16}$, at machine
precision.

This is \textbf{verified}, not proved, and no theorem depends on it.  Its
function is to exclude the failure mode a proof assistant cannot exclude: a
formally correct theorem about the wrong definition.  The script and its
transcript are in the repository.

\section{Formalisation notes}\label{sec:formal}

\paragraph{The generic half carries the work.}  Theorem~\ref{thm:generic} is
stated for an arbitrary symmetric kernel on an arbitrary finite type, and the
spatial content enters only when it is applied.  This was not generality for
its own sake: the induction that proves it is exactly the induction the
one-dimensional chain already used, and stating it generically is what let the
proof be reused rather than rewritten.

\paragraph{Where symmetry is actually needed.}  Only in
Theorem~\ref{thm:generic}, to absorb a bond into the left observable.  The
dressing identity itself is an identity between products and uses no symmetry
at all; it is what \emph{supplies} the symmetric kernel that
Theorem~\ref{thm:generic} then consumes.

\paragraph{A rewrite that had to be done in one step.}  The dressing identity
combines three product identities.  Rewriting them in sequence fails: the
second rewrite consumes the subterm the third one needs.  They are combined
into a single equation before rewriting.  The failure is silent in the sense
that matters --- it produces a stuck goal, not a wrong theorem --- but it is
recorded because the same shape recurs whenever two truncations of one product
appear on the same side.

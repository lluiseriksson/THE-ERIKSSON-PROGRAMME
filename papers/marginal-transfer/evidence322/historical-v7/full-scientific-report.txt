MATHEMATICAL ASSESSMENT, IN DEPENDENCY ORDER

1. Operator conventions and scope (pp. 2, 7; equations (1)–(3)).
The scalar normalization is consistent. With q=exp(-2 beta), M_beta is the tensor power of the positive-definite two-state matrix with diagonal one and off-diagonal q. Dividing by (1+q)^L gives Boolean noise with Walsh eigenvalues rho^{|S|}, rho=tanh beta. A_mu and T_r differ by the positive scalar 2^{-L}(1+q)^L when mu=2^{-L}r. The normalized spectral ratio is unchanged. The Perron stationary law is the square of the Euclidean Perron vector, not generally mu. For zero densities, the active principal block is strictly entrywise positive, while the complement contributes zero eigenvalues. These distinctions are used consistently.

2. Code existence, positive concentration and stationary marginals (Theorem 1, Lemma 2, Proposition 3, Lemma 4, Corollary 6; pp. 2–6).
The random-generator union bound simultaneously pays primal distance, dual distance, and full row rank. The inclusive binomial entropy estimate controls the strict primal failure event and the inclusive dual event without an integrality assumption. Absence of a small dual word gives full column rank for each coordinate set of the permitted size, hence exactly uniform projections.

For the positive code mixture, the C principal block has all m eigenvalues in a[1-s,1+s]. Interlacing controls every index through m, not just a single test vector. The outside block is positive semidefinite with trace at most eta; its norm is at most eta. The cross block's Frobenius norm is at most sqrt(eta), so the bound on lambda_1 is justified independently of the lower eigenvalue bound. Dividing by lambda_1>=a yields the stated finite estimate even when it is vacuous. Substitution of m and eta gives the coefficient 7 and positive gamma. I checked the ceiling factor in m<=2 exp(R log(2)L) and the restriction m<2^L.

The stationary claim is genuinely additional: C-translations commute with the transfer matrix, preserve its normalized positive Perron vector by uniqueness, and act transitively on every allowed coordinate projection. Thus the Perron stationary marginals are uniform. The Doob similarity gives the actual normalized spectrum. The information-theoretic corollary is a correct factorization argument: the same literal input cannot distinguish the slow construction from the product law. It is not a theorem about full conditionals.

The supplied source294 proof and both supplied scientific predecessors support the paper's distinction: antipodal concentration does not preserve pair marginals; the parity predecessor concerns heat-bath dynamics. The combined linear-order marginal / many-transfer-mode / stationary-marginal conclusion is not already stated by those two predecessor results.

3. Parity control (Proposition 5; pp. 5–6).
Multiplication by full parity exchanges a Walsh character and its complement. The displayed two-by-two block has trace rho^s+rho^{L-s} and determinant (1-t^2)rho^L. The largest trace is the empty/full pair and the next is a singleton/complement pair. The lower root of the leading block is no larger than rho^L and cannot replace the asserted second eigenvalue. The upper-root/trace monotonicity gives the ratio estimate in the correct direction. Maximizing rho(1+b)/(1+rho^2 b) at b=1 proves the uniform tanh(2 beta) bound, approached at L=2, |t| tending to one. This avoids a false identification of heat-bath and transfer gaps.

4. Upper density and supercritical moment control (Theorem 7, Corollary 8, Theorem 9; pp. 7–9).
The lower bound lambda_1>=1 follows by testing sqrt(r), since <r,P_rho r>>=(Er)^2. Sign parts of a second eigenvector have Rayleigh quotients at least lambda_2. Their pointwise sub-eigenfunction inequalities do not divide by r. Interpolation at an exponent below one is implemented by ordinary Hölder on positive powers, not an invalid triangle inequality. The reverse bilinear noise inequality gives a positive cross term.

Crucially, the compression to normalized sign parts has eigenvalues lambda_2 and lambda_2+D/(cd). Its top eigenvalue is bounded above by lambda_1 even though the two-dimensional space need not be invariant. Since cd<=1/2, small sign mass does not spoil the gap bound. I checked the exponent e_rho=(1+rho^2)/(1-rho)^2. In Theorem 9, Q=s(1+rho)/(s rho+1+rho)>1+rho is exactly the strict supercritical condition. The exponent obtained from interpolation is 2 rho s/[(1-rho)(s(1-rho)-1-rho)], as stated. The case lambda_2<lambda_1/2 and one-point support are separately covered.

5. Subcritical product-tilt collapse (Theorem 10; pp. 9–11).
The density has mean one, the prescribed L^s budget and the claimed positive floor by product integration and Minkowski. The transfer is correctly replaced by the isospectral Q M_r Q, which is linear in r. The one-bit top eigenvalue has positive quadratic logarithmic variation precisely below the critical exponent. Distinct top eigendirections of H_+ and H_- make the overlap omega strictly less than kappa. The argument for strictness uses norm attainment and invertibility of H_+-H_- and is valid.

The rectangular Gram comparison controls both of the largest actual eigenvalues of X_L+Y_L. Added Gram zeros do not invalidate those two positive eigenvalue bounds. Adding the positive noise term can raise the top eigenvalue by at most one and cannot lower the second. This establishes exponential collapse, not just growth of an operator norm. There is no claimed marginal matching for this family.

6. Auxiliary critical estimates and balanced critical tilts (Theorems 11–12; pp. 11–13).
The endpoint multiplier bound ||Q M_h Q||<=||h||_s is valid for signed h by Hölder and the dual hypercontractive endpoint. The convexity estimates for ||r-1||_s work in both s>=2 and 1<s<2, including r=0. These yield the advertised small-budget sufficient bound.

For the critical tilt, the quadratic term of log(kappa) vanishes, while log(tau) has coefficient -rho/[2(1-rho^2)]. Taking delta_L=d L^{-1/3} makes L delta_L^2 diverge and L delta_L^4 tend to zero. Exact balancing of the two diagonal top eigenvalues and the decaying cross norm give actual top and second eigenvalue control. The uniform component is one of the two channels, not a negligible perturbation. This matters at criticality. The B>2 estimate is an auxiliary all-dimension rate bound, not the final moment threshold.

7. Exact critical total-moment endpoint (Theorem 13; pp. 13–15).
For a unit vector h supported on C, endpoint hypercontractivity followed by Hölder gives <h,T_r h><=(E[r^s 1_C])^{1/s}. Applying this separately to the two sign regions of a second eigenvector yields 2 lambda_2^s<=Er^s. This argument requires orthogonality to the Perron vector, not a false uniform-mean-zero assertion. Together with lambda_1>=1 it gives the uniform gap below B_*=2^{1/s}.

The matching construction has every interior tilt moment tending to zero while the s-moment is exactly one. The mixed-power inequality controls the noninteger-power error and proves that the normalized total moment tends to two. Dilution toward density one enforces the exact norm cap in every dimension where it is needed. Its operator effect tends to zero by the signed multiplier bound, so it preserves both leading eigenvalue limits. Finite initial replacement enforces any fixed floor b<1. I found no circular use of the endpoint that is being established: the perturbative upper bound comes from classical hypercontractivity, not the spectral-gap assertion.

8. Escaped moment and fixed-cutoff endpoint (Theorem 14, Propositions 15–16, Theorem 17, Corollary 18; pp. 16–20).
In Theorem 14 the low/high split leaves a positive low p-norm because the high energy is controlled by the strict tail budget tau<1. Interpolation with exponent 1/s^2, then reverse hypercontractivity, gives the stated positive cross entry. The total-moment bound controls lambda_1. The argument only needs one common cutoff for the family.

The one-vertex construction of Proposition 15 is an important countercheck: escaped critical moment need not imply collapse. In probability-normalized L^2 the rank-one vector has the correct sqrt(2^L) normalization. Its operator effect decays because (1+rho)<2^{2 rho/(1+rho)}, which follows from strict convexity as stated. Thus arbitrary finite positive escaped moment can coexist with a stable gap.

Proposition 16 properly handles the supremum over dimensions by separating a finite prefix before taking the cutoff limit. It does not infer the escaped moment from fixed-cutoff convergence alone.

Theorem 17's exact cutoff-one event requires P(R_L>ER_L)->0, not merely R_L->0. The proof obtains the stronger statement from a fractional moment of R_L/ER_L. The coefficient 1-P(R_L>ER_L)^{1/s} reserves enough tail norm, and subsequent dilution preserves the strict event while reducing its integrand. This pays the cap in every dimension, preserves the spectral limit, and gives H(J)=1 for every J>1. Corollary 18 is a valid, intentionally nonoptimal consequence for K<1.

9. Scalar optimizer and finite parity backgrounds (Proposition 19, Theorem 20; pp. 20–23).
The affine minorant is tight at K and sK/(s-1). For K>(s-1)/s its probabilities lie strictly between zero and one. At the strict threshold, mass at K costs zero; the non-strict version has the same infimum but does not attain it in this regime. The scalar optimization alone does not establish a spectral threshold.

The Fourier multiplier identity ||Q M_chi_U Q||=rho^{|U|/2} is exact. Replacing finitely many background bits by long independent parities preserves the scalar background law and suppresses its nonconstant operator contribution. Tensor comparison controls both leading eigenvalues. The high and low sectors' tail moments are calculated using interior moments, not an unjustified uniform-integrability argument. Theorem 20 is correct after reading its erroneous reference “(32)” as its immediately preceding mixed-power equation (21); see Objection 1.

10. Attainment at the exact constructive budget (Theorem 21; pp. 23–26).
This is one of the strongest technical parts. The dyadic approximation approaches the scalar optimizer from below in probability, producing a nonnegative excess d_L that is explicitly paid. With k about L^{1/3} and M about L/(2k), M/k diverges; hence (2^k-1)rho^{M/2}->0 despite the growing number of Walsh coefficients. The construction never assigns a non-dyadic probability to a finite uniform cube.

Clipping the tilt creates exact mass at K and has operator error at most c_n. Mean compensation is subtracted only from the high background sector. Because beta_L E U_n<1-K eventually, that sector remains above K while the low sector stays at least K. Thus the mean is exactly one without a global normalization that would move the cutoff. The amplitude reserve beta_L^s=1-d_L-E_L pays both the dyadic excess and every upper error in the strict-tail moment. The coefficient of the pure tilt moment after averaging sectors is one. These details give the actual finite-budget inequality, rather than only its limiting value.

The four operator errors in the comparison to P_background tensor F_n all vanish. Both leading comparison eigenvalues tend to one; Weyl and the full-spectrum AA*/A*A identity transfer this to T_r. The floor and tail bound in every integer dimension follow by a finite uniform prefix. I checked the strict versus non-strict cutoff distinction: changing >K to >=K would indeed change the assertion.

11. Exact scalar total-moment envelope (Theorem 22; pp. 26–27).
Conditional Jensen gives tail>=h(p) with p=P(r>K), and the lower-sector mean bound yields m>=1-K+Kp. The smaller branch p_tau of h(p)=tau is therefore a lower bound for p, even though h is not globally decreasing. Combining tail<=tau with the bounded lower-sector contribution yields the exact envelope U(tau). The two-point law saturates both inequalities. At the minimum budget g all equalities force the unique law, with the stated dyadic realizability condition. For tau>g, approximating p_tau from above preserves tail feasibility and approaches the supremum. The scalar gap consequence is only sufficient, as the text acknowledges; it does not prove Theorem 23. The displayed s=2 formulas and the K=3/4 example are algebraically consistent.

12. Universal strict-tail necessity and the matching endpoint (Theorem 23; pp. 27–29).
I specifically attempted to break this proof using zero weights, unbalanced eigenvector signs, repeated second eigenvalues, divergent high values, and failure of moment-limit interchange. The argument survives these cases.

With Er^s<=B=tau+K^s, one has 1<=lambda_1<=B^{1/s}. If gamma=1-lambda_2/lambda_1, normalized sign compression gives d<=B^{1/s} gamma/2. Reverse hypercontractivity therefore forces at least one normalized sign part F=sqrt(r)h to have p-quasi-norm at most (B^{1/s} gamma/2)^{1/2}, while its energy is at least 1-gamma. This uses normalized sign parts; using the unnormalized parts here would be insufficient.

For fixed M, the low part has L^2 norm at most sqrt(M). Interpolation with theta=1/s^2 makes its energy vanish with gamma. Endpoint Hölder bounds high-part energy by H_M^{1/s}. The triangle inequality is used in the Hilbert space after applying Q, not in L^p with p<1. Consequently equation (32) forces liminf H_M>=1 for each fixed M along any collapsing family.

Applying the scalar affine minorant to r 1_{r<=M} on the original probability space costs at least g-AB/M^{s-1} in the disjoint middle tail. This is the decisive additional step: it accounts for the mean left in the background while the high tail has vanishing first moment. Equations (32) and (33), with limits in the specified order, imply tau>=1+g without assuming a floor or a background/tilt decomposition.

The explicit gamma_0 also checks out. Its second term makes the low-core square-root error at most D/4; its first gives sqrt(1-gamma)>=1-D/4. This forces H_M>(1-D)^{2s}=1-3 epsilon/4, contradicting (33). There is no equality loophole at tau=g or at gamma=gamma_0. Theorem 21 has exactly the same rho, s, K and strict-tail convention, so it supplies the matching endpoint. This genuinely closes the universal threshold in the stated interval (s-1)/s<K<1.

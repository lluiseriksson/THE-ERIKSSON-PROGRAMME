2. J1: feasibility, conditional Jensen and equality law -- PASS
Throughout, s>1, a=1-K>0 and k_s<K<1 imply 0<p_*=(s-1)a/K<1. The strict event H={r>K} must have positive probability: otherwise r<=K almost surely contradicts mean one. This avoids dividing by zero in conditional Jensen. Write p=P(H) and m=E[r 1_H]. On H^c, 0<=r<=K, so
m=1-E[r 1_Hc]>=1-K(1-p)=a+Kp.
Finite tail cap gives finite s moment on H; on H^c the s moment is bounded by K^s. Thus no unproved integrability is used. Conditional Jensen for the strictly convex function x^s yields
E[r^s 1_H]>=p(m/p)^s=m^s/p^(s-1)>=h(p).
This also works if p=1; there is no conditional expectation on the possibly empty low event.

The logarithmic derivative of h is
[Kp-(s-1)a]/[p(a+Kp)],
with positive denominator. Consequently p_* is its unique minimum on(0,1], h(p_*)=s^s a K^(s-1)/(s-1)^(s-1)=g, and 0<g<1 because p_*<1 and h increases strictly from p_* to h(1)=1. The tail cap is therefore infeasible for tau<g, on any probability space.

At tau=g, tail>=h(p)>=g must be equality everywhere. The unique minimizer forces p=p_*. Equality in the mean inequality gives E[(K-r)1_Hc]=0 and hence r=K almost surely on H^c. Equality in strict Jensen gives a single high value m/p_*=K+a/p_*=sK/(s-1)>K. Both event probabilities are strictly between zero and one. Thus the law, up to null sets and rearrangement of its atoms in the probability space, is unique. This is uniqueness of the distribution, not of its placement on a fixed space.

3. J2: lower root and exact total-moment maximum -- PASS
As p decreases to zero, h(p) is asymptotic to a^s p^(1-s) and diverges. On(0,p_*], it is continuous and strictly decreasing onto[g,infinity). Each finite tau>=g therefore has exactly one lower root p_tau in that interval. There can be a second root on the upper branch for g<tau<1; the statement correctly does not claim global uniqueness on(0,1]. When tau=1 the upper root can be p=1. Neither affects the needed implication:
if p<p_tau, then p is on the lower decreasing branch and h(p)>tau;
therefore every feasible law has p>=p_tau.

The low s-moment is at most K^s(1-p), without a density floor. Combining it with the actual tail cap gives
E r^s<=tau+K^s(1-p)<=tau+K^s(1-p_tau)=U(tau).
No assumption r>=K is used for this universal upper bound.

The two-point law at K and v_tau=K+a/p_tau has mean
K(1-p_tau)+p_tau v_tau=K+a=1.
Its high value exceeds K, so its strict tail is exactly
p_tau v_tau^s=(a+Kp_tau)^s/p_tau^(s-1)=tau.
Its low moment equals K^s(1-p_tau). It thus attains U(tau) on an appropriately chosen two-atom probability space, for every finite tau>=g. This is an actual maximum over all laws, not merely a supremum or limiting construction. It is not a promise that every preassigned probability space can realize the required atomic probability.

For completeness, equality in the total bound requires p=p_tau because K^s>0, saturated tail, and low moment K^s(1-p). Since x^s<K^s for0<=x<K, the last equality forces r=K on the low event. The mean then fixes m=a+Kp_tau. Tail equality equals h(p_tau), so strict Jensen forces the constant high value. Thus the same law is the unique maximizing distribution for every finite tau>=g, not only at the minimum budget. The source's fixed-cube attainment qualification is justified.

4. J3: Boolean dyadics, minimum-budget obstruction and approximation -- PASS
Every event on a uniform L-cube has probability j/2^L. At tau=g the law is uniquely the one with high probability p_*. Thus non-dyadic p_* makes the admissible class empty on every finite uniform cube, despite feasibility on a general two-atom space. If p_* is dyadic, it is realizable exactly in each dimension for which2^L p_* is integral; assigning the two prescribed values suffices. There is no hidden substitute law at the same budget.

For tau>g, the lower root satisfies p_tau<p_*. Define
p_L=(floor(2^L p_tau)+1)/2^L.
It always exceeds p_tau, including when p_tau itself is dyadic. Its excess is at most2^-L and tends to zero. For all sufficiently large L it lies strictly below p_*<1, so the number of high vertices is an integer strictly between zero and2^L. The witnesses have values K and K+a/p_L, both positive, with mean one and exact floor K.

Because this approximation stays on the decreasing branch, h(p_L)<h(p_tau)=tau. This is the crucial budget-preserving direction; rounding down would in general exceed the cap. Their total moments
h(p_L)+K^s(1-p_L)
converge by continuity to U(tau). Hence the supremum over all finite cubes is U(tau), and there is a witness in every sufficiently large dimension. At a particular finite cube exact attainment requires p_tau to be an allowed event probability; if it is allowed the two-point assignment realizes it. No claim of attainment on every cube is made. The source does not assert an all-small-dimensions sequence under a budget that might be infeasible there.

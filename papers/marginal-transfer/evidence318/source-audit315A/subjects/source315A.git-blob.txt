TAIL-ENVELOPE315A -- exact scalar envelope, sufficient transfer-gap consequence
Analytical source; no new standalone paper, numerical experiment or formal proof.
Detailed writing follows judges commit1505d58e240769a996176fa9086cbd8e5a476308.
The parent disclosed the candidate formulas in exploration314 before registration.

1. Statement and convention

Fix s>1 and (s-1)/s<K<1. Write a=1-K,
  p_*=(s-1)a/K, C_s=s^s/(s-1)^(s-1), g=C_s a K^(s-1).
Then 0<p_*<1 and 0<g<1. For 0<p<=1 define
  h(p)=(a+Kp)^s/p^(s-1).
For every finite tau>=g, let p_tau be the unique point of (0,p_*]
such that h(p_tau)=tau. Define U(tau)=tau+K^s(1-p_tau).

THEOREM A. Among all nonnegative random variables r on probability spaces,
with E r=1 and E[r^s 1_{r>K}]<=tau, the class is empty if tau<g.
For tau>=g, its maximum total moment is exactly U(tau), attained by
  P(r=K)=1-p_tau, P(r=v_tau)=p_tau,
  v_tau=K+a/p_tau>K.
The event is strictly r>K. This statement imposes no lower density floor
on the admissible class, although the maximizing two-point law has floor K.

At tau=g the only admissible law is this two-point law with p_tau=p_*.
On a finite uniformly weighted Boolean cube, it exists at tau=g if and only
if p_* is dyadic (and in dimension L precisely when 2^L p_* is integral).
For tau>g, the supremum over all finite uniform Boolean cubes is U(tau).
There are positive witnesses with floor K, in every sufficiently large
dimension, with tail cost<=tau and total moment tending to U(tau).
Exact attainment on a particular cube requires its high probability to be
p_tau; it is not asserted when that number is non-dyadic.

2. Scalar proof

The derivative has sign determined by
  h'(p)/h(p) = sK/(a+Kp) - (s-1)/p
            = [Kp-(s-1)a]/[p(a+Kp)].
Thus h decreases strictly from infinity to h(p_*)=g on (0,p_*],
and increases strictly from g to h(1)=1 on [p_*,1]. This proves the
root existence and uniqueness claimed above and g<1.

For an admissible r, put H={r>K}, p=P(H), m=E[r 1_H]. Since K<1 and
E r=1, p>0. On H^c the mean is at most K(1-p), hence
  m >= 1-K(1-p)=a+Kp.
Conditional Jensen on H gives
  E[r^s 1_H] >= m^s/p^(s-1) >= h(p) >= g.                 (A1)
If the finite tail cap holds, the total moment is finite because r<=K
on H^c. Equation(A1) excludes tau<g. When tau>=g it also forces
p>=p_tau: every p<p_tau lies on the decreasing branch and has h(p)>tau.
Consequently
  E r^s = E[r^s 1_H]+E[r^s 1_{H^c}]
        <= tau+K^s(1-p) <= tau+K^s(1-p_tau)=U(tau).       (A2)
The stated two-point law has mean K(1-p_tau)+p_tau v_tau=1.
Its high value exceeds K, its strict tail equals
p_tau v_tau^s=h(p_tau)=tau, and its total moment equals U(tau).
This proves the exact maximum on general probability spaces.

At tau=g all inequalities in(A1) are equalities. Strict monotonicity
on either side of p_* gives p=p_*. The lower event has mean exactly
K(1-p_*), hence r=K almost surely there, since 0<=r<=K. Strict convexity
in conditional Jensen forces r to be the constant v_* on H. This is the
unique law; neither event has probability zero. A uniform cube can realize
that law exactly if and only if its high-event probability p_* is an
integer multiple of 2^-L. This proves the equality-budget obstruction.

For tau>g, p_tau<p_*. Choose, for all sufficiently large L,
  p_L = (floor(2^L p_tau)+1)/2^L.
Then p_tau<p_L<p_* and p_L tends to p_tau. Give exactly 2^L p_L cube
vertices the value K+a/p_L and give the others the value K. The mean is
one; the strict tail is h(p_L)<h(p_tau)=tau. The total moment is
h(p_L)+K^s(1-p_L), which tends to U(tau) by continuity. These witnesses
are positive, have floor K, and use no dynamics or locality assumption.
For a fixed cube, equality in(A2) requires p=p_tau and r=K on the lower
event; the mean and tail equalities then force equality in conditional
Jensen, giving exactly the same two-point law. This establishes the
attainment qualification. There is no claim of a dimension-free error
rate in this source.

3. Sufficient spectral consequence, not an exact spectral threshold

Fix 0<rho<1 and s=(1+rho)/(1-rho). On the uniform L-dimensional Boolean
cube, let P_rho be the noise operator and
  T_r = M_sqrt(r) P_rho M_sqrt(r).
For r>=0 with E r=1 let lambda1>=lambda2>=0 be its two largest eigenvalues,
counting the zero block if necessary, and gap(T_r)=1-lambda2/lambda1.
For L>=1, the previously proved two-sign hypercontractive compression is
  2 lambda2^s <= E r^s,        lambda1>=1.                (S1)
Exact source reference: manuscript314A.tex, Theorem13, source commit
b72cfbcb74275ff7f80b6c262cb003c5cc6ea8eb, proof displayed(16)-(17).

For clarity about this dependency: a real second eigenvector is orthogonal
to the positive Perron vector on the active support, so it has both signs
unless lambda2=0, in which case the bound is immediate. Entrywise positivity
and its eigenequation bound the Rayleigh quotient of each sign part below
by lambda2. Endpoint forward hypercontractivity bounds that quotient above
by (E[r^s 1_C])^(1/s), where C is the sign support. Summing the two disjoint
supports gives the first inequality in(S1). The unit vector sqrt(r) has
Rayleigh quotient <r,P_rho r> >= (E r)^2=1 by nonnegative Walsh multipliers,
which gives the second inequality. No mean-zero assertion for the second
eigenvector in uniform measure and no floor assumption is used.

For every tau>=g with U(tau)<2, Theorem A and(S1) give the dimension-free
  gap(T_r) >= 1 - [U(tau)/2]^(1/s) > 0.                 (S2)
This includes densities with zeros. If tau<g, the scalar class is empty;
that is an explicit feasibility conclusion rather than a positive-gap
theorem about an existing family.

There is a unique q in (0,p_*) with
  h(q)+K^s(1-q)=2.
Indeed the left side decreases strictly there, diverges at zero, and at
p_* equals g+K^s(1-p_*)<2, because g<1 and K^s(1-p_*)<1.
Put tau_c=h(q)=2-K^s(1-q). Then
  2-K^(s-1) < 2-K^s < tau_c < 2,      tau_c>g.
The strict first inequality uses 0<K<1 and s>1; the second uses q>0;
the third uses q<1. As tau grows from g to infinity, p_tau decreases
continuously and U(tau) increases strictly from a number below2 to infinity.
Thus U(tau)<2 exactly for g<=tau<tau_c. This is a strictly larger sufficient
tail-budget interval than the earlier crude condition tau<2-K^(s-1).

At tau=tau_c, the bound(S2) is zero. The two-point law attains scalar total
moment2, but that does NOT imply that its transfer gap collapses or that
the inequalities in(S1) are simultaneously saturated. Theorem A is exact
as scalar optimization; tau_c is only a sufficient spectral boundary.
The universal spectral threshold remains open. In particular this source
does not close the gap to the constructive collapse budget1+g(K) attained
by Theorem21 of manuscript314. No grade or assessment of that PDF transfers.

4. Closed scalar crossing for s=2

Here K>1/2 and a=1-K. Expansion gives
  h(p)=a^2/p+2aK+K^2p,
  h(p)+K^2(1-p)=a^2/p+2K-K^2.
The unique crossing at2 is therefore
  q=(1-K)^2/[2-2K+K^2],
  tau_c=2-K^2/[2-2K+K^2].
For example K=3/4 gives tau_c=25/17, improving the old sufficient budget
5/4. The separate constructive collapse budget is7/4. The interval between
25/17 and7/4 remains unclassified by these sufficient/construction results.
These fractions follow exact algebra; no numerical oracle was run.

5. Scope, prior mechanism and falsifier

Conditional Jensen, a two-point extremal law and scalar moment optimization
are classical. Relevant primary methodological reference: D.Bertsimas and
I.Popescu, Optimal Inequalities in Probability Theory: A Convex Optimization
Approach, SIAM Journal on Optimization15(3)(2005),780-804,
https://doi.org/10.1137/S1052623401399903 . This citation credits the general
moment-optimization framework; it is not evidence that this exact formula is
new or absent there. No external priority claim is made. The inherited
Boolean hypercontractive input and the exact two-sign proof are identified
in source314; this note requires neither formal verification nor numerical
experiments to present its analytical argument, and claims neither.

Refutation targets: an admissible law violating(A1) or(A2), an overlooked
root branch, failure of the dyadic cap at tau>g, a non-dyadic equality-budget
cube law, or an invalid transfer use of(S1). The exact scalar witness does
not certify a sharp spectral result. No fixed-range Gibbs, heat-bath,
physical mass-gap or continuum conclusion follows. This note belongs with
the existing grouped paper if a future revision warrants it; it is not a
standalone paper or a reason to delay the already frozen314/v6 release.

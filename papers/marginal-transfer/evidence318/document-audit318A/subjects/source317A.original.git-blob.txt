2. Classical inputs with exact exponents
For q=1+rho, forward Boolean hypercontractivity gives
 <F,PF>=||P_sqrt(rho)F||_2^2 <= ||F||_q^2.                 (1)
This is Theorem3.1 of Mossel,O'Donnell,Regev,Steif,Sudakov, Non-interactive correlation distillation, inhomogeneous Markov chains, and the reverse Bonami-Beckner inequality, Israel Journal of Mathematics154(2006),299-336; author PDF https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~odonnell/papers/nicd.pdf, printedp5. Substitute input exponent1+rho, output2 and noiseparameter sqrt(rho). The tool and constants are classical, not our result.
For p=1-rho in(0,1), the two-function reverse inequality is
 <F,PG> >= ||F||_p ||G||_p  for nonnegative F,G.             (2)
It is exactly Corollary3.3 of the same primary paper, printedp5, with both exponents1-rho: rho=sqrt((1-p)(1-p)). That corollary permits zeros. Alternatively positive regularization and finite-sum continuity gives the same extension. SectionA proves its two-function form. The quantities ||.||_p are only quasi-norms; we use no triangle inequality for them.
For any z and h with ||h||_2<=1 and nonnegative z, Holder at exponents 2/q and 2/(2-q) gives

because q/(2-q)=s. With general h the right side is multiplied by ||h||_2^2. Hence ||T||<= (E r^s)^(1/s). The Rayleigh quotient of sqrt(r), whose squared norm is1, is E[rPr]>= (Er)^2=1 by the nonnegative Walsh spectrum. Thus

3. Normalized sign compression: avoid a small-sign loophole
The active set {r>0} is nonempty. The restriction of T to it has strictly positive matrix entries since 0<rho<1. Its Perron eigenvalue is simple and its Perron vector strictly positive on the active set. Outside it T is zero. If lambda2=0 then gamma=1 and the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise a real normalized second eigenvector v vanishes outside the active set and is orthogonal to its positive Perron vector. It has both signs. Write v=f_+-f_- with nonnegative disjoint parts, a=||f_+||_2>0, b=||f_-||_2>0 and a^2+b^2=1. Put c=<f_+,Tf_->>0.
The eigenvector equation gives
 <f_+,Tf_+>=lambda2 a^2+c,
 <f_-,Tf_->=lambda2 b^2+c.
In the orthonormal pair u=f_+/a,w=f_-/b, the compression of T is
 lambda2 I + c [[1/a^2,1/(ab)],[1/(ab),1/b^2]].
The second matrix is rankone positive with nonzero eigenvalue1/(a^2 b^2). Its largest eigenvalue is bounded by lambda1 by Rayleigh maximization. Therefore
 c/(a^2 b^2)<=lambda1-lambda2,
 d:=<u,Tw>=c/(ab)<= (lambda1-lambda2)ab <=(lambda1-lambda2)/2. (5)
Both <u,Tu> and <w,Tw> are >=lambda2. This argument remains valid however small a or b is; replacing d by the unnormalized c would not suffice below.
Apply(2) to F=sqrt(r)u and G=sqrt(r)w. Their p-quasi-norm product is at mostd. Choose the one with smaller p-quasi-norm and call its corresponding normalized nonnegative sign vector h. Then
 ||sqrt(r)h||_p <=sqrt(d)<=sqrt(B^(1/s) gamma/2),
 <sqrt(r)h,P sqrt(r)h>=<h,Th>>=lambda2>=1-gamma.            (6)
The last inequality uses lambda2=lambda1(1-gamma) andlambda1>=1. This choice of sign may depend onr; all bounds below are uniform.

4. The escaping critical-moment inequality
Fix any M>K, independent of dimension. Split F=sqrt(r)h into F0=F1_{r<=M} and F1=F1_{r>M}. Write H_M=E[r^s1_{r>M}]. Set theta=1/s^2. Direct substitution gives
 1/q=theta/p+(1-theta)/2.
For any nonnegative Z, ordinary Holder applied to Z^q=(Z^p)^(theta q/p)(Z^2)^((1-theta)q/2) yields
 ||Z||_q<=||Z||_p^theta ||Z||_2^(1-theta).                 (7)
The two powers theta q/p and (1-theta)q/2 are in(0,1) and sumto1, so this is ordinary Holder with exponents >1 even thoughp<1. No quasi-norm triangle is used.
Monotonicity of finite positive moments gives ||F0||p<=||F||p, and ||F0||2<=sqrt(M)||h||2=sqrt(M). By(1),(6),(7),
 E0:=<F0,PF0> <=M^(1-theta)(B^(1/s)gamma/2)^theta.          (8)
By(1),(3) applied to r1_{r>M},
 E1:=<F1,PF1> <=H_M^(1/s).                                (9)
P is positive semidefinite with square root P_sqrt(rho). The ordinary Hilbert triangle after applying this square root gives
 sqrt(<F,PF>)<=sqrt(E0)+sqrt(E1).
Together with(6)-(9) this proves the quantitative inequality
 H_M >= [sqrt(1-gamma)
          -M^((1-theta)/2)(B^(1/s)gamma/2)^(theta/2)]_+^(2s). (10)
For a collapsing family with the same fixed finiteB and gamma->0, (10) implies liminf H_M>=1 for each fixedM. The uniformity in dimension was proved, not assumed. In particular an escaped unit of critical moment is now justified by the actual eigenvalue ratio and reverse hypercontractivity, rather than appended to a scalar bound without a bridge.

5. The residual background cost without a floor
For every z>=0,
 z^s1_{z>K}>=A(z-K).                                      (11)
For z<=K the right side is nonpositive. For z>K, differentiation of z^s/(z-K) gives a derivative with sign(s-1)z-sK; its global minimum is at sK/(s-1), equalA. This proves(11), including z=K where both sides arezero. Taking expectations forz=r proves tau>=g, so tau<g is empty.
Apply(11) instead to z=r1_{r<=M}, defined on the full original probability space, not to a conditional law. Since r<=r^s/M^(s-1) onr>M,
 E z=1-E[r1_{r>M}]>=1-B/M^(s-1).
The event z>K is exactly K<r<=M. Thus
 E[r^s1_{K<r<=M}]>=g-AB/M^(s-1).
Adding the disjoint high-tail part gives
 tau>=H_M+g-AB/M^(s-1).                                  (12)
Suppose gamma_j->0 under a fixedtau cap. For every fixedM>K, (10),(12) imply tau>=1+g-AB/M^(s-1). Sending M toinfinity then yields tau>=1+g. The order of limits is fixedM first and M afterwards. This proves the asserted positive dimension-uniform infimum by contradiction. No compactness of densities, pointwise floor or interchange of s-moments and probability limits was used.

All quantities are finite and strictly positive and gamma0<1. If gamma<=gamma0 then the core square-root term in(10) is <=D/4. Also sqrt(1-gamma)>=1-gamma>=1-D/4. Consequently
 H_M>=(1-D/2)^(2s)>(1-D)^(2s)=1-3epsilon/4.

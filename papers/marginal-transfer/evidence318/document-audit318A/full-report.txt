ANALYTICAL REVIEW OF THE SCALAR PROOF

The grouping is faithful to original315's mathematical result, while correctly superseding its historical statement that universal necessity was still open. In the current text a=1-K>0 and p_*=(s-1)a/K lies strictly between zero and one because K>k_s. The derivative h'/h=(Kp-(s-1)a)/(p(a+Kp)) establishes the minimum g=C_s a K^(s-1) and identifies the lower root p_tau on (0,p_*]. Restricting to this root matters: for g<tau<1 there can also be a root on the increasing branch. The manuscript does not accidentally choose it.

For an arbitrary admissible law the upper-event probability p cannot vanish, since otherwise the mean is at most K<1. Its upper-event first moment m is at least a+Kp. Conditional Jensen therefore gives tail >=m^s/p^(s-1)>=h(p)>=g. The lower-root property forces p>=p_tau, including when tau>=1. Bounding the lower-event moment by K^s(1-p) then gives the advertised total moment U(tau). This argument allows zeros and assumes no lower floor. The two-atom attaining law has exactly mean one and a lower atom at K, excluded by the strict tail event. Thus strict versus non-strict event is essential, not an interchangeable convention.

At tau=g, equality through all those steps forces p=p_*, lower atom K and constant upper atom. This is uniqueness of the probability law, not uniqueness of labelling cube vertices. A uniform L-cube realizes it exactly if and only if 2^L p_* is integral. At tau>g the explicitly rounded-up dyadic p_L is eventually strictly between p_tau and p_* even when p_tau itself is dyadic; its tail is below tau and its total moment converges to U. This avoids pretending that an irrational event probability occurs on a finite uniform cube.

The inherited 2 lambda2^s <= E r^s and lambda1>=1 give the stated sufficient gap when U<2. On the decreasing branch h(p)+K^s(1-p) is strictly decreasing, diverges at zero and is below two at p_*. Hence q0 and tau_c are well-defined; the stated strict ordering follows from 0<K<1 and 0<q0<1. The s=2 algebra and K=3/4 comparison are consistent by symbolic inspection. None of this proves spectral sharpness by itself; the text expressly avoids that invalid inference. The proof was assessed analytically, not by a numerical run.

ANALYTICAL REVIEW OF UNIVERSAL NECESSITY AND SHARPNESS

The grouped necessity argument corresponds to original317, including its explicit constant and zero-density case. The following are the material attacks considered and their resolution; they state checkable proof obligations, not a private deliberation transcript.

1. Wrong hypercontractive parameter. The cited forward inequality at q=1+rho follows by applying the noise operator with parameter sqrt(rho) from Lq to L2. The cited two-function reverse inequality at p=1-rho has admissibility rho<=sqrt((1-p)(1-p)), exactly equality. The primary statement permits nonnegative functions; no forbidden strictly-positive floor is silently imported.

2. Normalization or missing upper spectral bound. Tail<=tau yields total moment<=B=tau+K^s. Holder plus forward hypercontractivity yields lambda1<=B^(1/s). The vector sqrt(r), with L2 norm one, has Rayleigh quotient <r,P_rho r>>=1 by the nonnegative Walsh spectral decomposition. Thus lambda1>=1 and lambda2=(1-gamma)lambda1>=1-gamma. This is the normalized ratio gap used throughout, not an unnormalized difference.

3. Zeros and second eigenvector signs. The active restriction has strictly positive entries, hence a positive Perron vector. When lambda2>0, its eigenvector vanishes off the active set and has both signs by orthogonality. If lambda2=0 the normalized gap is one, already larger than the displayed gamma0. No second sign is demanded in a rank-one case.

4. Vanishing sign masses. Writing sign norms a,b and c=<f+,T f->, the exact two-dimensional compression has eigenvalues lambda2 and lambda2+c/(a^2 b^2). Its upper eigenvalue cannot exceed lambda1. Therefore the NORMALIZED cross term d=c/(ab) is at most (lambda1-lambda2)ab<=B^(1/s)gamma/2. The estimate remains uniform when either sign mass tends to zero. Using the unnormalized c alone would not establish this; the actual proof does not make that error.

5. Unsupported quasi-norm manipulation. Reverse hypercontractivity bounds the product of the two Lp quasi-norms by d; one is at most sqrt(d). The selected normalized sign has energy at least lambda2. Interpolation uses theta=1/s^2 and 1/q=theta/p+(1-theta)/2. Applied to the positive powers of Z^p and Z^2, ordinary Holder has exponents greater than one. No triangle inequality is used in Lp for p<1.

6. Truncation cross terms. For the selected F=sqrt(r)h, the core F0 at r<=M has L2 norm<=sqrt(M), so its energy is bounded by M^(1-theta)(B^(1/s)gamma/2)^theta. The high part energy is at most H_M^(1/s). The cross terms are handled by the L2 triangle after P_sqrt(rho), not discarded. This gives the displayed lower bound on H_M. For a collapsing family and fixed M its liminf is at least one.

7. Losing background mass or double-counting tail. The pointwise affine inequality z^s 1(z>K)>=A(z-K), A=C_s K^(s-1), is valid below K because the right side is nonpositive, and above K by minimizing z^s/(z-K). It is applied to z=r 1(r<=M) on the ORIGINAL probability space. It is not applied after renormalizing the truncated distribution. The missing mean is at most B/M^(s-1), so the middle tail costs at least g-AB/M^(s-1). This middle event is disjoint from r>M. Adding the high tail is legitimate and yields tau>=H_M+g-AB/M^(s-1).

8. Illicit limit exchange. The proof first takes the collapsing-family liminf for fixed M, then lets M grow. Uniform B is available because rho,K,tau are fixed. No interchange of two uncontrolled limits or assumption of uniform integrability is made. It follows that every collapsing family obeys tau>=1+g.

9. Merely sequential necessity instead of a uniform bound. The explicit epsilon,M,D,gamma0 argument is present. Epsilon=1+g-tau belongs to (0,1] when g<=tau<1+g. The chosen M makes AB/M^(s-1)<=epsilon/4. If gamma<=gamma0, the core term is <=D/4 and sqrt(1-gamma)>=1-D/4. Consequently H_M>1-3epsilon/4, whereas the budget inequality requires H_M<=1-3epsilon/4. D is strictly between zero and one, so the strict inequality is valid. The resulting constant is positive, finite and dimension-free, albeit conservative. It also excludes equality gamma=gamma0.

10. Empty classes and boundary conventions. For tau<g there are no admissible mean-one laws; at tau=g the theorem applies whenever a finite-cube law exists. The theorem is not claiming existence on every dimension at tau=g. The endpoint construction used for tau=1+g is the preserved attained-cutoff theorem with the same s,rho,K, the same strictly greater cutoff, and floor K. That theorem supplies an actual sequence with gap tending to zero at the exact budget, not only an infimum reached from above. Therefore the necessity and construction match. No result for K<=k_s, K>=1, a varying rho, non-strict tails, finite-range interactions or a physical mass gap is inferred.

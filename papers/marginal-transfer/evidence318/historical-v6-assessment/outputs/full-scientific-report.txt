2. Scientific reading and adversarial proof checks

Conventions and Theorem 1 (pp. 2-5), Lemma 2, Proposition 3, Lemma 4.
The kernel q^{d(x,y)} is positive definite for 0<q<1. The scalar relating it
to Boolean noise is consistent with rho=tanh(beta); normalized eigenvalue
ratios are therefore compared for the same operator. The code existence
argument includes rank failure in the primal bad event and separately bounds
short dual words. Its floor/ceiling choices leave a strict exponential margin.
Surjectivity of a coordinate projection follows from the absence of a dual
relation on those columns, and uniform codewords then give exact labelled
product marginals. This does not confuse marginal data with conditionals.

The finite spectral estimate is stronger evidence than an unsupported
hard-support limiting argument. The code principal block has all eigenvalues
in a[1-s,1+s]. The outside positive semidefinite block has norm bounded by
its trace, and the cross block has Frobenius norm at most sqrt(eta). Interlacing
controls the m-th eigenvalue, while the perturbation estimate also controls
the top eigenvalue. Dividing by lambda_1>=a remains legitimate even when the
lower interlacing bound is negative. The choices eta=exp(-2 beta L)/m^2 and
R log 2<=beta/4 give the claimed 7 exp(-gamma L) bound. Indices exist because
k<L. Finite-size strict positivity is not being substituted for a uniform gap.

For the stationary law, translation by every codeword commutes with the
weighted kernel. Uniqueness of the positive normalized Perron vector makes
its square code-translation invariant. Surjective projections make its
small-coordinate marginals uniform too. This proves the extra stationary-law
assertion without assuming that the weighting law equals that stationary law.
The Doob transition and detailed-balance identities are correctly normalized.

Proposition 5 and Corollary 6 (pp. 5-6).
The parity calculation uses an invertible multiplier for |t|<1 and exhausts
the Walsh basis with complement pairs. Comparing the traces identifies the
largest upper root and the second root; the lower root from the empty/full
pair cannot displace the s=1 upper root. The monotonicity of upper-root/trace
gives the correct direction of the ratio inequality. L=2 and t tending to 1
verify the claimed supremum, not finite attainment. The heat-bath predecessor
really has a different generator; its slow gap cannot supply this transfer
result. The certificate corollary follows only for functionals of the stated
literal marginal tuple. It does not deny a positive product-chain gap or a
certificate using conditionals, bounds, membership, or the full matrix.

Theorem 7, Corollary 8, Theorem 9 (pp. 7-9).
The forward and reverse Boolean hypercontractive inequalities were checked
against the primary source [S1] below, including its time convention and the
factor one-half in the logarithm. With p=1-rho and its negative conjugate,
the reverse bilinear inequality used in the manuscript has the appropriate
noise parameter. Approximation by positive functions avoids an unjustified
negative moment at a zero. The interpolation at p<1 applies ordinary Holder
to powers rather than invoking a nonexistent norm triangle inequality.

For a second eigenvector, the disjoint nonnegative sign parts have nonzero
norm and each satisfies the required one-sided eigenrelation. The compression
identity gives lambda_1-lambda_2 >= D/(cd) >=2D; it does not lose control when
one sign part has small norm. The algebraic exponents in the displayed
estimates agree with the interpolation identities. The supercritical condition
is precisely Q>1+rho, and the resulting constants are dimension independent.
The upper bound lambda_1<=B uses a density norm, not a pointwise cap.
One-point supports and zero weights are treated without division by r.
The growth consequence in Corollary 8 is a necessary condition, correctly
not asserted sufficient. The constants are crude; the paper says so.

Theorem 10 (pp. 9-11).
The normalized product weights have L^s norm one before mixing, and the
mixture has the declared mean, norm budget and floor for every dimension.
The second variation of log(kappa) is positive precisely below s_c. The two
one-bit positive matrices cannot have a common top eigenvector, because their
difference is invertible at nonzero tilt; hence omega<kappa. The Gram block
argument controls both leading eigenvalues and handles its additional zero
eigenvalues correctly. Adding the positive product-noise matrix raises the
second eigenvalue and adds at most one to the top bound. This supplies actual
normalized gap collapse, not merely norm growth or two trial vectors. No
marginal matching is supplied by this construction, as the manuscript states.

Theorems 11 and 12 (pp. 11-13).
The signed multiplier estimate ||Q M_h Q||<=||h||_s follows from the dual
endpoint hypercontractive estimate and Holder. The convexity/Taylor estimates
for ||r-1||_s use the appropriate separate cases s>=2 and 1<s<2.
For the balanced tilt, the quadratic variation of log(kappa) cancels at s_c,
whereas log(tau) has negative coefficient -rho/[2(1-rho^2)]. Taking
delta_L=d L^{-1/3} separates L delta_L^2 from L delta_L^4. The coefficient
balances the two diagonal Gram top eigenvalues, and the cross norm tends
to zero. The all-L budget B>2 and the stated stretched-exponential rate are
consistent. This preliminary B>2 statement is not the final critical boundary.

Theorem 13 (pp. 13-15).
For a vector supported on C, endpoint hypercontractivity gives the restricted
Rayleigh bound (E[r^s 1_C])^{1/s}. Applying it separately to the positive and
negative regions of a second eigenvector gives 2 lambda_2^s<=E r^s. The
orthogonality is to the Perron vector, not to uniform constants; both signs
still follow. The lambda_1>=1 test is legitimate. This proves the lower-gap
bound below B_*=2^{1/s}.

On the construction side, the product tilt has every fixed interior positive
moment tending to zero, but its s-moment remains one. The mixed-power bound
justifies the limit E(1+a_L R_L)^s ->2 for noninteger s; convergence in
probability alone would not have justified it. Dilution is taken only when
the norm exceeds B_*, tends to zero, and meets the cap in every dimension.
The signed multiplier norm bound transfers both top eigenvalue limits after
dilution. A finite uniform prefix enforces any prescribed floor b<1.
There is no hidden assertion at b=1, which would force the uniform law.
This is a convincing exact classification of the worst-case dimension-free
critical total-norm budget, though not an optimal formula for the positive gap.

Theorem 14 and Propositions 15-16 (pp. 16-18).
In the tail argument, splitting the sign vectors at a fixed K leaves a
low-density component with a positive p-norm whenever lambda_2 is not already
small. The high part is controlled by tau^{1/(2s)}. The interpolation exponent
theta=s^{-2} and the resulting cross bound d^{2s^2} K^{1-s^2} are consistent.
The strict hypothesis tau<1 is essential, not a cosmetic endpoint exclusion.
A single K is chosen for the entire family when Theta<1.

For the stable spike example, the perturbation norm depends on the diagonal
noise kernel and on 2^{L/s}, not only on the raw spike size. Its base
(1+rho)/2^{2rho/(1+rho)} is strictly less than one by strict convexity.
Thus its moment can escape while the operator perturbation vanishes. The
finite-prefix replacement preserves all limits and ensures the all-L floor,
budget and gap requirements. Proposition 16 explicitly pays for the exchange
between a supremum over dimensions and a cutoff limit by controlling a tail
of dimensions and then eliminating the finite prefix. These arguments show
that Theta is a sharp worst-case sufficient boundary, not a criterion
classifying the gap of every individual family.

Theorem 17 and Corollary 18 (pp. 18-20).
The additional relative-mean probability estimate for R_L/m_L is necessary
and is provided by a t-moment with 0<t<1. The coefficient based on
P(R_L>m_L)^{1/s} pays for the strict tail at one via Minkowski. Exact mean
normalization does not require a global rescaling that would move the cutoff.
The later convex dilution preserves the strict event and decreases its tail
integrand. Norm perturbation preserves both eigenvalue limits, and the total
moment tends to two by an explicit truncation argument. The same family
therefore satisfies every cutoff K>=1 simultaneously. For K<1 the elementary
bound on the low-density s-moment keeps the total below two when the tail
budget is one. These statements have compatible, different cutoff regimes.

Proposition 19 and Theorem 20 (pp. 20-23).
The affine minorant establishes the scalar optimum without assuming finite
higher moments. For K>k_s its equality points and probabilities have mean
one, and for the non-strict event mass at K ceases to be an equality point.
The nonattainment statement in that regime is justified. For K<=k_s the
constant density attains the value one. This is an elementary scalar problem;
its solution is not itself a new spectral method.

The character-multiplier norm rho^{|U|/2} is correct in the Walsh basis. Long
parity blocks therefore make the fixed finite background spectrally negligible
without making its scalar law constant. The tensor comparison controls the
top as well as the second eigenvalue. The low and high sectors of the strict
tail have different constant terms; averaging yields 1+p v^s, as claimed.
Interior moments control the moving threshold. The strict budget and floor
slack allow a finite uniform prefix. The comparison on p. 23 correctly leaves
the universal spectral threshold between the sufficient and constructive
regions unresolved; the scalar optimum cannot be inserted as a universal
spectral lower bound.

Theorem 21 (pp. 23-26).
This is the most delicate new endpoint construction in the supplied PDF.
I checked the following potential failure points against the displayed proof:
non-dyadic optimizer probabilities, the growing number of Walsh coefficients,
strict versus non-strict cutoff events, clipping loss, exact mean compensation,
the density floor, all finite tail errors, top-eigenvalue control, and the
finite initial dimensions.

The choice k=floor(L^{1/3}) and M=floor(L/(2k)) has M/k tending to infinity,
so (2^k-1) rho^{M/2} tends to zero despite the growing background. Dyadic
rounding leaves p bounded away from zero eventually and v uniformly bounded.
The scalar excess d_L is nonnegative and tends to zero. Clipping at
c_n=a_n(theta)^{1/(2theta)} gives the stated probability and operator errors.
The reserve e_L=d_L+E_L tends to zero and contains both mixed powers, including
the case 1<s<2. In the low sector, clipping creates actual mass at K, so the
strict event is exactly U_n>0. In the high sector, subtracting gamma_L
compensates the mean while the displayed inequality keeps the value above K.
The coefficient of beta_L^s E U_n^s after averaging the two sectors is one;
this is the step needed to pay the endpoint budget rather than only its limit.
Equation (26) then follows from the stated reserve, not an unjustified passage
of moments to a limit.

Finally, the comparison with P_b tensor F_n has an operator-norm error tending
to zero and two actual leading eigenvalues tending to one. The AA*/A*A
identity transfers the full spectrum. Prefix replacement proves the all-L
statement. No main logical gap was found. The result attains a constructive
upper budget as an inequality in every dimension; it does not prove that this
is the universal optimal spectral budget. Changing >K to >=K would materially
change the theorem and is not justified.

3. Counterexample and hypothesis stress tests

The following analytical challenges did not produce a counterexample to the
stated results: uniform r and B=1; one-point support and lambda_2=0; repeated
second eigenvalues; very unequal sign-part norms; zero density on some points;
the parity limit |t|->1 at L=2; small dimensions before asymptotic constructions
are valid; noninteger s near one; non-dyadic p_*; b approaching one while
remaining below it; K at k_s in the scalar problem; K=0 in Corollary 18; and
strict-cutoff mass at K. Endpoint parameters rho=0 or rho=1 are excluded and
were not silently included. Constants are not uniform as rho approaches these
endpoints, as s approaches s_c from above, or as K approaches one from below.

Apparent shortcuts that would fail are not used by the paper: transferring a
heat-bath obstruction to the transfer kernel, proving gap collapse only by two
trial vectors, treating small density as sufficient for slowdown, inferring
s-moment convergence from convergence in probability, or identifying scalar
tails with invariant-measure operator tails. The explicit constructions and
qualifications address those hazards.

5. Complete objections and reservations

R1. Historical originality remains unresolved (substantive epistemic limit).
The exact joint theorems were not located in the targeted primary readings,
but the ingredients and nearby gap/budget mechanisms are established. A
specialist comparison could reveal a stronger implication or earlier exact
result. The paper already avoids priority claims; this reservation limits
the novelty rating and any promotion of the work as a major discovery. It
does not invalidate the displayed proofs.

R2. Importance and applicability remain specialized (substantive scope limit).
The construction permits global many-body weights and full-configuration
Doob dynamics. No fixed-range Gibbs theorem, algorithmic gain, local-dynamics
result, continuum field-theory theorem or solution of a recognized Millennium
problem follows. No external application demonstrating broad consequences
is supplied. A precise mathematical counterexample/classification can still
be useful; its broader consequence should not be inferred from the words
Ising, gap, or the author's wider programme.

R3. The universal fixed-cutoff spectral threshold below one is open (explicit
incompleteness outside the claims). The sufficient region tau<2-K^{s-1} and
the constructed collapse budget 1+g(K) do not classify the intervening
region. Scalar optimality plus one escaped unit is not a proved universal
necessity. Theorem 21 removes slack for its construction, not this open
interval. This limits completeness of any broader advertised phase diagram,
not correctness of the theorem as written.

R4. Endpoint constructions are principally asymptotic (quantitative limit).
The exact-budget/cutoff statements do not provide effective L0 values or
optimal rates. The finite-prefix existence argument is valid but offers
limited practical dimension estimates. The explicit rate in Theorem 12
belongs to B>2 and must not be exported to the exact boundary. No efficient
code construction is supplied by Lemma 2. These are disclosed limitations,
not missing hypotheses.

R5. Independent verification remains limited (assessment/provenance limit).
Exact file hashes and readable proofs make the work inspectable, but this
review did not reproduce the build, execute computations, inspect external
development directories, or verify every public source link/retained audit.
The supplied production report describes two byte-matching builds; that is
a document-provenance assertion, not a scientific check, and it was not
rerun. Shared-provider AI readings are not independent human confirmation.
This limits confidence rather than constituting a specific theorem error.

R6. The presentation makes the hierarchy harder to absorb (minor revision).
The abstract is long and carries several distinct sharpness notions. Sections
9-10 retain preliminary budget results before the exact classification, and
pp. 27-28 contain extensive production/review history. A compact table of
which family proves which properties, and a shorter main-text provenance
statement with a complete linked supplement, would improve inspectability.
Any such reorganization must preserve all original reports and adverse
findings; it must not convert this exact-PDF score into a score for new bytes.

Pages 21-22 label two equations (31) and (32), whereas the subsequent new
section on pp. 24-25 starts at (20) and continues through (26). The exact TeX

No clipped formulas, missing glyphs, blank pages, unreadable main text or

There are no other identified objections in this round. In particular, I do

6. Criterion justification and final technical verdict

Correctness confidence 4/5: all main finite-dimensional proof chains were
read and challenged; the delicate endpoint construction accounts for dyadic
rounding, strict events, mean/floor control and both top eigenvalues. No
invalidating error was found. One AI reading and unverified independence do
not justify maximal confidence.

Rigor and completeness 4/5: hypotheses, zero-support cases, eigenvalue
multiplicities, noninteger moments, finite prefixes and separate construction
families are handled explicitly. The universal sub-one spectral interval and
optimal quantitative constants/rates remain outside the proved results.

Novelty 3/5: the joint code/Perron marginal statement and the critical endpoint
realizations are plausible original specializations relative to the sources
actually checked. Hypercontractivity, good primal/dual codes, sign regions,
Gram comparisons and scalar affine bounds are not new. External priority
was not established.

Significance 3/5: the paper supplies useful sharp distinctions between local
information, raw density moments and transfer relaxation in a clean model.
The attained cutoff construction adds a precise endpoint refinement. No
field-wide implication or external application was demonstrated, and the
model deliberately lacks physical locality assumptions.

Reproducibility/inspectability 4/5: the exact PDF, all page renders, text,
TeX and identifiers are hash-bound and the proofs can be followed without
executing mathematics. Production provenance is separate and readable.
This round did not reproduce the build or inspect every external artifact;
asymptotic constants and extensive provenance text add practical friction.

Final technical verdict: a strong specialist working paper, with minor
presentation revision recommended and substantive originality/significance
reservations retained. Millennium 4.00/10.00 is a research-strength judgment,
not a school mark, correctness probability, or editorial acceptance. The
number is assigned once to the exact SHA256 stated above. No earlier score
was inherited and no rerun to improve this score was performed.

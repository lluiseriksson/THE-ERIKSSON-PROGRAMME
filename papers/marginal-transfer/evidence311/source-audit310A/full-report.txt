J2 -- WALSH OPERATOR NORM AND ACTUAL EIGENVALUES: PASS
For a Walsh character chi_S, multiplication by chi_U sends S to S symmetric-difference U. The two Q factors multiply it by rho^((|S|+|S symmetric-difference U|)/2). The elementary set identity turns this into rho^(|U|/2+|S outside U|). A permutation matrix with these positive weights has norm their maximum, exactly rho^(|U|/2), reached for any S contained in U. This proves the operator norm, not merely a bound on a matrix entry or on one vector. It also covers arbitrary nonempty U and all ambient dimensions containing U.

The nonconstant Walsh coefficients of fixed F are finitely many. Character I on k signs becomes a character of degree M|I| on background bits. Thus the difference from P has norm at most sum_{I nonempty}|fhat(I)|rho^(M|I|/2)<=D_F rho^(M/2). Possible large fixed D_F is harmless. The constant coefficient is exactly one by E F=1. No assumed spectral regularity is hidden here.

The expansion log N=(s-1)delta^2/2+O(delta^4) follows by even Taylor expansion. The unnormalized top numerator is 1+rho delta^2/(1-rho)+O(delta^4), so log kappa has zero quadratic coefficient at s-1=2rho/(1-rho). Factoring as C C* gives a block Gram matrix whose diagonal blocks each have top eigenvalue one and whose off-diagonal norm is eta_n. Its block diagonal therefore has two leading eigenvalues one. J3 -- SHARP SCALAR TAIL OPTIMIZATION: PASS, WITH A CLARIFICATION RECORDED

For K>k_s, differentiate h(y)=y^s/(y-K) for y>K. Its derivative has the sign of (s-1)y-sK. Both endpoint limits are infinite, giving unique minimum at v*=sK/(s-1). Evaluating h gives c=s^s K^(s-1)/(s-1)^(s-1). For y<=K, c(y-K)<=0, while for y>K the minimum proves y^s>=c(y-K). Integration yields c(1-K). With high probability p*=(1-K)/(v*-K)=(s-1)(1-K)/K, one has 0<p*<1, v*>1, mean K+p*(v*-K)=1, and equality at both support points K and v*. The same calculation can be checked in the finite two-point family with fixed lower value u: derivative of p[u+(1-u)/p]^s is v^(s-1)[su-(s-1)v].

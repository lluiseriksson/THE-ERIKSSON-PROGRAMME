1. Mossel, Oleszkiewicz and Sen, On Reverse Hypercontractivity,
https://arxiv.org/html/1108.1210v5 , Section1.1 Eq(1.1) and following
tensorization paragraph, read directly in the primary author manuscript.
They recall the classical Boolean hypercontractive inequality, including the
endpoint time, and explicitly state tensorization. Taking input exponent2,
output q=2s/(s-1), and e^(-t)=sqrt(rho) gives rho<=1/(q-1).
At s=(1+rho)/(1-rho) equality holds. This is exactly the classical input
in critical298 TheoremA. We claim neither that inequality nor its exponent
as new. Scalar norm control and eigenvalue perturbation in A are elementary.

2. Glueck, Spectral gaps for hyperbounded operators,
https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.09422 , version6, author abstract read.
The abstract treats positive power-bounded operators mapping an Lp space
into a strictly higher Lq and bounds the essential spectral radius below1.
That abstract does not identify the second eigenvalue uniformly over the
finite cube sequence in298. Endpoint control here is only an L2 operator
bound after inserting the density. No theorem-level novelty conclusion is
deduced from this abstract. The prior297 package retains the fuller separate
comparison and its limitations.

3. Gross, Invariance of intrinsic hypercontractivity under perturbation of
Schroedinger operators, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20282 , version2,
author abstract read. It concerns additive potentials and ground-state
transformations and explicitly announces dimension-independent bounds.
We have not identified those additive operators with the discrete weighted
transfer congruence in298. The density floor in TheoremB bounds inverse
moments, so missing inverse control is NOT a valid claimed distinction.
No implication or contradiction is asserted without the operator/exponent
correspondence. The previous297 whole-manuscript discussion is preserved.

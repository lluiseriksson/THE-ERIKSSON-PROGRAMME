G1 — ACCEPT: actual source ratio and the vanishing probability at the mean
Write E for uniform probability on the L-cube. The source299 single-site weight is w_delta(x)=(1+delta x)/n(delta), with n(delta)^s=E(1+delta x)^s. Independence gives R=product w_delta, E R=n(delta)^(-L)=m>0 and E R^s=1 exactly. Therefore R/m=product(1+delta x) exactly; no unknown normalization or balancing coefficient survives in this ratio.

Fix rho, hence s, then a sufficiently small fixed d in(0,1), and any fixed t in(0,1). Define F_t(z)=log(((1+z)^t+(1-z)^t)/2). The bracket is strictly positive on[-d,d]. The function is C^4 there (indeed smooth), even, with F_t(0)=F_t'(0)=F_t'''(0)=0 and F_t''(0)=t(t-1). Set C=max_{|z|<=d}|F_t''''(z)|/24, finite by continuity. Taylor with remainder yields
 |F_t(z)-t(t-1)z^2/2| <= C z^4.
This supplies an actual bounded remainder, without requiring a numerical enclosure. At delta_L=d L^(-1/3),
 log E(R_L/m_L)^t = L F_t(delta_L)
 <= -t(1-t)d^2 L^(1/3)/2 + C d^4 L^(-1/3) -> -infinity.
Thus E(R/m)^t->0. Positivity and Markov at threshold1 imply P(R>m)<=E(R/m)^t->0. Strict versus weak threshold does not cause a problem for this upper bound. Neither an interchange of t and L nor a uniform bound as t approaches an endpoint is used. The product R/m has expectation1 for every L even while its fractional moment vanishes; rare values account for that, so there is no contradiction.

For comparison, R->0 alone does not establish the needed relative event: a positive deterministic R=m tending to0 illustrates why information at an L-dependent threshold is distinct (its strict event is empty, but other R=mY with fixed nondegenerate mean-one Y has nonvanishing P(R>m)). The candidate proves the additional fact directly rather than making that inference.

G3 — ACCEPT: actual spectrum and moment; source299 rederived
First, the non-elementary forward hypercontractive input has the correct endpoint. The uniform Boolean noise Q=P_sqrt(rho) maps L^{1+rho} to L^2 and L^2 to L^{1+1/rho} contractively. Since 1+1/rho=2s/(s-1), Hölder with exponents(s,2s/(s-1),2s/(s-1)) gives, for real h,
 |<f,QM_h Qg>| <= ||h||_s ||f||_2 ||g||_2.
Thus ||QM_h Q||<=||h||_s. MOS Eq.(1.1) and its tensorization paragraph explicitly supply this classical endpoint with time -log(sqrt(rho)); reverse hypercontractivity is not being used.

I checked source299's balanced construction rather than assuming it. On one bit, H=n^(-1)[[1,sqrt(rho)delta],[sqrt(rho)delta,rho]], positive definite for |delta|<1. Its largest eigenvalue is
 kappa=(1+rho+sqrt((1-rho)^2+4rho delta^2))/(2n).
Expanding the even analytic functions gives log n=(s-1)delta^2/2+O(delta^4) and log kappa=(rho/(1-rho)-(s-1)/2)delta^2+O(delta^4)=O(delta^4). Hence beta_L=kappa(delta_L)^(-L)->1. Also m_L=exp(-((s-1)/2)d^2 L^(1/3)+O(L^(-1/3)))->0 and Z=1+beta m->1.

The overlap squared is the norm of Q_1 H Q_1=n^(-1)[[1,rho delta],[rho delta,rho^2]]. Calling its norm tau^2 yields
 log tau=1/2(rho^2/(1-rho^2)-(s-1)/2)delta^2+O(delta^4)
 =-rho/(2(1-rho^2))delta^2+O(delta^4).
Thus eta_L=sqrt(beta_L)tau(delta_L)^L->0. All remainders are bounded on a sufficiently small fixed compact interval by analyticity; multiplying by L makes fourth-order errors vanish while the strictly negative quadratic term diverges in magnitude.

Set U=P_rho+beta H^{tensor L}=AA*+BB*, with A=Q and B=sqrt(beta)(H^(1/2))^{tensor L}. The diagonal blocks of the block Gram matrix for[A B] each have norm1 and a top eigenvalue1. Its off-diagonal perturbation has norm||A*B||=eta_L. The diagonal block matrix consequently has first and second eigenvalues1, and min-max gives lambda1(U)<=1+eta and lambda2(U)>=1-eta (for the latter, eta<1 ensures that the positive second Gram eigenvalue is not one of the additional zeros). Monotonicity also gives lambda1(U)>=1. Thus both actual leading eigenvalues of S(w)=U/Z tend to1. This controls the full space, not a two-vector compression used as an equality.

For every nonnegative v, X=M_sqrt(v)Q is square of dimension2^L. S(v)=X*X and T_v=XX* have precisely the same eigenvalues, including zero multiplicities. There is no restricted-eigenvalue substitution. Since ||QM_R Q||<=1,

The finite-dimensional perturbation inequality follows immediately by inserting the operator-norm bound into every Rayleigh quotient in min-max; it has no dimension-dependent constant. Both actual eigenvalues of T_q therefore tend to1 and their ratio tends to1.

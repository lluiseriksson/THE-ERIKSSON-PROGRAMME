QUESTION AND SCOPE
Can an increasing number of exactly uniform labelled marginals, together with
the sharp critical moment cap, prevent transfer-gap collapse? We connect the
previously separate marginal and moment constructions. The answer below is
negative even for every power L^alpha with alpha<1/2. A positive result under
critical-power uniform integrability explains precisely which extra condition
this counterexample violates. The remaining linear-order regime is not settled.
All operators are finite Boolean transfer operators, not a local heat-bath
chain, a fixed-range Gibbs model, a physical mass gap or a continuum limit.
No statement about the Perron stationary marginals is made for this family.

DEFINITIONS
Use uniform probability u_L on {-1,1}^L and Walsh characters chi_S. Let
0<rho<1, s=(1+rho)/(1-rho)>1, B*=2^(1/s), and Q=P_sqrt(rho), where
P_t chi_S=t^|S| chi_S. For r>=0, E r=1, set
T_r=M_sqrt(r) P_rho M_sqrt(r), S_r=Q M_r Q.
They have identical spectra, including zeros, by the square AA*/A*A identity.
gap(T_r)=1-lambda2(T_r)/lambda1(T_r). On the active support r>0 the
kernel is strictly positive, so its Perron root is simple. If support is one
point lambda2=0; otherwise the usual second eigenvalue is used.
The probability law mu_L=r_L u_L has exactly uniform marginals on every set
of at most k coordinates iff E[r_L chi_S]=0 for 1<=|S|<=k. This Fourier
criterion and erasing low coefficients are classical, not claimed as new.

THEOREM A (critical cap survives growing exact marginal matching)
Fix 0<alpha<1/2 and 0<b<1. For every L>=1 there is a strictly positive
mean-one density r_L with
  r_L>=b, ||r_L||_s<=B*,
  E[r_L chi_S]=0 for 1<=|S|<=floor(L^alpha),
while lambda1(T_rL),lambda2(T_rL)->1 and gap(T_rL)->0.
Thus the exact worst-case critical norm boundary B* is unchanged even under
these growing-order marginal constraints and any prescribed floor b<1.
The uniform gap below B* follows from the predecessor's sign bound
2 lambda2^s<=E r^s and lambda1>=1; it does not need the marginal constraints.
We do not assert linear-order matching, an optimal growth order, many slow
modes, or Perron-stationary marginal matching for this construction.

PROOF
1. Balanced critical tilt, with its actual leading eigenvalues.
Choose a=3/8-alpha/4, so 1/4<a<(1-alpha)/2, and fixed sufficiently small
d>0. Set delta_L=d L^(-a), n(delta)=(((1+delta)^s+(1-delta)^s)/2)^(1/s),
R_L(x)=product_i [(1+delta_L x_i)/n(delta_L)], and m_L=E R_L=n(delta_L)^(-L).
Use d<1, so all factors are positive. The one-site matrix for Q M_R Q is
H(delta)=n(delta)^(-1) [[1,delta sqrt(rho)],[delta sqrt(rho),rho]].
Its top eigenvalue is
kappa(delta)=[1+rho+sqrt((1-rho)^2+4rho delta^2)]/[2n(delta)].
Let D=diag(1,rho) and tau(delta)=||D^(1/2)H(delta)^(1/2)||. Direct 2x2
eigenvalue calculation gives
tau(delta)^2=[1+rho^2+sqrt((1-rho^2)^2+4rho^2 delta^2)]/[2n(delta)].
Taylor expansion at zero, for fixed rho, gives
 log n(delta)=(s-1)delta^2/2+O(delta^4),
 log kappa(delta)=O(delta^4),
 log tau(delta)=-rho delta^2/[2(1-rho^2)]+O(delta^4).
These are the same explicit quantities as predecessor Theorems12-13; no
numerical experiment is used. Here L delta_L^4=d^4 L^(alpha-1/2)->0 and
L delta_L^2=d^2 L^(1/4+alpha/2)->infinity. Hence
c_L=kappa(delta_L)^(-L)->1, m_L->0, Z_L=1+c_L m_L->1.
Let v_L=(1+c_L R_L)/Z_L, a positive mean-one density.
The unnormalized S operator is F_L=P_rho+c_L H(delta_L)^(tensor L).
Both summands have top eigenvalue one. The Gram operator formed by their
positive square roots has diagonal blocks those two summands and cross-block
norm eta_L=sqrt(c_L) tau(delta_L)^L->0. Its block-diagonal counterpart has
its two leading eigenvalues exactly one. The variational eigenvalue bound
therefore places its first two eigenvalues in [1-eta_L,1+eta_L]. The nonzero
Gram eigenvalues equal those of F_L. For large L these top two eigenvalues
are positive and are actual eigenvalues of F_L, not extra Gram zeros.
Consequently lambda1(S_vL),lambda2(S_vL)->1 after division by Z_L.

2. Critical norm of the tilt.
E R_L^s=1 exactly. For each fixed 0<t<s,
 log E R_L^t= -t(s-t)L delta_L^2/2+O(L delta_L^4)->-infinity.
Set theta=min(1,s/2). The elementary mixed-power bound, for y>=0,
 0<=(1+y)^s-1-y^s<=s 2^(s-1)(y^theta+y^(s-theta))
follows from the mean-value theorem on y<=1 and the reciprocal substitution
on y>=1. Both theta and s-theta lie strictly between zero and s. Apply it
to y=c_L R_L. Since c_L->1, it gives E(1+c_L R_L)^s->2, hence
||v_L||_s->B*. This is a moment calculation, not convergence-in-probability
substituted for moment convergence.

3. Erase the low-degree coefficients with an L-infinity-small correction.
The exact Fourier expansion is R_L=m_L sum_S delta_L^|S| chi_S. Put
k_L=floor(L^alpha) and
 p_L=m_L sum_{1<=|S|<=k_L} delta_L^|S| chi_S.
Then E p_L=0 and
 ||p_L||_infinity <=m_L sum_{j=1}^{k_L} binom(L,j) delta_L^j
                 <=m_L k_L L^k_L ->0.
Indeed log m_L=-(s-1)d^2 L^(1/4+alpha/2)/2+O(L delta_L^4), whereas
log k_L+k_L log L=o(L^(1/4+alpha/2)); alpha<1/2 is essential to this
comparison. The estimate is deliberately crude and does not establish an
optimal marginal order. Define
 w_L=[1+c_L(R_L-p_L)]/Z_L.
It has mean one and all its nonconstant Fourier coefficients through degree
k_L vanish exactly. For sufficiently large L,
 w_L >=[1-c_L||p_L||_infinity]/Z_L ->1 from a possibly smaller side.
Thus it is strictly positive and eventually at least any prescribed b<1.
The perturbation satisfies
 ||w_L-v_L||_infinity <=c_L||p_L||_infinity/Z_L ->0,
so ||w_L||_s->B*. Linearity gives
 ||S_wL-S_vL|| <=||w_L-v_L||_infinity ->0,
because ||Q||=1. Therefore the two leading eigenvalues of S_wL tend to one.
Unlike a generic Fourier erasure, here positivity is paid for by the existing
uniform baseline and the explicitly vanishing sup-norm correction.

4. Enforce the exact critical cap without destroying the marginals.
Write B_L=||w_L||_s, which tends to B*>1. For the positive large-L part set
 eps_L=0 if B_L<=B*, and eps_L=(B_L-B*)/(B_L-1) otherwise.
The latter denominator is positive, 0<eps_L<1, and eps_L->0. Define
 r_L=(1-eps_L)w_L+eps_L.
Mean one, positivity, the eventually prescribed floor and the exact Fourier
zeros persist. Minkowski gives ||r_L||_s<=(1-eps_L)B_L+eps_L<=B*.
Endpoint Boolean hypercontractivity and Holder yield, for signed f,
 ||Q M_f Q||<=||f||_s,
since ||Q h||_(2s/(s-1))<=||h||_2. Hence
 ||S_rL-S_wL||<=eps_L||w_L-1||_s<=eps_L(B_L+1)->0.
Both actual leading eigenvalues still tend to one. Choose one finite L0 beyond
all the stated positivity and floor conditions and replace every earlier
r_L by the constant one. This satisfies all constraints in every dimension
and preserves the limits. Strict positivity gives a positive individual gap
at every finite L, while its limit is zero. This completes the construction.

THEOREM B (spectral rigidity with critical-power uniform integrability)
Suppose k_L->infinity, r_L>=0, E r_L=1, and all nonconstant Fourier
coefficients through k_L vanish. If
 lim_{M->infinity} sup_L E[r_L^s 1_(r_L>M)]=0,
then ||S_rL-P_rho||->0 and gap(T_rL)->1-rho.
More quantitatively, choose any rho<sigma<1, put t=sqrt(rho/sigma)<1,
m_L=floor(k_L/2), and eta_M=sup_L||r_L 1_(r_L>M)||_s. Then
 ||S_rL-P_rho||<=2(M+1)t^(m_L+1)+2 eta_M.
This is a sufficient condition, not a necessary characterization.

PROOF
Let Pi be the orthogonal projection onto Walsh degrees at most m_L. For
h=r_L-1 the matrix entries of Q M_h Q are
rho^((|A|+|B|)/2) hhat(A symmetric_difference B). Thus Pi(Q M_h Q)Pi=0.
Decompose h=h_b+h_t, where h_b=r_L 1_(r_L<=M)-1 and h_t=r_L 1_(r_L>M).
Set Qsigma=P_sqrt(sigma), D=P_t. The bounded part is
B_b=Q M_hb Q=D A_b D, A_b=Qsigma M_hb Qsigma, ||A_b||<=M+1.
D commutes with Pi and ||D(1-Pi)||<=t^(m_L+1). The identity
 B_b-Pi B_b Pi=(1-Pi)B_b+Pi B_b(1-Pi)
gives norm at most 2(M+1)t^(m_L+1). For B_t=Q M_ht Q, the endpoint
Holder/hypercontractive bound above gives ||B_t||<=eta_M and hence
||B_t-Pi B_t Pi||<=2 eta_M. Since Pi(B_b+B_t)Pi=0, summing proves the
bound. First take L->infinity at fixed M, then M->infinity. Ordinary
self-adjoint perturbation gives lambda1(S_rL)->1 and lambda2(S_rL)->rho,
so the gap limit follows. No interchange of the two limits is needed.
Theorem A disproves replacing this UI hypothesis by the mere critical cap2.

RELATED STRICT-EXPONENT ESTIMATE
If q>s, ||r_L||_q<=B and k_L->infinity, choose
rho<sigma<=(q-1)/(q+1), t=sqrt(rho/sigma), m_L=floor(k_L/2).
Then ||S_rL-P_rho||<=2(B+1)t^(m_L+1). Indeed A=Qsigma M_(r_L-1)
Qsigma has norm at most ||r_L-1||_q<=B+1 by Holder/hypercontractivity,
its Pi block vanishes by Fourier support, and the same D decomposition
proves the estimate. This auxiliary statement is grouped with TheoremsA-B,
not proposed as a separate paper. The letter q here is a moment exponent,
not the hypercontractive q=1+rho used in the predecessor.

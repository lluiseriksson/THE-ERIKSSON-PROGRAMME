# Primary-source reading notes

These are auditor notes, not copies or hashes of remote source bytes. Access occurred during the audit on 2026-11-13.

- [Mossel, Oleszkiewicz and Sen, On Reverse Hypercontractivity, arXiv:1108.1210v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.1210): opening equation (1.1) gives the ordinary uniform two-point inequality at the inclusive time boundary; its following paragraph states tensorization. The parameter conversion used here is time t = -log(sqrt(rho)), input exponent 2, output exponent q = 2s/(s-1). At critical s, q-1=1/rho, so the boundary is attained exactly. Equation (1.2) is the reverse input used in the earlier manuscript, not needed in the new Theorem A. These are established hypercontractive tools.
- [Gross, Invariance of intrinsic hypercontractivity under perturbation of Schroedinger operators, arXiv:2412.20282v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20282v2): introduction and Theorem 2.2 describe additive potential perturbations of a Riemannian Dirichlet-form operator, with positive and negative exponential moments. The theorem gives quantitative dimension-independent bounds. I did not establish any transformation identifying that operator and its hypotheses with the logarithm of the discrete transfer congruence here. The new collapse family has a uniform density floor, so absence of inverse-density control is not an adequate distinction. The unresolved operator and exponent correspondence is the relevant limitation.
- [Bonami, 1970 publisher record](https://aif.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/aif.357/) and [Beckner, 1975 publisher record](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1975/102-1/p11): bibliographic checks only. The old primary paper PDFs were located; the Beckner opening extraction is not evidence that its whole proof was read.

Conclusion: the threshold and ordinary inequality are classical. These limited primary checks do not establish novelty, priority, or superiority over perturbation literature. The construction is judged on its own proof, and the source correctly withholds priority.

SOLE QUESTION AFTER330
For k_L/sqrt(L)->C in(0,infinity), can the odd-erasure construction give an explicit positive-density spectral limit at the exact critical cap? This is an achievable-profile question, not a universal lower bound or optimality claim. Hypotheses: fixedrho in(0,1), s=(1+rho)/(1-rho), any fixedfloor b in(0,1), uniform probability cube. Mechanism: take tilt energy a sufficiently large fixed multiple of the odd erasure degree, retain the quartic loss instead of discarding it, and compare the complete block spectra. Falsifier: a wrong fourth-order coefficient, insufficient one-sided/norm control at finite scale, or misordered second eigenvalue. Abandon after three units without a proof or useful obstruction. The universal positive-gap question at fixedC remains open.

DEFINITIONS AND CLAIM
P_t chi_A=t^|A|chi_A, Q=P_sqrt(rho), S_r=Q M_r Q and T_r=M_sqrt(r)P_rho M_sqrt(r). Eigenvalues decrease with multiplicity, and gap=1-lambda2/lambda1. Write a_s=(s-1)/2, B*=2^(1/s), C_s=max(1,s-1), and
 gamma_rho=s(s^2-1)/24=rho(1+rho)/[6(1-rho)^3]>0.
Choose once and for all a finite K>=4 sufficiently large that
 a_s K>2+1/(2K)+log K,
 a_s K>(1/2)log(e C_s K).                                  (A)
Such K exists because the left side grows linearly and the right sides logarithmically. It depends onrho, notC orL.

For every integer sequence1<=k_L<=L with k_L/sqrtL->C in(0,infinity), there exist densities r_L>=b, E r_L=1, ||r_L||_s<=B*, and all nonconstant Fourier coefficients throughk_L equalzero, such that
 lambda1(T_r)->1,
 lambda2(T_r)->max{rho, exp(-gamma_rho K^2 C^2)}.             (B)
Consequently the achieved gap limit is
 min{1-rho, 1-exp(-gamma_rho K^2 C^2)}
 <=min{1-rho, gamma_rho K^2 C^2}.                            (C)
This is an upper bound on what can be achieved by suitable densities (and hence on a corresponding infimum), not a lower bound for all densities. No claim thatK is optimal is made.

QUARTIC TRANSFER LOSS
In the one-site Walsh basis,
 H=n(delta)^(-1)[[1,delta sqrt(rho)],[delta sqrt(rho),rho]], D=diag(1,rho).
Letkappa be its largest eigenvalue. Its exact numerator before division byn is
 ell=[1+rho+sqrt((1-rho)^2+4rho delta^2)]/2
 =1+[rho/(1-rho)]delta^2-[rho^2/(1-rho)^3]delta^4+O_rho(delta^6).
Writingrho=(s-1)/(s+1) gives
 logell=(s-1)delta^2/2-[(s-1)^2(s+2)/8]delta^4+O_s(delta^6),
 logn=(s-1)delta^2/2-[(s-1)(s^2+s-3)/12]delta^4+O_s(delta^6).
Subtracting cancels the quadratic and gives
 logkappa=-s(s^2-1)delta^4/24+O_s(delta^6).
Thereforekappa^L->t_C=exp(-gamma_rho K^2 C^2) in(0,1).
For tau=||D^(1/2)H^(1/2)||, the exact formula is
 tau^2=[1+rho^2+sqrt((1-rho^2)^2+4rho^2 delta^2)]/[2n(delta)].
Thus logtau=-rho delta^2/[2(1-rho^2)]+O_rho(delta^4), whence tau^L->0: the negative term has orderm->infinity and the remainder isO(1).

ATTRIBUTION AND BOUNDARY
Source319 supplies the critical product/Gram/cap mechanism;330 supplies the odd one-sided erasure estimates, checked here with finite ratios and explicit exponential margins(A). Classical inputs are hypercontractivity, Cauchy/Taylor estimates, Walsh expansions and min-max. The new calculation retains the quartic loss to give an achievable finite-C profile. It is not claimed historically novel or optimal, and does not establish that every admissible density has positive limiting gap. ForC>0 the displayed family has a strictly positive limit. AsC decreases tozero this upper profile decreases quadratically, but these are separate fixed-C families, not an interchange ofL/C limits.

1. Statement and conventions
Fix0<rho<1, s=(1+rho)/(1-rho)>1, k_s=(s-1)/s, C_s=s^s/(s-1)^(s-1), and k_s<K<1. Put g(K)=C_s(1-K)K^(s-1), so0<g(K)<1. For every integer L>=1 there exists a strictly positive density r_L on the uniform Boolean cube {-1,1}^L such that
 E r_L=1, r_L>=K, E[r_L^s 1_{r_L>K}]<=1+g(K),
 lambda1(T_r_L)->1, lambda2(T_r_L)->1, gap(r_L)->0.
Here T_r=M_sqrt(r) P_{rho,L} M_sqrt(r), P has Walsh eigenvalues rho^|S|, Q_L=P_{sqrt(rho),L}, S_r=Q_L M_r Q_L, and gap=1-lambda2/lambda1. The full spectra of T_r and S_r agree by AA*/A*A with A=M_sqrt(r)Q_L. Eigenvalues are decreasing, with multiplicity; probabilities and expectations are normalized uniform, not counting.

Consequently any prescribed0<b<=K is a valid fixed floor for this same family, and every tail budget tau>=1+g(K) is allowed. The novelty within the prior construction is removal of BOTH the strict budget slack and strict floor b<K. The displayed value is an attained constructive upper bound for collapse, not a proven optimal universal boundary. No statement is made for K<=k_s, varyingrho, b>K, locality, code marginal matching, heat-bath gaps, continuum physics or Yang-Mills. The event is strictly r>K: the construction uses an atom exactly at K, which the event excludes.

3. Growing dyadic background laws without an uncontrolled Walsh sum
The sharp scalar background law from310 has lower atomK, high atom
 v_*=sK/(s-1)>1, high probability p_*=(s-1)(1-K)/K in(0,1),
and high cost p_*v_*^s=g(K). For largeL define
 k=k_L=floor(L^(1/3)), M=M_L=floor(L/(2k)), n=n_L=L-kM,
 p=p_L=floor(2^k p_*)/2^k, v=v_L=K+(1-K)/p.
Eventually k,M,n>=1, p in[p_*/2,p_*], p->p_*, v->v_* and v<=V:=2v_*. All these assertions follow from k->infinity, dyadic rounding error<2^(-k), and v<=K+2(1-K)/p_*=2v_*-K<V. Both M,n tend to infinity, n>=L/2, while M/k->infinity.

Choose any set H_L of exactly2^k p sign patterns among {-1,1}^k, for example the first such patterns in lexicographic order. Define F_L(z)=v on H_L and K otherwise, so E F_L=1. Split kM background bits into k disjoint M-bit blocks, replace each signz_i by its block parity, and call the resulting function A_L(x). Let I_L(x) be the indicator that the parity pattern is in H_L. The block parities are independent unbiased signs, hence E I_L=p and A_L=K+(v-K)I_L has exactly the stated law and mean1.

For any nonempty Walsh setU on d bits,
 Q_d M_chi_U Q_d chi_S
 =rho^(|U|/2+|S outside U|) chi_{S symmetric_difference U}.
This weighted permutation has exact operator normrho^|U|/2. Every nonconstant Walsh coefficient of F_L has absolute value at most E F_L=1 because F_L>=0 and |chi_I|=1. There are at most2^k-1 such coefficients, and each substituted nonempty character has degree at leastM. Therefore
 ||Q_{kM} M_A_L Q_{kM}-P_{rho,kM}||
 <=(2^k-1)rho^(M/2)->0.                                      (1)
Indeed log(2^k rho^(M/2))=k log2+(M/2)logrho tends to minus infinity since M/k->infinity andlogrho<0. This explicit growing-coefficient bound replaces310's fixed-function estimate; no dimension-uniform bound on the number of coefficients is assumed.

The scalar result310, or its direct affine minorant y^s1_{y>K}>=C_sK^(s-1)(y-K), gives
 d_L:=p v^s-g(K)>=0, and d_L->0.                              (2)
This law has an exact low atomK and high atomv>K, so the strict-event objective is exactlyp v^s. Continuity atp_* gives the limit. No finite uniform cube is presumed to realize a non-dyadicp_* exactly.

4. Clipping the tilt and controlling the exact tail error
Set theta=min(1,s/2), so0<theta<s and0<s-theta<s. With n=n_L put
 c_n=a_n(theta)^(1/(2theta))>0,
 U_n=(R_n-c_n)_+,
 m'_n=E U_n.
Then c_n->0,0<=U_n<=R_n,m'_n<=a_n(1)->0. Also
 P(U_n>0)=P(R_n>c_n)<=a_n(theta)/c_n^theta=sqrt(a_n(theta))->0, (3)
 ||Q_n M_(U_n-R_n) Q_n||<=c_n,                               (4)
because0<=R_n-U_n=min(R_n,c_n)<=c_n and||Q_n||=1. Exact equalityR_n=c_n belongs to the complement of the strict event and is harmless.

For any c>0,z>=0 and reals>1 the elementary homogeneous estimate is
 0<=(c+z)^s-c^s-z^s
 <=B_s[c^(s-theta)z^theta+c^theta z^(s-theta)], B_s=s2^(s-1). (5)
Forz/c<=1 the mean value theorem gives an upper bound B_s(z/c), at most B_s(z/c)^theta; forz/c>=1 interchangec,z. Nonnegativity follows from convexity or integration of the positive derivative. Thus(5) is valid for noninteger exponents and both powers on its right are interior powers with vanishinga_n.

Define an explicit nonnegative error, with V=2v_* fixed,
 E_L=K^s sqrt(a_n(theta))
     +B_s[(K^(s-theta)+V^(s-theta))a_n(theta)
           +(K^theta+V^theta)a_n(s-theta)].                    (6)
All terms tend to zero. Let e_L=d_L+E_L>=0. For all sufficiently largeL, e_L<1/2 and m'_n<1-K. On that range define
 beta_L=(1-e_L)^(1/s) in(0,1],
 gamma_L=beta_L m'_n/p_L,
 r_L(x,y)=A_L(x)-gamma_L I_L(x)+beta_L U_n(y).                 (7)
Then beta_L->1 and gamma_L->0. Its mean is exactly
 E r_L=1-p_L gamma_L+beta_L m'_n=1.
On the low sector it equalsK+beta_LU_n>=K. On the high sector its constant part is
 v_L-gamma_L=K+(1-K-beta_L m'_n)/p_L>K.
Thus the global floor is exactly at leastK without any normalization that could move it belowK. In particular r_L is strictly positive. The vanished mean is compensated only in the high sector, where there is strict room; it is not subtracted from every point.

The low-sector strict event is exactlyU_n>0. Apply(5) withc=K,z=beta_L U_n restricted to that event. The constant part contributes at mostK^s P(U_n>0). The s-power contributes at mostbeta_L^s E U_n^s, and the mixed contributions are bounded by their unrestricted counterparts. In the high sector all points are strictly aboveK. There,
 (v_L-gamma_L+beta_L U_n)^s<=(v_L+beta_L U_n)^s,
so(5) withc=v_L bounds its full expectation. Averaging the two sectors, usingbeta_L<=1,v_L<=V,U_n<=R_n,E R_n^s=1, and(3), yields the exact upper bound
 E[r_L^s 1_{r_L>K}]<=p_L v_L^s+beta_L^s+E_L
 =g(K)+d_L+1-e_L+E_L=1+g(K).                                (8)
In this estimate the s-power terms have combined coefficient(1-p)+p=1; the two mixed-term bounds and low-sector probability are bounded by(6). No convergence of s-moments is inferred from convergence in probability. Unlike310's use ofu<K, this proof deliberately exploits the mass exactly at the strict cutoff.

Let P_b=P_{rho,kM}, P_t=P_{rho,n}; Q_b,Q_t denote the corresponding square-root noise operators. Tensorization and linearity give

Here ||H_n||->1 and beta_L,b_n->1. Every error is explicit and vanishes; no rate or unproved compactness assertion is needed.

6. What was removed, and what remains open
Theorem310 required0<b<K and a strict tail-budget slack above1+g(K). Formula(7), supported by clipping and a high-sector mean correction, proves the strongerfloorr>=K. Formula(8), supported by growing dyadiclaws and an explicit amplitude reservee_L, gives the exact upper-budget endpoint. Neither removal is a renaming of a desired conclusion as an assumption. The construction is explicit once sufficientlylargeL is chosen; all finite quantities are formulas or finite uniform expectations of the explicitR_n.

The scalar boundm_s(K)=g(K) forK>k_s is sharp among mean-one scalarbackgrounds. This theorem does NOT prove that every collapsing transfer family must have tailbudget>=1+g(K). The general sufficient gap regiontau<2-K^(s-1) survives. The interval between that region and this attained construction remains open. It is not justified to add an escaped unit to a scalar inequality for an arbitrary family. For the non-strict eventr>=K the entire density contributes its total moment, so the strict theorem cannot be relabelled as a non-strict one. Forb>K the earlier total-moment regime is separate; b=1forcesr1,b>1isempty. No claim here extends external originality or resolves physical/continuum assumptions.

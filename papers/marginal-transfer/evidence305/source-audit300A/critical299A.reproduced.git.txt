1. Setting and precise claim

Fix 0<rho<1 and s=(1+rho)/(1-rho)>1. On {-1,1}^L, L>=1, all expectations and Lp norms use the uniform probability measure. P_rho is the Boolean noise operator; its Walsh eigenvalues are rho^|S|. Write Q=P_sqrt(rho), so Q^2=P_rho. For r>=0 with E r=1, set T_r=M_sqrt(r) P_rho M_sqrt(r), and order its 2^L real eigenvalues lambda1>=lambda2>=...>=0 with multiplicity. Define gap(r)=1-lambda2/lambda1. No local-dynamics interpretation is imposed.

Let B*=2^(1/s). The proposed result is:
(i) 2 lambda2(T_r)^s <= E r^s. Consequently, if ||r||s<=B<B*, then gap(r)>=1-B/B*>0, uniformly in L. This numerical lower bound is not claimed to be the optimal positive gap value.
(ii) For every fixed b in(0,1), there is a sequence r_L>0, E r_L=1, ||r_L||s<=B*, r_L>=b for every L>=1, with gap(r_L)->0.
Thus for every B>=1, the infimum gap under ||r||s<=B is positive exactly when B<B*, both in the nonnegative-density class and in any subclass with a prescribed floor b in(0,1). At and above B* the infimum is0, not a claimed attained finite-dimensional zero gap. The floor restriction b<1 is essential: b=1 forces r=1. No rate at B* and no optimal positive-gap formula are claimed.

The classical input is Boolean hypercontractivity, in either equivalent endpoint form
||Q g||2 <= ||g||p, p=1+rho=2s/(s+1),
||Q f||q <= ||f||2, q=2s/(s-1), q-1=1/rho.
Both apply at equality of the noise parameter. These classical inequalities, Holder, positivity, the finite-dimensional min-max principle and Weyl's eigenvalue perturbation bound are not claimed as original.

2. Two disjoint sign budgets

The operator T_r is positive semidefinite. Its active support A={r>0} is nonempty and its kernel restricted to A is strictly entrywise positive, since every entry of P_rho is positive. Its top eigenvalue is positive and simple with a strictly positive eigenvector on A. Coordinates outside A form a zero block. If lambda2=0, (i) is immediate. Otherwise an associated real eigenvector f vanishes off A, is orthogonal to the positive Perron vector on A, and therefore has nonzero positive and negative parts f_+,f_-. This also covers multiplicity of lambda2; no base-measure mean-zero assumption is used. If A has one point, lambda2=0 already covered.

For a real h supported on a set C, hypercontractivity and Holder give
<h,T_r h> = ||Q(sqrt(r)h)||2^2
 <= ||sqrt(r)h||p^2
 <= (E[r^s 1_C])^(1/s) ||h||2^2.                         (1)
For clarity, before raising to2/p, Holder uses exponents2/(2-p) and2/p:
E[r^(p/2)|h|^p] <= (E[r^(p/(2-p))1_C])^((2-p)/2) (E|h|^2)^(p/2).
Here p/(2-p)=s. There is no missing counting-measure factor.

Since f=f_+-f_-, the eigenvalue equation yields
lambda2||f_+||2^2=<f_+,T_r f_+>-<f_+,T_r f_-> <= <f_+,T_r f_+>.
The cross term is nonnegative by kernel positivity. Applying(1) with C_+={f>0} and dividing by the nonzero norm shows lambda2^s<=E[r^s1_C+]. Pairing the eigenvalue equation with f_- gives the same bound on C_-={f<0}. These sets are disjoint. Adding proves2lambda2^s<=E r^s, including unassigned mass on {f=0}.

The unit vector sqrt(r) has Rayleigh quotient
<sqrt(r),T_r sqrt(r)>=<r,P_rho r> >= (E r)^2=1,
where the final inequality follows from nonnegative Walsh eigenvalues and the constant Walsh coefficient. Hence lambda1>=1 and lambda2/lambda1<=lambda2<=B/B*. The advertised gap bound follows. If B=1, Jensen's strict convexity forces r=1, and the actual gap is1-rho. Our weaker positive lower bound at B=1 is consistent with that value.

3. Balanced critical family and its actual spectrum

We give the needed construction and limits explicitly; it is the balanced critical family of unit298 (immutable manuscript/source45d91a667750b295ce096fd495863583ba56eced, Theorem12), now with its exact limiting moment evaluated. Fix d>0 sufficiently small, and put delta_L=d L^(-1/3), with d<1. For a single uniform sign x, define
n(delta)=[((1+delta)^s+(1-delta)^s)/2]^(1/s),
w_delta(x)=(1+delta x)/n(delta), m(delta)=1/n(delta).
Then w>0, E w=m and E w^s=1. In the orthonormal Walsh basis(1,x), put D=diag(1,rho), Q_1=diag(1,sqrt(rho)), and
H=Q_1 M_w Q_1 = n^-1 [[1,delta sqrt(rho)],[delta sqrt(rho),rho]].
Its top eigenvalue is
kappa(delta)=[1+rho+sqrt((1-rho)^2+4rho delta^2)]/(2n(delta)).
Set a_L=kappa(delta_L)^(-L)>0, R_L(x)=product_i w_delta_L(x_i),
Z_L=1+a_L m(delta_L)^L, r_L=(1+a_L R_L)/Z_L.              (2)
These are strictly positive normalized densities, and r_L>=1/Z_L.

The following Taylor statements are ordinary expansions of real analytic even functions near delta=0, with constants depending on fixed rho,s and uniform on a sufficiently small closed interval:
log n(delta)=(s-1)delta^2/2+O(delta^4),
log kappa(delta)=[rho/(1-rho)-(s-1)/2]delta^2+O(delta^4)=O(delta^4).
The cancellation uses s-1=2rho/(1-rho). Thus L log kappa(delta_L)=O(L^(-1/3)), a_L->1; also m(delta_L)^L->0 since Ldelta_L^2=d^2 L^(1/3)->infinity. In particular Z_L->1.

For completeness, the Gram overlap needed for the top two eigenvalues is governed by
tau(delta)^2=[1+rho^2+sqrt((1-rho^2)^2+4rho^2 delta^2)]/(2n(delta)).
Expansion gives
log tau(delta)=-rho/[2(1-rho^2)] delta^2+O(delta^4).       (3)
Indeed its quadratic coefficient is one half of rho^2/(1-rho^2)-(s-1)/2=-rho/(1-rho^2).
Hence eta_L=sqrt(a_L)tau(delta_L)^L->0.

The transformed operator is
Q M_rL Q = S_L/Z_L, S_L=P_rho+a_L H^tensor L.
Both summands of S_L have top eigenvalue1: P_rho by Markov normalization, and a_L H^tensor L by the definition of a_L. Factor S_L=AA*+BB*, with A=Q and B=sqrt(a_L)(H^(1/2))^tensor L. The nonzero spectra of S_L and the block Gram matrix [[A*A,A*B],[B*A,B*B]] agree, with multiplicity. Its diagonal blocks have norm1 and each has a top eigenvalue1. The off-diagonal block has norm eta_L, because
||Q_1 H^(1/2)||^2=||Q_1 H Q_1||=tau(delta)^2.
The equality of the two norms follows from equality of spectra of X*X and XX*; the last matrix is n^-1[[1,rho delta],[rho delta,rho^2]].
The block off-diagonal perturbation has norm eta_L. Therefore min-max/Weyl gives
lambda1(S_L)<=1+eta_L, lambda2(S_L)>=1-eta_L,
and positivity gives lambda1(S_L)>=1. For all sufficiently large L, 1-eta_L>0, so the second Gram eigenvalue is a genuine nonzero eigenvalue of S_L rather than an added zero. Consequently both top eigenvalues of Q M_rL Q tend to1 after division by Z_L->1.
The spectra of Q M_r Q and actual T_r agree: take X=M_sqrt(r)Q, so X*X=Q M_r Q and XX*=T_r, square matrices of the same size. This remains true even when zeros occur. Thus lambda1(T_rL),lambda2(T_rL)->1 and gap(r_L)->0.

4. Exact limiting critical moment

For each fixed t in(0,s), a direct single-site expansion gives
log E w_delta^t = [t(t-1)/2-t(s-1)/2]delta^2+O(delta^4)
 = t(t-s)delta^2/2+O(delta^4).
Product structure and delta_L yield E R_L^t->0. On the other hand E R_L^s=1 exactly for every L. The remainder constants may depend on t; no uniformity as t approaches0 or s is required.

Put theta=min(1,s/2), so 0<theta<s and0<s-theta<s. There is a fixed C_s with, for all y>=0,
0 <= (1+y)^s-1-y^s <= C_s(y^theta+y^(s-theta)).          (4)
The lower inequality follows from s>1. For0<=y<=1, the mean-value theorem bounds the left side above by s2^(s-1)y<=s2^(s-1)y^theta. For y>=1, factor y^s and use the preceding mean-value bound at1/y to obtain s2^(s-1)y^(s-1)<=s2^(s-1)y^(s-theta), since theta<=1. Thus C_s=s2^(s-1) suffices in(4).
Substitute y=a_L R_L, take expectations and use a_L->1 and the two strict-interior moment limits:
0 <= E(1+a_L R_L)^s-1-a_L^s
 <= C_s[a_L^theta E R_L^theta+a_L^(s-theta)E R_L^(s-theta)] ->0.
Dividing by Z_L^s->1 gives E r_L^s->2, so B_L:=||r_L||s->B*.
This is an asymptotic moment identity for the balanced family, not a triangle-bound assertion. No moment equality between distinct finite dimensions is claimed.

5. Enforcing the exact boundary, not only B>B*

Each r_L in(2) is nonconstant because d>0, a_L>0 and R_L is nonconstant. Thus B_L>1 by strict Jensen. Define
epsilon_L=0 if B_L<=B*, and epsilon_L=(B_L-B*)/(B_L-1) if B_L>B*.
There is no zero denominator in the second case. We have0<=epsilon_L<1 and epsilon_L->0, since B_L->B*>1. Set
tilde r_L=(1-epsilon_L)r_L+epsilon_L.
Then E tilde r_L=1, tilde r_L>0, and Minkowski/convexity gives
||tilde r_L||s <= (1-epsilon_L)B_L+epsilon_L <= B* exactly.
Also tilde r_L>=1/Z_L, because Z_L>1 and both r_L and1 have this lower bound.

For any real h and unit vectors f,g, Holder with exponents(s,q,q), 1/s+2/q=1, and endpoint hypercontractivity imply
|<f,Q M_h Qg>| <= ||h||s ||Qf||q||Qg||q <= ||h||s.
Therefore
||Q M_(tilde rL-rL) Q|| <= epsilon_L||1-r_L||s
 <= epsilon_L(1+B_L) ->0.                               (5)
This bounds the transformed operator difference without assuming a pointwise cap or estimating square-root differences. Weyl's finite-dimensional inequality and the exact X*X/XX* spectral identity now show lambda1(T_tilde rL),lambda2(T_tilde rL)->1. Hence gap(tilde r_L)->0.

Fix any b in(0,1). Since Z_L->1, there is a finite L0 with1/Z_L>=b for all L>=L0. Retain tilde r_L for L>=L0 and set the density identically1 for1<=L<L0. This finite-prefix completion satisfies positivity, normalization, ||r||s<=B*, and r>=b for every L. Its gap still tends to0. Strict positivity of each finite transfer matrix gives a simple Perron eigenvalue, hence a strictly positive finite-dimensional gap; the result is failure of a uniform lower bound. The same sequence is admissible for every B>=B*. Together with Section2 this proves the exact threshold classification in Section1, subject to the separate audit.

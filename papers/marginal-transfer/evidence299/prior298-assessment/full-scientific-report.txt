OPERATOR AND NORMALIZATION CHECK

On the configuration basis M_beta(x,y)=q^{d(x,y)}, q=exp(-2 beta), and M_beta=e^{-beta L}K_beta for the conventional Ising bond tensor. Its one-site eigenvalues are 1+q and 1-q, both positive for beta>0. The matrix A_mu=diag(sqrt(mu)) M_beta diag(sqrt(mu)) is therefore positive definite for strictly positive mu. Its normalized second eigenvalue at the uniform law is (1-q)/(1+q)=tanh(beta), with no omitted volume factor.

For r=2^L mu and rho=tanh(beta), P_rho=M_beta/(1+q)^L is the row-normalized Boolean noise matrix. Thus
A_mu=2^{-L}(1+q)^L T_r,
T_r=M_sqrt(r) P_rho M_sqrt(r).
The change from counting-measure coordinates to L^2(u_L) multiplies all inner products by the same scalar and does not change these eigenvalues. This is not an identification of the weighting law with the stationary law.

The Perron Doob formula P(x,y)=A_mu(x,y)v_y/(lambda_1 v_x) has row sums one and stationary probabilities v_x^2 when v has Euclidean norm one. Detailed balance follows by symmetry. Its spectrum is precisely that of A_mu/lambda_1. In density sections, when r has zeros, the support block is entrywise strictly positive; the full eigenvalue list includes zeros outside support. The unit vector sqrt(r) belongs to L^2(u_L), and its Rayleigh quotient is <r,P_rho r> >= (E r)^2=1. These conventions are used consistently.

In the tilt arguments Q=P_sqrt(rho), A=M_sqrt(r) Q gives AA*=T_r and A*A=Q M_r Q. The equal spectra include multiplicities because the matrices are square of the same finite dimension. The separate rectangular Gram comparison doubles the auxiliary dimension and adds exactly N zero eigenvalues; the two leading positive eigenvalues still transfer. Confusing these two identifications would invalidate the argument, but the manuscript does not do so.

PROOF-BY-PROOF FINDINGS

Theorem 1, Lemma 2 and Proposition 3 (pages 2-4).
The random k by L matrix proof handles full row rank as part of the primal bad event: a nonzero message sent to zero has weight below delta L. For each fixed nonzero message z, zG is uniform; for each nonzero w, Gw is uniform. The union bounds give the two displayed entropy terms. Inclusive binomial sums dominate the strict weight event, so noninteger delta L is harmless. The entropy bound using z=p/(1-p)<1 has the correct direction. A dependence among selected columns is exactly a dual word supported on those coordinates, so the projection statement is justified. The ceiling k=ceil(RL) changes the exponent by a bounded amount, and both strict entropy inequalities make the gate hold eventually.

For the positive mixture, each code point has a >= 1/(2m). The off-diagonal row norm of M_CC is <=s; interlacing controls lambda_m of the full matrix. The complement block is positive semidefinite and has trace at most eta; the rectangular block has squared Frobenius norm at most eta, since every entry of M_beta is at most one. The perturbation bound sqrt(eta)+eta is valid without an exponential dimension loss. Subtracting the lower eigenvalue bound and dividing by lambda_1>=a gives 2s+2m(sqrt(eta)+eta), even when 1-s is negative. No silent small-s assumption is needed.

With delta=1/4, the stated R is positive and strictly below 1-h(1/4). gamma=beta/2-R log 2 >= beta/4. The factor from ceil(RL) is at most two. The primal contribution is at most 4 exp(-gamma L); eta=exp(-2 beta L)/m^2 contributes at most 3 exp(-beta L), hence at most 3 exp(-gamma L). This verifies the constant 7. The assertion is for all sufficiently large L, not every L. The eigenvalue count m-1 is correct, with multiplicities; no equality of those eigenvalues is implied.

Lemma 4 and completion of Theorem 1 (page 5).
Code translations preserve the mixture and the transfer matrix. Uniqueness of the positive normalized Perron vector forces it to be invariant on code cosets. A surjective code projection lifts every coordinate translation, making each required stationary marginal uniform. This is a real addition to matching the input weight's marginals. It avoids the false shortcut pi=mu.

Proposition 5 (pages 5-6).
The similarity to P_rho D_t^2 is legitimate because |t|<1 makes D_t invertible. Multiplication by the full parity character exchanges S and S^c, producing the displayed, generally nonsymmetric coefficient block. The determinant is (1-t^2)rho^L, independent of S. There are 2^{L-1} unordered complement pairs; no block is missing and no self-complement set occurs. The top upper root is at s=0; among proper pairs it is at s=1. The lower root of the empty/full block is <=rho^L<rho for L>=2, while the s=1 upper root is >=rho. This correctly identifies lambda_2 even at t=0, where degeneracies occur. Comparing the upper-root-to-trace function proves the ratio bound; maximizing rho(1+b)/(1+rho^2 b) over b<=1 gives tanh(2 beta). L=2 and t approaching 1 establish sharpness as a supremum, not an attained positive-law maximum.

Corollary 6 (page 6).
The literal factorization through labelled marginals is explicit. Since the constructed pair (mu,pi) and the uniform pair have identical input tuples, any universally valid lower bound at that tuple is at most the constructed gap. The corollary does not say that the product gap is small or that every marginal functional vanishes. It properly excludes full conditionals and additional structural information.

Theorem 7 and Corollary 8 (pages 7-8).
The ordinary hypercontractive exponent q*=1+rho^{-2} is correctly paired with P_rho:L^2 -> L^{q*}. For the reverse inequality, p=1-rho and p'=p/(p-1) yield the Boolean time threshold -log rho. I checked this against equations (1.1)-(1.2) in Mossel-Oleszkiewicz-Sen, including the one-half factor specific to the uniform Boolean case. Reverse Holder then gives <f,P_rho g> >= ||f||_p ||g||_p. The passage from positive functions to functions with zeros is valid in finite dimension, without evaluating a negative moment at a zero function value.

The sign parts of a real second eigenvector are legitimate because, in the only relevant case lambda_2>=lambda_1/2, lambda_2>0 and the eigenvector is supported where r>0. Positivity yields lambda_2 h_+ <=T_r h_+ and the corresponding inequality for h_-. Multiplication by sqrt(r), not division by r, is used. Interpolation at p<1 is justified through positive Holder weights, not a nonexistent norm triangle inequality. The exponent simplifies to (1+rho^2)/(1-rho)^2. The two-dimensional compression has eigenvalues lambda_2 and lambda_2+d/(st), so the variational lower bound on the gap is correct. The constant c(B,rho) is conservative but positive. The one-point support case and the omitted gap>1/2 case are covered. Corollary 8 is a correct rearrangement; necessary maximum-density growth is not claimed sufficient.

Theorem 9 (pages 8-9).
Writing A=1+rho, p=1-rho, t=1+1/rho, Q=st/(s+t), the condition Q>A is exactly s>(1+rho)/(1-rho). Both forward hypercontractive uses have the right noise parameters. The Rayleigh upper bound uses A_0=2s/(s+1)>A and Holder, giving lambda_1<=B without a pointwise cap. From lambda_2 f<=r P_rho f one gets ||f||_Q<=(B/lambda_2)||f||_A. The sign-part Rayleigh lower bound supplies ||f||_A>=sqrt(lambda_2); interpolation and reverse hypercontractivity give the cross entry. The exponent is indeed
2 rho s / [(1-rho)(s(1-rho)-1-rho)].
In the near-top case 2lambda_2/lambda_1>=1, so the final constant (2B)^{-2e} has no missing B^{-1}. The min with 1/2 handles the other case. This proof genuinely establishes uniform gap control for the whole density class, not merely bounded top norm.

Theorem 10 (pages 9-10).
The product tilt has L^s norm one and mean m^L, with n>1. Minkowski and Z_L<=1+2epsilon=B give the moment and lower-density bounds for EVERY L>=1. The one-site matrices H_+ and H_- are the actual Q M_w Q matrices in the orthonormal Walsh basis. The second derivative of log(kappa) at zero is 2rho/(1-rho)-(s-1), positive precisely in the stated strict subcritical interval. Smoothness supplies a fixed small positive delta with kappa>1.

The inequality omega<kappa is justified: equality of the product norm would force a common top eigenvector of H_+ and H_-, but their difference is invertible. Tensorization gives the cross-block norm omega^L. The auxiliary Gram matrix's block diagonal has two leading eigenvalues kappa^L, so its perturbation yields both a top upper bound and a second-eigenvalue lower bound. Positivity of X+Y and N>=2 make the positive-spectrum transfer valid, including L=1. Adding P_rho contributes at most one to the upper bound and cannot lower lambda_2. The lower bound lambda_1>=epsilon kappa^L completes the normalized-gap bound. This is more than top-eigenvalue growth: the proof specifically establishes a second near-top mode. It makes no marginal-matching claim for these tilts, correctly.

Theorem 11 (pages 11-12).
At s=s_c, q=2s/(s-1) obeys rho=1/(q-1), hence Q=P_sqrt(rho) is a norm-one L^2 -> L^q map. Holder with s,q,q gives ||Q M_{r-1}Q||<=||r-1||_s. Weyl plus lambda_1>=1 then bounds the ratio, not just an additive gap.
For s>=2, phi_s(t)>=|t-1|^s holds on both sides of 1 by the given derivative/integral arguments. For 1<s<2, the negative power in the Taylor remainder is bounded below using t+1, with the correct inequality direction. Holder applied to W=(r-1)^2/(r+1)^{2-s} produces the stated d_s(B). Continuity handles t=0. The resulting sufficient near-one interval is nonempty. No conclusion is claimed when the bound reduces to zero.

Theorem 12 (pages 12-13).
The formulas for kappa and tau^2 follow from the actual two-by-two matrices; tau^2 is the top eigenvalue of D^{1/2}H D^{1/2}. At criticality log(n) has quadratic coefficient rho/(1-rho), log(kappa) has zero quadratic coefficient, and log(tau) has coefficient -rho/[2(1-rho^2)]. Even analyticity near zero justifies uniform fourth-order remainder bounds on a fixed neighborhood. This is an analytic existence proof, not an uncertified numerical Taylor fit.

Choosing d<=delta_* with C d^4<=log(B-1) is possible for every B>2. For delta_L=d L^{-1/3}, |log(a_L)|<=C d^4 L^{-1/3}<=log(B-1) for every L>=1. Thus a_L<=B-1, Z_L<=B, the density floor and moment budget hold for every L, and a_L tends to one. No large-L assertion is silently substituted for the all-L bounds.

The balancing a_L=kappa(delta_L)^{-L} makes both summands' leading eigenvalues exactly one. Their cross-block norm is sqrt(a_L)tau(delta_L)^L, with the claimed stretched-exponential estimate. The Gram perturbation gives lambda_1<=1+eta_L and lambda_2>=1-eta_L, also valid when the latter lower bound is negative. Since lambda_1>=1, subtracting and dividing gives gap<=2eta_L. Positivity supplies the strict finite-L lower inequality. The critical theorem is valid as stated; it does not prove a critical budget of 2 is necessary.

A useful nonfatal limitation is that the exponent 1/3 is not optimal even from the estimates already printed. Analytically, replacing delta_L by d L^{-1/4} keeps |log a_L|<=C d^4<=log(B-1), so all-L budgets and the same Gram argument give a bound with exp(-c d^2 L^{1/2}). It no longer supplies a_L->1 from that bound alone, but that convergence is unnecessary for the gap argument. This observation does not change the assessed theorem or the verdict; it supports describing the displayed rate as a convenient sufficient rate rather than a sharp critical law. Theorem 1.1 is an equivalence involving a tail norm and the invariant Markov operator's symmetrization. The current T_r is not Markov in u_L, and the Doob stationary measure is different. 6. Gross, Invariance of intrinsic hypercontractivity under perturbation of Schrodinger operators, arXiv:2412.20282v2.
https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20282v2
Theorem 2.2 assumes a Riemannian probability-space LSI and specified moments of e^V and e^{-V}; it supplies dimension-independent ground-state gap bounds. Those hypotheses concern an additive operator. No identification with the logarithm of the present discrete transfer congruence was established. The manuscript correctly acknowledges that its lower-density-bounded counterexamples also have inverse moments, so a missing inverse moment cannot distinguish those examples from Gross by itself.

7. Gluck, Spectral gaps for hyperbounded operators, arXiv:1802.09422v6.

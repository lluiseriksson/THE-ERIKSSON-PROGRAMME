CRITICAL DENSITY MOMENTS: SMALL-BUDGET STABILITY AND BALANCED-TILT COLLAPSE
Author-side analytical source, 2026-11-13. No formal or numerical verification.
Gates fixed in judges298.txt at c4f88b14. No claim of external originality.

SETUP. Fix 0<rho<1 and s=(1+rho)/(1-rho)>1. On {-1,1}^L with uniform
probability mu_L, P_rho is the product noise operator: its one-bit Walsh matrix
is D=diag(1,rho). For r>=0, E r=1, define
  T_r=M_sqrt(r) P_rho M_sqrt(r),  gap(T_r)=1-lambda_2(T_r)/lambda_1(T_r),
where eigenvalues are in decreasing order, with multiplicities. L>=1, so the
dimension is at least2. Put Q=P_sqrt(rho). The square matrices A=M_sqrt(r)Q
give T_r=AA* and S_r=A*A=Q M_r Q with identical eigenvalues, including zeros.
Both are positive semidefinite; lambda_1(S_r)>=<1,S_r1>=E r=1.

THEOREM A (small critical budget). If ||r||_s<=B, B>=1, then
  gap(T_r) >= max(0,1-rho-d_s(B)),
where
 d_s(B)=(B^s-1)^(1/s)                                    for s>=2,
 d_s(B)=[2(B^s-1)/(s(s-1))]^(1/2) (B+1)^((2-s)/2)       for 1<s<2.
In particular, for each fixed rho there is B_0>1 such that 1<=B<B_0
forces a strictly positive dimension-free gap. No pointwise upper/lower
density bound is required. This is a sufficient, not optimal, budget criterion.

Proof. The classical Boolean hypercontractive inequality gives
 ||Qf||_q<=||f||_2, q=2s/(s-1), because rho=(s-1)/(s+1)=1/(q-1).
This is the endpoint of that classical inequality, not a new inequality.
For f,g, Holder with exponents s,q,q (1/s+2/q=1) gives
 |<f,Q M_(r-1) Qg>| <= ||r-1||_s ||Qf||_q ||Qg||_q
                    <= ||r-1||_s ||f||_2 ||g||_2.
Thus ||S_r-P_rho||op<=||r-1||_s. Since lambda_2(P_rho)=rho,
the variational eigenvalue bound gives lambda_2(S_r)<=rho+||r-1||_s.
Use lambda_1(S_r)>=1 and positive semidefiniteness to obtain the asserted
gap once ||r-1||_s<=d_s(B) is proved. If the displayed lower bound is
negative, the trivial nonnegative gap gives the stated maximum.

Here is a direct scalar proof of that norm estimate. Let
 phi_s(t)=t^s-1-s(t-1), t>=0. E phi_s(r)=E r^s-1<=B^s-1.
For s>=2 and t=1+u>=1,
 phi_s(1+u)=s integral_0^u [(1+v)^(s-1)-1]dv >=u^s,
since (1+v)^a>=1+v^a for a=s-1>=1. This last inequality follows by
differentiating in v, starting at v=0. For t=1-u in[0,1], differentiate
 phi_s(1-u)-u^s: its derivative is
 s[1-(1-u)^(s-1)-u^(s-1)]>=0 because x^(s-1)<=x on[0,1].
The difference vanishes at u=0, so phi_s(t)>=|t-1|^s on the whole half-line.
Integration proves the s>=2 formula, including s=2.
For 1<s<2, Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives
 phi_s(t)=s(s-1)(t-1)^2 integral_0^1 (1-v)[1+v(t-1)]^(s-2)dv
          >=[s(s-1)/2] (t-1)^2/(t+1)^(2-s).
The formula extends to t=0 by continuity/integrability since s>1; the
inequality uses 1+v(t-1)<=1+t and s-2<0.
Set W=(r-1)^2/(r+1)^(2-s). Then E W<=2(B^s-1)/(s(s-1)). Holder with
conjugate exponents 2/s and 2/(2-s) yields
 E|r-1|^s <=(E W)^(s/2) [E(r+1)^s]^((2-s)/2).
Take the s-th root and use ||r+1||_s<=B+1. This is the second formula.
Both formulas tend to0 as B decreases to1, proving the last assertion. QED.

THEOREM B (large critical budget). For each fixed rho and every fixed B>2,
there exist strictly positive mean-one densities r_L, for all integers L>=1,
such that ||r_L||_s<=B, r_L>=1/B and
  0<gap(T_rL)<=2 sqrt(B-1) exp(-c d^2 L^(1/3)).
Here d,c>0 are fixed constants depending on rho,B, not L. The assertion
does not cover every B>1 and does not identify the optimal critical budget.

Proof. For a real tilt |delta|<1, define
 n(delta)=[((1+delta)^s+(1-delta)^s)/2]^(1/s),
 w_delta(x)=(1+delta*x)/n(delta), x in{-1,1}, m(delta)=1/n(delta),
 H(delta)=n(delta)^(-1) [[1,delta sqrt(rho)],[delta sqrt(rho),rho]].
All these matrices are positive definite. The top eigenvalue of H is
 kappa(delta)=[1+rho+sqrt((1-rho)^2+4rho delta^2)]/[2n(delta)].
Define also tau(delta)=||D^(1/2) H(delta)^(1/2)||op. Its square is the largest
eigenvalue of D^(1/2) H(delta) D^(1/2), since AB and BA have the same
nonzero eigenvalues. Consequently
 tau(delta)^2=
 [1+rho^2+sqrt((1-rho^2)^2+4rho^2 delta^2)]/[2n(delta)].
The two strictly positive radicands at zero, and positivity of the powers,
make log n, log kappa and log tau even real analytic near zero.

The following expansions are analytic Taylor formulas, with a bounded fourth
derivative remainder on a sufficiently small closed interval, not numerical
fits or symbolic-oracle output:
 log n(delta)=(s-1)delta^2/2+O(delta^4)
             =rho delta^2/(1-rho)+O(delta^4),
 log kappa(delta)=[rho/(1-rho)-(s-1)/2]delta^2+O(delta^4)=O(delta^4),
 log tau(delta)=1/2 [rho^2/(1-rho^2)-(s-1)/2]delta^2+O(delta^4)
               =-rho delta^2/[2(1-rho^2)]+O(delta^4).
For clarity, the numerator divided by2 in kappa is
 1+rho delta^2/(1-rho)+O(delta^4), and that in tau^2 is
 1+rho^2 delta^2/(1-rho^2)+O(delta^4), which verifies the coefficients.
It follows that there are delta_* in(0,1), C>=1 and c>0 (for instance
c=rho/[4(1-rho^2)] after reducing delta_*) such that for |delta|<=delta_*:
 |log kappa(delta)|<=C delta^4,   log tau(delta)<=-c delta^2.
Existence of C follows from continuity of the fourth derivative and vanishing
constant, linear, quadratic and cubic terms of log kappa. The strict negative
quadratic coefficient gives the second estimate. No sign assumption on the
quartic term of log kappa is needed.

Choose once and for all 0<d<=delta_* with C d^4<=log(B-1), possible since B>2.
Set delta_L=d L^(-1/3), kappa_L=kappa(delta_L), m_L=m(delta_L), and
 a_L=kappa_L^(-L), R_L(x)=product_(i=1)^L w_deltaL(x_i),
 Z_L=1+a_L m_L^L, r_L=(1+a_L R_L)/Z_L.
The density is strictly positive and E r_L=1. Product measure gives
||R_L||_s=1, E R_L=m_L^L, and strict convexity gives 0<m_L<1.
The Taylor bound gives
 |log a_L|<=C d^4 L^(-1/3)<=log(B-1),
so a_L<=B-1 and a_L tends to1. Thus
 ||r_L||_s <=(1+a_L)/Z_L<=B,  Z_L<=1+a_L<=B,  r_L>=1/B.
This establishes all constraints for every L>=1 with one fixed moment budget.

The transfer spectrum equals that of S_rL as above, and tensorization gives
 F_L=Z_L S_rL=P_rho+a_L X_L,  X_L=H(delta_L)^(tensor L).
The two blocks P_rho and a_L X_L each have top eigenvalue exactly1, the
second by definition of a_L. Form the N-by-2N matrix
 C_L=[P_rho^(1/2), sqrt(a_L) X_L^(1/2)], N=2^L.
Then C_L C_L*=F_L and C_L* C_L is the block matrix with those two diagonal
blocks and cross block sqrt(a_L) P_rho^(1/2)X_L^(1/2). Its norm is
 eta_L=sqrt(a_L) tau(delta_L)^L
      <=sqrt(B-1) exp(-c d^2 L^(1/3)).
The block diagonal has its two largest eigenvalues exactly1; none exceed1.
The off-diagonal selfadjoint perturbation has norm eta_L, by squaring that
block matrix. Hence lambda_1(C_L*C_L)<=1+eta_L and
lambda_2(C_L*C_L)>=1-eta_L. The N positive eigenvalues of C_L C_L* equal
the N positive eigenvalues of C_L*C_L with multiplicity, and the latter has
N additional zero eigenvalues. Since F_L is positive definite and N>=2,
the first two eigenvalues have the same labels in both matrices, even when
the lower estimate 1-eta_L is negative. Also F_L>=P_rho gives lambda_1(F_L)>=1.
It follows that gap(F_L)<=2eta_L. Scalar normalization and AA*/A*A preserve
the eigenvalue ratio. T_rL is strictly entrywise positive in the configuration
basis, since 0<rho<1 and r_L>0, so Perron simplicity gives gap(T_rL)>0. QED.

INTERPRETATION AND BOUNDARIES.
Published297 v2 proves the supercritical uniform gap for every finite moment
budget and subcritical collapse for every B>1. Theorem A and B here distinguish
small and large budgets at the previously unresolved critical exponent.
They do NOT determine the intermediate critical budgets, the optimal boundary,
or the boundary value itself. Neither theorem imposes or concludes matching
low-order marginals. Density is measured against the uniform cube reference,
not the Perron stationary law. Logarithmic weights may be nonlocal. The
normalized transfer gap is not a heat-bath gap or a physical Yang-Mills gap.
The exponent s_c and the ordinary hypercontractivity used in A are classical.
The transfer congruence/product Gram machinery comes from the cited programme
sources. Priority for the balanced finite-dimensional construction is not
established. This is an analytical candidate requiring separate audit before
manuscript integration or publication; there is no new PDF assessment here.

PRE-AUDIT DEVELOPMENT HISTORY (not independent failed audit gates).
The first endpoint idea retained both antipodal tilts and epsilon>1, and used
a three-block Gram matrix including the uniform channel; it would suffice
for B>3. The direct published297 estimate with a fixed uniform additive error
does NOT prove endpoint collapse because kappa(delta_L)^L tends to1. The
present argument removes that nonvanishing error by including the uniform
channel in the Gram comparison and balancing one tilted channel against it.
An unbalanced single tilt with a_L=1 would only yield a polynomial remainder
from 1-kappa(delta_L)^L; choosing a_L=kappa_L^(-L) avoids it. These preliminary
routes were not submitted to an auditor or represented as certified results.
No computation was run. Possible improvements to the B>2 threshold are left
outside the registered target; they are not claims or additional active tasks.

The unnormalized numerator eigenvalue for H is 1+rho delta^2/(1-rho)+O_rho(delta^4); log n has the same quadratic coefficient because (s-1)/2=rho/(1-rho). Thus log kappa=O_rho(delta^4). The analogous numerator for tau^2 has coefficient rho^2/(1-rho^2); subtracting log n gives log tau^2=-rho delta^2/(1-rho^2)+O_rho(delta^4). Halving gives line38. Therefore c=exp(-L log kappa)->1 and eta=sqrt(c)tau^L->0, with log eta=-rho u/[2(1-rho^2)]+O_rho(u^2/L).
For precision let A=P_rho and B=c H^(tensor L), both on an N=2^L dimensional space. Their eigenvalues are nonnegative and their largest eigenvalues are exactly one. The block diagonal top two eigenvalues are exactly 1,1 regardless of the rest of either spectrum. Min-max perturbation puts the two largest Gram eigenvalues in [1-eta,1+eta]. Dividing F by Z->1 proves the assertion for S_v. This closes the potentially dangerous rank/dimension issue without any unproved spectral separation among lower modes.

C10 -- TWO CASES AND ALTERNATION: PASS
Locations 71-76. Balance gives u->infinity and u/sqrt L->0, with no lower rate beyond divergence. If u>=L^(1/4), then (1/2)log u>=(1/8)log L and log(log L/epsilon) is eventually positive. Using j>=epsilon sqrt L/(2log L) gives the positive term at least epsilon sqrt L/16. Subtracting C_s u leaves a quantity tending to infinity because u=o(sqrt L).
If u<L^(1/4), eventual u>=1 lets the log u term be discarded; -C_s u>=-C_s L^(1/4). The positive term is at least epsilon sqrt L log(log L/epsilon)/(2log L). Divided by L^(1/4), it tends to infinity. Thus this lower bound also diverges. For any fixed target, both bounds exceed it for all sufficiently large eligible indices in their respective cases. Their minimum therefore diverges, establishing divergence on the whole selected subsequence even if the cases alternate infinitely often. Extremely slow u, such as iterated-log growth, creates no missing case.

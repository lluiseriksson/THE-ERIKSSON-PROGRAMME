1. Definitions and claims
Fix0<rho<1 and s=(1+rho)/(1-rho)>1. Each density r_L>=0 has mean one on the uniform Boolean L-cube, L>=1. Let T_r=M_sqrt(r) P_rho M_sqrt(r), whose ordered eigenvalues include multiplicities and zeros; gap(T)=1-lambda2/lambda1. Define
 H(K)=sup_{L>=1} E[r_L^s 1_{r_L>K}],
 Theta=lim_{K->infinity} H(K)=inf_{K>=1}H(K) in[0,infinity]. (1)
H is nonnegative and nonincreasing, so this extended-real limit always exists. Theta=0 is uniform integrability of the critical powers. No common coupling of the probability spaces is needed.

Theorem A (finite cutoff). Suppose K>=1 and0<=tau<1 satisfy E[r^s 1_{r>K}]<=tau. Set
 t=tau^(1/(2s)), m=(1+t)/2, d=(1-t)/2,
 B_K=(K^(s-1)+tau)^(1/s).
Then
 gap(T_r)>=min{1-m^2, 2 d^(2s^2) K^(1-s^2)/B_K}>0. (2)
Constants need not be optimal. They depend on rho,K,tau, not dimension or r.

Theorem B (sharp worst-case tail boundary). Theta<1 implies inf_L gap(T_{r_L})>0. The value1 cannot be included in this universal guarantee: the exact299 family has Theta=1 and gap tending to zero, with any prescribed fixed positive floor below one. On the other hand, for every finite Theta0>0 there are stable families with Theta=Theta0, gap tending to1-rho and a common lower gap (1-rho)/2. Thus Theta>=1 is necessary for a collapsing family, but is not sufficient for collapse.

Comparison consequence. If sup_L ||r_L||s<=B<infinity, then
 Theta<=B^s-1. (3)
For B<2^(1/s), Theorem B recovers a positive gap from this estimate. The direct theorem299 already gives the better explicit bound1-B/2^(1/s); (3) is a comparison, not an independent priority claim.

2. Proof of the finite-cutoff theorem
Put Q=P_sqrt(rho), p=1+rho, a=1-rho, theta=1/s^2. The standard forward/reverse Boolean inequalities in the normalization above are
 ||Qf||2<=||f||p,
 <f,P_rho g> >=||f||a ||g||a for f,g>=0. (4)
As in the audited source300, the reverse inequality for functions with zeros follows by adding positive constants and taking a limit on the finite cube. The power-Holder interpolation formula is
 ||g||p<=||g||a^theta ||g||2^(1-theta),
 1/p=theta/a+(1-theta)/2. (5)
Its proof applies ordinary Holder to powers; it does not use a false quasi-norm triangle inequality.
For w>=0 and ||h||2=1, Holder with s=p/(2-p) and (4) imply
 ||Q sqrt(w)h||2^2<=||w||s. (6)
Hence lambda1(T_r)<=||r||s<=B_K, because
 E r^s<=K^(s-1) E[r1_{r<=K}]+tau<=K^(s-1)+tau.
Also lambda1>=1: sqrt(r) is a unit vector and <r,P_rho r>>=(Er)^2=1 by the nonnegative Walsh spectrum. All eigenvalues of T_r are nonnegative.

The active support {r>0} has a strictly positive kernel and a simple positive Perron eigenvector. If it has rank-one support, lambda2=0 and gap=1. More generally if lambda2<m^2, then lambda1>=1 gives gap>1-m^2. Otherwise lambda2>=m^2>0; choose a real second eigenvector orthogonal to the Perron vector, and write
 h=u h_+-v h_-, u,v>0, u^2+v^2=1,
where h_+,h_- are disjointly supported nonnegative unit vectors on the active support. No balance under the uniform measure is assumed. Set D=<h_+,T_r h_->>0 and A_+/-=<h_+/-,T_r h_+/->. The eigenvector equations give
 A_+=lambda2+(v/u)D, A_-=lambda2+(u/v)D. (7)
For either sign let f=sqrt(r)h_sign and split it into f_low at r<=K and f_high at r>K. Equations (6)-(7) imply
 ||Qf||2>=sqrt(lambda2)>=m,
 ||Qf_high||2<=||r1_{r>K}||s^(1/2)<=tau^(1/(2s))=t.
The ordinary L2 triangle inequality gives ||Qf_low||2>=m-t=d>0. By (4), ||f_low||p>=d, while ||f_low||2<=sqrt(K). Applying (5) and dividing by positive quantities yields, for each sign,
 ||f_low||a>=d^(1/theta) K^(-(1-theta)/(2theta)). (8)
Positivity and the reverse inequality in (4) therefore give
 D>=<f_{+,low},P_rho f_{-,low}>
   >=d^(2/theta)K^(-(1-theta)/theta)
    =d^(2s^2)K^(1-s^2). (9)
The compression of T_r to span(h_+,h_-) has one eigenvalue lambda2 by (7); its other eigenvalue is lambda2+D/(uv). The largest Rayleigh quotient in that subspace is at most lambda1, so lambda1-lambda2>=D/(uv)>=2D. Divide by lambda1<=B_K. Combined with the earlier alternative, this proves (2). Repeated second eigenvalues are harmless. At tau=0, t=0, m=d=1/2, so there is no singular endpoint in the definitions. At tau=1, d=0 and the argument deliberately ceases to give a positive bound.

3. From finite cutoff to Theta<1
If Theta<1, choose a number tau with Theta<tau<1. By the definition as an infimum in (1), some finite K>=1 satisfies H(K)<=tau. Theorem A then applies to every L with exactly the same constants. This also supplies a finite uniform total critical-moment bound B_K; a separate total-moment assumption is not needed. The selected K cannot depend on L. The contrapositive says inf_L gap=0 implies Theta>=1. It does not assert that every family with Theta>=1 collapses.

4. Escaped-moment identity and sharpness
Here is the limit/supremum fact needed to use the audited examples. Suppose, on finite cubes, r_L->1 in probability and E r_L^s->M<infinity. For each fixed K>1, F_K(x)=x^s1_{x<=K} is bounded and continuous at1. Thus E F_K(r_L)->1. This can be seen directly by splitting a neighborhood of1 from its probability-small complement, so no common probability-space coupling or unproved dominated-convergence premise is required. It follows that
 E[r_L^s1_{r_L>K}]->M-1. (10)
Hence H(K)>=M-1 for every K>1, and Theta>=M-1.
For the opposite inequality fix epsilon>0 and a finite K0>1. Choose L0 such that for every L>=L0,
 E r_L^s<=M+epsilon,
 E F_{K0}(r_L)>=1-epsilon.
For every K>=K0 the corresponding tail moment is therefore at most M-1+2epsilon for L>=L0. The finite prefix L<L0 consists of finitely many finite functions, so choosing K above all of their pointwise maxima makes its tail moments zero. It follows that Theta<=M-1+2epsilon. Letting epsilon down to zero proves
 Theta=M-1. (11)
This explicitly handles the supremum before the cutoff limit; the fixed-K limit alone would not establish equality.

The exact-budget family at source49705281f05ab18b71bd9ebc2da1985a0433340c, analysed in the audited300 source14f6c1afda58d925cdf1481ecb7791d0ade9395b, has r_L->1 in probability, E r_L^s->2, and gap tending to zero. Its finite-prefix adjustment and arbitrary prescribed floor below one are part of that actual construction. Equation (11) gives Theta=1 exactly. This establishes the sharpness boundary in Theorem B using the same source-bound family, not a newly assumed counterexample.
For any Theta0>0, source301 at6bc50cc52599b0df915895458002a3245e78e343 supplies the stable one-spike family with M=1+Theta0, any chosen strict norm slack B>M^(1/s), and any fixed floor0<b<1. It has r_L->1 in probability, E r_L^s->M, and the stated positive limiting and uniform gaps. Applying (11) gives Theta=Theta0. These stable examples refute sufficiency of Theta>=1 for collapse and also show that Theta<1 genuinely allows failure of UI when0<Theta0<1. No exact total-budget equality is imported from301.

5. Comparison with a total moment budget
Suppose E r_L^s<=B^s for all L; necessarily B>=1 by Jensen. For K>=1,
 E[r_L1_{r_L>K}]<=K^(1-s) E[r_L^s1_{r_L>K}]<=B^s K^(1-s).
Write X_L=r_L1_{r_L<=K}. Then
 E X_L>=(1-B^s K^(1-s))_+,
 E X_L^s>=(E X_L)^s>=(1-B^s K^(1-s))_+^s
by Jensen on the full probability space, including the zero values. Subtracting from E r_L^s gives the uniform estimate
 H(K)<=B^s-(1-B^s K^(1-s))_+^s.
Taking K to infinity proves (3). This derivation does not equate base-density tails with Perron stationary tails or assume the density converges in probability.

6. Scope, novelty and operational boundary
This is a quantitative refinement of the audited sign-truncation argument300: its numerical cutoff8^(-s) is relaxed to every tau<1, and the largest universal asymptotic threshold is identified by the already audited299 example. Uniform integrability remains sufficient but is not required; source301 already supplied a direct nonnecessity example. A tail hypothesis below one remains essential to this particular universal guarantee. No unproved integrability fact is packaged into a structure or asserted for Yang-Mills.
Forward/reverse hypercontractivity, positive-kernel sign compression, Jensen and the escaped-moment identity are classical. Mossel--Oleszkiewicz--Sen (arXiv:1108.1210v5) supplies the inequality conventions. Wang (arXiv:1305.4460v6, abstract and Theorem1.1) concerns an invariant Markov operator and an operator-tail functional optimized over unit L2 inputs; this note's Theta instead concerns base-density powers across a family. No unproved change-of-measure equivalence, novelty from different notation, or contradiction of Wang's criterion is claimed. Miclo's Section6/Proposition11 norm-budget threshold2 is for the fourth power of an L2-to-L4 norm, not a raw density norm. External priority of this specialized sharp tail boundary is unresolved.
The positive gap bound is not optimized. rho is fixed, not allowed to approach0 or1 uniformly. The total-budget small-gap bound in299 is not improved.

\author{Lluis Eriksson}

\begin{abstract}
A formal development of a two-dimensional spatial transfer kernel proves its
uniform spectral statements by Schur's test on \emph{constant} row sums, and
carries a theorem, labelled \textsc{the obstruction} in its own source, stating
that those row sums stop being constant the moment the spatial coupling is
switched on.  That theorem obstructs the \emph{method}.  It was read as
obstructing the \emph{conclusion}, and the reading outlived its own retraction.

We prove, in Lean~4 with no \texttt{sorry} and no project axiom, a chain of
six headline theorems, plus the star-cell specialisation they compose to.  \textbf{First}, the one-bond influence envelope: flipping one
neighbour across a bond of strength $J\ge0$ moves a two-state site's conditional
by at most $\tanh J$, and by exactly $\tanh J$ at one field, so $\tanh J$ is the
\emph{least field-uniform} bound.  It is a field-uniform interdependence
majorant and need not equal the intrinsic coefficient of a given finite
model, whose reachable fields form
a discrete set --- a distinction Section~2 makes precisely, and both of whose
sides occur: equality is proved below for the one-bond cell.  \textbf{Second},
those envelopes assembled into a matrix discharge every \emph{matrix-side}
hypothesis of the third (the two conditions on the ambient distance remain
hypotheses), and turn a purely \emph{local} covering condition into the
remaining row-sum bound holding at every site of every volume --- which is the
coupling window, and which we compute: at a site carrying two bonds of strength
$\beta$ and two of strength $\gamma$ it is exactly
$2\tanh|\beta|+2\tanh|\gamma|$, so the window is the hypothesis of a theorem
rather than a sentence.  \textbf{Third}, the
resolvent bound: for a nonnegative matrix $C$ over a finite index type, supported
on pairs at distance at most one and with row sums \emph{bounded} by $\alpha<1$,
without any constancy assumption,
\[
  \sum_{n<N}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\;\le\;\frac{\alpha^{\dist(i,j)}}{1-\alpha}
  \qquad\text{for every }N,
\]
with no reference of any kind to the cardinality of the index type.
\textbf{Fourth}, \emph{Dobrushin's comparison estimate itself}, which an
earlier version of this paper named as its one open analytic step: for a
probability weight $\mu$ on a finite product space
$S^{\iota}$, invariant under a family of single-site conditionals whose
influence is dominated by $C$,
\[
  \bigl|\mathrm{Cov}_\mu(f,g)\bigr|\;\le\;
  \tfrac14\sum_{i,j}\delta_i(f)\Bigl(\sum_{n}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\Bigr)\delta_j(g),
\]
where $\delta_i$ is oscillation in the single coordinate $i$; composing with
the third theorem replaces the inner series by $\alpha^{\dist(i,j)}/(1-\alpha)$
and yields exponential decay of connected correlations at a rate read off
single-site data.  The constant $\tfrac14$ is \emph{attained}, on a witness
inside the kernel.  \textbf{Fifth}, the instantiation at an arbitrary weight: from a strictly
positive weight $w$ on $S^{\iota}$
\emph{alone} we construct the measure $\mu_w=w/Z$ and its heat-bath kernel,
\emph{prove} that $\mu_w$ is invariant --- by an involution on
$S^{\iota}\times S$, so that step is an identity rather than an estimate ---
and \emph{prove} that the intrinsic Dobrushin matrix $c^w$ has vanishing
diagonal and dominates the influence.  Every hypothesis of the fourth theorem
is thereby discharged except one: $\sum_k c^w_{ik}\le\alpha<1$, Dobrushin's
condition itself, which is a condition on the interaction and not on the
machinery.  (The \emph{geometric} form of the decay consumes one further
hypothesis, stated where it is used: that $c^w$ has range one for a distance
obeying the two metric conditions.  The resolvent form needs nothing else.)
\textbf{Sixth}, the interaction itself: for the \emph{Ising} weight of a
symmetric zero-diagonal coupling $J$ we prove that the heat-bath conditional
is the sigmoid $(1+\tanh h)/2$ at the local field
$h_i=\sum_k J_{ik}\,\sigma_k$, and that the intrinsic matrix is dominated by
the envelope of the first theorem, $c^{w}_{ik}\le\tanh|J_{ik}|$ --- so every
hypothesis of the fifth theorem's endpoints becomes a computation on $J$.  On
the one-bond system the domination is an \emph{equality}: the optimal field is
reachable there, and the intrinsic coefficient \emph{is} $\tanh\beta$; that
system moreover satisfies Dobrushin's condition at every coupling, so its
decay bound carries no smallness hypothesis at all.  And on the star cell the
window $2\tanh|\beta|+2\tanh|\gamma|<1$ --- the second theorem's row-sum
computation --- becomes the hypothesis of an exponential-decay theorem about
the anisotropic Ising weight itself.
Each rung carries a non-vacuity witness, and every claimed endpoint is
oracled with exactly Lean's three standard axioms.

Separately, and by measurement rather than proof, we remove the wall from where
it was being placed.  For the coupled kernel the second-eigenvalue ratio
$\specRatio(L)$ saturates strictly below one in each of six cells tested inside
Onsager's disordered region --- with the spatial weight switched on --- and
reaches one to six decimal places in the two ordered cells tested.  The
control at zero spatial coupling reproduces the \emph{proved} identity
$\specRatio=\tanh\beta$ to six decimals, so the harness is calibrated against a
theorem rather than against itself.  These data are eight cells at $L\le12$ with
an extrapolation.  On the tested grid they \emph{contradict} the blanket
attribution of the degeneracy to the mere presence of the spatial weight; they
are not, and are not offered as, a refutation of an asymptotic claim, since a
sequence can appear to saturate at $L\le12$ and turn afterwards.  They do
\emph{not} locate the transition, which would need a dense sweep and
finite-size scaling that we do not attempt.

\textbf{What is not proved.}  The volume-uniform bound on $\specRatio$ for the
coupled kernel is \emph{not} established here: what is proved is decay of
correlations of the Gibbs weight, and the finite-time transport from that
decay into the operator formulation is the one open step named in the frontier
section.  The intrinsic Dobrushin condition is now a computation on the
coupling: it holds unconditionally for the one-bond system, reduces to the
window for the star cell, and remains unverified for lattice families beyond
those cells.  Dobrushin's uniqueness theorem and
his comparison estimate are classical \cite{Dobrushin1968,Dobrushin1970,%
Simon1993}; nothing in this paper is new mathematics.  What is offered is a
mechanised, non-vacuous chain from a single bond to exponential decay of
correlations, and measured counterevidence against an attribution.  No
consequence for Yang--Mills theory is stated, suggested, or implied.
\end{abstract}

\section{The setting, and the theorem that was read too widely}
\label{sec:reading}

Fix an extent $L$ and consider configurations $\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{L}$ of one
slice.  The development under discussion carries a time-bond kernel that
factorises over sites,
\[
  K_\beta(\sigma,\tau)\;=\;\prod_{j<L} b_\beta(\sigma_j,\tau_j),
  \qquad b_\beta(s,s')=e^{\pm\beta},
\]
and an arbitrary strictly positive slice weight $w$, the Gibbs weight of a
space-time configuration being the product of the slice weights and the time
bonds.  When $w$ is itself a product over spatial bonds with coupling $\gamma$,
the system is the anisotropic two-dimensional Ising model.

Two facts from that development matter here.

\begin{proposition}[proved elsewhere in the development]
\label{prop:decoupled}
At $\gamma=0$ and for every $L\ge1$, $\specRatio=\tanh\beta$ exactly.
\end{proposition}

The proof is Schur's test \cite{Schur1911}: the decoupled kernel has \emph{constant} row sums, and
the loss on the odd part of an observable is exactly compensated by the odd bond
eigenvalue at every step.

\begin{proposition}[\texttt{coupled\_rowSums\_not\_constant}]
\label{prop:obstruction}
At $\gamma>0$ the row sums of the coupled kernel are not constant: two explicit
configurations of a two-site slice have different row sums.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruction} is labelled \textsc{the obstruction} in the
source.  \emph{It obstructs Schur's test.}  It says nothing whatever about
$\specRatio$, it is proved on a two-site witness, and no statement quantified
over $L$ appears in it.

A stronger reading --- that the spatial weight destroys uniformity --- was
entered into the development and then \emph{retracted of record} as unproved,
leaving behind only a measurement of degeneracy.

\textbf{What is documented, and can be checked.}  The retraction of the sentence
from the manuscript is recorded at Addendum~561 of the development's
verification ledger; its removal from the module docstring, which the first
retraction had missed, at Addendum~563; and its survival in a hand-typed
submission form, caught only because the form was read in full before delivery,
at Addendum~568.  Three removals were required for one sentence, and the third
was the one that would have been published.

\textbf{What is inference, and is labelled as such.}  We observe that no
statement quantified over $L$ for the coupled kernel was attempted in the
interval, and we attribute this to the retracted reading continuing to govern
target selection.  That attribution is ours; it is not established by a
diff, and no artefact in the development records a decision not to attempt
something.  The verifiable half of the observation is the narrower one, and it
is enough for this paper: the guards that caught the sentence three times
compare identifiers and substrings inside files, so whatever else was carrying
the reading was not a file.

\section{The chain}

Three theorems, each machine-checked, composing into one statement.  The first
is about a single bond, the second assembles bonds into a matrix, the third is
about that matrix and never sees the model.

\subsection{The one-bond influence envelope, and that it is least among
field-uniform bounds}

A two-state site in a real field $h$ has conditional $P(+1)=(1+\tanh h)/2$,
written from the Boltzmann weights and nothing else.  Flipping one neighbour
across a bond of strength $J$ moves that field to $h-2J$.  Write
$\mathrm{tv}(h,h')=|P_h(+1)-P_{h'}(+1)|$.

\begin{theorem}[machine-checked]
\label{thm:coeff}
For every $J\ge0$,
\[
  \mathrm{tv}\bigl(h,\,h-2J\bigr)\;\le\;\tanh J \quad\text{for all }h,
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \mathrm{tv}\bigl(J,\,-J\bigr)\;=\;\tanh J .
\]
Hence $\tanh J$ is the \emph{least} upper bound of
$\{\mathrm{tv}(h,h-2J):h\in\R\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\tanh a-\tanh b=\sinh(a-b)/(\cosh a\cosh b)$, and
$\cosh a\cosh b=\bigl(\cosh(a+b)+\cosh(a-b)\bigr)/2\ge\bigl(1+\cosh(a-b)\bigr)/2$
since $\cosh\ge1$.  With $a=h$ and $b=h-2J$ the difference of the fields is
$2J$, so the denominator is at least $(1+\cosh 2J)/2=\cosh^2 J$ while the
numerator is $\sinh 2J=2\sinh J\cosh J$.  Dividing by two,
$\mathrm{tv}\le\sinh J\cosh J/\cosh^2 J=\tanh J$.  At $h=J$ the two fields are
$+J$ and $-J$, their sum is zero, $\cosh 0=1$, and every inequality above is an
equality.
\end{proof}

\textbf{What this is, stated carefully, because the obvious reading of it is
wrong.}  The supremum is taken over \emph{all} $h\in\R$.  In a concrete finite
system with the remaining couplings and any external field fixed, the effective
fields actually reachable at a site form a discrete subset of $\R$, and the value
$h=J$ at which equality holds need not correspond to any admissible boundary
condition of that system.  So the true Dobrushin coefficient
\[
  c_{ij}\;=\;\sup\bigl\{\|\mu_i(\cdot\mid\eta)-\mu_i(\cdot\mid\eta')\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
  \;:\;\eta,\eta'\text{ differ only at }j\bigr\}
\]
of a particular model may satisfy $c_{ij}<\tanh|J_{ij}|$ strictly.

Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff} is therefore a \textbf{sharp field-uniform one-bond
influence envelope}: sharp as a bound over arbitrary real fields, and a valid
interdependence majorant, $c_{ij}\le\tanh|J_{ij}|$, for every model with that
bond.  That is all Dobrushin's condition needs and it invalidates nothing
downstream --- but it is not the claim that $\tanh|J|$ is the minimal
interdependence matrix of \emph{every} system with that bond: on multi-bond
cells the reachable fields form a discrete set and the intrinsic coefficient
can sit strictly below the envelope.  Where the two coincide is itself
settled below: on the one-bond cell of Section~\ref{sec:gibbs} the
coincidence is exact, and it is a theorem
(Proposition~\ref{prop:ising-witness}).  The window below is a sufficient
condition, explicit and not optimal.

The attainment still earns its place: without it the envelope would be some
number above the influence, and one could not say the envelope is the best
field-uniform one.  In Lean the three statements are \texttt{tvField\_le},
\texttt{tvField\_attained} and \texttt{tvField\_isLUB}.

\subsection{The matrix, and the window as a hypothesis}

Given sites $\iota$, bond strengths $J$ and a distance $d$, put
\[
  C_{ij}\;=\;\begin{cases}\tanh|J_{ij}| & i\ne j \text{ and } d(i,j)\le1,\\
  0 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]

\begin{theorem}[machine-checked]
\label{thm:matrix}
$C$ is entrywise nonnegative, vanishes on the diagonal, and vanishes whenever
$d(i,j)>1$.  Consequently \emph{every matrix-side hypothesis} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:d1} --- (i) and (ii) --- holds by construction.  Furthermore, if
at every site the influence support is contained in a finite set whose bond
coefficients sum to at most $\alpha$, then the row-sum hypothesis holds at every
site, with neither the statement nor $\alpha$ mentioning the cardinality of the
index type.  Finally, at a site carrying two bonds of strength $\beta$ and two of
strength $\gamma$,
\[
  \sum_j C_{ij}\;=\;2\tanh|\beta|+2\tanh|\gamma| .
\]
\end{theorem}

Three remarks, in decreasing order of how easily the statement is over-read.

\emph{What is not discharged.}  The construction settles the matrix-side
hypotheses.  It does \emph{not} discharge the two conditions on the ambient
distance --- $d(i,i)=0$ and the triangle inequality --- which
Corollary~\ref{cor:chain} still takes as hypotheses, as does the Lean endpoint.
For a graph distance they are immediate; for an arbitrary $d$ they are
assumptions, and the paper does not pretend otherwise.

\emph{One site versus a family.}  The last display is arithmetic at one site of
one five-point graph, and by itself it would say nothing about a family of
volumes.  The middle clause is what makes it say something: it converts a
\emph{local} geometric fact --- each site's influence support is covered by a set
whose coefficients sum below one --- into the row-sum hypothesis holding
simultaneously at every site of every volume with that local geometry.  In Lean
these are \texttt{rowSum\_bound\_of\_local\_cover} and
\texttt{dobrushin\_bound\_of\_local\_cover}.

\emph{The window.}  It has been carried in this lane's prose as
$2\tanh\beta+2\tanh\gamma<1$; it is now computed rather than asserted and is
consumed by Corollary~\ref{cor:chain}.  It remains a sufficient condition and,
by Section~\ref{sec:prior}, a conservative one.  The distance used in the
computation is the graph distance on a star, proved reflexive at zero and
triangular by exhaustive checking over the $125$ triples.

\subsection{The resolvent bound}

\begin{theorem}[machine-checked]
\label{thm:d1}
Let $\iota$ be a finite type, $d:\iota\times\iota\to\N$ satisfy $d(i,i)=0$ and
the triangle inequality, and let $C:\iota\times\iota\to\R$ satisfy
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $C_{ij}\ge0$ for all $i,j$;
\item[(ii)] $C_{ij}=0$ whenever $d(i,j)>1$;
\item[(iii)] $\sum_j C_{ij}\le\alpha$ for all $i$, with $0\le\alpha<1$.
\end{enumerate}
Then for all $i,j$ and every $N\in\N$,
\[
  \sum_{n\in[0,N)}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\;\le\;\frac{\alpha^{\,d(i,j)}}{1-\alpha},
\]
and consequently $\sum_{n}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\le\alpha^{\,d(i,j)}/(1-\alpha)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Three steps, each an induction on $n$.  Nonnegativity is preserved by powers.
Row sums multiply: expanding $(C^{n+1})$ and exchanging the two finite sums,
$\sum_j(C^{n+1})_{ij}=\sum_k(C^{n})_{ik}\sum_jC_{kj}\le\alpha\sum_k(C^{n})_{ik}
\le\alpha^{n+1}$, so each entry, being at most its row sum, is at most
$\alpha^{n}$.  Support: if $n<d(i,j)$ then $(C^{n})_{ij}=0$, since in
$(C^{n+1})_{ij}=\sum_k(C^{n})_{ik}C_{kj}$ each $k$ has either $d(i,k)>n$, killing
the first factor by induction, or $d(i,k)\le n$, whence
$d(i,j)\le d(i,k)+d(k,j)\le n+d(k,j)$ forces $d(k,j)>1$ and kills the second.
Combining, $(C^{n})_{ij}\le\alpha^{n}$ when $n\ge d(i,j)$ and vanishes otherwise;
splitting $[0,N)$ at $d(i,j)$ and reindexing the surviving block by
$n=d(i,j)+m$ gives $\alpha^{d(i,j)}\sum_{m<N-d(i,j)}\alpha^{m}$, and the
geometric sum is at most $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[where the volume-freeness is]
The cardinality of $\iota$ appears nowhere in the hypotheses or the conclusion.
That is the entire content of the theorem: the index type may be as large as one
likes and neither $\alpha$ nor the exponent notices.  Every constant is read off
single-site data.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the hypothesis that matters here]
\label{rem:crack}
Hypothesis (iii) asks for row sums \emph{bounded} by $\alpha$.  It never asks for
them to be constant.  Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruction} refutes constancy and
leaves (iii) untouched: it is precisely the crack through which the coupled
kernel passes after Schur's test has died.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[non-vacuity, machine-checked]
\label{prop:nonvacuity}
On the three-point chain with $d$ the graph distance and $C_{ij}=1/4$ for
$d(i,j)\le1$ and $0$ otherwise, hypotheses (i)--(iii) hold with $\alpha=3/4<1$,
the matrix is not the zero matrix, the two endpoints are at distance exactly
two, and the conclusion reads: the whole resolvent series between the endpoints
is at most $9/4$.
\end{proposition}

The witness is not decoration.  Hypotheses (ii) and (iii) are jointly
satisfiable by the zero matrix, for which the conclusion is trivial, and by data
with $d\equiv0$, for which the exponent is trivial.  Proposition
\ref{prop:nonvacuity} excludes both.

\subsection{The composition}

\begin{corollary}[machine-checked]
\label{cor:chain}
Let $J$ be bond strengths on a finite set of sites with a distance $d$ satisfying
$d(i,i)=0$ and the triangle inequality, and let $C$ be the matrix of
Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix}.  If every row sum of $C$ is at most $\alpha<1$, then
for all $i,j$ and every $N$,
\[
  \sum_{n<N}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\;\le\;\frac{\alpha^{\,d(i,j)}}{1-\alpha},
\]
and the same holds for the full series.
\end{corollary}

This is the whole chain in one line: the envelope of
Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff} enters the matrix of Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix}, which
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:d1}, whose conclusion mentions no
cardinality.  In Lean it is \texttt{dobrushin\_resolvent\_bound}, and its proof
is the application of one theorem to three others.

\section{The comparison estimate}
\label{sec:comparison}

Theorems~\ref{thm:coeff}--\ref{thm:d1} bound a resolvent.  Nothing in them
mentions a measure, and so nothing in them says that correlations are
controlled by that resolvent.  That implication is Dobrushin's comparison
estimate, and the previous version of this paper named it as the single open
analytic step.  This section proves it.

\subsection{The objects}

Let $\iota$ be a finite index type and $S$ a finite nonempty state space, so
that the configuration space $S^{\iota}$ is finite.  For $f:S^{\iota}\to\R$ put
\[
  \delta_i(f)\;=\;\max\bigl\{|f(\eta)-f(\eta')| \;:\;
    \eta,\eta'\text{ agree off }i\bigr\},
\]
the oscillation of $f$ in the single coordinate $i$.  A \emph{single-site
kernel} is a family $p_i(\eta,\cdot)$ of probability weights on $S$ that is
\emph{local}: $p_i(\eta,\cdot)$ does not depend on $\eta_i$.  It acts on
observables by
\[
  (E_i f)(\eta)\;=\;\sum_{s\in S}p_i(\eta,s)\,f(\eta^{i\to s}),
\]
where $\eta^{i\to s}$ is $\eta$ with the value at $i$ replaced by $s$.  We say a
matrix $C$ \emph{dominates the influence} of the kernel if, for $k\ne i$,
\[
  \bigl\|p_i(\eta,\cdot)-p_i(\eta',\cdot)\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\;\le\;C_{ik}
  \qquad\text{whenever }\eta,\eta'\text{ agree off }k .
\]
The orientation is the one fixed before any of this Lean was written: the
\emph{first} index of $C$ is the site being conditioned, the \emph{second} is
the site whose value moved.  On a symmetric cell the two conventions are
indistinguishable, which is why fixing it in advance mattered; the gate that
discriminates is described at the end of this section.

\subsection{The key lemma}

\begin{lemma}[machine-checked]
\label{lem:key}
Let $p$ be a local single-site kernel and $C$ dominate its influence.  Then for
every observable $f$:
\[
  \delta_i(E_i f)\;=\;0,
  \qquad
  \delta_k(E_i f)\;\le\;\delta_k(f)+C_{ik}\,\delta_i(f)\quad(k\ne i).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first clause is semantics, not estimation: by locality $E_if$ does not
depend on the coordinate $i$ at all.  For the second, split the difference of
$E_if$ at two configurations agreeing off $k$ into a \emph{transport} term,
where the kernel is held fixed and $f$ is evaluated at two configurations that
still agree off $k$ --- here $k\ne i$ is load-bearing --- and a \emph{kernel
variation} term $\sum_s\bigl(p_i(\eta,s)-p_i(\eta',s)\bigr)f(\eta'^{\,i\to s})$.
The first is at most $\delta_k(f)$ because $p_i(\eta,\cdot)$ is a probability
weight.  The second is a signed mass of total zero tested against a section of
$f$ along coordinate $i$; a zero-sum mass cannot see a constant, so subtracting
the midpoint of the range gives
$\bigl|\sum_s a_s g_s\bigr|\le\tfrac12\bigl(\sum_s|a_s|\bigr)\operatorname{osc}(g)$,
which is total variation times oscillation, at most
$C_{ik}\,\delta_i(f)$.
\end{proof}

The three statements are kept apart in the source on purpose.  The vanishing
clause is \texttt{deltaAt\_condExp\_self} and mentions no matrix; the transport
clause is \texttt{deltaAt\_condExp\_le} and asks only that $C$ \emph{dominate},
never that it be minimal --- the same distinction that cost this lane two
versions over $\tanh|J|$; and their assembly into a single vector inequality,
\texttt{deltaAt\_condExp\_le\_matrix}, is the \emph{only} statement that assumes
$C_{ii}=0$.  That the zero diagonal is a declared hypothesis of the matrix form
and not an accident of the physical cell was decided, and written into the lane
charter, before this module existed.

\subsection{The one-step quarter}

\begin{lemma}[machine-checked]
\label{lem:gruss}
For a probability weight $p$ on a finite set and any $f,g$ with ranges in
$[m_1,M_1]$ and $[m_2,M_2]$,
\[
  \bigl|\mathbb{E}_p[fg]-\mathbb{E}_p[f]\,\mathbb{E}_p[g]\bigr|
  \;\le\;\tfrac14(M_1-m_1)(M_2-m_2),
\]
and the constant $\tfrac14$ is attained.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Not Cauchy--Schwarz.  The centred mass $a_x=p_x(f_x-\mathbb{E}_p f)$ has total
zero, so the signed-mass inequality above gives
$|\mathrm{Cov}|\le\tfrac12\bigl(\sum_x|a_x|\bigr)\operatorname{osc}(g)$, and
$\sum_x|a_x|$ is the mean absolute deviation of $f$ about its mean, which is at
most $(M_1-m_1)/2$: split it into its positive and negative parts, observe they
are \emph{equal} because the mass is centred, bound one by $(M_1-\mathbb{E}f)T$
and the other by $(\mathbb{E}f-m_1)(1-T)$ with $T$ the mass above the mean, and
multiply.  The fair two-point weight with $f=g$ an indicator attains it.
\end{proof}

Each factor $\tfrac12$ is born in exactly one lemma and the quarter is their
product.  Popoviciu's variance bound, also machine-checked in the same module,
is \emph{not} used: it is the $L^2$ companion, kept separate so that a
difficulty in either could not block the other.

\subsection{The estimate}

\begin{theorem}[machine-checked]
\label{thm:comparison}
Let $\mu$ be a probability weight on $S^{\iota}$, invariant under every $E_i$
(that is, $\mathbb{E}_\mu[E_i F]=\mathbb{E}_\mu[F]$ for all $F$ and $i$), let $C$
dominate the influence of the kernel with $C_{ii}=0$ and row sums at most
$\alpha<1$.  Then for all observables $f,g$,
\[
  \bigl|\mathrm{Cov}_\mu(f,g)\bigr|\;\le\;
  \tfrac14\sum_{i,j}\delta_i(f)\Bigl(\sum_{n}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\Bigr)\delta_j(g),
\]
and, composing with Theorem~\ref{thm:d1} when $C$ is supported at distance at
most one,
\[
  \bigl|\mathrm{Cov}_\mu(f,g)\bigr|\;\le\;
  \frac{1}{4(1-\alpha)}\sum_{i,j}\delta_i(f)\,\alpha^{\dist(i,j)}\,\delta_j(g).
\]
For observables depending on single sites $i_0$ and $j_0$ this reads
\[
  \bigl|\mathrm{Cov}_\mu(f,g)\bigr|\;\le\;
  \frac{\delta_{i_0}(f)\,\delta_{j_0}(g)}{4(1-\alpha)}\;
  \alpha^{\dist(i_0,j_0)} :
\]
exponential decay in the distance, at a rate free of the volume.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $P=\frac1m\sum_iE_i$ be the random-scan average, $m=\#\iota$.  It preserves
$\mu$-expectations, so telescoping over $N$ steps,
\[
  \mathrm{Cov}_\mu(f,g)=\sum_{n<N}\Bigl(\mathrm{Cov}_\mu(P^nf,g)
  -\mathrm{Cov}_\mu(P^{n+1}f,g)\Bigr)+\mathrm{Cov}_\mu(P^Nf,g).
\]
Each increment is $\mathbb{E}_\mu\bigl[g\cdot(h-Ph)\bigr]$ with $h=P^nf$;
averaging over the site chosen and using invariance once more, the
$i$\nobreakdash-th contribution equals $\mathbb{E}_\mu\bigl[E_i\bigl(g\,(h-E_i
h)\bigr)\bigr]$, whose integrand is \emph{pointwise} the covariance of the two
sections of $g$ and $h$ along coordinate $i$ under $p_i(\eta,\cdot)$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:gruss} and the fact that a section oscillates at most
$\delta_i$, each increment is at most
$\frac{1}{4m}\sum_i\delta_i(g)\,\delta_i(P^nf)$.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:key} the oscillation vector of the iterates is transported by
$v\mapsto(1-\frac1m)v+\frac1m C^{\!\top}v$, so $\delta(P^nf)$ is dominated
coordinatewise by the $n$-th iterate of that map applied to $\delta(f)$.  Two
things follow, both by finite induction and neither taking a limit.  Its
$\ell^1$ norm contracts by $1-\frac{1-\alpha}{m}$ per step, so the telescoping
remainder $|\mathrm{Cov}_\mu(P^Nf,g)|$, bounded crudely by
$\frac14\operatorname{osc}(g)\sum_i\delta_i(P^Nf)$, dies geometrically.  And the
\emph{accumulated} transport $\sum_{n<N}$ of those iterates is dominated
entrywise by $m$ times the $N$-th partial sum of $\sum_n(C^{\!\top})^n$ ---
which is the step where the factor $m$ introduced by the averaging is exactly
cancelled, leaving a bound with no $m$ in it.  Assembling gives the finite-$N$
statement; letting $N\to\infty$ in the \emph{bound} gives the series form.
\end{proof}

Every statement up to the last sentence of that proof is a finite inequality
over a finite configuration space.  The single limit is taken at the level of
the bound, which is a design decision this lane recorded before the module
existed, so that no infinite-volume object is constructed anywhere.

\begin{proposition}[non-vacuity and attainment, machine-checked]
\label{prop:comparison-witness}
One site, two states, the fair kernel, the uniform weight, $C=0$, $\alpha=0$,
$d\equiv0$.  Every hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:comparison} holds by
computation, and on the indicator observable the two-point conclusion is an
\emph{equality}: $|\mathrm{Cov}|=\tfrac14$ against a bound whose value is
$\tfrac14$.
\end{proposition}

The witness does two jobs.  It shows the hypotheses are jointly satisfiable ---
invariance of $\mu$ under all $E_i$ is a real constraint, not a formality.  And
because the bound is \emph{attained} there, the constant $\tfrac14$ cannot be
lowered by any argument, in this or any other proof of the same inequality.

\subsection{The gates, and the one that discriminated}

Five gates were registered before the corresponding Lean and are in
\repofile{scripts/judge\_dobrushin\_d3.py}{judge\_dobrushin\_d3.py} and
\repofile{scripts/judge\_dobrushin\_d3b.py}{judge\_dobrushin\_d3b.py}.  Two are
worth naming.

\textbf{The gate that could have killed the rung.}  Lemma~\ref{lem:key} was
checked by brute force on small Ising cells over \emph{all} $2^{|S^{\iota}|}$
Boolean observables --- $256$ of them at three sites --- computing both sides
exactly for every site pair.  The gate reports the worst value of
$\mathrm{lhs}-\mathrm{rhs}$, and a positive value would have refuted the lemma
before a line of it was written.  Measured: $+1.11\times10^{-16}$, one unit in
the last place of a float64 comparison at scale one.

\textbf{The gate that discriminated the orientation.}  On an Ising cell the
minimal influence coefficients are symmetric, so $C=C^{\!\top}$ and a
transposed index would pass every other gate and surface much later.  A
three-site cell built from eight distinct positive weights has
$\max_{ij}|C_{ij}-C_{ji}|>0$, and there the declared orientation passes with
zero violations while the transposed one \textbf{fails with $24$}.  The
convention in Lemma~\ref{lem:key} is therefore the one the lemma needs, and
that is established by measurement rather than by assertion.

Both certifiers were run in \texttt{normal} and in \texttt{optimized}
(\texttt{python -O}) mode on the Linux plane, all four runs exiting zero with
their check counters matching.

\section{The instantiation: from a weight, not from data}
\label{sec:gibbs}

Theorem~\ref{thm:comparison} takes \emph{data} --- a weight, a kernel, a
majorant --- and six hypotheses.  A reader is entitled to ask whether that data
exists for anything, and whether the hypotheses are met by the object one
actually has, which is an interaction, not a kernel.  This section removes the
question.  Its input is a single function.

\begin{definition}
\label{def:heatbath}
Let $w:S^{\iota}\to\R$ be strictly positive.  Its \emph{Gibbs weight} is
$\mu_w=w/Z$ with $Z=\sum_\eta w(\eta)$, and its \emph{heat-bath kernel} is
\[
  p^w_i(\eta,s)\;=\;\frac{w(\eta^{i\to s})}{\sum_{t\in S}w(\eta^{i\to t})} .
\]
Its \emph{intrinsic Dobrushin matrix} is
\[
  c^w_{ik}\;=\;\max\Bigl\{\bigl\|p^w_i(\eta,\cdot)-p^w_i(\eta',\cdot)
    \bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\;:\;\eta,\eta'\text{ agree off }k\Bigr\},
\]
the maximum over the finite configuration space --- the coefficient itself, not
a majorant chosen from outside.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[machine-checked]
\label{thm:gibbs}
For every strictly positive $w$: $\mu_w$ is a probability weight; $p^w$ is
nonnegative, normalised and local; $\mu_w$ is \emph{invariant} under every
$E^w_i$; $c^w_{ii}=0$; and $c^w$ dominates the influence of $p^w$.
Consequently, if $\sum_k c^w_{ik}\le\alpha<1$ at every site, then for all
observables
\[
  \bigl|\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu_w}(f,g)\bigr|\;\le\;
  \tfrac14\sum_{i,j}\delta_i(f)\Bigl(\sum_n (c^w)^n_{ij}\Bigr)\delta_j(g),
\]
and if moreover $c^w$ vanishes beyond distance one for a distance $d$, then for
single-site observables at $i_0$ and $j_0$,
\[
  \bigl|\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu_w}(f,g)\bigr|\;\le\;
  \frac{\delta_{i_0}(f)\,\delta_{j_0}(g)}{4(1-\alpha)}\,\alpha^{\,d(i_0,j_0)} .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Normalisation and locality are computations: the local partition sum
$\sum_t w(\eta^{i\to t})$ is positive, and it does not see $\eta_i$ because
$\eta^{i\to t}$ does not.

\emph{Invariance is the only step with content, and it is an identity rather
than an estimate.}  Write $\mathbb{E}_{\mu_w}[E^w_iF]$ as a sum over
$S^{\iota}\times S$ and apply the involution
\[
  \tau_i(\eta,s)\;=\;\bigl(\eta^{i\to s},\,\eta_i\bigr),
  \qquad \tau_i\circ\tau_i=\mathrm{id}.
\]
Under $\tau_i$ the integrand
$\mu_w(\eta)\,p^w_i(\eta,s)\,F(\eta^{i\to s})$ becomes
$\mu_w(\eta^{i\to s})\cdot\frac{w(\eta)}{\sum_t w(\eta^{i\to t})}\cdot F(\eta)$,
using that the local partition sum is unchanged along the fibre.  Summing that
over $s$ first reconstitutes $\sum_s\mu_w(\eta^{i\to s})$, which is the local
partition sum over $Z$; it cancels the denominator exactly, leaving
$\mu_w(\eta)F(\eta)$.  Reindexing a finite sum by an involution is free, and
that is the whole proof.

The zero diagonal is a theorem, not a hypothesis: $c^w_{ii}$ is a maximum of
total variations between $p^w_i(\eta,\cdot)$ and $p^w_i(\eta^{i\to s},\cdot)$,
which are equal by locality.  Domination is immediate because $c^w$ is defined
as the maximum.  The conclusions are Theorem~\ref{thm:comparison} and its
corollaries applied to this data.
\end{proof}

Three remarks, in the order in which the statement is most easily over-read.

\emph{What is left as a hypothesis.}
Dobrushin's condition $\sum_k c^w_{ik}\le\alpha<1$ --- plus, for the distance
form, that $c^w$ has range one.  Both are conditions on the \emph{interaction},
not on the machinery: nothing about kernels, invariance or majorants survives
for a user to discharge.  In Lean the endpoints are
\texttt{gibbs\_covar\_le\_resolvent}, \texttt{gibbs\_covar\_exp\_decay} and
\texttt{gibbs\_covar\_two\_point}.

\emph{Why the heat-bath kernel and not another.}  The comparison estimate holds
for \emph{any} invariant local kernel; the heat-bath one is chosen because its
invariance is an involution rather than a computation, and because its
influence coefficients are the ones Dobrushin's condition is usually stated
for.  Nothing downstream depends on the choice.

\emph{The intrinsic matrix versus the envelope.}  $c^w$ is the coefficient, so
for a bonded interaction it is bounded by the envelope $\tanh|J_{ij}|$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff} and may be strictly smaller.  That domination is no
longer only measured: for the Ising weight it is Theorem~\ref{thm:ising}
below.  What stays measured-only is the \emph{strictness} of the gap on
multi-bond cells (gate G14) --- and the one-bond witness shows the gap is not
universal, because there the two coincide exactly.

\begin{proposition}[non-vacuity of the instantiation, machine-checked]
\label{prop:gibbs-witness}
For the uniform weight on one site with two states, every hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:gibbs} holds by computation, its Gibbs weight \emph{is} the
witness weight of Proposition~\ref{prop:comparison-witness}, and the
instantiated two-point bound is again an equality at $\tfrac14$.
\end{proposition}

So the instantiated hypotheses are jointly satisfiable and the instantiated
bound is still the sharp one.  This matters more than the usual non-vacuity
check, because invariance is a genuine constraint on the pair (weight,
kernel) and a construction that failed it would make Theorem~\ref{thm:gibbs}
say nothing.

\textbf{Gates.}  \repofile{scripts/judge\_dobrushin\_d4.py}{judge\_dobrushin\_d4.py},
registered before this Lean.  G12 checks invariance as an \emph{identity} to
$10^{-12}$ on deliberately asymmetric weights, exhaustively over all Boolean
observables and all sites --- a residual above roundoff would have killed the
involution argument before it was written.  G13 checks that the heat-bath
conditional of an Ising weight is $(1+\tanh h)/2$ at the local field.  G14
checks that the intrinsic coefficients are dominated by $\tanh|J|$ entrywise,
which is the measured content of ``the envelope is a majorant, not the
minimum'' at the level of the measure.

\subsection*{The interaction, instantiated}

Theorem~\ref{thm:gibbs} starts at an arbitrary positive weight.  A reader's
weight is not arbitrary: it is built from a coupling.  The last rung
instantiates that too.

\begin{definition}
\label{def:ising}
Let $J:\iota\times\iota\to\R$ be symmetric with zero diagonal.  The
\emph{Ising weight} of $J$ is
$w_J(\eta)=\exp\bigl(\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}J_{ab}\,\sigma_a\sigma_b\bigr)$ with
$\sigma_a=\pm1$ the spin of $\eta_a$, and the \emph{local field} at $i$ is
$h_i(\eta)=\sum_k J_{ik}\,\sigma_k$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[machine-checked]
\label{thm:ising}
For every such $J$: the heat-bath conditional of $w_J$ at site $i$ is the
sigmoid of the local field,
$p^{w_J}_i(\eta,+1)=\tfrac{1+\tanh h_i(\eta)}{2}$; the intrinsic matrix is
dominated by the envelope,
\[
  c^{w_J}_{ik}\;\le\;\tanh\lvert J_{ik}\rvert ,
\]
vanishing wherever the coupling does; and consequently, if the \emph{envelope}
row sums obey $\sum_k\tanh|J_{ik}|\le\alpha<1$ at every site, every conclusion
of Theorem~\ref{thm:gibbs} holds for $w_J$ with the same $\alpha$ --- the
comparison estimate, and, when $J$ has range one for a distance, exponential
decay in that distance.  In Lean the endpoints are
\texttt{heatBath\_ising\_pPlus}, \texttt{dobCoeff\_ising\_le} and
\texttt{ising\_covar\_\{le\_resolvent, exp\_decay, two\_point\}}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The quadratic energy of a configuration updated at $i$ splits as an $i$-free
block plus $\sigma_i\,h_i(\eta)$ (\texttt{energy\_update}: split the double
sum at $a=i$ and $b=i$, kill the diagonal with $J_{ii}=0$, merge the two
cross blocks by symmetry).  The local partition sum is then
$e^{h}+e^{-h}$ times an $i$-free factor, which cancels in the heat-bath
quotient: the sigmoid identity.  Updating site $k$ moves the field by
$J_{ik}(\sigma'_k-\sigma_k)$, and $\sigma'_k-\sigma_k\in\{0,\pm2\}$, so the
total variation of the two conditionals is the envelope's
$\mathrm{tv}(h,h-2J')$ at $|J'|=|J_{ik}|$ --- Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff}
bounds it by $\tanh|J_{ik}|$.  Taking the supremum gives the entrywise
domination; the endpoints compose with Theorem~\ref{thm:gibbs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the one-bond witness, and the domination is an equality
there]
\label{prop:ising-witness}
For two sites joined by one bond $\beta\ge0$: the intrinsic coefficient
\emph{equals} the envelope, $c^{w}_{01}=\tanh\beta$
(\texttt{dobCoeff\_bond\_attained}), because the all-up configuration
realises the optimal field $h=\beta$ and flipping the neighbour realises
$-\beta$.  Moreover the envelope row sum is $\tanh|\beta|<1$ at \emph{every}
coupling, so this system's two-point decay bound
(\texttt{bond\_covar\_two\_point}) carries no smallness hypothesis at all.
\end{proposition}

The equality matters beyond non-vacuity: Section~2's distinction between the
envelope and the minimal coefficient is a genuine gap on multi-bond cells
(G14 measures it), and the one-bond system shows the gap can also close ---
the reachable fields decide, exactly as Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff}'s attainment
said they would.

\begin{theorem}[the window, instantiated at a weight; machine-checked]
\label{thm:star}
On the star of Section~3 --- a centre carrying two bonds $\beta$ and two
bonds $\gamma$ --- mask the bond function to the star's edges, so the
coupling is symmetric, zero-diagonal and of range one for the star distance.
If
\[
  2\tanh|\beta|+2\tanh|\gamma|\;<\;1,
\]
then for single-site observables $f$ at $i_0$ and $g$ at $j_0$,
\[
  \bigl|\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}(f,g)\bigr|\;\le\;
  \frac{\delta_{i_0}(f)\,\delta_{j_0}(g)}
       {4\,\bigl(1-(2\tanh|\beta|+2\tanh|\gamma|)\bigr)}
  \;\bigl(2\tanh|\beta|+2\tanh|\gamma|\bigr)^{\,d_\star(i_0,j_0)} ,
\]
where $\mu$ is the Gibbs weight of the star's Ising weight and $d_\star$ the
star distance.  In Lean: \texttt{star\_covar\_two\_point}.  The row-sum
hypothesis is discharged by computation: the centre's envelope row sum is
exactly the window, each leaf's is $\tanh|\beta|$ or $\tanh|\gamma|$.
\end{theorem}

The window of Section~3 was ``the hypothesis of a theorem rather than a
sentence''; it is now the hypothesis of a theorem \emph{about the anisotropic
Ising weight itself}, which is the composition the abstract promises.  The
star is a cell, not a lattice, and the theorem does not evaluate the window
for any numerical $(\beta,\gamma)$; no interval arithmetic is used anywhere
in this paper.

\section{What remains, named exactly}
\label{sec:frontier}

Theorems~\ref{thm:coeff}--\ref{thm:gibbs} are an engine, not a conclusion.
Turning them into a volume-uniform statement about $\specRatio$ requires, in
order:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{1.}] a valid single-site interdependence majorant, and the
      sufficient window it produces.  \textbf{Done}: Theorems~\ref{thm:coeff}
      and~\ref{thm:matrix}, machine-checked, with the window computed rather
      than asserted, and holding at every site of every volume with the given
      local geometry.  Not needed, and done only where the one-bond witness
      makes it coincide with the envelope: the \emph{minimal}
      interdependence matrix of a general system;
\item[\textbf{2.}] Dobrushin's comparison estimate \cite{Dobrushin1970,Simon1993}
      --- that connected correlations are controlled by the resolvent of $C$,
      hence decay exponentially at a rate free of the extent.  \textbf{Done}:
      Theorem~\ref{thm:comparison};
\item[\textbf{3.}] construction of the single-site conditionals of a Gibbs
      weight, and discharge of every hypothesis that estimate consumes ---
      invariance above all, which is a genuine constraint on the pair and not a
      formality.  \textbf{Done}: Theorem~\ref{thm:gibbs}, from a strictly
      positive weight alone, leaving Dobrushin's condition on the intrinsic
      matrix as the hypothesis a user supplies --- plus, for the geometric
      form of the decay, the range-one support of that matrix; and
      Theorem~\ref{thm:ising} instantiates the interaction itself, turning
      that hypothesis into a computation on the coupling, discharged on the
      one-bond and star cells;
\item[\textbf{4.}] transport of that decay into the operator formulation: from
      correlations of the Gibbs weight to $\specRatio$ of the transfer kernel,
      through the finite-time interface described below.
      \textbf{Open.}  Conditional on a volume-uniform Dobrushin window for
      the chosen lattice family --- discharged in this paper only on the
      one-bond and star cells, which are cells and not yet a family of
      volumes --- it is the only remaining \emph{structural} step in this
      list.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What changed, stated at its exact size.}  An earlier version of this
paper named step~2 as the only currently identified new analytic estimate and
the bottleneck of the chain; a later one proved it, for arbitrary invariant
data, and left step~3 open.  Both are now closed.  The honest description of
what remains is that step~4 is not an estimate but a \emph{translation}: from a
statement about a measure to a statement about an operator.  The vacuum pairing
is an infinite-time object, so that translation must run through finite time
extent with explicit boundary control, and nothing here shortens it.  What this
paper does claim is that between a positive weight and exponential decay of its
correlations there is no longer a gap: the chain is closed end to end, and the
inputs a user supplies are Dobrushin's condition and, for the geometric form
of the decay, the range-one support of the intrinsic matrix.


\textbf{The consumer, named.}  It is \texttt{volumeUniform\_gap} in
\repofile{YangMills/OS/TransferGap.lean}{YangMills/OS/TransferGap.lean}, and its
signature, read off the elaborated declaration rather than from memory, is: for a
family of real inner-product spaces $H_i$ indexed by $\iota$, transfer operators
$T_i$ and vectors $\Omega_i$ with \texttt{VacuumTransfer}~$T_i~\Omega_i$ for every
$i$, and a rate $0<r<1$, the hypothesis
\[
  \forall i,\;\forall v\in H_i,\;\exists C,\;\forall n,\quad
  \bigl|\texttt{connCorr}\,T_i\,\Omega_i\,v\,n\bigr|\;\le\;C\,r^{\,n}
\]
yields $\exists m>0$ with
$\|\texttt{projectedTransfer}\,T_i\,\Omega_i\|\le e^{-m}$ for every $i$.  It
takes, over a family of finite volumes, decay at a \emph{common} rate and returns
a \emph{common} mass.  Its quantifier order is worth stating because it is
load-bearing: the
prefactor $C$ may depend on the volume and on the observable, and only $r$ is
shared.  The development's own standing objection --- that a bound
$C(L)\rho^{N}$ with $C(L)\to\infty$ says nothing --- therefore does not apply to
it, the conclusion being an operator-norm bound.

A design decision is recorded here rather than discovered later: the vacuum
pairing is an infinite-time object, so step~3 must proceed through finite time
extent with explicit boundary control, taking the limit only at the level of the
bound.  No infinite-volume state is constructed anywhere in this paper, and the
word \emph{clustering} is accordingly not used for one.

\section{Prior art, and the exact delta}
\label{sec:prior}

\textbf{Dobrushin's uniqueness theorem is classical}
\cite{Dobrushin1968,Dobrushin1970,Simon1993} and nothing in this paper
is new mathematics.  We locate the contribution precisely.

\emph{Not explicit constants.}  The cell here is the anisotropic
two-dimensional Ising model, whose transfer spectrum has been available in
closed form since Onsager \cite{Onsager1944} and Kaufman \cite{Kaufman1949}.  A constant obtained from Dobrushin's
condition is \emph{worse} than the exact answer --- on the cells we measured, by
a factor between roughly two and thirty-six.  Any claim to have improved a
constant for this model would be false, and we make none.

\emph{Generality, instead.}  Dobrushin's coefficient is read off the single-site
conditionals of an \emph{arbitrary} positive finite-range slice weight, which is
what the development's Gibbs weight quantifies over, and where the exact
solution is silent.

\emph{Mechanisation.}  A search conducted on 1 August 2026 over the public
proof-assistant repositories and the arXiv listings for Lean, Coq and
Isabelle returned no mechanisation of the finite-lattice Dobrushin
interdependence matrix or of the comparison estimate.  We state it that
narrowly on purpose: the name \emph{Dobrushin} attaches to several distinct
results, and mechanised work exists that uses others of them, so a blanket
claim of first formalisation would be both unsupported by the search we ran and
ambiguous about which theorem it referred to.  The
same search does return a Lean~4 formalisation of the transverse-field Ising
chain \cite{LeanIsing2026}, with momentum-mode decomposition and a
Jordan--Wigner transformation; it
concerns the quantum chain rather than the classical two-dimensional transfer
matrix, and displaces nothing here, but it means a blanket claim that the Ising
model has not been formalised would not survive review, and we make none.  A
formalisation of the Perron--Frobenius theorem for irreducible nonnegative
matrices also exists \cite{Mathlib2020}, as does a mechanisation of the
Osterwalder--Schrader axioms for the Gaussian free field \cite{OSforGFF2026}.  Reflection positivity and finite-lattice spectra for
related models have been treated without mechanisation \cite{WashburnSimons2026}.

\section{Limitations}

All six theorems are elementary.  Theorem~\ref{thm:d1} is three inductions;
Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff} is a hyperbolic identity and a bound on a denominator;
Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix} is a construction and a finite computation;
Theorem~\ref{thm:comparison} is a telescoping sum, one pointwise application of
a ninety-year-old covariance bound, and two finite inductions;
Theorem~\ref{thm:gibbs} is a reindexing by an involution;
Theorem~\ref{thm:ising} is a split of a quadratic sum at one index and the
envelope applied to a field move of $0$ or $\pm2$.  Their value
is that they are machine-checked, non-vacuous, composed, and carry the hypothesis
the dead method violated --- not that any of them is deep.

Section~\ref{sec:measurement} is numerics at $L\le12$ with an Aitken
extrapolation.  It provides measured counterevidence against an attribution and
contradicts it on the tested grid; it does not prove where the wall is,
and an extrapolation can mislead near a transition.  The window is not optimal
and is never called sharp.

\textbf{Step~4 of Section~\ref{sec:frontier} is open}, and until it closes the
volume-uniform statement about $\specRatio$ for the coupled kernel remains
unproved.  This is the substantive limitation, and it is a translation between
two languages rather than a missing estimate --- which does not make it small.
Two further boundaries are worth naming.  Theorem~\ref{thm:gibbs} discharges
every hypothesis \emph{except} Dobrushin's condition; beyond the one-bond and
star-cell instances proved with Theorem~\ref{thm:ising}, whether that
condition holds uniformly for a specified lattice \emph{family} remains open.
The envelope of Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff} is what a user
would apply, and it is a sufficient condition, not a characterisation.  And the
decay proved is decay of correlations of a measure on a \emph{finite} product
space: no infinite-volume state is constructed anywhere, and the word
\emph{clustering} is accordingly not used for one.

\title{The Row Sums Were the Method, Not the Theorem:\\
a Machine-Checked Dobrushin Resolvent Chain,\\
and a Misattributed Uniformity Wall}
\author{Lluis Eriksson}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A formal development of a two-dimensional spatial transfer kernel proves its
uniform spectral statements by Schur's test on \emph{constant} row sums, and
carries a theorem, labelled \textsc{the obstruction} in its own source, stating
that those row sums stop being constant the moment the spatial coupling is
switched on.  That theorem obstructs the \emph{method}.  It was read as
obstructing the \emph{conclusion}, and the reading outlived its own retraction.

We prove, in Lean~4 with no \texttt{sorry} and no project axiom, a chain of
three theorems.  \textbf{First}, the one-bond influence envelope: flipping one
neighbour across a bond of strength $J\ge0$ moves a two-state site's conditional
by at most $\tanh J$, and by exactly $\tanh J$ at one field, so $\tanh J$ is the
\emph{least field-uniform} bound.  It is an interdependence majorant, not the
minimal coefficient of any particular finite model, whose reachable fields form
a discrete set --- a distinction Section~2 makes precisely.  \textbf{Second},
those envelopes assembled into a matrix discharge every \emph{matrix-side}
hypothesis of the third (the two conditions on the ambient distance remain
hypotheses), and turn a purely \emph{local} covering condition into the
remaining row-sum bound holding at every site of every volume --- which is the
coupling window, and which we compute: at a site carrying two bonds of strength
$\beta$ and two of strength $\gamma$ it is exactly
$2\tanh|\beta|+2\tanh|\gamma|$, so the window is the hypothesis of a theorem
rather than a sentence.  \textbf{Third}, the
resolvent bound: for a nonnegative matrix $C$ over a finite index type, supported
on pairs at distance at most one and with row sums \emph{bounded} by $\alpha<1$,
without any constancy assumption,
\[
  \sum_{n<N}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\;\le\;\frac{\alpha^{\dist(i,j)}}{1-\alpha}
  \qquad\text{for every }N,
\]
with no reference of any kind to the cardinality of the index type.  Each rung
carries a non-vacuity witness and every declaration is oracled.

Separately, and by measurement rather than proof, we remove the wall from where
it was being placed.  For the coupled kernel the second-eigenvalue ratio
$\specRatio(L)$ saturates strictly below one in each of six cells tested inside
Onsager's disordered region --- with the spatial weight switched on --- and
reaches one to six decimal places in the two ordered cells tested.  The
control at zero spatial coupling reproduces the \emph{proved} identity
$\specRatio=\tanh\beta$ to six decimals, so the harness is calibrated against a
theorem rather than against itself.  These data are eight cells at $L\le12$ with
an extrapolation.  On the tested grid they \emph{contradict} the blanket
attribution of the degeneracy to the mere presence of the spatial weight; they
are not, and are not offered as, a refutation of an asymptotic claim, since a
sequence can appear to saturate at $L\le12$ and turn afterwards.  They do
\emph{not} locate the transition, which would need a dense sweep and
finite-size scaling that we do not attempt.

\textbf{What is not proved.}  The volume-uniform bound on $\specRatio$ for the
coupled kernel is \emph{not} established here.  Dobrushin's uniqueness theorem is
classical \cite{Dobrushin1968,Simon1993}; nothing in this paper is new
mathematics.  What is offered is a
mechanised, non-vacuous chain, measured counterevidence against an attribution,
and the naming of the only currently identified new analytic estimate:
Dobrushin's comparison estimate.  That is not the only remaining work ---
downstream instantiation still requires the finite-time operator interface of
Section~\ref{sec:frontier}.  No consequence for Yang--Mills theory is stated,
suggested, or implied.
\end{abstract}

\section{The setting, and the theorem that was read too widely}
\label{sec:reading}

Fix an extent $L$ and consider configurations $\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{L}$ of one
slice.  The development under discussion carries a time-bond kernel that
factorises over sites,
\[
  K_\beta(\sigma,\tau)\;=\;\prod_{j<L} b_\beta(\sigma_j,\tau_j),
  \qquad b_\beta(s,s')=e^{\pm\beta},
\]
and an arbitrary strictly positive slice weight $w$, the Gibbs weight of a
space-time configuration being the product of the slice weights and the time
bonds.  When $w$ is itself a product over spatial bonds with coupling $\gamma$,
the system is the anisotropic two-dimensional Ising model.

Two facts from that development matter here.

\begin{proposition}[proved elsewhere in the development]
\label{prop:decoupled}
At $\gamma=0$ and for every $L\ge1$, $\specRatio=\tanh\beta$ exactly.
\end{proposition}

The proof is Schur's test \cite{Schur1911}: the decoupled kernel has \emph{constant} row sums, and
the loss on the odd part of an observable is exactly compensated by the odd bond
eigenvalue at every step.

\begin{proposition}[\texttt{coupled\_rowSums\_not\_constant}]
\label{prop:obstruction}
At $\gamma>0$ the row sums of the coupled kernel are not constant: two explicit
configurations of a two-site slice have different row sums.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruction} is labelled \textsc{the obstruction} in the
source.  \emph{It obstructs Schur's test.}  It says nothing whatever about
$\specRatio$, it is proved on a two-site witness, and no statement quantified
over $L$ appears in it.

A stronger reading --- that the spatial weight destroys uniformity --- was
entered into the development and then \emph{retracted of record} as unproved,
leaving behind only a measurement of degeneracy.

\textbf{What is documented, and can be checked.}  The retraction of the sentence
from the manuscript is recorded at Addendum~561 of the development's
verification ledger; its removal from the module docstring, which the first
retraction had missed, at Addendum~563; and its survival in a hand-typed
submission form, caught only because the form was read in full before delivery,
at Addendum~568.  Three removals were required for one sentence, and the third
was the one that would have been published.

\textbf{What is inference, and is labelled as such.}  We observe that no
statement quantified over $L$ for the coupled kernel was attempted in the
interval, and we attribute this to the retracted reading continuing to govern
target selection.  That attribution is ours; it is not established by a
diff, and no artefact in the development records a decision not to attempt
something.  The verifiable half of the observation is the narrower one, and it
is enough for this paper: the guards that caught the sentence three times
compare identifiers and substrings inside files, so whatever else was carrying
the reading was not a file.

\section{The chain}

Three theorems, each machine-checked, composing into one statement.  The first
is about a single bond, the second assembles bonds into a matrix, the third is
about that matrix and never sees the model.

\subsection{The one-bond influence envelope, and that it is least among
field-uniform bounds}

A two-state site in a real field $h$ has conditional $P(+1)=(1+\tanh h)/2$,
written from the Boltzmann weights and nothing else.  Flipping one neighbour
across a bond of strength $J$ moves that field to $h-2J$.  Write
$\mathrm{tv}(h,h')=|P_h(+1)-P_{h'}(+1)|$.

\begin{theorem}[machine-checked]
\label{thm:coeff}
For every $J\ge0$,
\[
  \mathrm{tv}\bigl(h,\,h-2J\bigr)\;\le\;\tanh J \quad\text{for all }h,
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \mathrm{tv}\bigl(J,\,-J\bigr)\;=\;\tanh J .
\]
Hence $\tanh J$ is the \emph{least} upper bound of
$\{\mathrm{tv}(h,h-2J):h\in\R\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\tanh a-\tanh b=\sinh(a-b)/(\cosh a\cosh b)$, and
$\cosh a\cosh b=\bigl(\cosh(a+b)+\cosh(a-b)\bigr)/2\ge\bigl(1+\cosh(a-b)\bigr)/2$
since $\cosh\ge1$.  With $a=h$ and $b=h-2J$ the difference of the fields is
$2J$, so the denominator is at least $(1+\cosh 2J)/2=\cosh^2 J$ while the
numerator is $\sinh 2J=2\sinh J\cosh J$.  Dividing by two,
$\mathrm{tv}\le\sinh J\cosh J/\cosh^2 J=\tanh J$.  At $h=J$ the two fields are
$+J$ and $-J$, their sum is zero, $\cosh 0=1$, and every inequality above is an
equality.
\end{proof}

\textbf{What this is, stated carefully, because the obvious reading of it is
wrong.}  The supremum is taken over \emph{all} $h\in\R$.  In a concrete finite
system with the remaining couplings and any external field fixed, the effective
fields actually reachable at a site form a discrete subset of $\R$, and the value
$h=J$ at which equality holds need not correspond to any admissible boundary
condition of that system.  So the true Dobrushin coefficient
\[
  c_{ij}\;=\;\sup\bigl\{\|\mu_i(\cdot\mid\eta)-\mu_i(\cdot\mid\eta')\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
  \;:\;\eta,\eta'\text{ differ only at }j\bigr\}
\]
of a particular model may satisfy $c_{ij}<\tanh|J_{ij}|$ strictly.

Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff} is therefore a \textbf{sharp field-uniform one-bond
influence envelope}: sharp as a bound over arbitrary real fields, and a valid
interdependence majorant, $c_{ij}\le\tanh|J_{ij}|$, for every model with that
bond.  That is all Dobrushin's condition needs and it invalidates nothing
downstream --- but it is not the claim that $\tanh|J|$ is the minimal
interdependence matrix of any particular system, and this paper does not make
that claim.  The window below is a sufficient condition, explicit and not
optimal.

The attainment still earns its place: without it the envelope would be some
number above the influence, and one could not say the envelope is the best
field-uniform one.  In Lean the three statements are \texttt{tvField\_le},
\texttt{tvField\_attained} and \texttt{tvField\_isLUB}.

\subsection{The matrix, and the window as a hypothesis}

Given sites $\iota$, bond strengths $J$ and a distance $d$, put
\[
  C_{ij}\;=\;\begin{cases}\tanh|J_{ij}| & i\ne j \text{ and } d(i,j)\le1,\\
  0 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]

\begin{theorem}[machine-checked]
\label{thm:matrix}
$C$ is entrywise nonnegative, vanishes on the diagonal, and vanishes whenever
$d(i,j)>1$.  Consequently \emph{every matrix-side hypothesis} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:d1} --- (i) and (ii) --- holds by construction.  Furthermore, if
at every site the influence support is contained in a finite set whose bond
coefficients sum to at most $\alpha$, then the row-sum hypothesis holds at every
site, with neither the statement nor $\alpha$ mentioning the cardinality of the
index type.  Finally, at a site carrying two bonds of strength $\beta$ and two of
strength $\gamma$,
\[
  \sum_j C_{ij}\;=\;2\tanh|\beta|+2\tanh|\gamma| .
\]
\end{theorem}

Three remarks, in decreasing order of how easily the statement is over-read.

\emph{What is not discharged.}  The construction settles the matrix-side
hypotheses.  It does \emph{not} discharge the two conditions on the ambient
distance --- $d(i,i)=0$ and the triangle inequality --- which
Corollary~\ref{cor:chain} still takes as hypotheses, as does the Lean endpoint.
For a graph distance they are immediate; for an arbitrary $d$ they are
assumptions, and the paper does not pretend otherwise.

\emph{One site versus a family.}  The last display is arithmetic at one site of
one five-point graph, and by itself it would say nothing about a family of
volumes.  The middle clause is what makes it say something: it converts a
\emph{local} geometric fact --- each site's influence support is covered by a set
whose coefficients sum below one --- into the row-sum hypothesis holding
simultaneously at every site of every volume with that local geometry.  In Lean
these are \texttt{rowSum\_bound\_of\_local\_cover} and
\texttt{dobrushin\_bound\_of\_local\_cover}.

\emph{The window.}  It has been carried in this lane's prose as
$2\tanh\beta+2\tanh\gamma<1$; it is now computed rather than asserted and is
consumed by Corollary~\ref{cor:chain}.  It remains a sufficient condition and,
by Section~\ref{sec:prior}, a conservative one.  The distance used in the
computation is the graph distance on a star, proved reflexive at zero and
triangular by exhaustive checking over the $125$ triples.

\subsection{The resolvent bound}

\begin{theorem}[machine-checked]
\label{thm:d1}
Let $\iota$ be a finite type, $d:\iota\times\iota\to\N$ satisfy $d(i,i)=0$ and
the triangle inequality, and let $C:\iota\times\iota\to\R$ satisfy
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $C_{ij}\ge0$ for all $i,j$;
\item[(ii)] $C_{ij}=0$ whenever $d(i,j)>1$;
\item[(iii)] $\sum_j C_{ij}\le\alpha$ for all $i$, with $0\le\alpha<1$.
\end{enumerate}
Then for all $i,j$ and every $N\in\N$,
\[
  \sum_{n\in[0,N)}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\;\le\;\frac{\alpha^{\,d(i,j)}}{1-\alpha},
\]
and consequently $\sum_{n}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\le\alpha^{\,d(i,j)}/(1-\alpha)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Three steps, each an induction on $n$.  Nonnegativity is preserved by powers.
Row sums multiply: expanding $(C^{n+1})$ and exchanging the two finite sums,
$\sum_j(C^{n+1})_{ij}=\sum_k(C^{n})_{ik}\sum_jC_{kj}\le\alpha\sum_k(C^{n})_{ik}
\le\alpha^{n+1}$, so each entry, being at most its row sum, is at most
$\alpha^{n}$.  Support: if $n<d(i,j)$ then $(C^{n})_{ij}=0$, since in
$(C^{n+1})_{ij}=\sum_k(C^{n})_{ik}C_{kj}$ each $k$ has either $d(i,k)>n$, killing
the first factor by induction, or $d(i,k)\le n$, whence
$d(i,j)\le d(i,k)+d(k,j)\le n+d(k,j)$ forces $d(k,j)>1$ and kills the second.
Combining, $(C^{n})_{ij}\le\alpha^{n}$ when $n\ge d(i,j)$ and vanishes otherwise;
splitting $[0,N)$ at $d(i,j)$ and reindexing the surviving block by
$n=d(i,j)+m$ gives $\alpha^{d(i,j)}\sum_{m<N-d(i,j)}\alpha^{m}$, and the
geometric sum is at most $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[where the volume-freeness is]
The cardinality of $\iota$ appears nowhere in the hypotheses or the conclusion.
That is the entire content of the theorem: the index type may be as large as one
likes and neither $\alpha$ nor the exponent notices.  Every constant is read off
single-site data.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the hypothesis that matters here]
\label{rem:crack}
Hypothesis (iii) asks for row sums \emph{bounded} by $\alpha$.  It never asks for
them to be constant.  Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruction} refutes constancy and
leaves (iii) untouched: it is precisely the crack through which the coupled
kernel passes after Schur's test has died.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[non-vacuity, machine-checked]
\label{prop:nonvacuity}
On the three-point chain with $d$ the graph distance and $C_{ij}=1/4$ for
$d(i,j)\le1$ and $0$ otherwise, hypotheses (i)--(iii) hold with $\alpha=3/4<1$,
the matrix is not the zero matrix, the two endpoints are at distance exactly
two, and the conclusion reads: the whole resolvent series between the endpoints
is at most $9/4$.
\end{proposition}

The witness is not decoration.  Hypotheses (ii) and (iii) are jointly
satisfiable by the zero matrix, for which the conclusion is trivial, and by data
with $d\equiv0$, for which the exponent is trivial.  Proposition
\ref{prop:nonvacuity} excludes both.

\subsection{The composition}

\begin{corollary}[machine-checked]
\label{cor:chain}
Let $J$ be bond strengths on a finite set of sites with a distance $d$ satisfying
$d(i,i)=0$ and the triangle inequality, and let $C$ be the matrix of
Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix}.  If every row sum of $C$ is at most $\alpha<1$, then
for all $i,j$ and every $N$,
\[
  \sum_{n<N}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\;\le\;\frac{\alpha^{\,d(i,j)}}{1-\alpha},
\]
and the same holds for the full series.
\end{corollary}

This is the whole chain in one line: the envelope of
Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff} enters the matrix of Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix}, which
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:d1}, whose conclusion mentions no
cardinality.  In Lean it is \texttt{dobrushin\_resolvent\_bound}, and its proof
is the application of one theorem to three others.

They are consistent with the degeneracy being a property of the \emph{ordered}

and control of the extrapolator, none of which is attempted here.  A reader
should take from this section that the wall is \emph{not} where the development
had been treating it as being, and nothing about where it is.

non-zero on failure; none bundles a theorem with its witness; the bound gate
predicts a number rather than sampling.  One of them was later found not to
exercise the hypothesis of Remark~\ref{rem:crack} --- it normalised every row to
\emph{exactly} $\alpha$ --- and was redesigned to carry unequal row sums,
strictly harder, declared in its own docstring and committed before being run.
The gates emit a verdict only after an explicit counter confirms that the
expected number of checks actually executed, because an empty failure list is
also what zero checks produce.  No acceptance decision anywhere in this lane
depends on a Python \texttt{assert}.

\section{What remains, named exactly}
\label{sec:frontier}

Theorems~\ref{thm:coeff}--\ref{thm:d1} are an engine, not a conclusion.  Turning
them into a volume-uniform statement about $\specRatio$ requires, in order:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{1.}] a valid single-site interdependence majorant, and the
      sufficient window it produces.  \textbf{Done}: Theorems~\ref{thm:coeff}
      and~\ref{thm:matrix}, machine-checked, with the window computed rather
      than asserted, and holding at every site of every volume with the given
      local geometry.  Not done, and not needed: the \emph{minimal}
      interdependence matrix of any particular system;
\item[\textbf{2.}] Dobrushin's comparison estimate \cite{Dobrushin1970,Simon1993}
      --- that the connected correlations of the Gibbs measure are controlled by
      the resolvent of $C$, hence decay exponentially at a rate free of the
      extent.  \textbf{Open, and this is the bottleneck};
\item[\textbf{3.}] transport of that decay into the operator formulation.
\end{enumerate}

Step~2 is the only \emph{new analytic estimate} currently identified, and nothing
in this paper reduces its difficulty.  It is not the only remaining work: the
downstream consumer exists but its instantiation still needs the finite-time
interface described below, so ``one obligation left'' would be too tidy a
sentence for the actual state.  One structural remark, offered as
orientation and not as progress: in this model the configuration space of a
finite system is finite, so the conditionals are ratios of finite sums and the
comparison estimate does not require the measure-theoretic apparatus in which
it is usually stated.  Whether that makes it tractable is not established here.


\textbf{The consumer, named.}  It is \texttt{volumeUniform\_gap} in
\repofile{YangMills/OS/TransferGap.lean}{YangMills/OS/TransferGap.lean}, and its
signature, read off the elaborated declaration rather than from memory, is: for a
family of real inner-product spaces $H_i$ indexed by $\iota$, transfer operators
$T_i$ and vectors $\Omega_i$ with \texttt{VacuumTransfer}~$T_i~\Omega_i$ for every
$i$, and a rate $0<r<1$, the hypothesis
\[
  \forall i,\;\forall v\in H_i,\;\exists C,\;\forall n,\quad
  \bigl|\texttt{connCorr}\,T_i\,\Omega_i\,v\,n\bigr|\;\le\;C\,r^{\,n}
\]
yields $\exists m>0$ with
$\|\texttt{projectedTransfer}\,T_i\,\Omega_i\|\le e^{-m}$ for every $i$.  It
takes, over a family of finite volumes, decay at a \emph{common} rate and returns
a \emph{common} mass.  Its quantifier order is worth stating because it is
load-bearing: the
prefactor $C$ may depend on the volume and on the observable, and only $r$ is
shared.  The development's own standing objection --- that a bound
$C(L)\rho^{N}$ with $C(L)\to\infty$ says nothing --- therefore does not apply to
it, the conclusion being an operator-norm bound.

A design decision is recorded here rather than discovered later: the vacuum
pairing is an infinite-time object, so step~3 must proceed through finite time
extent with explicit boundary control, taking the limit only at the level of the
bound.  No infinite-volume state is constructed anywhere in this paper, and the
word \emph{clustering} is accordingly not used for one.

\section{Prior art, and the exact delta}
\label{sec:prior}

\textbf{Dobrushin's uniqueness theorem is classical}
\cite{Dobrushin1968,Dobrushin1970,Simon1993} and nothing in this paper
is new mathematics.  We locate the contribution precisely.

\emph{Not explicit constants.}  The cell here is the anisotropic
two-dimensional Ising model, whose transfer spectrum has been available in
closed form since Onsager \cite{Onsager1944} and Kaufman \cite{Kaufman1949}.  A constant obtained from Dobrushin's
condition is \emph{worse} than the exact answer --- on the cells we measured, by
a factor between roughly two and thirty-six.  Any claim to have improved a
constant for this model would be false, and we make none.

\emph{Generality, instead.}  Dobrushin's coefficient is read off the single-site
conditionals of an \emph{arbitrary} positive finite-range slice weight, which is
what the development's Gibbs weight quantifies over, and where the exact
solution is silent.

\emph{Mechanisation.}  A search conducted on 1 August 2026 over the public
proof-assistant repositories and the arXiv listings for Lean, Coq and
Isabelle returned no mechanisation of the finite-lattice Dobrushin
interdependence matrix or of the comparison estimate.  We state it that
narrowly on purpose: the name \emph{Dobrushin} attaches to several distinct
results, and mechanised work exists that uses others of them, so a blanket
claim of first formalisation would be both unsupported by the search we ran and
ambiguous about which theorem it referred to.  The
same search does return a Lean~4 formalisation of the transverse-field Ising
chain \cite{LeanIsing2026}, with momentum-mode decomposition and a
Jordan--Wigner transformation; it
concerns the quantum chain rather than the classical two-dimensional transfer
matrix, and displaces nothing here, but it means a blanket claim that the Ising
model has not been formalised would not survive review, and we make none.  A
formalisation of the Perron--Frobenius theorem for irreducible nonnegative
matrices also exists \cite{Mathlib2020}, as does a mechanisation of the
Osterwalder--Schrader axioms for the Gaussian free field \cite{OSforGFF2026}.  Reflection positivity and finite-lattice spectra for
related models have been treated without mechanisation \cite{WashburnSimons2026}.

\section{Limitations}

All three theorems are elementary.  Theorem~\ref{thm:d1} is three inductions;
Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff} is a hyperbolic identity and a bound on a denominator;
Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix} is a construction and a finite computation.  Their value
is that they are machine-checked, non-vacuous, composed, and carry the hypothesis
the dead method violated --- not that any of them is deep.

Section~\ref{sec:measurement} is numerics at $L\le12$ with an Aitken
extrapolation.  It refutes an attribution; it does not prove where the wall is,
and an extrapolation can mislead near a transition.  The window is not optimal
and is never called sharp.

\textbf{Step~2 of Section~\ref{sec:frontier} is open}, and until it closes the
volume-uniform statement about $\specRatio$ for the coupled kernel remains
unproved.  This is the substantive limitation and the chain above does not
shorten it.  What the chain does is close the envelope-to-resolvent segment and
isolate the comparison estimate as the only currently identified new analytic
estimate, while leaving the finite-time instantiation work explicit --- not
reduce the remaining programme to a single item.

\textbf{Anchor.}  All sources cited above are read at commit
\nolinkurl{c3d8e32d2eecf85cf2dba26ce9b0a8bb42005c9c} of

\textbf{Job counts, and how each delta was obtained.}  The development machine
measured the core at $8465$ jobs with Theorem~\ref{thm:d1}'s module removed from

Theorems~\ref{thm:coeff} and~\ref{thm:matrix} added, the Linux plane measures
$8468$ with zero errors: the $+2$ is the difference between two measurements on

with exit code zero over $2817$ axiom reports and \textbf{zero} occurrences of
\texttt{sorryAx}.  The accounting, spelled out because a count of \emph{newly

Theorem~\ref{thm:d1}'s module, present since the first anchor, are also in the

\texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}: Lean's three standard axioms,
no project axiom, and no \texttt{sorry} anywhere.

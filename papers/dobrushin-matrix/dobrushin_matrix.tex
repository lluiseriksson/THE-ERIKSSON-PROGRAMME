\title{The Row Sums Were the Method, Not the Theorem:\\
a Machine-Checked Volume-Free Resolvent Bound,\\
and a Misattributed Uniformity Wall}
\author{Lluis Eriksson}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A formal development of a two-dimensional spatial transfer kernel proves its
uniform spectral statements by Schur's test on \emph{constant} row sums, and
carries a theorem, labelled \textsc{the obstruction} in its own source, stating
that those row sums stop being constant the moment the spatial coupling is
switched on.  That theorem obstructs the \emph{method}.  It was read as
obstructing the \emph{conclusion}, and the reading outlived its own retraction.

We prove, in Lean~4 with no \texttt{sorry} and no project axiom, an elementary
resolvent bound whose hypothesis asks for row sums \emph{bounded} by
$\alpha<1$ and never constant: for a nonnegative matrix $C$ over a finite index
type, supported on pairs at distance at most one,
\[
  \sum_{n<N}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\;\le\;\frac{\alpha^{\dist(i,j)}}{1-\alpha}
  \qquad\text{for every }N,
\]
with no reference of any kind to the cardinality of the index type.  We exhibit
a non-vacuity witness and record the oracle.

Separately, and by measurement rather than proof, we remove the wall from where
it was being placed.  For the coupled kernel the second-eigenvalue ratio
$\specRatio(L)$ saturates strictly below one in each of six cells tested inside
Onsager's disordered region --- with the spatial weight switched on --- and
reaches one to six decimal places in the two ordered cells tested.  The
control at zero spatial coupling reproduces the \emph{proved} identity
$\specRatio=\tanh\beta$ to six decimals, so the harness is calibrated against a
theorem rather than against itself.  These data are eight cells at $L\le12$ with
an extrapolation: they refute the attribution of the degeneracy to the spatial
weight, and they do \emph{not} locate the transition, which would need a dense
sweep and finite-size scaling that we do not attempt.

\textbf{What is not proved.}  The volume-uniform bound on $\specRatio$ for the
coupled kernel is \emph{not} established here.  Dobrushin's uniqueness theorem is
classical \cite{Dobrushin1968,Simon1993}; nothing in this paper is new
mathematics.  What is offered is a
mechanised, non-vacuous ingredient, a measured relocation of a wall, and an
exactly named remaining obligation.  No consequence for Yang--Mills theory is
stated, suggested, or implied.
\end{abstract}

\section{The setting, and the theorem that was read too widely}
\label{sec:reading}

Fix an extent $L$ and consider configurations $\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{L}$ of one
slice.  The development under discussion carries a time-bond kernel that
factorises over sites,
\[
  K_\beta(\sigma,\tau)\;=\;\prod_{j<L} b_\beta(\sigma_j,\tau_j),
  \qquad b_\beta(s,s')=e^{\pm\beta},
\]
and an arbitrary strictly positive slice weight $w$, the Gibbs weight of a
space-time configuration being the product of the slice weights and the time
bonds.  When $w$ is itself a product over spatial bonds with coupling $\gamma$,
the system is the anisotropic two-dimensional Ising model.

Two facts from that development matter here.

\begin{proposition}[proved elsewhere in the development]
\label{prop:decoupled}
At $\gamma=0$ and for every $L\ge1$, $\specRatio=\tanh\beta$ exactly.
\end{proposition}

The proof is Schur's test \cite{Schur1911}: the decoupled kernel has \emph{constant} row sums, and
the loss on the odd part of an observable is exactly compensated by the odd bond
eigenvalue at every step.

\begin{proposition}[\texttt{coupled\_rowSums\_not\_constant}]
\label{prop:obstruction}
At $\gamma>0$ the row sums of the coupled kernel are not constant: two explicit
configurations of a two-site slice have different row sums.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruction} is labelled \textsc{the obstruction} in the
source.  \emph{It obstructs Schur's test.}  It says nothing whatever about
$\specRatio$, it is proved on a two-site witness, and no statement quantified
over $L$ appears in it.

A stronger reading --- that the spatial weight destroys uniformity --- was
entered into the development and then \emph{retracted of record} as unproved,
leaving behind only a measurement of degeneracy.

\textbf{What is documented, and can be checked.}  The retraction of the sentence
from the manuscript is recorded at Addendum~561 of the development's
verification ledger; its removal from the module docstring, which the first
retraction had missed, at Addendum~563; and its survival in a hand-typed
submission form, caught only because the form was read in full before delivery,
at Addendum~568.  Three removals were required for one sentence, and the third
was the one that would have been published.

\textbf{What is inference, and is labelled as such.}  We observe that no
statement quantified over $L$ for the coupled kernel was attempted in the
interval, and we attribute this to the retracted reading continuing to govern
target selection.  That attribution is ours; it is not established by a
diff, and no artefact in the development records a decision not to attempt
something.  The verifiable half of the observation is the narrower one, and it
is enough for this paper: the guards that caught the sentence three times
compare identifiers and substrings inside files, so whatever else was carrying
the reading was not a file.

\section{The volume-free resolvent bound}

\begin{theorem}[machine-checked]
\label{thm:d1}
Let $\iota$ be a finite type, $d:\iota\times\iota\to\N$ satisfy $d(i,i)=0$ and
the triangle inequality, and let $C:\iota\times\iota\to\R$ satisfy
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $C_{ij}\ge0$ for all $i,j$;
\item[(ii)] $C_{ij}=0$ whenever $d(i,j)>1$;
\item[(iii)] $\sum_j C_{ij}\le\alpha$ for all $i$, with $0\le\alpha<1$.
\end{enumerate}
Then for all $i,j$ and every $N\in\N$,
\[
  \sum_{n\in[0,N)}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\;\le\;\frac{\alpha^{\,d(i,j)}}{1-\alpha},
\]
and consequently $\sum_{n}(C^{\,n})_{ij}\le\alpha^{\,d(i,j)}/(1-\alpha)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Three steps, each an induction on $n$.  Nonnegativity is preserved by powers.
Row sums multiply: expanding $(C^{n+1})$ and exchanging the two finite sums,
$\sum_j(C^{n+1})_{ij}=\sum_k(C^{n})_{ik}\sum_jC_{kj}\le\alpha\sum_k(C^{n})_{ik}
\le\alpha^{n+1}$, so each entry, being at most its row sum, is at most
$\alpha^{n}$.  Support: if $n<d(i,j)$ then $(C^{n})_{ij}=0$, since in
$(C^{n+1})_{ij}=\sum_k(C^{n})_{ik}C_{kj}$ each $k$ has either $d(i,k)>n$, killing
the first factor by induction, or $d(i,k)\le n$, whence
$d(i,j)\le d(i,k)+d(k,j)\le n+d(k,j)$ forces $d(k,j)>1$ and kills the second.
Combining, $(C^{n})_{ij}\le\alpha^{n}$ when $n\ge d(i,j)$ and vanishes otherwise;
splitting $[0,N)$ at $d(i,j)$ and reindexing the surviving block by
$n=d(i,j)+m$ gives $\alpha^{d(i,j)}\sum_{m<N-d(i,j)}\alpha^{m}$, and the
geometric sum is at most $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[where the volume-freeness is]
The cardinality of $\iota$ appears nowhere in the hypotheses or the conclusion.
That is the entire content of the theorem: the index type may be as large as one
likes and neither $\alpha$ nor the exponent notices.  Every constant is read off
single-site data.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the hypothesis that matters here]
\label{rem:crack}
Hypothesis (iii) asks for row sums \emph{bounded} by $\alpha$.  It never asks for
them to be constant.  Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruction} refutes constancy and
leaves (iii) untouched: it is precisely the crack through which the coupled
kernel passes after Schur's test has died.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[non-vacuity, machine-checked]
\label{prop:nonvacuity}
On the three-point chain with $d$ the graph distance and $C_{ij}=1/4$ for
$d(i,j)\le1$ and $0$ otherwise, hypotheses (i)--(iii) hold with $\alpha=3/4<1$,
the matrix is not the zero matrix, the two endpoints are at distance exactly
two, and the conclusion reads: the whole resolvent series between the endpoints
is at most $9/4$.
\end{proposition}

The witness is not decoration.  Hypotheses (ii) and (iii) are jointly
satisfiable by the zero matrix, for which the conclusion is trivial, and by data
with $d\equiv0$, for which the exponent is trivial.  Proposition
\ref{prop:nonvacuity} excludes both.

is used by Section~3.

\noindent
$r(\infty)$ is an Aitken extrapolation of the last three extents.  Values above
one in the ordered rows are extrapolation overshoot at the sixth decimal and are

Each of the six cells tested inside Onsager's disordered region,
$\sinh 2\beta\,\sinh 2\gamma<1$, saturates strictly below one, with increment
ratios near $0.6$; both ordered cells tested reach one to six decimal places.
The control at $\gamma=0$ reproduces $\tanh\beta$ to six decimals, which is
Proposition~\ref{prop:decoupled} --- the harness is calibrated against a theorem
of the development rather than against itself.

\textbf{What these data support, stated at their strength and no further.}
They are consistent with the degeneracy being a property of the \emph{ordered}
phase, and they are \emph{not} consistent with the degeneracy being caused by
switching on the spatial weight: six disordered cells with the weight switched
on fail to degenerate.  That second, negative reading is the one this section is
for, and it is the one that reopened the question.

\textbf{What they do not support.}  They do not locate the transition.  Eight
cells at $L\le12$, with a three-term Aitken extrapolation whose overshoot is
visible in the ordered rows above, cannot place a phase boundary; doing so would
require a dense sweep near $\sinh2\beta\,\sinh2\gamma=1$, finite-size scaling,
and control of the extrapolator, none of which is attempted here.  A reader
should take from this section that the wall is \emph{not} where the development
had been treating it as being, and nothing about where it is.

\textbf{Gates.}  Three gates were registered in
\repofile{scripts/judge\_dobrushin.py}{scripts/judge\_dobrushin.py} together with
the lane charter, before a line of the Lean of Section~3 was written.  Each exits
non-zero on failure; none bundles a theorem with its witness; the bound gate
predicts a number rather than sampling.  One of them was later found not to
exercise the hypothesis of Remark~\ref{rem:crack} --- it normalised every row to
\emph{exactly} $\alpha$ --- and was redesigned to carry unequal row sums,
strictly harder, declared in its own docstring and committed before being run.
The gates emit a verdict only after an explicit counter confirms that the
expected number of checks actually executed, because an empty failure list is
also what zero checks produce.  No acceptance decision anywhere in this lane
depends on a Python \texttt{assert}.

\section{What remains, named exactly}
\label{sec:frontier}

Theorem~\ref{thm:d1} is an engine, not a conclusion.  Turning it into a
volume-uniform statement about $\specRatio$ requires, in order:

\begin{enumerate}
\item the single-site Dobrushin coefficient of the model, giving the explicit
      window $2\tanh\beta+2\tanh\gamma<1$;
\item Dobrushin's comparison estimate \cite{Dobrushin1970,Simon1993} --- exponential decay of connected
      correlations at a rate free of the extent.  \textbf{This is the
      bottleneck}; everything else is assembly;
\item transport of that decay into the operator formulation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Step 1, measured but not proved}

A two-state site in field $h$ has conditional $P(+1)=(1+\tanh h)/2$, and flipping
one neighbour across a bond of strength $J$ moves that field to $h-2J$.  The
claim of step~1 is the identity
\[
  \sup_{h\in\R}\bigl|P_h(+1)-P_{h-2J}(+1)\bigr| \;=\; \tanh|J|,
  \qquad\text{attained at } h=J .
\]
A gate predicting this \emph{identity} --- not a range, and not a threshold
chosen afterwards --- was committed before the quantity was computed even once.
It checks the attained value against $\tanh|J|$, checks that a sweep of
$2\cdot10^{5}+1$ fields never beats the attained value, and fails on any
violation.

\textbf{The Lean proof of this identity was attempted and does not compile.}
The intended mechanism is
$\tanh a-\tanh b=\sinh(a-b)/(\cosh a\cosh b)$ together with
$\cosh a\cosh b\ge\cosh^{2}\!\bigl((a-b)/2\bigr)$, which minimises the
denominator exactly at fields symmetric about zero; the attempt fails on library
naming and on several tactic steps, and is recorded with its diagnosis rather
than removed.  Consequently step~1 carries the label \emph{measured}, not
\emph{proved}, and no theorem of this paper depends on it.

Sharpness is the reason the gate checks attainment separately.  A coefficient
that were merely an upper bound would leave the window undetermined at its own
boundary; the supremum being reached is what would make $\tanh|J|$ the constant
rather than a number above it.

The consumer already exists in the development.  It takes, over a family of
finite volumes, decay at a \emph{common} rate $r<1$ and returns a \emph{common}
mass $m>0$ with $\|\text{projected transfer}\|\le e^{-m}$ for every member of the
family.  Its quantifier order is worth stating because it is load-bearing: the
prefactor $C$ may depend on the volume and on the observable, and only $r$ is
shared.  The development's own standing objection --- that a bound
$C(L)\rho^{N}$ with $C(L)\to\infty$ says nothing --- therefore does not apply to
it, the conclusion being an operator-norm bound.

A design decision is recorded here rather than discovered later: the vacuum
pairing is an infinite-time object, so step~3 must proceed through finite time
extent with explicit boundary control, taking the limit only at the level of the
bound.  No infinite-volume state is constructed anywhere in this paper, and the
word \emph{clustering} is accordingly not used for one.

\section{Prior art, and the exact delta}
\label{sec:prior}

\textbf{Dobrushin's uniqueness theorem is classical}
\cite{Dobrushin1968,Dobrushin1970,Simon1993} and nothing in this paper
is new mathematics.  We locate the contribution precisely.

\emph{Not explicit constants.}  The cell here is the anisotropic
two-dimensional Ising model, whose transfer spectrum has been available in
closed form since Onsager \cite{Onsager1944} and Kaufman \cite{Kaufman1949}.  A constant obtained from Dobrushin's
condition is \emph{worse} than the exact answer --- on the cells we measured, by
a factor between roughly two and thirty-six.  Any claim to have improved a
constant for this model would be false, and we make none.

\emph{Generality, instead.}  Dobrushin's coefficient is read off the single-site
conditionals of an \emph{arbitrary} positive finite-range slice weight, which is
what the development's Gibbs weight quantifies over, and where the exact
solution is silent.

\emph{Mechanisation.}  A search conducted on 1 August 2026 returned no
formalisation of Dobrushin's uniqueness condition in any proof assistant.  The
same search does return a Lean~4 formalisation of the transverse-field Ising
chain \cite{LeanIsing2026}, with momentum-mode decomposition and a
Jordan--Wigner transformation; it
concerns the quantum chain rather than the classical two-dimensional transfer
matrix, and displaces nothing here, but it means a blanket claim that the Ising
model has not been formalised would not survive review, and we make none.  A
formalisation of the Perron--Frobenius theorem for irreducible nonnegative
matrices also exists \cite{Mathlib2020}, as does a mechanisation of the
Osterwalder--Schrader axioms for the Gaussian free field \cite{OSforGFF2026}.  Reflection positivity and finite-lattice spectra for
related models have been treated without mechanisation \cite{WashburnSimons2026}.

\section{Limitations}

Theorem~\ref{thm:d1} is elementary and its proof is three inductions; its value
is that it is machine-checked, non-vacuous, and has the hypothesis the dead
method violated.  Section~\ref{sec:measurement} is numerics at $L\le12$ with an
Aitken extrapolation: it locates a wall, it does not prove where the wall is,
and an extrapolation can mislead near a transition.  The window of
Section~\ref{sec:frontier} is not optimal and is never called sharp.  Step~2 of
Section~\ref{sec:frontier} is open, and until it closes, the volume-uniform
statement about $\specRatio$ for the coupled kernel remains unproved.

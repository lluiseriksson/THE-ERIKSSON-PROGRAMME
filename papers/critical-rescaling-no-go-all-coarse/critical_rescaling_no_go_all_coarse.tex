\begin{abstract}
Let the fine periodic lattice have side $LN'$ and let $Q_L$ be the
$L^{-d}$-normalized block average of length-$L$ line integrals.  In four
dimensions, the rescaling $Q_L\mapsto LQ_L$ exactly removes the elementary
constant-field scaling obstruction to a volume-uniform Poincar\'e estimate.
We prove that it does not remove the full-space obstruction.  For every
positive coarse side $N'$, every even block side $L=2M$, every pair of
distinct directions $i\ne j$, and every internal vector $w$, we construct a
block-periodic transverse one-cochain $A_{i,j,w}$.  It satisfies
\[
 Q_LA_{i,j,w}=0,\qquad \operatorname{div}A_{i,j,w}=0,
\]
and its flat Hodge energy and norm are exactly
\[
 \ip{A_{i,j,w}}{K_0A_{i,j,w}}
 =8N'(LN')^{d-1}\norm{w}^{2},\qquad
 \norm{A_{i,j,w}}^{2}=(LN')^d\norm{w}^{2}.
\]
Thus the Rayleigh quotient of
$K_0+(LQ_L)^*(LQ_L)$ on this family is $8/L$, independently of $N'$.
For $N_c\ge2$, the current constant-before-volume critical coercivity gate is
therefore false on every positive coarse torus.  More generally, no scalar
rescaling $s_LQ_L$ can control this family, because it lies in the exact
kernel of $Q_L$.

The construction, energy count, and quantified no-go theorem are formalized
in Lean~4 against pinned Mathlib.  A focused six-declaration axiom audit
reports only \lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice}, and
\lean{Quot.sound}; there are no project axioms or \lean{sorry}.  The result
does not disprove repaired sector or quotient estimates and does not address
an interacting Hessian, an infinite-volume limit, a continuum limit, or a
Yang--Mills mass gap.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Finite-range coercive operators admit Combes--Thomas decay estimates whose
rates are controlled by the lower spectral edge~\cite{CombesThomas}.  In a
block-spin treatment of lattice gauge fields, a natural candidate precision
is a flat Hodge operator plus a positive block penalty.  At a fixed volume
this is a standard finite-dimensional mechanism.  The difficult question is
whether its coercivity constant survives uniformly as the block side tends
to infinity.

The block map considered here is the linearized line-integral average used in
lattice gauge renormalization~\cite{BalabanI,BalabanII}.  With its unscaled
normalization, constant one-cochains force a growing Poincar\'e constant in
dimension four.  Multiplying the map by the critical factor $L$ repairs that
specific scaling: on direction-wise constant fields, $LQ_L$ is isometric at
the level relevant to the four-dimensional norm count.  This makes the
following proposal mathematically natural:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gate-intro}
 \norm{A}^{2}\le
 \CP\left(\ip{A}{K_0A}+\norm{LQ_LA}^{2}\right),
 \qquad \CP\text{ independent of }L.
\end{equation}

The purpose of this paper is to settle that proposal on its stated full
domain.  The answer is negative for a structural reason.  The block map has
transverse low-energy modes in its exact kernel.  Repeating a mean-zero
square profile within every coarse block makes the cancellation local to
each block, so the obstruction persists for every positive coarse side
$N'$, rather than only for the one-site coarse torus.  Its energy is carried
by two interfaces per block, while its norm is carried by the entire volume;
the resulting quotient is exactly $8/L$.

Three features distinguish the result from a heuristic low-momentum
argument.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The witness is an explicit periodic cochain on the exact finite lattice
  consumed by the block map.
\item Both kernel identities and the Hodge energy are exact, including all
  finite-volume factors.
\item The constant is quantified before the volume, and the contradiction is
  formalized at that quantifier order.
\end{enumerate}

The theorem closes the present full-space flat route.  It does not close the
physically different alternatives of passing to a justified sector or
quotient, or adding a positive interacting term that lifts the transverse
family.  That distinction is essential: a no-go theorem is useful precisely
when it identifies which input must change.

\section{Finite periodic lattice and the critical block form}

\subsection{One-cochains and the flat Hodge operator}

Fix integers $d\ge2$, $L\ge1$, $N'\ge1$, and $N_c\ge1$.  The fine site set is
\[
 \Lambda_{L,N'}=\left(\Z/(LN')\Z\right)^d,
\]
and a positive bond is a pair $(x,\mu)$ with $x\in\Lambda_{L,N'}$ and
$\mu\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}$.  The internal coordinate space is
\[
 E_{N_c}=\R^{N_c^2-1},
\]
the coordinate model used by the formalization for $\su(N_c)$.  A physical
one-cochain is a function $A(x,\mu)\in E_{N_c}$ with the finite $\ell^2$
norm
\[
 \norm{A}^2=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{L,N'}}\sum_{\mu=0}^{d-1}
 \norm{A(x,\mu)}^2.
\]

At the trivial gauge background, let $D_1$ be the discrete covariant exterior
derivative on ordered plaquettes and let $\delta$ be the gauge constraint
(discrete divergence).  The flat Hodge operator $K_0$ is normalized so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hodge-form}
 \ip{A}{K_0A}=\norm{D_1A}^2+\norm{\delta A}^2.
\end{equation}
Only this quadratic-form identity is needed below.  The formal development
constructs $D_1$, $\delta$, and $K_0$ as continuous linear maps on the finite
Hilbert spaces, rather than postulating~\eqref{eq:hodge-form}.

\subsection{The line-integral block map}

Write $B_y$ for the fine block associated with a coarse site
$y\in(\Z/N'\Z)^d$.  It contains the sites
\[
 x=Ly+r,\qquad r\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^d.
\]
For a coarse positive bond $(y,\mu)$, the linearized block map is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-map}
 (Q_LA)(y,\mu)=L^{-d}\sum_{x\in B_y}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}
 A(x+ke_\mu,\mu).
\end{equation}
Thus $Q_L$ averages, over the source block, the length-$L$ straight line
integral in the coarse bond direction.  The critical four-dimensional
rescaling is
\[
 \widetilde Q_L=LQ_L.
\]
For a scalar sequence $s_L$, set more generally
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precision}
 H_{L,s}=K_0+(s_LQ_L)^*(s_LQ_L).
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[critical full-space gate]\label{def:gate}
In $d=4$, for fixed $N'$ and $N_c$, the critical gate asserts that there is
a number $\CP>0$, chosen independently of $L$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gate}
 \norm{A}^2\le \CP\left(
 \ip{A}{K_0A}+\norm{LQ_LA}^2\right)
\end{equation}
for every positive block side $L$ and every fine one-cochain $A$.
\end{definition}

The quantifier order is part of the claim.  At any fixed $L$ the problem is
finite-dimensional, whereas Definition~\ref{def:gate} asks one constant to
work before all volumes are chosen.

\section{A block-periodic transverse sector}

Let $L=2M$ with $M\ge1$.  Define the square sign on the within-block cycle by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-sign}
 \sigma_M(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 +1,&0\le r<M,\\
 -1,&M\le r<2M,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad r\in\Z/(2M)\Z,
\end{equation}
and extend it to the full fine cycle by block periodicity:
\[
 \sigma_{M,N'}(m)=\sigma_M(m\bmod 2M),
 \qquad m\in\Z/(2MN')\Z.
\]
The two elementary identities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-identities}
 \sigma_{M,N'}(m)^2=1,
 \qquad
 \sum_{r=0}^{2M-1}\sigma_M(r)=0.
\end{equation}

Choose distinct directions $i\ne j$ and $w\in E_{N_c}$.  Define the
one-cochain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witness}
 A_{i,j,w}(x,\mu)=
 \begin{cases}
 \sigma_{M,N'}(x_j)w,&\mu=i,\\
 0,&\mu\ne i.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The field points in direction $i$ and varies only in the transverse direction
$j$.  For a fixed phase of the square wave, the collection
\[
 \mathcal T_{M,N'}=\operatorname{span}
 \{A_{i,j,w}:i\ne j,\ w\in E_{N_c}\}
\]
is the explicit obstructive family used in this paper.  We do not claim that
$\mathcal T_{M,N'}$ is the entire kernel or the entire low-energy spectral
subspace.

\begin{lemma}[exact norm]\label{lem:norm}
For every $i\ne j$ and $w\in E_{N_c}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
 \norm{A_{i,j,w}}^2=(LN')^d\norm{w}^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Only bonds in direction $i$ contribute.  There is one such bond at each of
the $(LN')^d$ fine sites, and every coefficient has squared magnitude one by
\eqref{eq:square-identities}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[blockwise kernel identity]\label{lem:block-kernel}
For every positive coarse side $N'$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qzero}
 Q_LA_{i,j,w}=0.
\end{equation}
Consequently $(s_LQ_L)A_{i,j,w}=0$ for every scalar $s_L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If the coarse bond direction is not $i$, every summand in
\eqref{eq:block-map} vanishes.  In direction $i$, shifting along the line does
not change the transverse coordinate $x_j$.  The line sum therefore
contributes a factor $L$, but the sum over the $j$-offset inside the source
block contains
\[
 \sum_{r_j=0}^{L-1}\sigma_M(r_j)=0.
\]
This cancellation occurs separately in every source block.  It is therefore
independent of the number $N'$ of blocks along each direction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[transverse divergence]\label{lem:divzero}
The witness is divergence-free at the trivial background:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divzero}
 \delta A_{i,j,w}=0.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Only the $i$ component can enter the divergence.  That component is unchanged
by a forward or backward shift in direction $i$, because it depends only on
$x_j$ and $i\ne j$.
\end{proof}

The preceding two lemmas locate the obstruction: it lies simultaneously in
the block-map kernel and the flat gauge-constraint kernel.  Its remaining
energy is purely curvature energy.

\section{Exact interface count and Rayleigh quotient}

Let $S$ be the successor map on the full fine cycle of length $LN'=2MN'$.
There are two sign changes in each block, including the boundary between the
end of one block and the start of the next.  At a sign change the difference
has magnitude $2$ and squared magnitude $4$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycle-count}
 \sum_{m\in\Z/(LN')\Z}
 \left(\sigma_{M,N'}(m)-\sigma_{M,N'}(Sm)\right)^2=8N'.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[finite-volume interface count]\label{lem:box-count}
For any direction $j$,
\begin{align}
 &\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{L,N'}}
 \left(\sigma_{M,N'}(x_j)-
 \sigma_{M,N'}((x+e_j)_j)\right)^2\notag\\
 &\hspace{3.3cm}=8N'(LN')^{d-1}.
 \label{eq:box-count}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Split the finite box into its $j$ coordinate and the remaining $d-1$
coordinates.  Equation~\eqref{eq:cycle-count} gives the first factor; the
remaining coordinates give $(LN')^{d-1}$ identical copies.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[exact flat Hodge energy]\label{prop:hodge-energy}
For $i\ne j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hodge-energy}
 \ip{A_{i,j,w}}{K_0A_{i,j,w}}
 =8N'(LN')^{d-1}\norm{w}^2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:divzero}, the divergence term in
\eqref{eq:hodge-form} vanishes.  In the plaquette curl, every ordered
direction pair except the pair containing $i$ and $j$ cancels: either the
field component is zero, or the transverse profile is unchanged by the
shift.  On the $(i,j)$ plaquettes, the squared norm is exactly the scalar
square difference in Lemma~\ref{lem:box-count} times $\norm{w}^2$.  The two
possible orderings of $i$ and $j$ give the same value under the convention
that each geometric plaquette is counted once.  This proves
\eqref{eq:hodge-energy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[exact Rayleigh quotient]\label{thm:rayleigh}
If $w\ne0$, then for every scalar $s_L$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rayleigh}
 \frac{\ip{A_{i,j,w}}{H_{L,s}A_{i,j,w}}}
 {\norm{A_{i,j,w}}^2}=\frac{8}{L}.
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\cL$ denotes the best coercivity constant of
$H_{L,s}$ on the full one-cochain space, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cl-upper}
 \cL\le\frac{8}{L}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:block-kernel} removes the block term in
\eqref{eq:precision}.  Divide Proposition~\ref{prop:hodge-energy} by
Lemma~\ref{lem:norm} and cancel the positive factors $N'$,
$(LN')^{d-1}$, and $\norm{w}^2$.
\end{proof}

The cancellation of $N'$ is the central finite-volume point.  The obstruction
is neither diluted nor repaired by enlarging the coarse torus.  It is repeated
block by block, and the interface and norm counts scale compatibly.

\section{No volume-uniform critical coercivity}

We now specialize to physical dimension $d=4$.  The internal space is
nonzero when $N_c\ge2$, since $N_c^2-1>0$.

\begin{theorem}[all-coarse-volume critical no-go]\label{thm:no-go}
Fix $N'\ge1$, $N_c\ge2$, and any adjoint-model parameter used to define the
flat operators.  There is no $\CP>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-poincare}
 \norm{A}^2\le \CP\left(
 \ip{A}{K_0A}+\norm{LQ_LA}^2\right)
\end{equation}
holds for every block side $L\ge1$ and every fine physical one-cochain $A$.
Equivalently, the proposition
\[
 \lean{VolumeUniformCriticalRescaledFlatPoincareGate}\ N'\ N_c\ \rho
\]
is false for every positive $N'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume that such a $\CP$ exists.  Choose an even block side $L=2M$ and a
nonzero internal vector $w$.  Substituting $A_{i,j,w}$ into
\eqref{eq:uniform-poincare}, using Lemma~\ref{lem:block-kernel}, and applying
\eqref{eq:norm} and \eqref{eq:hodge-energy} gives
\[
 (LN')^4\norm{w}^2
 \le \CP\,8N'(LN')^3\norm{w}^2.
\]
All cancelled quantities are positive, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lbound}
 L\le8\CP.
\end{equation}
The asserted gate supplies the same $\CP$ before all volumes are chosen.
Choose $M$ so that $2M>8\CP$.  This contradicts~\eqref{eq:Lbound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Poincar\'e constants diverge]\label{cor:cp-growth}
At each even scale, every valid full-space constant satisfies
\[
 \CP(L)\ge\frac{L}{8}.
\]
This lower bound is independent of $N'$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[scalar rescaling cannot repair the gate]
Replacing $LQ_L$ by $s_LQ_L$, for an arbitrary real scalar sequence $s_L$,
does not produce full-space volume-uniform coercivity.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The same witness satisfies $(s_LQ_L)A_{i,j,w}=0$ exactly, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:rayleigh} and the contradiction are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Consequence for localization estimates}

Suppose a covariance-root estimate uses a coercivity input $\cL$ and has an
amplitude bounded below by the usual spectral scale $2/\sqrt{\cL}$.  The
witness forces
\[
 \frac{2}{\sqrt{\cL}}\ge\sqrt{\frac{L}{2}}.
\]
Thus no bound built from this full-space coercivity input can have a
volume-uniform root amplitude.  This statement identifies the failing
parameter; it does not invalidate separate finite-range, kernel-amplitude,
ball-count, or tilt-budget estimates.

\section{What the theorem closes, and what it leaves open}

Theorem~\ref{thm:no-go} is a domain diagnosis.  It rules out exactly the
full one-cochain space equipped with the flat Hodge term and a scalar multiple
of the present block map.  Several logically different continuations remain.

\paragraph{Restricted or quotient sector.}
One may remove or quotient the block-periodic transverse sector, provided the
restriction is physically justified and stable under the operations required
by the renormalization step.  Merely declaring the witness absent is not
enough: the repaired domain must be defined, its invariance proved, and its
coercivity established with a constant uniform in $L$.

\paragraph{Additional positive Hessian.}
An interacting Wilson Hessian, or another positive term $V_L$, could lift the
family if it satisfies
\[
 \ip{A_{i,j,w}}{V_LA_{i,j,w}}\ge c_*\norm{A_{i,j,w}}^2
\]
with $c_*>0$ independent of $L$ and $N'$.  Such a statement is outside the
flat calculation and is not assumed here.

\paragraph{Modified block operator.}
A non-scalar modification could in principle see the transverse family.
The corollary above shows why changing only the scalar normalization cannot:
the problem is the exact kernel, not the size of a nonzero singular value.

\paragraph{No continuum claim.}
The argument is finite-dimensional and exact.  It proves neither existence
of an infinite-volume measure nor a continuum limit, and it supplies no
positive Yang--Mills mass gap.  Its role is to prevent a false uniform input
from entering those later arguments.

\section{Conclusion}

Critical rescaling repairs the constant-field normalization but cannot repair
an exact kernel.  The block-periodic transverse family constructed here lies
in that kernel on every positive coarse torus, is divergence-free, and has
only $O(L^{-1})$ flat Hodge energy per unit norm.  Therefore the current
full-space critical block/Hodge form has no volume-uniform positive lower
bound, for any scalar normalization of the block map.

The conclusion is negative but operational: the localization machinery is
not the failing component.  The lower spectral edge is.  Any successful
continuation must alter the physical domain or add an operator that gives
uniform energy to the explicitly identified transverse sector.

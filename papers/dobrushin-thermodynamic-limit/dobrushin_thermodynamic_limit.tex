\begin{abstract}
We give a Lean~4 formalization of a thermodynamic-limit argument for the
anisotropic nearest-neighbour Ising model on odd centred squares.  The new
analytic brick is a comparison theorem for two finite Gibbs specifications on
one product space.  A random-scan telescoping identity and the classical
Dobrushin resolvent turn single-site total-variation defects into an
expectation bound.  When every defect row is at graph distance at least $R$
from the support of a local observable $g$, the result is
\[
  \abs{\mu(g)-\nu(g)}
  \le \frac{\alpha^R}{1-\alpha}\sum_j\delta_j(g),
  \qquad 0\le\alpha<1.
\]
The order of summation is essential: the defect sites are summed inside the
resolvent, so no cardinality of the ambient volume appears.

We then construct exact restriction and reindexing maps between genuine Gibbs
measures on two different squares.  For a kernel $g$ supported in the square
of radius $r$ and $r\le n\le m$, the machine-checked comparison is
\[
  \abs{\E_m g-\E_n g}
  \le
  \frac{\alpha^{n-r}}{1-\alpha},
  \abs{\Lambda_r}\,\osc(g).
\]
It yields convergence of the complete free-boundary sequence, not merely of a
subsequence.  The limit is first packaged as a compatible family of positive,
normalized, real-linear cylinder functionals.  It is independent of the
admissible auxiliary envelope $\alpha$, of every cofinal sampling of the
centred-square sequence, and of enlargement of the declared support.
Finally, a second application of the comparison theorem proves stability under
arbitrary symmetric boundary-row perturbations receding from the support and,
as a concrete corollary, equality of the free and periodic thermodynamic
limits.

We then construct finite-site cylinder presentations directly on $\Z^2$ and
quotient them by equality of the represented function on the full spin space.
Pointwise addition, multiplication and real scalar multiplication descend to
this algebraic local carrier.  Translation of supports gives a literal
additive $\Z^2$ action, including inverses.  Exact cyclic reindexing of every
finite periodic Gibbs sum, combined with the free--periodic limit theorem,
produces a positive normalized real-linear functional $\omega$ satisfying
\[
  \omega(\tau_z f)=\omega(f),\qquad z\in\Z^2.
\]
The real cylinder algebra is then given its intrinsic uniform norm, faithfully
complexified as a star algebra, and represented in the bounded continuous
functions on the product spin space.  The closure is a commutative C$^*$-
algebra, and the cylinder functional extends continuously to a positive complex
linear functional of norm one.  We also construct, for every finite ambient
spin system and every finite conditioning set, the exact Gibbs conditional
kernel.  Lean verifies positivity, normalization, exterior locality,
idempotence, and the finite-volume DLR tower identity by an involutive
reindexing of the finite sum.  The passage of this finite-set identity to the
infinite-volume C$^*$-state is not claimed here.
All concrete results use only
\[
  2\tanh\abs\beta+2\tanh\abs\gamma\le\alpha<1.
\]
No Kotecky--Preiss hypothesis enters.  The parameter region remains the
classical Dobrushin region; no beyond-Dobrushin phase-diagram improvement and
no Yang--Mills consequence is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Dobrushin's uniqueness method replaces a global comparison of Gibbs measures
by a matrix of one-site influences.  Its finite-volume form is classical
\cite{Dobrushin1968,Dobrushin1970}; covariance estimates based on the same
resolvent appear, for example, in Follmer's work \cite{Follmer1982}.  The
mathematical theorem proved here is therefore not a new high-temperature phase
diagram.  The contribution is an end-to-end formal chain whose interfaces are
usually suppressed on paper:
\begin{enumerate}
\item two probability weights and their one-site kernels are compared on a
  common finite product space;
\item differences of conditionals are telescoped site by site;
\item the resolvent is summed in an order that avoids a spurious volume factor;
\item Gibbs measures on genuinely different rectangles are transported to a
  common chart without changing either Hamiltonian or normalization;
\item the resulting pairwise estimate constructs a whole-sequence limit;
\item positivity, normalization, linearity, support-presentation coherence,
  cofinal schedule independence, and free--periodic boundary independence are
  all passed to the limit;
\item intrinsic integer-site cylinders are quotiented by equality of their
  global realization, and exact periodic translations prove invariance of the
  resulting state under the full group $\Z^2$.
\item the local quotient is complexified and completed in its faithful uniform
  representation, and the state is extended to a positive norm-one functional
  on the resulting commutative C$^*$-algebra;
\item arbitrary finite conditioning sets produce positive normalized
  exterior-local idempotent Gibbs kernels, and the finite Gibbs measure is
  proved to satisfy their exact tower identity.
\end{enumerate}

The formalization was motivated by a precise gap in an earlier finite-volume
Dobrushin development: exponential estimates held uniformly in the volume,
but no infinite-volume state value had been constructed.  A uniform
finite-volume estimate is not itself a thermodynamic limit.  The missing
statement is a comparison of \emph{two volumes} with an error controlled by
the distance between a fixed support and the smaller boundary.

The main pitfall is easy to state.  Bounding the effect of every changed row
separately and then summing can produce a term such as
$\abs{\Lambda_m}\alpha^{m-r}$.  Such a bound may still vanish along some
exhaustions, but it obscures the mechanism and does not give the uniform
boundary stability one expects from Dobrushin comparison.  The proof below
instead sums the defect field against the resolvent before estimating the
geometric tail.

\subsection*{What is not claimed}

The paper constructs both an algebraic local state with a full $\Z^2$ action
and a positive norm-one state on a concrete commutative C$^*$ completion; it
also proves free--periodic equality for the centred square exhaustion.  It
does not compare every possible exhaustion shape, extend the full $\Z^2$
action to the completed algebra, or prove that the completed state satisfies
the infinite-volume DLR equations.  The finite-set tower theorem below is a
load-bearing finite identity, not a relabelling of the completed state as DLR.

\section{Finite products and one-site influence}

Let $I$ be a finite nonempty site set and $S$ a finite nonempty spin set.  A
configuration is $\eta\in S^I$.  For a probability weight $\mu$ and an
observable $f:S^I\to\R$, write
\[
  \E_\mu f=\sum_{\eta\in S^I}\mu(\eta)f(\eta).
\]
The one-coordinate oscillation is
\[
  \delta_i(f)=
  \sup_{\eta\in S^I,\,s\in S}
  \abs{f(\eta)-f(\eta^{i\leftarrow s})}.
\]
All suprema are finite suprema in the Lean development.

A one-site kernel $p_i(\eta,\cdot)$ is normalized and local: it depends on
$\eta$ only outside $i$.  Its conditional-expectation operator is
\[
  P_i f(\eta)=\sum_{s\in S}p_i(\eta,s)
     f(\eta^{i\leftarrow s}).
\]
The matrix $C=(C_{ik})$ is an influence majorant when, for $k\ne i$ and
configurations differing only at $k$,
\[
  \TV\bigl(p_i(\eta,\cdot),p_i(\eta',\cdot)\bigr)\le C_{ik}.
\]
The formal comparison theorem permits any nonnegative majorant with zero
diagonal and row sums
\[
  \sum_k C_{ik}\le\alpha<1.
\]

\subsection{Random-scan telescoping}

Define the random-scan operator
\[
  Pf=\frac1{\abs I}\sum_{i\in I}P_i f.
\]
If $\mu$ is invariant under all $P_i$, it is invariant under $P$.  For a
second specification $q$ with invariant weight $\nu$, the difference
$\mu(f)-\nu(f)$ is telescoped through iterates of the two scan operators.
At a single step, the discrepancy separates into:
\begin{itemize}
\item propagation of the current coordinate oscillations through $C$; and
\item a source term $b_i\delta_i(f)$, where
  $\TV(p_i(\eta),q_i(\eta))\le b_i$.
\end{itemize}
Iteration produces partial sums of $I+C+C^2+\cdots$.  The remainder vanishes
because the scan contracts the total oscillation under the row-sum condition.
This is formalized in
\leanfile{DobrushinMeasureComparison}{DobrushinMeasureComparison}.

\begin{proposition}[resolvent comparison; machine-checked]
Under the hypotheses above,
\[
  \abs{\E_\mu f-\E_\nu f}
  \le
  \sum_i b_i\sum_j\left(\sum_{q\ge0}(C^q)_{ji}\right)\delta_j(f).
\]
No Cauchy sequence, limiting state, or boundary-tail estimate occurs among the
hypotheses.
\end{proposition}

\section{The volume-free boundary tail}

Assume a graph distance $d$ on $I$ and range-one support:
\[
  d(j,i)>1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C_{ji}=0.
\]
Matrix multiplication and the triangle inequality imply
\[
  q<d(j,i)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (C^q)_{ji}=0.
\]
The row-sum hypothesis propagates to every power,
\[
  \sum_i(C^q)_{ji}\le\alpha^q.
\]

Suppose $0\le b_i\le1$ and every nonzero defect lies at distance at least $R$
from coordinate $j$.  Then
\begin{align*}
  \sum_i b_i\sum_{q\ge0}(C^q)_{ji}
  &=\sum_{q\ge0}\sum_i b_i(C^q)_{ji}\\
  &=\sum_{q\ge R}\sum_i b_i(C^q)_{ji}\\
  &\le\sum_{q\ge R}\alpha^q
   =\frac{\alpha^R}{1-\alpha}.
\end{align*}
The finite sum over sites is moved inside the infinite sum before the row-sum
bound is applied.  No estimate of the number of defect sites is used.

\begin{theorem}[boundary comparison; machine-checked]
\label{thm:abstract-boundary}
Let $\mu,\nu$ be invariant probability weights for two one-site
specifications $p,q$.  Let $C$ majorize the influence of $p$, with
$0\le\alpha<1$, row sums at most $\alpha$, and range one in $d$.  Suppose
\[
  \TV(p_i(\eta),q_i(\eta))\le b_i\le1
\]
and
\[
  b_i\ne0,\quad\delta_j(f)\ne0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad R\le d(j,i).
\]
Then
\[
  \abs{\E_\mu f-\E_\nu f}
  \le\frac{\alpha^R}{1-\alpha}\sum_j\delta_j(f).
\]
\end{theorem}

The terminal Lean declaration is
\leanline{DobrushinMeasureComparison}{488}{measure_comparison_boundary_decay}.
The theorem is genuinely two-specification: its only cross-model input is the
pointwise total-variation defect.  In particular, it does not assume the
conclusion in the form of a Cauchy estimate or a pre-existing boundary tail.

\section{Specialization to Ising Hamiltonians}

For a symmetric zero-diagonal coupling $J:I\times I\to\R$ and two-state spins
$\sigma:S\to\{-1,+1\}$, the finite Ising weight is
\[
  w_J(\eta)=\exp\!\left(
    \frac12\sum_{i,k\in I}J_{ik}\sigma(\eta_i)\sigma(\eta_k)
  \right).
\]
The associated heat-bath conditional is strictly positive and normalized.
Changing spin $k$ moves the local field at $i$ by at most $2\abs{J_{ik}}$, and
the two-point conditional calculation yields the classical envelope
\[
  c_{ik}\le\tanh\abs{J_{ik}}.
\]

Let $J$ and $K$ live on the same finite site set.  If their complete rows agree
at $i$, their heat-bath kernels agree exactly at $i$.  On a changed row, total
variation is bounded by one.  Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-boundary} applies
with the defect predicate
\[
  D(i)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \exists k,\;J_{ik}\ne K_{ik}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Ising row-perturbation comparison; machine-checked]
\label{thm:ising-boundary}
Assume the envelope row sums of $J$ are at most $\alpha<1$, and $J$ has range
one.  If every row changed between $J$ and a second symmetric zero-diagonal
coupling $K$ is at distance at least $R$ from the active coordinates of $f$,
then
\[
  \abs{\E_J f-\E_K f}
  \le\frac{\alpha^R}{1-\alpha}\sum_j\delta_j(f).
\]
No Dobrushin condition is required of $K$.
\end{theorem}

The last observation is useful: comparison is oriented.  The reference model
$J$ supplies the resolvent; the perturbed Hamiltonian needs positivity and
normalization, but its own influence matrix need not be controlled.  The Lean
endpoint is
\leanline{DobrushinIsingComparison}{57}{ising_boundary_comparison}.

\section{Two genuine rectangles}

Let
\[
  \Lambda_n=\{0,\ldots,2n\}^2
\]
with anisotropic free nearest-neighbour coupling $\beta$ horizontally and
$\gamma$ vertically.  The square $\Lambda_n$ is embedded centrally in
$\Lambda_m$ by adding $m-n$ to each coordinate.

Comparing two different finite products requires more than identifying their
cardinalities.  The formal development performs three exact operations.

\paragraph{Restriction.}
Delete every bond of the $m$-coupling with at least one endpoint outside the
central copy of $\Lambda_n$.  The outside spins become uniform spectators.
The partition sum factors, and normalized expectations of observables that
ignore the spectators are unchanged.

\paragraph{Reindexing.}
The active subtype of the large chart is explicitly equivalent to
$\Lambda_n$.  Under that equivalence the restricted coupling is exactly the
free $n$-coupling, not merely isomorphic in an informal sense.  Gibbs sums and
normalizations are transported through the equivalence.

\paragraph{Observable lifting.}
For $r\le n$, a kernel $g$ on $\Lambda_r$ is lifted to $\Lambda_n$ by
restriction to the centred core and reindexing.  These lifts compose exactly.
Outside the core, every coordinate oscillation vanishes.  Inside,
\[
  \sum_{j\in\Lambda_n}\delta_j(g^{\uparrow n})
  \le\abs{\Lambda_r}\osc(g).
\]
This is the second place where the ambient volume disappears.

The files \leanfile{DobrushinVolumeRestriction}{DobrushinVolumeRestriction},
\leanfile{DobrushinVolumeEquiv}{DobrushinVolumeEquiv}, and
\leanfile{DobrushinRectangleVolume}{DobrushinRectangleVolume} implement these
steps separately.  Their separation is deliberate: it makes the normalization
and spectator cancellation auditable independently of the comparison bound.

\begin{theorem}[two-volume estimate; machine-checked]
\label{thm:two-volume}
Let $g:(\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_r})\to\R$, and let $g^{\uparrow n}$ denote its
canonical lift to $\Lambda_n$.  If $r\le n\le m$ and
\[
  0\le\alpha<1,
  \qquad
  2\tanh\abs\beta+2\tanh\abs\gamma\le\alpha,
\]
then
\[
  \abs{\E_m(g^{\uparrow m})-\E_n(g^{\uparrow n})}
  \le
  \frac{\alpha^{n-r}}{1-\alpha},
  \abs{\Lambda_r}\osc(g).
\]
\end{theorem}

This is
\leanline{DobrushinRectangleVolume}{278}{centered_local_observable_comparison}.
Every quantity on the right is fixed once the observable and the smaller
radius are fixed, except for the decaying distance factor.

\subsection{The visible coupling window}

Every site of a free rectangle has at most two horizontal and two vertical
neighbours.  Hence the envelope row sum is bounded by
\[
  2\tanh\abs\beta+2\tanh\abs\gamma.
\]
In the isotropic case $\beta=\gamma$, the condition becomes
\[
  4\tanh\abs\beta<1,
  \qquad
  \abs\beta<\operatorname{atanh}(1/4)\approx0.2554.
\]
This is a sufficient high-temperature window, not the exact critical point
and not a sharp uniqueness criterion.  Its role here is transparency: the
same macroscopic Dobrushin window is visible in the finite comparison, the
limit theorem, and the boundary-independence results.  No cluster-expansion or
Kotecky--Preiss constant is inherited from the proof architecture.

\section{Whole-sequence thermodynamic limit}

Fix $r$ and $g$ on $\Lambda_r$.  Define the complete sequence
\[
  E_n(g)=\E_{r+n}(g^{\uparrow(r+n)}),\qquad n\in\N.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-volume} gives, for $n\le m$,
\[
  \abs{E_m(g)-E_n(g)}
  \le
  \alpha^n\frac{\abs{\Lambda_r}\osc(g)}{1-\alpha}.
\]
Since $\alpha^n\to0$, this is a direct Cauchy proof for the entire sequence.
Completeness of $\R$ selects a value $\omega_r(g)$, and the same construction
proves $E_n(g)\to\omega_r(g)$.

\begin{theorem}[complete free-boundary sequence; machine-checked]
\label{thm:limit}
In the window of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-volume}, the sequence $E_n(g)$ is Cauchy
and converges to a constructed real value $\omega_r(g)$.
\end{theorem}

The Cauchy statement is
\leanline{DobrushinThermodynamicLimit}{64}{cauchySeq_centeredLocalGibbsExpectation};
the convergence statement is
\leanline{DobrushinThermodynamicLimit}{98}{tendsto_infiniteCenteredLocalGibbsExpectation}.
The declaration order is itself a circularity check: the finite comparison is
imported by the Cauchy module, the Cauchy theorem constructs the limit, and the
limit is consumed by convergence.  No earlier declaration takes a Cauchy
sequence, a limit, or a tail estimate as input.

\section{A compatible positive normalized cylinder-state family}
\label{sec:state}

At each fixed radius $r$, the finite-volume expectation is linear, positive,
and normalized.  These properties pass to the complete-sequence limit by
uniqueness of limits and order closure.

\begin{theorem}[state properties at fixed radius; machine-checked]
For kernels $f,g:\{0,1\}^{\Lambda_r}\to\R$ and $c\in\R$,
\begin{align*}
  \omega_r(f+g)&=\omega_r(f)+\omega_r(g),\\
  \omega_r(cf)&=c\,\omega_r(f),\\
  \omega_r(\one)&=1,\\
  f\ge0&\Longrightarrow\omega_r(f)\ge0.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

The four declarations begin at
\leanline{DobrushinInfiniteState}{86}{infiniteCenteredLocalGibbsExpectation_add}
and end at
\leanline{DobrushinInfiniteState}{151}{infiniteCenteredLocalGibbsExpectation_nonneg}.

A local observable may be presented at many radii.  If $r\le s$, let
$\iota_{r,s}g$ be its canonical lift from $\Lambda_r$ to $\Lambda_s$.
The finite sequences associated with the two presentations are not termwise
identical: their $n$th terms live in squares of radii $r+n$ and $s+n$.
The two-volume estimate nevertheless shows that their difference tends to
zero.  This proves the coherence relation
\[
  \omega_s(\iota_{r,s}g)=\omega_r(g).
\]

\begin{theorem}[compatible cylinder-state family; machine-checked]
\label{thm:family}
The family $(\omega_r)_{r\in\N}$ consists of positive normalized real-linear
functionals and is compatible with every canonical support enlargement.
\end{theorem}

Lean bundles exactly these fields in
\leanline{DobrushinInfiniteState}{262}{infiniteCenteredLocalGibbsStateFamily}.
Section~\ref{sec:covariance} constructs the corresponding quotient-level
carrier and proves that this compatible family descends to it.

\subsection{Two further canonicality statements}

First, the construction is independent of the auxiliary $\alpha$.  If two
numbers $\alpha,\alpha'<1$ both dominate the same Ising row sum, both
completeness constructions are limits of the same finite sequence and hence
are equal.  This is
\leanline{DobrushinInfiniteState}{183}{infiniteCenteredLocalGibbsExpectation_independent_alpha}.

Second, if $v:\N\to\N$ is cofinal, then
\[
  E_{v(k)}(g)\longrightarrow\omega_r(g).
\]
Thus skipping radii, changing the rate at which centred squares grow, or
repeating finitely many volumes does not change the limit, provided the
schedule tends to infinity.  The theorem is
\leanline{DobrushinInfiniteState}{168}{tendsto_infiniteCenteredLocalGibbsExpectation_comp}.
This is schedule independence within the same shape family; it is not yet a
comparison with arbitrary non-square F\o lner sets.

\section{Boundary-condition stability}
\label{sec:boundary}

The finite comparison gives more than free-boundary convergence.  For each
$n$, let $K_n$ be any symmetric zero-diagonal coupling on $\Lambda_{r+n}$.
Let $D_n$ be a set of rows such that
\[
  i\notin D_n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  K_n(i,k)=J_n^{\mathrm{free}}(i,k)\quad\text{for every }k.
\]
Suppose every active coordinate of the lifted observable is at distance at
least $n$ from every row in $D_n$.  No row-sum hypothesis is imposed on
$K_n$.

\begin{theorem}[receding row perturbations; machine-checked]
\label{thm:receding}
Under the Dobrushin hypothesis for the free reference coupling,
\[
  \abs{E_n^{\mathrm{free}}(g)-E_n^{K}(g)}
  \le
  \frac{\alpha^n}{1-\alpha}\abs{\Lambda_r}\osc(g),
\]
and
\[
  E_n^{K}(g)\longrightarrow\omega_r(g).
\]
The assumptions on $(K_n)$ are local row equality, symmetry, zero diagonal,
and separation from the fixed support.  They do not include Cauchy or
convergence of $E_n^K$.
\end{theorem}

The generic convergence endpoint is
\leanline{DobrushinInfiniteState}{328}{tendsto_centeredPerturbedGibbsExpectation}.
This result covers boundary modifications expressible by changed interaction
rows on the common chart.  Fixed external fields would require an explicit
one-body or ghost-site encoding and are not silently included.

\subsection{Periodic rectangles}

For an actual boundary condition, the formalization defines cyclic adjacency
on each finite coordinate and the corresponding anisotropic periodic coupling.
Periodic and free rows agree away from the four edges.  If the observable is
supported in $\Lambda_r\subset\Lambda_{r+n}$, every edge row is at Manhattan
distance at least $n$ from every active coordinate.  Theorem~\ref{thm:receding}
therefore applies directly.

\begin{theorem}[free--periodic equality; machine-checked]
\label{thm:periodic}
Throughout
\[
  0\le\alpha<1,
  \qquad
  2\tanh\abs\beta+2\tanh\abs\gamma\le\alpha,
\]
the complete periodic sequence satisfies
\[
  E_n^{\mathrm{per}}(g)\longrightarrow\omega_r(g),
\]
the same limit as the complete free-boundary sequence.
\end{theorem}

The concrete endpoint is
\leanline{DobrushinInfiniteState}{468}{tendsto_centeredPeriodicGibbsExpectation}.
The periodic model itself need not be re-analysed by a second Dobrushin
argument: the oriented comparison uses the free resolvent and the fact that
periodicity changes only boundary rows.

\section{The integer site-cylinder carrier and full covariance}
\label{sec:covariance}

The centred family is now converted into a state on an intrinsic local
carrier.  Write $\mathcal X=\{0,1\}^{\Z^2}$.  A finite presentation consists
of a finite set $S\subset\Z^2$ and a kernel
\[
  F_S:\{0,1\}^{S}\longrightarrow\R.
\]
It represents the global cylinder function
\[
  \widehat F_S(\sigma)=F_S(\sigma|_S),\qquad \sigma\in\mathcal X.
\]
Presentations are equivalent precisely when their represented functions agree
on every $\sigma\in\mathcal X$.  Lean forms the quotient
\[
  \mathcal A_{\mathrm{loc}}
   =\{\text{finite site-cylinder presentations}\}/\!\sim .
\]
This is an actual quotient carrier, not merely the assertion that a compatible
family could be quotiented.  Union of finite supports defines pointwise
addition and multiplication; negation and real scalar multiplication preserve
the support.  All operations descend because their global realizations are
pointwise operations.  The realization map from the quotient to
$\mathcal X\to\R$ is injective.

For $z\in\Z^2$, translation sends the support $S$ to $S+z$ and satisfies
\[
  \widehat{\tau_z F}(\sigma)
   =\widehat F\bigl(q\mapsto\sigma(q+z)\bigr).
\]
The quotient carries a literal additive action:
\[
  \tau_0F=F,\qquad
  \tau_{z+w}F=\tau_z(\tau_wF),\qquad
  \tau_{-z}(\tau_zF)=F.
\]
The construction is typed for Ising sites.  It does not coerce the separate
gauge-link observable type used elsewhere in the repository.

\subsection{Exact finite periodic translations}

Let $\rho_N$ be cyclic successor on $\operatorname{Fin}N$.  Direct arithmetic
shows that cyclic nearest-neighbour adjacency is invariant under $\rho_N$.
Consequently the anisotropic periodic coupling obeys
\[
  J_N^{\mathrm{per}}(\rho_Np,\rho_Nq)
   =J_N^{\mathrm{per}}(p,q)
\]
for a shift in either coordinate.  Reindexing the finite configuration sum
then gives, for every test function $H$ and without a small-coupling premise,
\[
  \E_N^{\mathrm{per}}(H\circ\tau_{e_i})
   =\E_N^{\mathrm{per}}(H),\qquad i=1,2.
\]
These are the machine-checked declarations
\leanline{DobrushinPeriodicTranslation}{200}{expect_periodic_torusShiftX}
and

  \omega(\one)&=1,&
  F\ge0&\Longrightarrow\omega(F)\ge0,\\
  \omega(\tau_zF)&=\omega(F)&&\text{for every }z\in\Z^2.
\end{align*}
Negative translations are included as group inverses.  The hypothesis list
contains neither a thermodynamic-limit premise nor translation invariance.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{A concrete commutative C$^*$-algebra and its state}

The quotient realization is now used as a norm producer, rather than as
informal notation.  For $F\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{loc}}$ put
\[
  \norm F_\infty=\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal X}\abs{\widehat F(\sigma)}.
\]
Faithfulness of the global realization makes this a genuine norm.  Positivity
and normalization of the state imply the machine-checked estimate
\[
  \abs{\omega(F)}\le \norm F_\infty.
\]
The proof applies positivity to the two nonnegative cylinders
$\norm F_\infty\one\pm F$; contractivity is therefore derived from the state
laws and is not an extra continuity hypothesis.

A complex local cylinder is the explicit pair $F+iG$ with
$F,G\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{loc}}$.  Multiplication is
$(F+iG)(H+iK)=(FH-GK)+i(FK+GH)$ and the involution is pointwise conjugation.
The map
\[
 F+iG\longmapsto
 \bigl(\sigma\mapsto\widehat F(\sigma)+i\widehat G(\sigma)\bigr)
\]
is proved to be an injective star-algebra homomorphism into the bounded
continuous complex functions on the compact product space $\mathcal X$.
Define
\[
  \mathcal A=\overline{\mathcal A_{\mathrm{loc}}+i\mathcal A_{\mathrm{loc}}}
     ^{\,\norm\cdot_\infty}
  \subset C_b(\mathcal X;\mathbb C).
\]
In Lean, $\mathcal A$ is literally the topological closure of the range star
subalgebra.  Its closed-subalgebra instance supplies completeness and the
C$^*$ identity; neither is postulated in a record field.  The local embedding
is isometric and has dense range.

\begin{theorem}[quasi-local C$^*$-state; machine-checked]
\label{thm:cstar-state}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:covariant-state}, there is a complex
linear functional $\widetilde\omega:\mathcal A\to\mathbb C$ satisfying
\begin{align*}
 \widetilde\omega(F+iG)&=\omega(F)+i\omega(G),\qquad F,G\in\mathcal A_{\rm loc},\\
 x\ge0&\Longrightarrow \widetilde\omega(x)\ge0,\\
 \widetilde\omega(\one)&=1,\qquad \norm{\widetilde\omega}=1.
\end{align*}
Thus $\widetilde\omega$ is a positive normalized state on the explicit
commutative C$^*$-algebra $\mathcal A$.
\end{theorem}

density is
\leanline{DobrushinCStarState}{737}{denseRange_localToQuasilocal}; positivity

\leanline{DobrushinCStarState}{812}{quasilocalStateCLM_nonneg}; and the terminal

\subsection{Arbitrary finite conditioning sets}

Let $I$ and $S$ be finite nonempty types, let $w:S^I\to\R_{>0}$, and let
$\Lambda\subset I$ be any finite set.  If
$\eta[x]_\Lambda$ replaces precisely the spins of $\eta$ in $\Lambda$ by
$x\in S^\Lambda$, define
\[
 Z_\Lambda(\eta)=\sum_{x\in S^\Lambda}w(\eta[x]_\Lambda),\qquad
 \gamma_\Lambda(x\mid\eta)=
   \frac{w(\eta[x]_\Lambda)}{Z_\Lambda(\eta)},
\]
and
\[
 (\Gamma_\Lambda F)(\eta)=
   \sum_{x\in S^\Lambda}\gamma_\Lambda(x\mid\eta)
       F(\eta[x]_\Lambda).
\]
No rectangular geometry is built into these definitions.

\begin{theorem}[finite-set Gibbs tower; machine-checked]
\label{thm:finite-dlr}
For every positive finite weight and every finite $\Lambda\subset I$,
$\gamma_\Lambda$ is strictly positive, sums to one, and depends on $\eta$ only
through $I\setminus\Lambda$.  The operator $\Gamma_\Lambda$ preserves one and
positivity, is exterior-local and idempotent, and the Gibbs probability
$\mu_w=w/\sum_\xi w(\xi)$ satisfies
\[
   \E_{\mu_w}[\Gamma_\Lambda F]=\E_{\mu_w}[F]
\]
for every $F:S^I\to\R$.
\end{theorem}

The tower identity is not passed as an antecedent.  Its Lean proof reindexes
the double sum by the involution
\[
  (\eta,x)\longmapsto(\eta[x]_\Lambda,\eta|_\Lambda),
\]
then cancels the positive local and global partition functions.  The main
endpoints are
\leanline{DobrushinFiniteSetDLR}{78}{finiteSetGibbsKernel_sum_one},
\leanline{DobrushinFiniteSetDLR}{146}{finiteSetCondExp_idem}, and
\leanline{DobrushinFiniteSetDLR}{209}{expect_finiteSetCondExp}.
This theorem supplies the finite algebraic DLR layer.  Identifying the stable
nearest-neighbour kernel on $\Z^2$ and passing the identity through the
thermodynamic limit remain separate obligations.

\section{Proof architecture and kill tests}

The proof was designed around failure modes that can survive informal
notation.

\paragraph{No hidden limit premise.}
The abstract comparison theorem is finite.  The rectangle theorem imports it.
Only then is Cauchy proved, and only Cauchy is passed to completeness.  The
boundary-perturbed sequence is shown convergent from a quantitative comparison
with the already constructed free sequence; its convergence is not assumed.

\paragraph{No synthetic common-volume shortcut.}
The smaller Gibbs measure is not defined by assertion on the larger space.
Spectator spins are eliminated by an exact partition-sum factorization, and an
explicit equivalence reindexes the remaining Gibbs sum.  These modules
participate in the terminal comparison theorem.

\paragraph{No volume factor.}
The sum over defect sites is performed inside the resolvent.  The vanishing of
short matrix powers and the row-sum estimate are used before any cardinality
bound.  The only cardinality in Theorem~\ref{thm:two-volume} is the fixed
support size $\abs{\Lambda_r}$.

\paragraph{No drifting observable norm.}
The canonical lift has zero coordinate oscillation outside the core.  Its
total coordinate oscillation is bounded by the fixed core cardinality times
the global oscillation of the original kernel.

\paragraph{No hidden cluster-expansion window.}
The terminal theorem types mention only $0\le\alpha<1$ and the explicit Ising
row-sum inequality.  The proof reuses the architecture of a separate
thermodynamic-limit development but none of its Kotecky--Preiss hypotheses.

\paragraph{No false translation coercion.}
The new support coordinates are Ising sites in $\Z^2$.  The action laws,
finite periodic symmetry and infinite-state invariance are proved on that
carrier itself.  The gauge-link action elsewhere in the repository is not
imported into any statement of the construction.

\paragraph{No asserted C$^*$ completion.}
The norm is induced by an injective realization into bounded continuous
functions.  The quasi-local carrier is the topological closure of the actual
local range, and density is proved.  Positivity is first proved on local
star-squares, propagated by continuity, and then converted to positivity on
all positive elements using the C$^*$ spectral order.  Normalization and the
operator-norm lower and upper bounds are separate theorems.

\paragraph{No DLR conclusion as premise.}
The finite-set kernel is defined from the positive weight and a replacement
map.  The fixed-point equation for the finite Gibbs measure is the conclusion
of an explicit involutive sum reindexing.  Conversely, no infinite-volume DLR
label is attached to the completed state until the stable lattice kernel and
limit passage have been formalized.

\section{Relation to classical work}

Dobrushin's 1968 paper formulates regularity and uniqueness conditions through
conditional probabilities \cite{Dobrushin1968}; the 1970 paper develops the
prescription of random fields by conditional distributions and the associated
comparison machinery \cite{Dobrushin1970}.  The resolvent-based covariance
estimate is classically associated with Follmer \cite{Follmer1982}.  The
finite-spin Ising specialization used here is standard: the single-bond
conditional has a hyperbolic-tangent variation, and summing those envelopes
gives the Dobrushin coefficient.

The new content is not a stronger uniqueness region.  It is the explicit,
kernel-checked assembly of the following interfaces in Lean~4:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{sitewise TV defects}
\Longrightarrow
\text{two-specification comparison}
\Longrightarrow
\text{two genuine rectangles}\\
\Longrightarrow
\text{whole-sequence Cauchy}
\Longrightarrow
\text{compatible positive normalized family}\\
\Longrightarrow
\text{receding-boundary stability}
\Longrightarrow
\text{free--periodic equality}\\
\Longrightarrow
\text{integer-site quotient}
\Longrightarrow
\text{exact periodic translations}
\Longrightarrow
\text{full $\Z^2$-invariant local state}\\
\Longrightarrow
\text{uniform complex star closure}
\Longrightarrow
\text{positive norm-one C$^*$-state},
\end{gathered}
\]
Alongside this thermodynamic chain, the finite product layer now constructs
arbitrary finite-set conditional kernels and proves their Gibbs tower
identity.  This auxiliary theorem is parameter-free and does not enlarge the
classical Dobrushin uniqueness region.
We make no categorical priority claim about formalizations in other proof
assistants.  The reproducible contribution is the particular Lean theorem
chain and its audited source anchor.

\section{Limitations and next theorem}

The current result is materially stronger than a centred scalar limit: it is
a positive normalized real-linear functional on an explicit quotient of
finite $\Z^2$ site cylinders, invariant under the complete translation group
and stable under the free--periodic boundary change, together with a positive
norm-one extension to a concrete commutative C$^*$-algebra.  It also includes
the exact finite-system, finite-set Gibbs tower.  Three boundaries remain.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Infinite-volume DLR passage.}  The arbitrary finite-set kernel,
  its Markov laws and the finite Gibbs fixed-point identity are formalized.
  The stable nearest-neighbour kernel on the infinite product space and the
  limit theorem $\widetilde\omega(\Gamma_\Lambda F)=\widetilde\omega(F)$ are
  not yet proved.
\item \textbf{Arbitrary exhaustion geometry.}  Cofinal schedules of centred
  squares and the free--periodic pair are covered.  Arbitrary aspect ratios,
  general F\o lner exhaustions, and all one-body boundary fields are not.
\item \textbf{Completed covariance and parameter frontier.}  The local real
  state has the full $\Z^2$ invariance theorem, while the continuous
  automorphism action and its invariance on the C$^*$ closure are not bundled.
  The analytic window remains the classical, conservative entrywise
  Dobrushin envelope.  No ``non-classical'' region is inferred from the new
  algebraic completion.
\end{enumerate}

The next theorem is therefore the infinite-volume interface: define the
finite-range Ising specification on $\Z^2$, prove its cylinder locality, and
pass Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-dlr} through the already established complete
thermodynamic-limit sequence.  Completed covariance can then be obtained by
extending the isometric local translations.  Any genuine enlargement beyond
the classical Dobrushin region requires a separate analytic mechanism and is
not supplied by terminology.

\section{Conclusion}

The thermodynamic limit is obtained from the estimate that was actually
missing: a two-volume comparison whose cost is the distance from a fixed
support to a changed boundary.  Random-scan telescoping produces the
Dobrushin resolvent; finite propagation kills powers shorter than that
distance; row sums bound the remaining geometric tail.  Exact restriction and
reindexing then identify the comparison with two genuine Gibbs rectangles.

The final Lean development proves the complete free sequence convergent,
packages its values into a compatible positive normalized cylinder-state
family, removes dependence on the auxiliary envelope and on cofinal sampling,
and proves that periodic and free rectangles select the same limit.  It then
  constructs the integer-site quotient, proves exact periodic symmetries and
  obtains a positive normalized state invariant under every translation in
  $\Z^2$.  The quotient is then faithfully complexified and uniformly closed;
  the state extends to a positive functional of norm one on the resulting
  commutative C$^*$-algebra.  Arbitrary finite conditioning sets satisfy an
  exact finite Gibbs tower identity.  The visible region remains the classical
  Dobrushin window, rather than a constant imported from a cluster expansion;
  infinite-volume DLR passage, completed covariance, and a beyond-Dobrushin
  region remain explicit future work.

\section{Glossary of scopes}

\begin{description}
\item[Complete sequence.] Every $n\in\N$ is used; no compactness subsequence is
  selected.
\item[Cofinal schedule.] A map $v:\N\to\N$ tending to infinity; monotonicity is
  unnecessary.
\item[Centred cylinder presentation.] A real kernel on configurations of one
  finite centred square, with canonical lifts to larger centred squares.
\item[Compatible state family.] One positive normalized linear functional at
  each radius, with equality under canonical lift.
\item[Integer site-cylinder quotient.] Finite supports in $\Z^2$, identified
  exactly when their represented functions on $\{0,1\}^{\Z^2}$ agree.
\item[Full covariance.] The identity $\omega(\tau_zF)=\omega(F)$ for every
  $z\in\Z^2$, including negative displacements as group inverses, on the real
  local cylinder algebra.
\item[Quasi-local C$^*$-algebra.] The uniform topological closure of the
  faithfully represented finite-support complex star algebra inside
  $C_b(\{0,1\}^{\Z^2};\mathbb C)$.
\item[C$^*$-state.] The positive complex-linear norm-one extension
  $\widetilde\omega$ to that closure.  Completed translation covariance is not
  part of this term here.
\item[Finite-set Gibbs tower.] The fixed-point identity
  $\E_{\mu_w}\Gamma_\Lambda F=\E_{\mu_w}F$ for every positive finite weight
  and every finite conditioning set, derived by a finite involution.
\item[Receding boundary perturbation.] A coupling whose changed rows are at
  graph distance at least $n$ from the fixed support at stage $n$.
\item[Periodic exhaustion.] The explicit cyclic nearest-neighbour coupling on
  every odd square; not an informal boundary-condition label.
\item[Not included.] Arbitrary F\o lner shapes, infinite-volume DLR for the
  completed state, completed $\Z^2$ covariance, a beyond-Dobrushin parameter
  region, or a Yang--Mills conclusion.
\end{description}

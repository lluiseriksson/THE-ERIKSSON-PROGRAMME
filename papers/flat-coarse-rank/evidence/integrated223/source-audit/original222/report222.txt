4. Balanced integer geometry, including odd roots
At depth k, recursively halving an n-site interval gives lengths floor(n/2^k) or ceil(n/2^k), until singleton coordinates remain singletons. This follows inductively because halving the two adjacent possible parent lengths again yields two adjacent lengths. With h=max(1,floor(n/2^k)), all actual depth-k side lengths lie in[h,h+1], hence[h,2h]. Cartesian choices may give different lengths between coordinates, but never more than one.
An ancestor boundary and descendant-hole boundary are boundaries in the same nested one-dimensional partition. Any positive intervening integer interval is a union of depth-k intervals and therefore has length at least the GLOBAL h. At h=1 the same conclusion holds even for coordinates that have already stopped subdividing.
The use of global h is essential and correct. For example, n=3 and the depth-one hole {1,2}^D have a clearance of one although the hole's own minimum side is two. The candidate does not incorrectly use that minimum as h.
If every side of Q has length at least2, its next generation strictly shortens every coordinate, so every proper descendant I is shorter in every coordinate. Each axis therefore has at least one positive clearance. Expanding I by h on every positive-clearance face, clipped to Q, gives B with side lengths between2h and4h: one available extension of h provides the lower bound; I-side<=2h plus at most two h extensions gives the upper bound.
If min side(Q)=1, balance makes every side1 or2. Every proper descendant is a corner singleton. Q minus that corner is a Boolean-cube graph minus a vertex, connected when nonempty (or a singleton in the effective one-dimensional case). This is exactly the small geometry separately handled later. There is no hidden full-dimensional slab assumption in that case.

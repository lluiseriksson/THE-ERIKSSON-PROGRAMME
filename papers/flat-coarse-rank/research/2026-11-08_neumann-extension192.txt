QUESTION AND FALSIFIER
The active main question remains actual rectangular coarse means for fully
variable coefficients with a bounded normalized critical inverse moment.
One possible route would transfer an internal Neumann low mode on an actual
rectangle into a global low-energy field using a uniformly controlled exact
extension. We test the precise auxiliary assertion:
  E_a(Tu) <= C(D,M) E^N_{a,R}(u), with (Tu)|_R=u,
for every local mean-zero scalar u and every admissible coefficient profile.
The constant must be independent of n and coefficient contrast. No boundary
energy is included in the right side. T need not be linear. This assertion
is false even for a fixed two-vertex rectangle and M=2.

DEFINITIONS
Let D>=3, n>=3, X=(Z/nZ)^D, N=n^D, p=D/2 and H=n^2.
Positive-start energy means
  E_a(v)=sum_{x in X} sum_{i=1}^D a(x)|v(x+e_i)-v(x)|^2.
The internal Neumann energy retains only those positive-start edges whose
two endpoints lie in the indicated rectangle. Norms use counting measure.
Set a(0)=1 and a(x)=H for x!=0. Write
  mu=N^{-1}sum_x a(x)=H-(H-1)/N.
The normalized critical inverse moment is
  M_actual=mu^p N^{-1}sum_x a(x)^{-p}.

1. THE PROFILE IS ADMISSIBLE WITH FIXED M=2
Since N>=2, H/2<=mu<H, hence 1<=a(x)<=2mu. Also H^p=N, so
  M_actual=mu^p/N+(1-1/N)(mu/H)^p < 1+1 = 2.
Thus neither the moment bound nor the upper comparability constant grows
with n. The usual scale max(1,N/(1+mu)^p) is at most 2^p, since mu>=H/2.
This is an all-size family, not a finite numerical experiment.

2. AN ACTUAL INTERNAL NEUMANN DEFECT
Take R={0,e_1}, a Cartesian product of one interval of length two and
D-1 singleton intervals. Because n>=3 its only internal positive-start
edge is (0,1), of coefficient one. For u(0)=1,u(e_1)=-1,
  sum_R u=0, ||u||_R^2=2, E^N_{a,R}(u)=4.
Every mean-zero function on this two-point set is a multiple of u. Its
sharp internal Neumann gap is exactly 2.

3. EVERY EXACT EXTENSION HAS LARGE ENERGY
For any global v agreeing with u on R, use the three edges of the path
  0 -> -e_2 -> e_1-e_2 -> e_1.
Their positive starts are -e_2 (directions 2 and 1) and e_1-e_2
(direction 2), respectively. They are distinct edges with nonzero starts,
so all three have coefficient H. Telescoping and Cauchy--Schwarz give
  E_a(v) >= (H/3)|v(0)-v(e_1)|^2 = 4H/3.
Consequently any proposed exact extension estimate above requires
  C(D,2)>=H/3=n^2/3,
which is impossible uniformly in n. This includes nonlinear extension
rules and arbitrary choices away from R. Adding a global constant cannot
alter this endpoint-difference lower bound. The result is stronger than
failure of extension by zero alone.

4. THE GLOBAL ENERGY IS COERCIVE, NOT A GLOBAL LOW MODE
For completeness the single-defect comparison can be proved directly.
Write E_0=E_weak+E_strong, where E_weak comprises the D positive-start
edges from 0 and E_strong all other unweighted edges. Choose a cyclic
permutation sigma of {1,...,D}. Replace each weak edge (0,i) by
  0 -> -e_{sigma(i)} -> e_i-e_{sigma(i)} -> e_i.
All replacement edges have nonzero positive starts. For a fixed positive
edge (y,k), occurrences as first, middle and last bypass segments require,
respectively, weak source positions
  y+e_k, y+e_{sigma(k)}, y-e_{sigma^{-1}(k)}+e_k.
These three positions are pairwise distinct on X when D>=3,n>=3:
k,sigma(k),sigma^{-1}(k) are distinct coordinate indices, and the last
position has a nonzero sigma^{-1}(k) component absent in the first two.
Only the source 0 is weak; hence a fixed edge is used at most once among
all replacement paths. Cauchy--Schwarz on each three-edge path yields
  E_weak<=3E_strong; E_0<=4E_strong; E_a>=H E_strong>=H E_0/4.
The standard torus Fourier eigenvalues are
4 sum_i sin^2(pi k_i/n); every nonzero frequency has eigenvalue at least
4 sin^2(pi/n)>=16/n^2, using sin(t)>=2t/pi on [0,pi/2]. Therefore
  E_a(v)>=4 ||v-mean_X(v)||^2.
This also holds componentwise for real vector fields. In particular for
c=3 one actual global normalized mean gives the coarse inequality with
K=c, while the internal gap on the displayed rectangle is 2<c.
A local Neumann defect is therefore not by itself a global low mode.

5. EXACT SCOPE AND NEXT STEP
This refutes the specified exact-trace, internal-energy-only, uniformly
contrast-controlled transfer lemma within fixed M=2. It does not refute
the main rectangle partition theorem, additive-constant counting bounds,
all extension methods, or boundary/capacity methods that explicitly pay
cut-edge energy or change the local data. Only one bad cell is exhibited;
no claim of arbitrarily many independent defects is made. No local coexact
witness is claimed. The scalar statement is enough to falsify the proposed
scalar auxiliary lemma. No hypothesis has been removed by this result.

Abandon this unmodified transfer immediately; the family is its explicit
falsifier. Keep just the same main question active. The next candidate
must derive a coefficient-dependent boundary/capacity correction, and
must charge the strong bypass in this example rather than silently assume
a bound by the internal energy. Before proposing a counting conclusion,
test whether the corrected charge can be bounded at the fixed critical
moment scale. Abandon a candidate if this family or three units without
substantial progress defeat it. No new structure may assume the desired
global estimate as a premise.

Inspected introduction and Theorem 1.4 (PDF page index 2) only. That
weighted Sobolev extension result assumes Muckenhoupt weights and includes
weight dependence in its constants. It does not supply the particular
finite discrete, internal-energy-only, contrast-uniform estimate refuted
here. We do not claim to contradict that theorem or to identify quantitative
equivalence between its weight conditions and this finite moment bound.
Boundary sensitivity at high contrast and path comparison are classical;
this note supplies a concrete obstruction with the programme's start
convention and a fixed critical moment. No priority or exhaustive novelty
claim is made. The global comparison specializes the existing single-weak
site bypass argument (unit190/G4); it is included to make the contrast
with local Neumann energy explicit, not claimed as a new theorem of priority.

1. Positive detour theorem — source sections 1–2
The convention is a separate positive-start edge (x,i), with coefficient evaluated at x even when its path traversal is reversed. This is essential and correctly maintained. The three-step path replaces a weak edge from x to x+e_i by a reverse step in direction sigma(i), a positive step in i, and a positive step in sigma(i). Its positive starts are x-e_sigma(i) twice in different directions and x+e_i-e_sigma(i). Since i and sigma(i) differ, both vertices are distinct from x and at periodic maximum-coordinate distance one from x. Separation at least two makes them strong. For n>=3, the path edges are distinct as positive-start edge labels; the two edges of direction sigma(i) have starts differing by e_i. No coefficient at the weak source is inadvertently used on the reversed step.

Inverse edge enumeration is complete. For a fixed edge (y,k), first-step use requires i=sigma^-1(k) and source A=y+e_k; middle-step use requires i=k and source B=y+e_sigma(k); last-step use requires i=sigma^-1(k) and source C=y-e_sigma^-1(k)+e_k. Each type determines one source and one path, so this counts uses, not merely paths or endpoints.

For D>=3 with sigma a single cycle through all D directions, k, sigma(k), and sigma^-1(k) are pairwise distinct. A-B=e_k-e_sigma(k), A-C=e_sigma^-1(k), and B-C=e_sigma(k)+e_sigma^-1(k)-e_k are nonzero vectors having entries in {-1,0,1}. Their periodic maximum-coordinate distances are exactly one for every n>=3. In particular n=3 does not identify any pair of candidate sources or introduce an extra source type. Thus at most one candidate source is weak, proving use multiplicity at most one. Path Cauchy–Schwarz contributes the factor three, giving Ew<=3Es, hence E0<=4Es and Ea>=H E0/4. The proof works equally for complex or finite Hilbert-valued data by the Hilbert-space Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and summation.

For D=2, sigma(k)=sigma^-1(k). A remains incompatible with B and C, while B-C=2e_sigma(k)-e_k. At most B and C may both be weak, so use multiplicity is at most two and Ew<=6Es follows. At n=3, 2 is congruent to -1 and B,C are also distance one, so in fact multiplicity one holds there. The uniform factor six is still valid, as is E0<=7Es and Ea>=H E0/7. The candidate does not claim this D=2 constant is sharp. The argument does not by itself prove that a better D=2 energy comparison is impossible.

The Fourier gap for the specified positive-start counting energy is 4 sin^2(pi/n). The elementary inequality sin(pi/n)>=2/n for n>=2 proves the lower bound 16/n^2. Multiplication by H/4 or H/7 gives exactly the stated sufficient conditions for a global mean observation with K=c. For vector/cochain fields, “one normalized global mean” denotes one spatial cell with all selected component means, not one scalar measurement for the whole vector field. This distinction should remain explicit on integration.

The weakening from periodic separation at least three to at least two is strictly weaker for available larger tori, not a renaming. For example n=4 permits W={0,2e_1}, whose pair has maximum-coordinate distance two, so the new hypothesis admits a set excluded by radius three. For n=3 any two distinct vertices are at distance one; both hypotheses force at most one weak vertex. This small-size coincidence does not impair either theorem.

Then CA=0, RA=0, Phi(CA)=Phi(0)=0, and the repair quadratic term vanishes regardless of self-adjointness. Since Ea(A)<=2D epsilon ||A||^2<c||A||^2, the proposed inequality fails. This argument uses a strictly smaller energy bound than the target; no claim at equality c=2D epsilon is justified by this estimate alone. The source correctly uses a strict inequality.

Dimensions and linearity in this statement must be over one consistent scalar field. Its displayed construction and m-1 dimension directly prove the real statement, including arbitrary real-linear measurements.

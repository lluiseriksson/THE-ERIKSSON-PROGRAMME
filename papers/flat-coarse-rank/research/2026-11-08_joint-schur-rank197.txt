UNIT197: A growing-rank discrepancy between joint and separate elimination

QUESTION AND RESULT
The separate harmonic-cost sum from194 cannot be controlled by the original
energy. This unit asks whether passing to the correct joint reduction leaves
only finitely many exceptional coupling modes. It does not: at fixed critical
inverse-moment bound3, a subspace of dimension
  L_s=(2s^(D-2)+1)^(D-1)-1
has uniformly bounded joint Rayleigh quotients and separate boundary-cost
Rayleigh quotients tending to infinity. Consequently a size-independent
comparison cannot be repaired by observations and quadratic repairs whose
combined rank stays bounded. The result is a concrete comparison diagnostic,
not a new general Schur, coarse-space or rank-nullity principle.

1. ACTUAL COEFFICIENTS AND THE FIXED MOMENT
Fix D>=4, d=D-1>=3, and an even integer s>=2. Put
  n=s^(2D), H=s^4, p=D/2, m=n/2, N=n^D,
  T=(Z/nZ)^d, X=(Z/nZ) x T.
Thus H^p=n exactly. Let a(t,y)=1 on t=0 and t=m, except a(0,0)=H;
let a=H elsewhere. Define the actual scalar energy
  E_a(f)=sum_(x in X) sum_(i=1)^D a(x)|f(x+e_i)-f(x)|^2.
Each positive-start edge is counted once. Since n>=6 these are distinct
unordered torus edges. Let W=2n^d-1 and mu=(1-W/N)H+W/N.
As W/N<2/n<=1/3,
  2H/3<=mu<H, 1<=a<=2mu,
and
  M_actual=mu^p[W/N+(1-W/N)H^(-p)]
          < H^p W/N+1 <3.
Jensen gives M_actual>=1. The exact mean is mu; M=3 is fixed as s grows.

2. TWO DISTINCT REDUCTIONS, DEFINED WITHOUT AN ASSUMED COMPARISON
For y!=0 let R_y={(0,y),(1,y)}. These are disjoint actual product
rectangles. Set U=union_(y!=0) R_y and B=X\U.
The joint Schur form is
  S_U(u)=min_(f|U=u) E_a(f).
It is a quadratic form: connectedness and B nonempty make the grounded
L_BB positive definite. Indeed a zero grounded energy, extended by zero on
U, is constant on connected X and hence zero. Completing the square gives
the unique minimizing extension and the usual Schur matrix. S_U is PSD,
with kernel exactly the constant traces.

For each INDIVIDUAL pair let
  S_y(v)=min_(f|R_y=v) E_a(f),
  Q_y(v)=S_y(v)-|v(0,y)-v(1,y)|^2.
The subtracted term is its single internal coefficient-one edge. All other
edge energies are nonnegative, so Q_y is PSD. Define on the whole trace
space R^U the block-diagonal separate boundary cost
  B_sep(u)=sum_(y!=0) Q_y(u|R_y).
The minimizers in this sum are different global fields; S_U uses one field.

One can also subtract ONLY these designated internal pair edges jointly:
  Q_joint(u)=S_U(u)-sum_(y!=0)|u(0,y)-u(1,y)|^2 >=0.
All other edges, including transverse edges whose endpoints both lie in U
but in different pairs, remain in Q_joint. This is NOT the correction
obtained by subtracting the entire induced energy on U. For any actual f,
  Q_joint(f|U)<=E_a(f)-sum_(y!=0)|f(0,y)-f(1,y)|^2.
This valid joint inequality has no separate-cell additivity premise.

3. THE INDIVIDUAL PAIR COST, DERIVED
For the unit torus T, normalized Fourier coefficients and Parseval give
  |w(y)-w(z)|^2 <= C_d E_T(w), C_d=d*3^(d-1)/2,
where E_T=sum_(y,l)|w(y+e_l)-w(y)|^2.
Here is a complete constant bound. Choose frequency representatives
k_l in {-n/2,...,n/2-1}. Their eigenvalues satisfy
  lambda(k)=4 sum_l sin^2(pi k_l/n)>=16|k|^2/n^2.
Fourier inversion and weighted Cauchy--Schwarz bound point differences by
  n^(2-d)/4 sum_(k!=0)|k|^(-2) E_T(w).
The shell max_l|k_l|=r has at most
  (2r+1)^d-(2r-1)^d<=2d*3^(d-1)r^(d-1)
representatives. Since d>=3,
  sum_(r=1)^(n/2) r^(d-3)<=n^(d-2).
Substitution proves C_d. The estimate is independent of n and of w.

For fixed y!=0, telescope any global f along the five changes
  (0,y), (n-1,y), (n-1,0), (0,0), (1,0), (1,y).
There are three distinct actual coefficient-H edges. The other two changes
are controlled by the full transverse layer energies at t=n-1 and t=1,
each with coefficient H and point-difference constant C_d. These five
energy groups are disjoint for this one comparison: y!=0 separates the
two incoming edges; layers and directions separate all remaining groups.
With K_d=3+2C_d, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz yields
  |f(0,y)-f(1,y)|^2 <= K_d E_a(f)/H.
Translation of endpoint values by a constant costs no energy, so the
two-point minimized quadratic form is g_y|v(0,y)-v(1,y)|^2. Therefore
  g_y>=H/K_d,
  Q_y(v)=(g_y-1)|v(0,y)-v(1,y)|^2.
For H>=2K_d and any g with g(0)=0, the trace
  u_g(0,y)=g(y), u_g(1,y)=-g(y), y!=0
thus satisfies
  ||u_g||^2=2||g||^2,
  B_sep(u_g)>=4(H/K_d-1)||g||^2 >=(H/K_d)||u_g||^2.       (1)
Norms here and below are unnormalized real counting norms.

4. A GROWING REAL BAND AND ONE COMMON EXTENSION
Put R=s^(D-2), so R^2=n/H and R<n/2. Let G_R be the real scalar
Fourier subspace on T supported on the symmetric box |k_l|<=R. It has
real dimension (2R+1)^d: the zero character contributes one real
dimension, and each nonzero conjugate pair contributes two. There is no
Nyquist frequency because R<n/2. Evaluation at0 is nonzero on G_R,
as witnessed by constants. Hence
  G_R^0={g in G_R:g(0)=0}
has real dimension L_s=(2R+1)^d-1. No mean-zero condition on g is imposed.
Fourier diagonalization and |sin t|<=|t| give for every g in this band
  E_T(g)<=4pi^2 d R^2/n^2 ||g||^2.                        (2)

Define sigma(t)=1 for t=0,m+1,...,n-1 and sigma(t)=-1 for t=1,...,m.
There are m positive and m negative t sites. For g in G_R^0 set
  f_g(t,y)=sigma(t)g(y).
The whole field is mean-zero since sum_t sigma(t)=0. It restricts to u_g.
The map g->u_g is injective: its values give g at every y!=0, and g(0)=0
already. Thus W_s={u_g:g in G_R^0} has exactly L_s real dimensions and
lies in the mean-zero trace space (each pair cancels).

Only direction1 edges starting at t=0 or t=m have a normal difference.
Their total energy is exactly
  8||g||^2+4(H-1)|g(0)|^2=8||g||^2.
The transverse energy is exactly
  sum_y [(n-2)H+2+(H-1)1_(y=0)] sum_l |D_l g(y)|^2.
The bracket is at most nH, including y=0. This explicitly retains the
transverse strong-hole terms: g(0)=0 does NOT set D_l g(0) to zero.
Using (2) and R^2=n/H now proves
  E_a(f_g)<=[8+4pi^2 d]||g||^2.
Consequently, with beta_D=4+2pi^2 d,
  S_U(u)<=beta_D||u||^2                    (u in W_s),    (3)
and the designated-pair joint correction satisfies the stronger
  Q_joint(u)<=[2+2pi^2 d]||u||^2            (u in W_s).   (4)
Neither inequality assumes that f_g is the minimizing extension.

5. RANK OBSTRUCTION FOR ANY FINITE-RANK REPAIR OF THE COMPARISON
Fix A>0 independent of s. For H>=2K_d and H>A K_d beta_D, (1)-(3) give
  B_sep(u)>A S_U(u) for every nonzero u in W_s.            (5)
Suppose F is a finite-dimensional real space, C:R^U->F and V:R^U->R^U
are arbitrary real-linear maps, and Phi:F->R is any function with Phi(0)=0.
If, for every u (or just every u in a neighborhood of0),
  B_sep(u)<=A S_U(u)+Phi(Cu)+<u,V u>,                     (6)
then necessarily
  rank C + rank V >= L_s.                               (7)
Proof: otherwise (C,V) restricted to W_s has a nonzero kernel vector u.
Both added terms vanish, contradicting (5). If (6) is only local, scale
this kernel vector towards0; all quadratic terms scale alike and Phi(Cu)
stays Phi(0)=0. Symmetry, positivity or norm bounds on V are unnecessary.
The same argument with C=0 proves that a single additive matrix correction
T of rank<L_s cannot make B_sep<=A S_U+<.,T.> hold.

Equivalently, since S_U is positive definite on mean-zero trace space,
the generalized quotient B_sep/S_U has at least L_s eigenvalues at least
  H/(K_d beta_D).
This is the finite min-max consequence of the exhibited W_s, not a new
spectral principle. The analogous comparison with Q_joint in place of S_U
also cannot be repaired with fewer ranks, since Q_joint<=S_U.

The bound is polynomial:
  L_s >= s^((D-2)(D-1))=n^((D-2)(D-1)/(2D)).
It therefore excludes every uniformly bounded correction rank, not merely
one scalar exceptional mode. It does NOT claim a matching lower bound or
necessity of this many means in the original coercivity problem.

6. SCOPE, CLASSICAL CONTEXT AND NEXT STEP
The retained set U and its disjoint pairs are prescribed for this diagnostic.
The allowed target order in the original question is of order n^(D-1) on
this family, while L_s has strictly smaller exponent. No failure of the
selectable-rectangle target count follows. The result is scalar, D>=4,
on a concrete subsequence of194; no D=3, coexact, interacting or continuum
claim is made. It eliminates a proposed UNIFORMLY BOUNDED-RANK rescue of
the joint-versus-separate comparison, not a physical hypothesis and not
the invariant-coordinate assumption in the positive theorem.

The Fourier-band, min-max, rank-nullity, harmonic extension and adaptive
coarse-space mechanisms are classical. Primary comparison checked:
Spillane et al., Abstract robust coarse spaces for systems of PDEs via
generalized eigenproblems in the overlaps, DOI10.1007/s00211-013-0576-y,
author manuscript https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/44827/1/10.1007_s00211_013_0576_y.pdf .

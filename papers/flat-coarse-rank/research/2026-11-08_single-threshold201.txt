QUESTION AND SCOPE
Can the point-isolation construction199 be extended to all admissible
coefficients by choosing one threshold optimally? It gives a genuine general
positive inequality but loses half of the mean exponent in its rank budget.
An explicit fixed-M2 family proves that this loss cannot be repaired inside
the defined single-threshold/refinement/singleton-isolation strategy, even
though D+1 adapted rectangles solve that family at the original target order.
This is a route limitation, not a counterexample to the unrestricted theorem.

1. ACTUAL FINITE CONVENTIONS
Let integers D>=3,n>=3, p=D/2,N=n^D, X=(Z/nZ)^D. Let c>0 and a:X->[1,infty)
have actual arithmetic mean mu>=1 and critical actual inverse moment
  M_actual=N^(-1)sum_X(mu/a(x))^p<=M.
The original extra envelope a<=2mu is allowed but not needed in the upper
bound. Define
  E_a(f)=sum_(x in X)sum_(i=1)^D a(x)|f(x+e_i)-f(x)|^2,
  ||f||_N^2=N^(-1)sum_X |f|^2.
Each positive-start edge occurs once; internal rectangle edges never wrap.
For a partition into nonempty integer rectangles R set
  (P f)_R=sum_R f/sqrt(N|R|)=sqrt(|R|/N)bar f_R.
Then rank(P)=J=#rectangles for real scalar fields, by disjoint row supports.
For r independent real components the corresponding scalar rank is rJ.
No color/coexact or physical rank identification is implicit.

2. TWO ELEMENTARY LEMMAS, WITH THEIR WITNESSES
For a product rectangle whose integer side lengths are <=L,
  Var_R(f)=sum_R|f-bar f_R|^2 <= (L^2/4) E_R^0(f),      (2.1)
where E_R^0 sums unweighted internal nonwrapping edges. On an l-site path,
variance=(1/l)sum_(u<v)|f_u-f_v|^2. Telescoping/Cauchy-Schwarz and v-u<=l
bound the coefficient at edge k by k(l-k)<=l^2/4 after cancelling 1/l.
For l=1 variance and energy both vanish. To tensorize, coordinate averaging
projections A_i commute and are orthogonal in counting L2. Expand into
components indexed by subsets S of coordinates. Each nonconstant component
occurs once in total variance and |S|>=1 times in sum_i||(I-A_i)f||^2.
Apply the path estimate on each fiber to prove(2.1), without a factor D.

To isolate a vertex z in R=product_i[alpha_i,beta_i], partition into {z}
and up to two pieces per coordinate i: coordinates j<i fixed at z_j;
coordinate i in [alpha_i,z_i-1] or [z_i+1,beta_i]; coordinates j>i keep
their original intervals. Drop empty pieces. The first coordinate where
x!=z differs, and its sign, select its unique piece. This is an exact
disjoint cover, of at most2D+1 nonempty rectangles. A split adds <=2D
boxes, never increases a side length, and leaves earlier singleton boxes
untouched when distinct marked vertices are processed. Consequently m marked
vertices can all be isolated using at most J0+2Dm final boxes from an initial
J0-box partition. This is an existence/count statement, not a measured
implementation complexity claim. No logarithmic depth factor is needed.

3. GENERAL POSITIVE RESULT FROM ONE THRESHOLD
Fix any real h>=1. Set b=max(1,sqrt(c)),
  L(h)=min(n,max(1,floor(sqrt(h)/b))), B_h={x:a(x)<h}, m_h=|B_h|.
Start with the regular grid of chunks of length L(h), retaining a final
shorter nonempty chunk on each coordinate. Isolate B_h by Section2. Every
non-singleton final box has a>=h at all its vertices and side lengths<=L(h).
If L(h)>=2, then L(h)<=sqrt(h)/b and(2.1) gives its actual internal energy
>=4h/L(h)^2 Var_R>=c Var_R. For L(h)=1 every variance vanishes.
Internal edge sets are disjoint; omitted interface/wrap terms are nonnegative.
Therefore, for all real f,
  E_a(f)>=c sum_R Var_R(f).
Orthogonal decomposition into the cell mean and fluctuation gives exactly
  ||f||_N^2=N^(-1)sum_R Var_R(f)+||Pf||_2^2.
Thus the same partition proves the global finite-penalty inequality
  E_a(f)/N+c||Pf||_2^2>=c||f||_N^2.                  (3.1)
This holds for all f, not just near zero or on ker(P).

For x=sqrt(h)/b, max(1,floor x)>=x/2, considering x<1 and x>=1 separately.
Hence L(h)>=min(n,sqrt(h)/(2b)), and with ceil(t)<=2t for t>=1,
  J0=ceil(n/L(h))^D <=(4b)^D max(1,N/h^p).
The exact actual count before any overestimate is bounded by
  J(h)<=(4b)^D max(1,N/h^p)+2D m_h.                  (3.2)
Each member of B_h contributes more than (mu/h)^p to the unnormalized
critical sum. Consequently
  m_h<=M N(h/mu)^p,                                 (3.3)
with the weak inequality valid also when B_h is empty. Equations(3.2)-(3.3)
are valid for genuine intermediate coefficients; no coefficient is replaced
by1. Select h=sqrt(mu)>=1. Then
  rank(P)<=[(4b)^D+2DM] max(1,N/mu^(D/4))
          <=C1(D,c,M) max(1,(n/(1+mu)^(1/4))^D),     (3.4)
  C1(D,c,M)=2^(D/4)[(4max(1,sqrt(c)))^D+2DM].
Here mu>=1 implies 1+mu<=2mu. This is a fully variable scalar positive
bound under the actual moment. It is weaker than the original target
max(1,(n/sqrt(1+mu))^D): the mean exponent is D/4 rather than D/2.
It supplies no physical mass gap and no unrestricted target-order theorem.

4. FIXED-M2 FAMILY THAT DEFEATS EVERY THRESHOLD IN THIS STRATEGY
Fix D>=3 and integer s>=2, set
  n=s^2, N=s^(2D), A=s^2, H=s^4, p=D/2,
  Q={0,...,s-1}^D subset X, |Q|=s^D, delta=|Q|/N=s^(-D).
Let a=A on Q and H on X\Q. These are actual coefficients, not a nominal
low value rounded down to1. The exact actual mean is
  mu=(1-delta)H+delta A=H[1-delta(1-s^(-2))].
Since delta<=1/8, H/2<=mu<H, 1<=a<=2mu. Moreover (H/A)^p=s^D=delta^(-1),
so the exact moment is
  M_actual=(mu/H)^p[(1-delta)+delta(H/A)^p]
          =(mu/H)^p(2-delta)<2.
Jensen gives M_actual>=1. Thus M=2 is fixed for this whole sequence.
The original target T_s=max(1,N/(1+mu)^p) satisfies
  1<=T_s<=2^p,                                      (4.1)
because N=H^p and mu>=H/2. This is a nonvacuous uniformly bounded target.

Specify the strategy whose limitation is being proved. Set c=1, so b=1.
Choose any h>=1, possibly depending arbitrarily on the full coefficient
field and on s. Begin with ANY nonempty rectangular partition whose every
side length is <=L(h)=min(n,max(1,floor(sqrt(h)))). Refine it (no later
coarsening), requiring EVERY vertex with a<h to become a distinct singleton.
The point-isolation construction of Section3 is one member of this class.

If h<=A, then L(h)<=sqrt(A)=s. Each initial rectangle has <=s^D vertices,
so its count is at least N/s^D=s^D. Refinement cannot decrease this count.
If h>A, every vertex of Q lies in B_h, so the final partition has at least
|Q|=s^D distinct singleton boxes. This includes h=H and h>H as well.
Therefore every choice of h and every partition strategy just specified has
  J>=s^D.                                          (4.2)
The divergence versus(4.1) is unavoidable in this precise strategy. For the
regular-grid strategy the bound is exact: choose h=A. Then L=s, n=s^2 is
divisible by s, and B_h is empty because all a>=A. Its grid has exactly
s^D boxes. Thus min_h J(h)=s^D for that regular-grid-and-isolation strategy.
On this sequence N/mu^(D/4) is comparable to s^D (between s^D and
2^(D/4)s^D), so the general half-exponent bound is order-sharp for the
defined single-threshold route, not established sharp for the full problem.

5. D+1 RECTANGLES SOLVE THE SAME FAMILY AT THE ORIGINAL ORDER
The field of Section4 has an actual admissible partition with D+1 boxes.
Keep Q as one box. For each i=1,...,D use the nonempty rectangle
  x_j in [0,s-1] for j<i,
  x_i in [s,n-1],
  x_j in [0,n-1] for j>i.
For x outside Q, its first coordinate outside [0,s-1] determines its unique
box. Hence these D boxes partition X\Q disjointly. All starts inside any
of these strong boxes have coefficient H. Their maximum side length is <=n,
so(2.1) gives internal energy>=4H/n^2 Var_R=4 Var_R. On Q, coefficient A
and side length s give internal energy>=4A/s^2 Var_Q=4 Var_Q. These constants
are valid even for thin strong rectangles. Retained edge sets are disjoint,
so E_a(f)>=4 sum_R Var_R(f). Define P by the normalized means of these
D+1 boxes. It follows, in particular for c=1, that
  E_a(f)/N+||Pf||_2^2>=||f||_N^2,
  rank(P)=D+1 <=(D+1)T_s.                           (5.1)
For any fixed 0<c<=4 the same partition works with penalty c. Thus(4.2)
is a failure of the defined algorithmic certificate/selection route, not of
all selectable partitions. Allowing local coefficient scales or grouping
weak vertices instead of mandatory singleton isolation escapes the negative
premises. No conclusion is made about all alternative one-parameter rules.

6. WHAT IS CLOSED, WHAT REMAINS OPEN
Derived: an actual fully variable scalar positive finite-penalty bound(3.4)
with suboptimal rank exponent. Refuted: any target-order claim for the exact
single-global-threshold / maximum-side grid / refine-only / all-subthreshold-
vertices-singleton route, by(4.1)-(4.2), even on two growing levels with M<2.
Verified positive escape: an explicit D+1-box partition(5.1) for that same
family. The original all-coefficient critical target is NOT refuted or proved.
No main physical or mathematical hypothesis has been removed by moving it
into a certificate. No spectral/coexact/color/continuum extension is inferred.
For constant f=1, (3.1) requires K>=c in this mean convention; K=c is valid,
without implying optimal total rank.

This closes the attempt to repair the single-threshold route just by tuning h.

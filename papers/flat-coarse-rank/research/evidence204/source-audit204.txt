DETAILED ADVERSARIAL ANALYTIC CHECK
1. Geometry and finite edge cases.
For D>=2,R>=1,0<=r<R, the J=2D slabs are nonempty rectangular products,
cover exactly A and have equal v=t L^(D-1). Opposite same-coordinate slabs
are disjoint; different-coordinate slabs intersect in t^2 L^(D-2) vertices.
The root reaches the J-2 slabs of all other coordinates in one edge and
reaches (1,-) through (2,+) in two edges. There are J-1 edges and no cycle.
All used intersections therefore restrict a long coordinate of each
participating slab. Delta<=J-1 is a valid conservative bound. D=2, R=1,
r=0 and t=1 remain valid, with no rounding, empty sets or undefined means.
D=1 is genuinely excluded: a componentwise constant field on its two
separate intervals has zero energy but nonzero global variance.

2. Interval-to-subset comparison, especially no hidden |T| loss.
The difference of the two arithmetic means is exactly the average of
(g(i)-g(k)) over I times T. Squaring and Jensen bounds it by the average
pair square. Every pair is linked inside the same interval by at most L-1
edges; Cauchy-Schwarz bounds its square by L times the full interval
energy. Averaging cancels the L|T| pair count, proving (3.1) for every
nonempty T, even one site. This argument pays energy and does not apply
a false local-error gluing estimate or divide by overlap measure.

3. Fiber normalization and the shared intersection mean.
For B=I times F, the difference of B and T-times-F means is the uniform
average over the SAME w fibers of each interval mean difference. Jensen
then contributes 1/w times the sum of those squared differences. Thus
(3.2) has L/w times the total directional counting energy, exactly as
stated, not L*w or an additional |T| denominator.
For neighboring slabs their distinct restricted coordinates mean that
S_i intersect S_j is obtained by selecting t sites of one of S_i's long
L directions and leaving its other factors untouched. Here w=v/L, so
the coefficient is L^2/v. Reversing i,j gives the same actual intersection
mean, although the controlled directional energy is a different one.
Triangle and (a+b)^2<=2(a^2+b^2) prove (4.1). Replacing directional by
full slab energy enlarges the right side. This is the decisive step that
bypasses the small-overlap obstruction of203B.

4. Tree path and root-mean constants.
A path of length at most two gives squared root-to-i mean difference at
most twice the sum of edge mean differences. Substituting (4.1) gives
4 L^2/v times the sum of endpoint energies over that path. Enlarging to
the full tree and counting endpoint occurrences gives at most Delta H.
Thus (5.1) is valid, including the root with zero left side. No factor of
v or of the number of slabs is lost. Summing v times the root-mean bound
over J slabs gives the stated 4J Delta L^2 H term.

5. Box variance, orthogonality and energy multiplicity.
The interval counting identity is V=(1/ell)sum_(a<b)|g(a)-g(b)|^2 for
an interval of ell sites. A fixed edge is crossed by k(ell-k)<=ell^2/4
unordered pairs. Bounding path length by ell and dividing by ell gives
coefficient at most ell^2/4. Using ell<=L proves the conservative L^2
bound, including length-one directions with zero variance. Conditional
variance under the uniform product measure and Jensen for directional
differences of the coordinate-averaged function tensorize this estimate.
Multiplying by the box cardinality gives unnormalized counting internal
energy without an extra transverse-volume or dimension factor.
The local centered sum vanishes, so the decomposition about m_root in
(5.2) is exact. The union variance is at most the candidate root-mean error,
and a covering sum may repeat nonnegative terms, giving the correct first
inequality. Each slab-internal edge lies inside A; each shell edge appears
at most J times in H. Therefore H<=J E_A and
V_A<=J[1+4J(J-1)] L^2 E_A follows. Every cover/tree choice is geometric
and independent of f, giving the required all-field quantifier.

6. Weighting and smallest-scale indicator energy.
Under a(x)>=alpha>0 on A, the start of every retained positive-direction
internal edge is in A. Hence the weighted energy is at least alpha times
the unweighted sum, proving only the conditional (6.1). This does not
produce alpha comparable to the global coefficient mean from its moment.
For t=1, f=1_{x_1=R} has no jumps in transverse directions or between
points sharing its first coordinate. The only possible jumps are the
x_1 edges from R-1 to R. The second endpoint is always in A, while the
first is in A exactly when at least one transverse coordinate has absolute
value R. There are L^(D-1)-(L-2)^(D-1) such transverse choices and each
jump is one. This remains correct for R=1,r=0,D=2; the excluded central
point is not accidentally used. There is no outside or wrapped edge.

THEOREM AND SCOPE
Let D>=3,n>=3 be integers, c>0,H>=1 real, p=D/2,N=n^D, and
X=(Z/nZ)^D. Let a:X->{1,H} be ANY arrangement of these two values.
Use the actual mean mu=N^(-1)sum_X a and assume the actual critical moment
  M_actual=N^(-1)sum_X (mu/a(x))^p <= M.
There is a partition of X into nonempty axis-aligned integer rectangles,
with normalized arithmetic means P, such that for every real scalar f,
  E_a(f)/N+c||Pf||_2^2 >= c||f||_N^2,                 (T1)
  rank(P) <= C(D,c,M)max{1,(n/sqrt(1+mu))^D},          (T2)
where
  E_a(f)=sum_(x in X)sum_(i=1)^D a(x)|f(x+e_i)-f(x)|^2,
  ||f||_N^2=N^(-1)sum_X|f|^2,
  C(D,c,M)=2^(D/2)[(4 max(1,sqrt(c)))^D+2DM].
The partition depends on a,c, not f. The coefficient bound a<=2mu is NOT
needed for this restricted theorem. Binary fields satisfying that additional
condition are therefore included. No weak-plane geometry, separation of
weak vertices, or coefficient invariance is assumed. H need not be attained.

This is a scalar two-valued positive result, not the unrestricted moment-only
coefficient theorem. Its purpose is to replace the planar geometry restriction
of the preceding family construction by an actual proof within this class.
No originality of elementary Poincare or partition operations is asserted.

1. A DISCRETE RECTANGLE POINCARE LEMMA, REPROVED
For R=product_i I_i, with nonempty integer intervals of length l_i<=L,
write bar f_R=|R|^(-1)sum_R f and
E_R^0(f)=sum_(i,x:x,x+e_i in R; no wrapping)|f(x+e_i)-f(x)|^2.
Then
  sum_R|f-bar f_R|^2 <= (L^2/4) E_R^0(f).              (1.1)
For a path of l>=2 sites, variance equals
  (1/l)sum_(u<v)|w_u-w_v|^2.
Telescoping and Cauchy-Schwarz bound each pair by (v-u) times the sum of
squared differences on its intervening path. Replace v-u by l. The edge
between sites k and k+1 occurs in k(l-k)<=l^2/4 pairs, for k=1,...,l-1.
After cancellation with 1/l this proves the claimed path constant.
For l=1 variance and energy vanish.

Let A_i be averaging in coordinate i on counting L2(R). These are commuting
orthogonal projections. Expand the identity as product_i[A_i+(I-A_i)].
Its components indexed by subsets S of coordinates are orthogonal.
Every nonconstant component occurs once in ||f-product_i A_i f||^2 and
|S|>=1 times in sum_i ||(I-A_i)f||^2. Thus the first is at most the second.
Apply the path estimate to each coordinate fiber, sum, and use l_i<=L.
This proves(1.1) without a factor D or a missing volume normalization.
Singleton coordinates and a singleton rectangle are included.

2. POINT ISOLATION WITH A LINEAR, NOT LOGARITHMIC, COUNT
Given a rectangle R=product_i[alpha_i,beta_i] and z in R, partition R into
{z} and the following possible pieces for i=1,...,D and signs -,+:
  x_j=z_j for j<i;
  alpha_i<=x_i<=z_i-1 for sign -, or z_i+1<=x_i<=beta_i for sign +;
  alpha_j<=x_j<=beta_j for j>i.
Drop every empty piece. Each surviving piece is a nonempty integer product
rectangle. For any x!=z the first coordinate i where x_i!=z_i and its sign
choose its unique piece. Thus these pieces are disjoint and cover R exactly.
There are at most 2D+1 pieces including {z}. Replacing R therefore increases
the partition cardinality by at most 2D. All side lengths weakly decrease.
This is a direct finite construction; no time/RSS complexity is claimed.

Consequently, start with any rectangular partition of J0 boxes and a finite
set B of m marked vertices. Process those vertices in any fixed order.
If the current box containing z in B is singleton, retain it. Otherwise
perform the preceding split. Previously isolated singleton boxes remain
untouched because marked vertices are distinct and current boxes are disjoint.
After at most m operations the result is an exact partition with
  J <= J0+2Dm,                                        (2.1)
each marked vertex singleton and each non-singleton box disjoint from B.
No point-separation assumption or recursive dyadic depth is used. The bound
has no log(n), log(H), or log of the distance between marked points.

3. STRONG BACKGROUND GRID AND ALL CAP/FLOOR CASES
Set b=max(1,sqrt(c)) and
  L=min(n,max(1,floor(sqrt(H)/b))).
This is an integer in [1,n]. Put x=sqrt(H)/b. If x<1, the uncapped length
is1>=x/2. If x>=1, floor x>=x/2. Taking the cap at n proves
  L>=min(n,sqrt(H)/(2b)),
  n/L<=max(1,2bn/sqrt(H)).
If L>=2, the uncapped maximum cannot have chosen1, so
  L<=floor(sqrt(H)/b)<=sqrt(H)/b.                     (3.1)
This also holds when L=n. Large H and large c create no omitted cases.

Split each of the D coordinate intervals [0,n-1] into chunks of length L
and one final shorter nonempty chunk if needed. Their products form an
initial nonwrapping grid partition with J0=ceil(n/L)^D. Since n/L>=1,
  J0<=(2n/L)^D
     <=2^D max(1,(2b)^D N/H^p)
     <=(4b)^D max(1,N/H^p).                          (3.2)
Residual chunks are retained, so no divisibility assumption appears.

Define B={x:a(x)<H}. If H>1 this is exactly {a=1}; if H=1 it is empty.
Apply Section2 inside the grid to isolate B. Every side length stays <=L.
For every remaining non-singleton box, all its vertices have coefficient H.
Its retained internal edge energy is exactly H E_R^0(f), regardless of the
values of neighboring vertices outside R. Positive-start weights matter:
this assertion uses the actual start of each retained edge inside R.
Wrapped edges and all inter-box edges are omitted but have nonnegative energy.

If L>=2, (1.1) and (3.1) give on every non-singleton box
  E_R^a(f)>=4H/L^2 sum_R|f-bar f_R|^2
              >=c sum_R|f-bar f_R|^2.
Singleton boxes have variance zero, even for coefficient1. If L=1, all
boxes are singleton and every variance is zero. Internal edge sets of different
boxes are disjoint. Thus, in every case,
  E_a(f)>=c sum_R sum_(x in R)|f(x)-bar f_R|^2.        (3.3)
No separate harmonic minimizers or boundary-cost additivity enter the proof.

4. THE ACTUAL MOMENT PAYS FOR ALL THE MARKED VERTICES
Let m=|B|. For H>1, the contribution from B to the actual critical moment
is m mu^p/N. For H=1, m=0 and the same inequality is trivial. Hence
  m <= M N/mu^p.                                    (4.1)
Here mu, not the nominal value H, is used. In particular all-low fields
are covered. Also 1<=mu<=H follows immediately from the coefficient range.
Combining(2.1),(3.2),(4.1) and mu<=H gives
  J <= [(4b)^D+2DM] max(1,N/mu^p).                   (4.2)
Since mu>=1 and 1+mu<=2mu,
  max(1,N/mu^p)<=2^p max(1,N/(1+mu)^p).
This proves the count(T2), with C as displayed. J never actually exceeds N
because it counts nonempty disjoint boxes; the loose upper bound is permitted
to exceed N. No asymptotic limit or large-n threshold is required.

5. NORMALIZED MEANS AND A GLOBAL FINITE PENALTY
For each final rectangle R let
  (Pf)_R=sqrt(|R|/N) bar f_R=sum_R f/sqrt(N|R|).
These rows have nonempty disjoint supports, hence full real scalar row rank
J. The counting-measure orthogonal decomposition is exact:
  ||f||_N^2=N^(-1)sum_R sum_R|f-bar f_R|^2+||Pf||_2^2.
Combine with(3.3) to obtain(T1) for all real f. Thus K=c is an explicit
finite global quadratic penalty, which in particular is locally quadratic.
For constant f=1 the energy is0 and both squared norms are1, showing K>=c
is necessary in this exact convention. We do not assert rank optimality.
For Q_R=sum_R f/sqrt(|R|), the equivalent unnormalized statement is
  E_a(f)+c||Qf||_2^2>=c sum_X|f|^2;
Q=sqrt(N)P. Mixing P and Q conventions is invalid.
For r independent real components, applying the scalar construction
componentwise yields rJ scalar coordinates, not J. No identification with a
particular Nc/color/coexact target is silently made.

6. ENDPOINT AND FIELD ATTACKS, ANALYTIC ONLY
H=1: B empty, a=mu=1, M_actual=1; the ordinary grid proof applies.
All a=1 with H>1: B=X, mu=1, M_actual=1; final partition is singleton,
J=N and(T1) is immediate. H need not be an attained maximum for this case.
All a=H: B empty, mu=H,M_actual=1; the grid alone applies, including L=n.
If c>>H: L=1, all singleton, no spurious positive singleton internal gap.
If H>>n^2: cap L=n may occur; (3.1) remains true when L>=2.
At a boundary or for adjacent marked points, empty cut pieces are simply
discarded; Section2's first-differing-coordinate proof still gives exact
coverage. A marked point may already be singleton and incurs zero new cost.
A strong-hole or arbitrary weak/strong arrangement creates no new case.
Fields constant on each box are controlled entirely by the penalty. A
mean-zero field within one non-singleton box is controlled by internal energy.
All dropped energies are positive; no interface flux cancellation is assumed.
These are symbolic argument checks, not executed numerical tests.

7. LIMITATION: INTERMEDIATE VALUES ARE NOT PAID AT UNIT COST
The cardinal step(4.1) uses that every isolated vertex has coefficient1.
For arbitrary a in [1,H], counting all a<H as coefficient1 is false.
For example choose an even torus size, H>=2, and put a=H/2 on half the
vertices and a=H on the rest. Then mu=3H/4, a<=2mu, and
  M_actual=[(3/2)^p+(3/4)^p]/2,
a constant independent of H. Yet |{a<H}|=N/2 does not satisfy
|{a<H}|<=M_actual N/mu^p for all sufficiently large H. The right side
tends to zero relative to N. This is a counterexample to that attempted
cardinal lemma, not to the original partition target. Rounding H/2 down to1
would change the actual mean/moment and is not licensed by the hypothesis.
It remains possible that a different adaptive partition handles intermediate
values. This note proves no such result and no no-go theorem for it.

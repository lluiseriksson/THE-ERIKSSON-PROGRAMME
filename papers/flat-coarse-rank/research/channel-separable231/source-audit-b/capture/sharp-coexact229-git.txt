1. Definitions (one internal channel)
T=(Z/nZ)^D, V=n^D. Real scalar functions and real vector fields carry their usual
finite Euclidean inner products; multiplying all by the common volume factor
changes no rank or adjoint-image statement. Let partial_j^+ f(x)=f(x+e_j)-f(x).
The scalar gradient Gf has components partial_j^+f. Define the forward curl
(d_1u)_{ij}=partial_i^+u_j-partial_j^+u_i for i<j. The actual coexact domain is
U=im(d_1^*), not the output codomain, and therefore U^perp=ker(d_1).
With antisymmetric B_{ij}, B_{ji}=-B_{ij}, d_1^*B has component
sum_j partial_j^-B_{ij}, where partial_j^-f(x)=f(x)-f(x-e_j). This is the actual
codifferential convention used in228. The two signs follow from
(partial_j^+)^*=-partial_j^-; no central difference or continuum curl is substituted.

For a partition P={B_1,...,B_R} of T into nonempty subsets, let S be the real
R-dimensional scalar block-constant space and W=S^D the DR-dimensional vector
block-constant space. Observe all components by
(Cu)_{a,i}=|B_a|^(-1/2) sum_{x in B_a}u_i(x).
The adjoint range of C is W, since each nonzero block factor is invertible.
For g channels use U^g and the direct sum C^g, with h=Dg real components.

THEOREM. For every such partition,
  rank(C^g|U^g) >= g(D-1)(R-1).
This lower bound is sharp as a dimension-only bound: coordinate-fibre partitions
with n=R attain equality for every R>=1. For D=1 the analogous bound is0 and
the coexact domain is0; D>=2 is the nontrivial case.

2. Complete finite closed-field decomposition
We show ker(d_1)=constant vector fields direct-sum im(G).
Complexify and use finite Fourier characters exp(2*pi*i*k.x/n). Write
q_j(k)=exp(2*pi*i*k_j/n)-1. At frequency k the closed-field equations are
q_i uhat_j-q_j uhat_i=0. If k!=0, some q_l!=0; thus uhat_j=q_j(uhat_l/q_l)
for every j, including indices q_j=0. Equivalently uhat=q*phihat, with
phihat=(sum_j conjugate(q_j)*uhat_j)/(sum_j|q_j|^2).
Set phihat(0)=0. For a real field the displayed formula has the correct conjugate
symmetry, so it defines a real scalar phi of mean zero. Its gradient equals every
nonzero-frequency mode of u. The zero-frequency vector is constant. Conversely
constants and gradients have zero forward curl; their intersection is0 because a
periodic gradient has global component mean zero, and G has only constant kernel.
This also handles n=1: there are no nonzero modes, U=0 and a nonempty partition has R=1.
No imported topological or physical identification is required for this finite claim.

3. Annihilator identity and forward drift
Let F={phi:T->R : sum_x phi=0 and Gphi in W}. Since constants lie in W and every
periodic gradient has zero component means, Section2 gives the direct-sum identity
W intersect U^perp = constant vectors direct-sum G(F).
G is injective on mean-zero scalars, hence this intersection has dimension D+dim F.
Finite adjoint rank-nullity, applied to the projection of C^* onto U, gives exactly
  rank(C|U)=dim W-dim(W intersect U^perp)=DR-D-dim F.               (1)
This is a restricted-image rank, not an assumption that all DR outputs are independent.

Define the scalar forward drift L=sum_j partial_j^+. Its kernel on real scalar
functions consists only of constants. Indeed, at a point of global maximum, each
forward increment is nonpositive. If Lphi=0, all these increments vanish. Therefore
each forward coordinate neighbour also attains the maximum. Iterating the forward
steps reaches every point of the finite torus, including via periodic wrap, so phi
is constant. For n=1 this assertion is immediate. Thus L is injective on mean-zero
scalars. Neither reversibility nor aperiodicity of the corresponding directed walk
is needed; reachability and the finite maximum principle suffice.

If phi belongs to F, every gradient component is in S, so Lphi is in S. Also its
global sum vanishes by telescoping periodic differences. Consequently
  L:F -> S intersect 1^perp
is injective. The target has dimension R-1, because S has dimension R and the global
sum is a nonzero functional on S. Hence dim F<=R-1. Substituting in (1) proves
rank(C|U)>=(D-1)(R-1). Independent internal channels form literal direct sums of
input spaces and output rows; ranks add, proving the stated g-channel result.

4. Sharpness
Take n=R and let B_a={x:x_1=a}, a=0,...,n-1. Every scalar phi(x)=f(x_1) with
sum_{a=0}^{n-1}f(a)=0 belongs to F: its first forward derivative is constant on
each B_a, and the remaining components vanish. These functions form a space of
dimension n-1=R-1. Section3 bounds dim F by R-1, hence equality holds in (1):
rank(C|U)=(D-1)(R-1), and rank(C^g|U^g)=g(D-1)(R-1).
For R=1 the claim is the zero-mean identity and both sides are0. No rectangle
connectivity, coefficient hypotheses or assumed hR row rank enter the general bound.

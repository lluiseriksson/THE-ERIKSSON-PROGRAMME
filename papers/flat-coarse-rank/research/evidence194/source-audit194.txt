1. Fourier normalization and transverse estimate — section1
With hat(w)=n^-d sum w exp(-2pi i k.y/n), Fourier inversion has no prefactor and Parseval gives E_T(w)=n^d sum_k lambda(k)|hat(w)(k)|^2. Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the point difference therefore gives exactly n^-d sum_(k!=0) |exp(2pi i k.y/n)-exp(2pi i k.z/n)|^2/lambda(k) as the energy coefficient. The zero mode cancels. This remains valid for real w despite use of complex Fourier coordinates; no real-mode factor two is missing.
For the centered even-n representatives, |k_l|<=n/2, including the negative Nyquist endpoint. The sine lower bound on [0,pi/2] yields lambda(k)>=16|k|^2/n^2. Bounding the squared phase difference by four gives the stated n^(2-d)/4 factor.
The max-norm shell at integer radius r is contained in the full integer shell of size (2r+1)^d-(2r-1)^d. At the largest radius some positive endpoints are absent, which only decreases this count. Integrating the derivative of t^d over [2r-1,2r+1] bounds the difference by 2d(2r+1)^(d-1)<=2d3^(d-1)r^(d-1). Since |k|>=r, the inverse-square sum is bounded as claimed. For d>=3, the exponent d-3 is nonnegative, and sum_(r=1)^(n/2) r^(d-3)<=n^(d-2) is a safe coarse bound. Multiplying proves C_d=d3^(d-1)/2. All constants are independent of n. The proof does not establish such a uniform constant for d=2: its shell sum would be harmonic. The stated D>=4 restriction is substantive, not cosmetic.

3. Internal edges and five-change estimate — section3
For y!=0 the two-vertex rectangle has just the positive-start internal edge ((0,y),1), with weight one. At n>=6 no reverse start or wrap edge has both endpoints in this pair. Its counting mean-zero gap is exactly two. Rectangles with distinct y have disjoint vertices and are valid product rectangles.
The first telescope change traverses the positive edge ((n-1,y),1) backwards. The third traverses ((n-1,0),1), and the fourth ((0,0),1). Their weights are H. The first two of these labels are distinct precisely because y!=0; the third starts on a different layer. The two other changes are within the transverse layers n-1 and1. Both layers are entirely strong: neither equals0 or m for n>=6. Their complete transverse energies contain only directions transverse to t, so they share no edge with the three longitudinal edges. The two transverse layer energy sets are mutually disjoint. These observations also hold at the smallest allowed n=6, m=3.
If the five signed differences are b_j and the five disjoint weighted-energy amounts are E_j, the proven estimates are |b_j|^2<=alpha_j E_j/H with alpha=(1,C_d,1,1,C_d). Cauchy–Schwarz in this weighted form gives |sum b_j|^2<=sum alpha_j times sum |b_j|^2/alpha_j<=K_d sum E_j/H<=K_d E_a(v)/H. This verifies the factor K_d=3+2C_d without silently counting shared edges twice within one trace problem.

4. Global step field and divergent ratio — section4
There are m plus layers (0 and m+1 through n-1) and m minus layers (1 through m), so f has counting mean zero. All transverse differences vanish. The only longitudinal jumps start at0 and m, each of magnitude two. Exactly one of the corresponding2n^d starts has weight H, at the hole (0,0), and all other2n^d-1 starts have weight one. Thus E_a(f)=4(2n^d-1+H), with no missing periodic closing jump: layer n-1 and layer0 both have value plus one.
For each of the n^d-1 retained pairs, the difference is two, giving the boundary-cost sum >=4(n^d-1)(H/K_d-1). For fixed D the constants C_d,K_d are fixed and H tends to infinity through even n. Eventually H>=2K_d. Also H<=n^d because2/D<=D-1, and n^d-1>=n^d/2. Therefore the numerator after cancelling four is at least n^d H/(4K_d), while its denominator is at most3n^d. The claimed ratio >=H/(12K_d) follows and diverges.
This is a family valid at every permitted size, with the explicit divergent lower bound needed only on its tail. It is not a claim that the lower bound is positive or sharp at every small size. The step field is a single global test function, while each Q_R is computed by minimizing over a different entire complement. Disjoint traces do not imply disjoint minimizers or disjoint energy expenditures. Reuse of the hole and transverse layers across different trace comparisons is exactly what makes the proposed sum invalid; it is not illicit reuse within any one comparison. The source correctly refrains from asserting a unique physical route for each minimizer.

5. Counting scope and consequences — section5
Since 1+mu<=2H and 1+mu>=2H/3, division of N by their p-th powers yields 2^-p n^d<=N/(1+mu)^p<=(3/2)^p n^d. Here H^p=n is used. The max-with-one target scale is likewise comparable to n^d. Hence the J=n^d-1 disjoint pairs are only of the same order as the permitted count, not an unbounded multiple of it. This counterexample does not contradict the main selectable rectangle-count estimate.
The stated falsified inequality ranges over disjoint rectangles, not necessarily a complete partition. If that wording were strengthened to all full partitions, the present collection can be completed with singleton rectangles: singleton traces have zero harmonic boundary energy because a constant global extension realizes each one-value trace at zero energy. Thus completeness alone would not rescue that unqualified energy-sum inequality. This observation supplies no favorable count constraint for such a completed partition and no impossibility for a partition chosen to satisfy other requirements.
The result excludes only a size-independent C(D,M) bound for the sum of separately eliminated harmonic costs of arbitrary such traces. It does not rule out a growing-factor estimate, a joint retained-set reduction, a partition-dependent selection rule, boundary charges with overlap compensation, or direct internal-energy constructions. It yields no coexact witness, rank lower bound or physical continuum result. It is a genuine next negative test beyond193, but remains a scoped diagnostic rather than a positive advance on the endpoint theorem. No hypothesis-removal or priority claim follows.

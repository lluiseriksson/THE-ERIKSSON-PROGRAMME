1. Quantifiers and the precise predicate attacked
The asserted uniformity is C=C(J,d,rho), with J=3, fixed d>=2 and rho=1/4, for the unnormalized best-constant L2 error. Volume ratios may diverge. Every consecutive pair must have overlap at least rho times the smaller individual volume. Disjoint nonconsecutive pairs are allowed. The source also claims its examples satisfy the same rule for every pair that has positive overlap; this is true because precisely the two bridge/outer pairs have positive overlap. It does not assert positive intersection for all pairs. Those distinctions are essential.
The average minimizes the squared unnormalized error by expansion of the square. The union error is the distance to one common constant, rather than independently chosen constants on separate cells. Changing that target to a piecewise-constant approximation would change the claim and remove this obstruction. Normalizing each set's norm by its volume would also change the problem. None of these alternative predicates is inferred from this audit.

2. Counting construction: full field attack
Each S1 and S3 has L sites in every coordinate, hence L^d sites. S2 has two sites in every coordinate, hence 2^d sites; these are actual axis-parallel lattice cubes even at L=2. Its first-coordinate slice L-1 belongs to S1 and its slice L belongs to S3. The transverse coordinates {0,1} lie inside the large cubes for every L>=2. Thus S2 is contained in their disjoint union and each bridge overlap has 2^(d-1) sites. The overlap/min-volume ratio is exactly 1/2, including L=2. In particular rho=1/4 has strict slack.
The outer local errors vanish. The bridge has equal populations with values 0 and 1, so its mean is 1/2 and its squared error is 2^d/4. The union consists of 2L^d sites, split equally, giving mean 1/2 and squared error L^d/2. Division gives squared norm ratio 2L^d/2^d, exactly as in (2.1). For fixed d the ratio diverges with L, defeating every finite C(3,d,1/4). The example contains no zero-measure set, missing edge, extra union volume, approximation to an infimum, or growing number of cells.

4. Corrected rooted-tree bound and boundary cases
Finite positive set measures and f in L2 imply well-defined finite averages, by Cauchy-Schwarz. Every chosen tree edge has positive overlap, so theta_e^(-1) is meaningful. On an overlap the constant difference m_i-m_j equals (m_i-f)+(f-m_j); restriction and the triangle inequality give sqrt(theta_e)|m_i-m_j|<=e_i+e_j. Squaring uses (a+b)^2<=2(a^2+b^2).
For a path, weighted Cauchy-Schwarz applies with reciprocal weights theta_e^(-1), giving R_i times the path sum of theta_e|D_e m|^2. Extending that nonnegative sum to the whole tree is legitimate. Each e_j^2 then occurs degree_T(j) times, at most Delta_T times. This establishes the factor 2R_i Delta_T without hiding an extra J, path length or volume factor. At the root both sides are zero.
The union integral against candidate constant m_r is bounded by the cover sum because multiplicities are at least one; disjointness is not assumed. On each S_i the cross term vanishes because the average residual integrates to zero. Consequently the coefficient of the local residual sum is exactly 1+2 Delta_T sum_i v_i R_i. This proves the stated sufficient estimate. Q_T is invariant under common measure rescaling and is dimensionless. For J=1, Delta_T=Q_T=0 and the estimate reduces to equality. If all local errors vanish, connected positive overlaps force agreement of the constants, consistent with the bound. Disconnected or zero-overlap edges are outside its assumptions. The argument does not require cubes, Euclidean dimension or bounded multiplicity beyond the given finite family.

The step's union squared error is V/2 and its sum of local squared errors is v/4, so the exact ratio is 2V/v. The norm version of the sufficient estimate follows from sqrt(sum e_i^2)<=sum e_i. Since only the bridge error is nonzero in the counterexample, the lower bound concerns both choices of local aggregation. This establishes order V/v in the squared coefficient, and sqrt(V/v) in the norm coefficient, along this family. It does not establish sharp constants or optimality for every cover/root.
At the next induction step U2=S1 union S2 has volume V+v/2, S3 has volume V, and U2 intersect S3 has volume v/2. Hence its accumulated-union overlap/min-volume ratio is v/(2V). The adjacent ratio was 1/2. The purported equality of the two minima would require v=V in this family; it fails as v/V tends to zero.

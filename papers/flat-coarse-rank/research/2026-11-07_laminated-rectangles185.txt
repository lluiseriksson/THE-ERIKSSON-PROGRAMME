OPTIMAL-ORDER RECTANGULAR OBSERVATIONS FOR ARBITRARY LAMINATED COEFFICIENTS

0. Statement and scope

Fix D>=3, n>=2, c>0 and p=D/2. Let X=(Z/nZ)^D have counting measure. Suppose

    a(x)=alpha(x_1)>0

for an arbitrary positive sequence alpha(0),...,alpha(n-1). No monotonicity,
two-value property, contrast bound, separation, minimum independent of n,
coefficient-gap assumption or prescribed partition is imposed. Define

    E_a(U)=sum_{x,j}a(x)|U(x+e_j)-U(x)|^2.

The field U may take values in any fixed finite-dimensional real or complex
Euclidean space of dimension h. The argument is componentwise and hence also
holds on any actual flat coexact subspace. For a rectangular partition P let
C_P U=(sqrt(|R|)*mean_R U)_{R in P}, with the Euclidean output norm.

THEOREM. There is an explicitly constructed axis-aligned integer rectangular
partition P such that, on all fields,

    c||U||^2 <= E_a(U)+c||C_P U||^2,                           (A)

and, writing d=D-1,

    #P <= 2^d + B_D*(c/2)^p*sum_{x in X}a(x)^(-p),
    B_D = 2*6^d/[1-(2/3)^d].                                 (B)

The output dimension is h#P. In particular, if the original coefficient
assumptions hold with exact mean mu>=1 and mean((mu/a)^p)<=M, then

    dim F <= h[2^d+B_D*(c/2)^p*2^p*M]
                         *max(1,n^D/(1+mu)^p).                (TARGET)

Thus the original target rank order is proved for every one-coordinate
laminate, with actual penalty K=c, not an unproved kernel premise. The upper
bound a<=2mu and exact-mean assumption are not needed for the stronger actual
inverse-sum statement (A)-(B); they are used only when identifying the original
admissible class and its parameter mu. The lamination assumption is essential
to this proof and is NOT removed from the full original question. An explicit
actual coexact falsifier to a representative-fibre extension is given below.

1. Derive the local estimate from actual edges

Take a cut coordinate interval I of length m, and let

    R_I=sum_{x_1 in I}alpha(x_1)^(-1).

Including the last start, whose longitudinal edge exits the interval, only
makes this quantity larger. No outside edge is used as if it were internal.
Let Z be a product of D-1 integer intervals, each of length at most m, and
R=I times Z. Internal energies keep only positive-start edges with both
endpoints in R, with all full-period coordinates cut into paths as well.
Discarding these nonnegative cross-leaf/periodic edges is legitimate.

For a scalar u, put v(y)=mean_I u(.,y). Orthogonal averaging gives

    Var_R(u)=sum_y Var_I(u(.,y))+m Var_Z(v).                    (1)

For each fixed y, the exact pair-variance formula and Cauchy along the
one-dimensional weighted path give

    Var_I(u)=m^(-1)sum_{r<s}|u(r)-u(s)|^2
             <=((m-1)/2)*R_I*E_1(u)
             <=(m R_I/2)*E_1(u).                              (2)

Indeed each pair difference is bounded by R_I times that whole path energy,
and there are m(m-1)/2 pairs. This is an intentionally safe resistance bound.

For an unweighted path of length l<=m, the pair formula, unweighted Cauchy
and at most l^2/4 pairs crossing an edge yield Var<=l^2 E/4. Commuting
coordinate averaging projections therefore give

    Var_Z(v)<=(m^2/4)*sum_{y,j>=2}|D_j v(y)|^2.                (3)

For each transverse edge, using the actual same alpha sequence on every
fibre, weighted Cauchy implies

    |D_j v(y)|^2 <= (R_I/m^2)*sum_{x_1 in I}
                                    alpha(x_1)|D_j u(x_1,y)|^2.

Combining with (1)-(3) proves the actual local estimate

    Var_R(u) <=(m R_I/2)*E_1(u)+(m R_I/4)*E_perp(u)
              <=(m R_I/2)*E_R(u).                             (LOCAL)

This is a derived weighted local gap, not an assumption supplied to a new
structure. It uses line resistances, not the full strong Green trace rejected
in unit184. The proof explicitly needs the coefficient to be independent of
y; a selected representative fibre cannot replace that hypothesis.

2. Construct a finite balanced interval tree

Start with I={0,...,n-1}. Split a current interval of length m>=2 into its
floor(m/2) and ceil(m/2) consecutive children exactly when

    m R_I > 2/c.                                              (SPLIT)

Do not split a singleton. This terminates after finitely many splits at
depth at most ceil(log_2 n), since sizes decrease. The decision uses only
finite sums of actual inverse coefficients; for rational data and rational
c it is an exact rational comparison even when D is odd. Equality is accepted.

For each final I of length m, partition every transverse coordinate into
consecutive intervals of length m, except for a possible final shorter one.
The rectangles I times their Cartesian products form an actual disjoint
covering partition P. A singleton I gives only singleton rectangles. Every
other leaf has m R_I<=2/c, so (LOCAL) gives c Var_R<=E_R. The exact identity
||U||^2=sum_R Var_R(U)+||C_P U||^2 and E_a>=sum_R E_R now prove (A), including
the specified penalty K=c. No Schur compatibility or extra output hypothesis
is omitted.

This construction is not a D-dimensional full cube tree. The refinement is
one-dimensional and its leaf cost includes the actual transverse grids. Long
and short terminal transverse intervals are allowed; no aspect-ratio premise
is silently introduced.

3. Critical moment controls the entire tree cost

Set d=D-1=2p-1, w(I)=|I|^(-d) and b(I)=sum_I alpha^(-p). If I is an internal
split node, Holder gives

    R_I <= m^(1-1/p)*b(I)^(1/p).

Together with (SPLIT), this proves

    b(I) > (2/c)^p*m^(-d),
    w(I) < (c/2)^p*b(I).                                     (4)

This implication is derived; fractional inverse-p powers are not needed in
the actual splitting decisions. Let T be the internal split nodes having no
internal split children. They are pairwise disjoint intervals. Every split
node has at least one descendant in T. Assign each split node to one such
terminal split descendant. Every child has length at most2/3 of its parent
(also for the length-two split), so along a path the ancestor w costs decay
by at least the factor r=(2/3)^d<1 per level. Nodes assigned to a fixed
terminal descendant lie on its single ancestor path, at distinct levels.
Consequently

    sum_{split I} w(I) <= [1-r]^(-1)*sum_{J in T}w(J)
                       <= [(c/2)^p/(1-r)]*sum_{x_1}alpha^(-p). (5)

There is no logarithmic charge of a point across all scales: its assigned
ancestor costs form this convergent geometric series. If the root is not
split, the internal sum is empty and the following identity still applies.

The sum of leaf costs telescopes exactly from w(root)=n^(-d). For a split
of a length-m interval, each child is at least m/3, so the increase of leaf
cost is at most2*3^d*w(I). Hence

    sum_{leaf I}|I|^(-d) <= n^(-d)+2*3^d*sum_{split I}w(I).     (6)

The actual transverse cell count for I is ceil(n/m)^d <=(2n/m)^d.
Multiply (6) by (2n)^d and use (5) and
sum_X a^(-p)=n^d sum_{x_1}alpha^(-p). This gives exactly (B).

Finally sum_X a^(-p)<=M n^D/mu^p. Since mu>=1, mu^(-p)<=2^p(1+mu)^(-p).
Applying this inequality and max(1,.) proves (TARGET), with constants
independent of n, the number of coefficient levels or their positions.

4. A fixed-M positive family outside the fixed coefficient-gap class

Let D>=3 and take n=t^D, t>=2. Put mu=n^2, b=n^(2-2/D)=t^(2D-2),
alpha(0)=b, and alpha(j)=H=(n*mu-b)/(n-1) for j!=0. The coefficient has
one entire weak layer. Its actual mean is mu, 1<=a<=2mu, and

    mean((mu/a)^p)=1+(1-1/n)*(mu/H)^p<2.                        (7)

The first summand is exactly1 because (mu/b)^p=n. On the root,

    n R_root=n/b+n(n-1)/H
             =n^(-1+2/D)+(n-1)^2/(n^2-b/n)<2.                 (8)

For the last inequality use n^(-1+2/D)<=1, b<=n^2, and the strict bound
(n-1)^2/(n^2-b/n)<=(n-1)/n<1. At c=1 the algorithm accepts the root:
one global rectangular mean works on all fields. For D=3,t=2 the actual
rational data are n=8,b=16,H=496/7 and nR_root=40/31.

As n grows, b tends to infinity but b/mu tends to zero. Thus no fixed
constants B and theta>0 make every site satisfy a<=B or a>=theta*mu.
This is a positive family not covered by the earlier fixed-gap theorem.
The laminate theorem proves its coefficients' effect rather than assuming
the missing gap in another structure.

Moreover every partition satisfying the minimum-only local certificate
min_R(a)*lambda_1^0(R)>=1 has at least

    40^(-(D-1)/2)*n^((D-1)/D) cells.                           (9)

For a weak-layer-containing nonsingleton cell, a coordinate test gives
lambda_1^0(R)<=40/l_j^2 for every transverse side l_j>=2, whether a
full-period side is cut or retained. Thus every transverse length is at
most sqrt(40b); length-one sides satisfy this too. Such a cell contains at
most (40b)^((D-1)/2) weak-layer vertices. Cover the n^(D-1) layer vertices
and insert b=n^(2-2/D) to obtain (9). Singleton cells also obey this count.
This rejects a specific minimum-coefficient certificate, not rectangular
observations: the actual successful partition here has one cell by (8).

5. Actual coexact falsifier to dropping the fibre hypothesis

This falsifies only the proposed extension using one representative fibre.
It does not falsify (A) or the original unrestricted rectangular question.
Take D=3,n=4,c=15/2. Give coefficient1 to the cube {0,1,2}^3 and64 elsewhere.
Its exact mean is mu=2395/64, and 1<=a<=2mu. Its normalized critical inverse
moment is less than100: use mu<38, sqrt(38)<31/5 and mu/64<1 to bound it by

    (27/64)*(1178/5)+37/64 =31991/320<100.                     (10)

On the fibre y=(3,3), all four coefficients are64, so its root resistance
test has nR=1/4<2/c=4/15 and would accept a single global mean.

Let G be the scalar unit point mass at z=(1,1,1), and construct the actual
coexact field from a plaquette potential by

    U_1=D_2^-G, U_2=-D_1^-G, U_3=0.

Backward differences commute, so div^- U=0, and the displayed potential
gives coexactness, not just a supplied divergence premise. Every forward
gradient start with a nonzero difference lies in {0,1,2}^3. Directly summing
the four signed point values gives

    mean_X U=0, ||U||^2=4, E_a(U)=28<30=c||U||^2.              (11)

For each of the two occupied components, a pair of opposite adjacent point
masses has unweighted gradient energy4D+2=14; there is no n=2 wrap alias
at n=4. All those starts have actual coefficient1, which proves (11).
The one-mean penalty vanishes. Thus using a representative fibre in place
of a uniform laminate is an invalid extension even on the actual coexact
domain, with original finite admissible data c=15/2,M=100 and a<=2mu.
The original problem may use a different partition and finite output, so
no general negative result follows from this example.

6. Status, attribution and verification boundaries

This note supplies a complete finite analytical proof for a substantial
positive subclass of the sole original question. It does not supply a full
arbitrary-coefficient partition theorem or any interacting/continuum result.
One-dimensional weighted Cauchy/Hardy bounds and adaptive tree accounting
are classical mechanisms; no independent originality certificate is claimed.

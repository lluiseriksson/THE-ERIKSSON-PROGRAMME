3. Exact family, mean, envelope and critical moment

With P=D/2,
 H^P=n^D=N and A^P=s^D=R.
Therefore
 S=w/R+(N-w)/N=2D+1-w/N
is exactly sum a^(-P), and the actual mean is H-w(H-A)/N.
Moreover n^D>=2R*3^(D-1), giving w/N<=D/3^(D-1)<=1/3. The final inequality holds at D=3 and decreases thereafter. Hence mu>=H(1-w/N)>=2H/3, and H<=3mu/2<=2mu. Also mu>=2H/3>=1, so all original lower/upper/mean hypotheses hold, including the smallest s=1 member.
The normalized inverse moment is
 M_a=mu^P S/N=(mu/H)^P S<2D+1.
The strict upper bound follows because both mu<H and w>0. Jensen for positive a and the actual arithmetic mean gives M_a>=1.
Since mu>=2n^2/3, n^2/(1+mu)<=3/2<=2, so the displayed target-order upper bound 2^(D/2), including the max with one, is safe. The moment bound is the fixed dimension-dependent M_D=2D+1, not an arbitrary smaller prescribed M or a near-one-moment example. No surrogate density assumption is used.

for every real scalar f, with precisely the stated C_D. The local-mu formulation follows if the actual local normalized moment is supplied. 4. Dependency cube theorem

For a set U occupying at most half a cube, at most |U|/L fibers per direction can be full. Every nonempty nonfull interval fiber has an internal crossing edge. Different fibers/directions supply different edges. Projection plus AM-GM therefore gives
 boundary_E(U) >= d |U|^((d-1)/d) - d|U|/L

Finite coarea and the triangle inequality in the counting norm consequently supply the l^(d/(d-1)) bound for functions supported on at most half the cube.
For d>=3, alpha=2(d-1)/d has alpha*d/(d-1)=2 and (alpha-1)qstar=2 with qstar=2d/(d-2), the conjugate of q. 6. Slab cover, overlap and mean transport
The 2D slabs cover exactly those outer-cube sites with some coordinate outside [-r,r], hence cover A. Each has tL^d sites. Slabs with opposite signs on the same coordinate do not meet. Slabs on distinct coordinates meet in t^2L^(d-1) sites, for either choice of signs. The specified tree connects all 2D slabs: each other-normal slab attaches to (1,+), and (1,-) attaches through (2,+). Every root path has at most two edges with distinct normals. This works already at R=1,r=0,t=1.
For the interval mean comparison, averaging differences over I times T0 cancels the subinterval length through the normalized average. Path Holder then leaves L^(q-1), with q>=1; q=1 is legitimate. In a product slab, Jensen over the same fibers leaves the factor |F|^(-1/q). On a tree intersection |F|=tL^(d-1), so the source's transport coefficient is
 H=L^(1-1/q)(tL^(d-1))^(-1/q)

Every relevant directional edge is internal to that slab, and hence internal to A. Bounding each such norm by the full G is legitimate; it does not claim disjoint slab energy.

8. Isolated starting-site star: exact audit
Take x0=(R,R,0,...,0). It belongs to A since its first two coordinates equal the outer radius. All x0-e_i belong to A, are within the outer cube and are distinct, including at R=1 and for r=0. W therefore has exactly h=D+1 sites.
The smallest shell volume occurs at R=1,r=0, giving N>=3^D-1>=2(D+1) for D>=3. One way to see the first inequality is to minimize volume at r=R-1, then note that (2R+1)^D-(2R-1)^D increases for integer R>=1. Therefore epsilon=(h/N)^(1/p0) lies strictly between zero and one for every p0>0.
The moment is exactly
 mean a^(-p0)=(h/N)*(N/h)+(N-h)/N=2-h/N<2.
The indicator variance is exactly 1-1/N. There are D incoming retained edges, started at x0-e_i, and D-2 outgoing retained edges, started at x0; the two positive first/second-coordinate steps leave the outer cube. Every one of the 2D-2 starts lies in W. These are the only edges with nonzero indicator difference. Consequently E=(2D-2)epsilon exactly and (6) follows from V>=1/2.
Weakening x0 alone fails: incoming starts would retain coefficient one and contribute D to the energy. Thus the stated star, including all incoming starting sites, is essential under this energy convention and correctly implements the sharpness test.

9. Thin-shell critical exponent and thickness power
The exact inner cube side is 2r+1=L-2t, so N=L^D-(L-2t)^D. Two disjoint coordinate-1 slabs give N>=2tL^d; the full cover gives N<=2DtL^d. Both are valid at r=0 and t=1.
At p0=d/2, the isolated-star lower bound is a dimension-dependent positive constant times N^(2/d), hence times L^2t^(2/d), while its normalized moment stays below two. The proved upper bound has this same power dependence. This establishes sharpness of powers for the class of arbitrary positive original site weights, not optimal constants.
To rule out beta<2/d in an otherwise uniform L^2t^beta normalized-moment estimate, the sequence r=0,R increasing has t=R increasing and N comparable to tL^d; division of the lower bound by L^2t^beta gives a diverging multiple of t^(2/d-beta).
For fixed thickness t=1 and p0<d/2, the lower bound divided by L^2 grows at least as L^(d/p0-2), whose exponent is positive. Thus no dimension-only uniform L^2 normalized-p0-moment inequality of the proposed form can hold at that smaller exponent, already on thickness-one shells. This is the precise scope of “lowest possible”; it does not prohibit other inequalities with additional geometry, ellipticity, observations or stronger size factors.

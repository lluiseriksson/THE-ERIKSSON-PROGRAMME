STRONG GREEN-MOMENT CERTIFICATES: EXACT PARTITION-COST PHASE DIAGRAM

0. The question being filtered

The sole original critical rectangular observation problem stays open. A
candidate sufficient local method would replace a local inverse spectral-gap
bound by a strong Schatten moment of the local Green operator. This note
proves that the replacement itself imposes unnecessary partition costs even
on the constant coefficient family. It supplies matching upper constructions,
not merely a failed particular partition. It does not exclude spectral-gap,
capacity, truncated counting or weak-Schatten methods in general.

Fix integer D>=3 and s>0, independent of n. Put X=(Z/nZ)^D, N=n^D, n>=2,
and a(x)=mu=n^2. Counting norms and unit forward differences are used. The
global scalar energy is E(u)=n^2 sum_{x,i}|u(x+e_i)-u(x)|^2.
The original coefficient assumptions hold with critical p=D/2 and M=1:
1<=a<=2mu, mean(a)=mu and mean((mu/a)^p)=1. Moreover
max(1,N/(1+mu)^p)=1. No contrast or geometric defect is present.

A rectangle R is a product of cyclic integer intervals of lengths l_i in
{1,...,n}. Its induced internal energy is defined by cutting each coordinate
to a path. One may alternatively retain the periodic edge in any coordinate
whose length is exactly n. All conclusions below hold under either convention,
including mixtures of those conventions. No wrap edge of a shorter interval
is silently added. Let L_R be the weighted scalar graph Laplacian, with edge
weight n^2 and the usual counting inner product. It has one constant kernel
when |R|>0. Define G_R=L_R^dagger, zero on constants, and

    T_s(R)=tr(G_R^s)=sum_{lambda_j(L_R)>0}lambda_j(L_R)^(-s).

For a singleton T_s=0. The strong local certificate considered here is

    T_s(R)<=1 for every leaf R.                                  (C_s)

Since lambda_1^(-s)<=T_s, this really proves variance_R(u)<=E_R(u),
so it is a sufficient condition, not an invented failing oracle. Summing
over a partition and adding the squared normalized rectangular means proves
the original observation inequality with c=K=1 on all scalar fields and,
componentwise, on every fixed-dimensional cochain field. For the unrestricted
observation problem the number of output coordinates is h times the cell
count, for fixed component dimension h. No necessary coexact output rank is
inferred from a certificate's cell count.

Let q_s(n) be the minimum number of disjoint rectangles covering X that pass
(C_s). Singletons make q_s(n) finite. The minimum may use arbitrary cuts,
translations, aspect ratios and non-hierarchical partitions.

THEOREM. For each fixed D>=3 and fixed s>0, as n tends to infinity,

    q_s(n) = Theta_{D,s}(n^(D-2s))       if 0<s<D/2;
    q_s(n) = Theta_D(log n)             if s=D/2;
    q_s(n) = Theta_{D,s}(1)             if s>D/2.                 (P)

Nevertheless the true local spectral-gap test already passes on the single
root rectangle at c=1, even with every periodic edge cut. The original
periodic energy admits one global mean at c=K=1 for all n. A weak critical
Green test, stated precisely below, also passes with that same one cell.

1. The actual finite spectra and a successful one-cell control

For the unweighted path of length l, the vectors
v_k(x)=cos(pi*k*(x+1/2)/l), 0<=k<l, have eigenvalues
4sin^2(pi*k/(2l)). The interior difference identity follows by addition of
cosines. At the first endpoint v_k(0)-v_k(1) equals the displayed eigenvalue
times v_k(0); the final endpoint uses sin(pi*k)=0. These l distinct
eigenvalues and symmetry give a full orthogonal eigenbasis. For a length-n
cycle the usual finite Fourier characters have eigenvalues
4sin^2(pi*k/n), 0<=k<n. At n=2 both positive-start periodic edges are retained,
so the nonzero cycle eigenvalue is4, whereas the path eigenvalue is2.

Tensor products diagonalize the actual Cartesian product Laplacian. For the
cut rectangle its positive eigenvalues are therefore

    lambda_k=4n^2 sum_i sin^2(pi*k_i/(2l_i)),
    k_i in {0,...,l_i-1}, k not all zero.                        (S)

Retaining a full-period edge only adds a positive quadratic form, preserves
the one-dimensional constant kernel, and increases every ordered positive
eigenvalue by min-max. This eigenvalue comparison, not an unjustified
operator-power Loewner inequality, permits the upper estimates below for
every s>0. For lower estimates we explicitly cover both spectra.

The elementary inequalities sin t>=2t/pi on [0,pi/2] and sin t<=t imply
lambda_k(root cut)>=4|k|^2. Thus its positive gap is at least4 for all n.
For the actual periodic root, centered frequency r gives
lambda_r=4n^2 sum_i sin^2(pi*r_i/n)>=16|r|^2, so its gap is at least16.
Consequently one normalized mean C(u)=sqrt(N)*mean_X(u) gives

    ||u||^2 <= E(u)+|C(u)|^2.                                  (ONE)

This works componentwise, and hence on the actual flat coexact domain as
well; the latter has zero global mean and needs no constant-mode repair on
this homogeneous family. The unit-spacing finite operator has not been
identified with an interacting or continuum operator.

2. All-partition subcritical lower bound

Every such internal product graph has lambda_max<=4D n^2, under either
edge convention. If V=|R|, exactly V-1 eigenvalues are positive. Hence

    T_s(R)>=(V-1)/(4D n^2)^s.

Condition (C_s) implies V<=1+(4D n^2)^s. Summing the actual disjoint cell
volumes gives

    q>=n^D/[1+(4D n^2)^s].                                    (L_s)

For 0<s<D/2 this is Omega_{D,s}(n^(D-2s)). The proof does not assume cube
shapes, a tree, a mesh or any prescribed arrangement. In particular, for
D>=3 the ordinary Green trace s=1 forces polynomially many cells while
(ONE) uses one. Arbitrary positive cross-cell edges in the original energy
cannot improve this certificate cost, because the certificate discards them
by its own definition; the original energy itself does not discard them.

3. Critical lower bound for arbitrary aspect ratios

Put p=D/2. Let m=min_i l_i, V=product_i l_i, and

    J(m)=max(0,floor(log_2 m)-2),
    A_D=2^(-D)*(256D)^(-ceil(D/2)) > 0.

We prove the finite inequality

    T_p(R)>= A_D*(V/n^D)*J(m).                                 (G)

When m<8 its right side is zero. Otherwise set L=floor(log_2 m). For
j=0,...,L-3 put t_j=2^(j+1)/m and use the product of integer intervals

    ceil(t_j l_i) <= k_i < ceil(2t_j l_i), for every i.          (B_j)

For each coordinate x=t_j l_i>=2, the number of these integers is
ceil(2x)-ceil(x)>=x-1>=x/2. Thus (B_j) has at least V*t_j^D/2^D modes.
Every mode is nonconstant, belongs to the actual spectrum, and satisfies
0<k_i/l_i<2t_j<=1/2. The blocks for different j are disjoint, including
their exact ceiling endpoints: the next block starts at the previous
block's excluded upper endpoint.

For a path coordinate use sin(pi*k_i/(2l_i))<=pi*k_i/(2l_i). For a retained
cycle coordinate use sin(pi*k_i/l_i)<=pi*k_i/l_i. In either case

    lambda_k <=16*pi^2*D*n^2*t_j^2 <256D*n^2*t_j^2.

The pth inverse powers summed in this block are consequently at least

    2^(-D)*(256D)^(-p)*V/n^D >= A_D*V/n^D.

There are exactly J(m)=L-2 blocks. This proves (G), with a deliberately
small rational constant. No continuum Weyl remainder is invoked.

Now partition X into q rectangles. Call a cell large if its volume is at
least N/(2q). The other cells cover less than N/2, so large cells cover
at least N/2. Every side of a large cell is at least n/(2q), since all
other D-1 sides have length at most n. Summing (G) over large cells gives

    sum_R T_p(R) >= (A_D/2)*
             max(0,floor(log_2(n/(2q)))-2).

If every cell passes (C_p), the left side is at most q, whence

    q >= (A_D/2)*max(0,floor(log_2(n/(2q)))-2).                 (L_p)

For n>=2^16 this implies the explicit bound

    q >= (A_D/8)*log_2 n.                                     (LOG)

Indeed, if q>=sqrt(n), use sqrt(n)>=log_2 n and A_D/8<=1. Otherwise
log_2(n/(2q))>(log_2 n)/2-1 and the floored expression in (L_p) is
at least (log_2 n)/2-4 >=(log_2 n)/4. This proves the critical Omega(log n)
bound for every rectangular partition, not merely isotropic or hierarchical
ones. Quantities n, q and side lengths remain integers throughout.

4. Matching upper constructions, including the critical logarithm

Take an ordinary Cartesian mesh whose coordinate interval lengths are at
most an integer M<=n. All leaves are rectangles; the last interval in a
coordinate may be shorter. The path spectrum in (S) obeys

    lambda_k>=4n^2*|k|^2/M^2.

For r=max_i k_i>=1 the number of nonnegative integer modes in a shell is
at most (r+1)^D-r^D<=D*2^(D-1)*r^(D-1). Therefore every leaf satisfies

    T_s(R)<= D*2^(D-1)*4^(-s)*(M/n)^(2s)
                      *sum_{r=1}^{M-1}r^(D-1-2s).              (U)

Singleton leaves have trace zero. The same estimate holds if the permitted
full-period edges are retained, by the ordered-eigenvalue comparison above.

If 0<s<p, put b=D-2s>0. The elementary integral estimate bounds the sum in
(U) by (1+1/b)M^b. Let B=max(1,D*2^(D-1)*4^(-s)*(1+1/b)). Choose

    M=max(1,min(n,floor((n^(2s)/B)^(1/D)))).

When the floor is zero the leaves are singletons. Otherwise (U)<=1.
For all sufficiently large n, M is at least half the positive quantity
inside that floor, so q=ceil(n/M)^D=O_{D,s}(n^(D-2s)). Together with
(L_s), this proves the first line of (P).

If s=p, (U) becomes

    T_p(R)<=(D/2)*(M/n)^D*H_{M-1}
           <=(D/2)*(M/n)^D*(1+log M).

With B=max(1,D/2), take

    M=max(1,floor(n/[B*(1+log n)]^(1/D))).

Again the floor-zero case is a singleton mesh; otherwise all traces are
at most1. For large n the cell count is O_D(1+log n). Combining with
(LOG) proves the critical Theta(log n), not just a divergent lower bound.

If s>p, the sum in (U) is at most 1+1/(2s-D). Define
B=D*2^(D-1)*4^(-s)*(1+1/(2s-D)) and
epsilon=min(1,B^(-1/(2s))). Take M=max(1,floor(epsilon*n)).
Then (U)<=1 whenever M>=2, and singleton cases also pass. For sufficiently
large n, q<=ceil(2/epsilon)^D, a constant depending only on D,s. Since q>=1,
this proves the last line of (P).

5. The weak endpoint does not pay the logarithm on this family

For the cut root and p=D/2 define

    W_p(root)=sup_{lambda>0} lambda^(-p)*
           #{positive eigenvalues of L_root strictly below lambda}.

From lambda_k>=4|k|^2, there are none for lambda<4. For lambda>=4 their
number is at most (1+sqrt(lambda)/2)^D<=lambda^p. Thus

    W_p(root)<=1                                               (WEAK)

uniformly in n. Retaining periodic edges decreases the counting function,
so the periodic root satisfies this too. This is equivalently the pth
power of the weak-S_p quasi-norm of its Green operator. The bound W_p<=1
implies no positive eigenvalue below1, since the counting function is an
integer and lambda^p<1 for lambda<1. Thus this weak endpoint test is itself
a legitimate successful one-cell certificate here, not an appeal to the
unknown rectangular theorem.

The underlying weak/strong trace-ideal distinction is classical. The
specific all-rectangle cost lower bound (L_p), matched by the mesh above,
is a finite programme adapter; no independent originality is asserted.
Replacing coefficient inverse moments by Green inverse spectral moments
is not a harmless change of hypothesis. These are different objects. The
original critical COEFFICIENT moment is exactly1 in this example and is
not refuted or removed from its global sufficient theorem.

6. Ordinary resistance interpretation (classical identity)

For a connected R of V vertices, define the actual effective resistance
R_eff(x,y)=(e_x-e_y)^T G_R(e_x-e_y). Since G_R*1=0, summing this identity
over unordered pairs gives

    sum_{x<y}R_eff(x,y)=V*tr(G_R).

Thus requiring (1/V)sum_{x<y}R_eff(x,y)<=1 is exactly the s=1 certificate,
and for D>=3 its minimum rectangle count grows like n^(D-2). This does
not refute using selected resistances, operator norms or capacities. It
only rejects replacing the true gap by this complete all-pairs trace.

7. Two exact finite controls, derived without an executed oracle

First let D=4,n=4,mu=16 and retain the periodic edges of the root. A single
coordinate has eigenvalues0,32,64,32. Thus the root has eigenvalues32m
with multiplicity binomial(8,m), 0<=m<=8: the multiplicity polynomial is
(1+2z+z^2)^4=(1+z)^8. Its true positive gap is32, but already the m=3,4
groups give

    T_1(root)>=56/96+70/128=217/192>1.

So the s=1 trace test at c=1 rejects the one-cell solution, although the
actual periodic energy satisfies the original inequality with that cell.
A mesh of sixteen length-two boxes has T_1=103/384<1 on each cut box:
its eigenvalues are32m with multiplicities binomial(4,m). These statements
are exact finite spectral identities, not floating-point observations.

Second, a critical-endpoint finite example uses c=16 instead of c=1.
Let n=2 and any fixed D>=3, p=D/2, mu=4. The periodic root has true gap16,
so one mean and penalty K=c=16 work. Use the corresponding strong test

    16^p*T_p(R)<=1,                                            (C_p,16)

and retain every full-period edge. Every rectangle has r full coordinates
of length2 and D-r singleton coordinates, for some r. Its positive spectrum
is16m with multiplicity binomial(r,m), 1<=m<=r. Therefore the left side
of (C_p,16) is sum_{m=1}^r binomial(r,m)/m^p. It is0 for r=0, equals1
for r=1, and is at least r>=2 when r>=2. Thus passing rectangles have at
most2 vertices. Every passing partition has q>=2^(D-1), and a partition
into disjoint full-period coordinate lines attains equality. The exact
critical certificate cost is2^(D-1), although the true one-mean solution
cost is1. With D=4, the rejected root value is865/144. This example keeps
both positive-start periodic edges at n=2; replacing a cycle by a single
path edge would change the example and is not permitted silently.

This second control has c=16 and is separate from the c=1 normalization of
(P). For any other fixed c>0, inserting c^s in (C_s) changes only the
constants in the three partition-cost estimates: multiply the lower trace
bounds by c^s and the upper bound constants B by c^s. No fixed rescaling
of the strong critical trace removes its logarithmic asymptotic overhead.

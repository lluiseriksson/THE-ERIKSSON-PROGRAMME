SHARP ORDER OF THE UNAVOIDABLE LOGARITHMIC LOSS
Assume D>=3,c>0,Nc>=2, original1<=a<=2mu, actual arithmetic meanmu>=1.
Set P=D/2, Y(x)=mu/a(x), normalized counting probability on X_n, and
W=sup_(tau>0) tau^P P_X(Y>tau)<=M_w.
Because a>=1,0<Y<=mu. The finite layer-cake identity gives exactly
 E[Y^P]=P integral_0^mu tau^(P-1) P_X(Y>tau) d tau.
Bounding probability by1 on(0,1), and by M_w tau^(-P) on[1,mu], yields
 E[Y^P]<=1+P M_w log(mu).
This also holds atmu=1: the second integral is zero. No finite atom at a
strict cutoff affects an ordinary integral. This is an elementary finite
distribution calculation, not a new general integration theorem.

The dependence is logarithmic in the coefficient scale mu; no missing

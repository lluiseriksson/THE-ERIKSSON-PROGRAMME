INTEGER SHELL203: AN ACTUAL THICK-SHELL COVER AND ITS THIN-SHELL LIMIT
2026-11-08. Internal research dossier, not a new paper or publication.

1. Scope and inventory
The only active main question remains the critical-moment, coefficient-only
rectangle partition question recorded in checkpoint202. This note tests its
proposed ring mechanism. It does not answer that question. The earlier
threshold construction and binary corollary are already in the manuscript;
neither is repeated or counted as new here. Source201B established a general
rooted-overlap inequality and a three-set counterexample to an unjustified
uniform gluing assertion. Here the set geometry is actually constructed.

All sets below have counting measure, all functions are real scalar fields,
and all sums and errors are unnormalized. D>=2, R and r are integers with
R>=1 and 0<=r<R. Put t=R-r, L=2R+1, I=[-R,R] intersect Z, and
 A=I^D \ ([-r,r] intersect Z)^D.
Use only nonwrapping nearest-neighbor internal edges, counted once.
Define e_S(f)^2=sum_(x in S)|f(x)-mean_S(f)|^2.

2. Exact finite cover, including the smallest scale
For each coordinate j and sign + or -, let
 S_(j,+)={x in I^D: r+1<=x_j<=R},
 S_(j,-)={x in I^D: -R<=x_j<=-r-1}.
These are J=2D nonempty integer rectangles, each contained in A, and their
union equals A: being outside the inner cube means exactly that at least
one coordinate satisfies one of the displayed inequalities. Duplicated
membership on edges/corners is allowed; this is a COVER, not a partition.
Every slab has v=t L^(D-1) vertices. Opposite slabs of the same coordinate
are disjoint. Slabs from different coordinates, regardless of signs, have
exact intersection theta=t^2 L^(D-2), so theta/v=t/L.

The intersection graph is connected for D>=2. Explicitly choose root(1,+),
join it to all 2(D-1) slabs of coordinates j>=2, then join (1,-) to (2,+).
This is a spanning tree with J-1 edges and depth at most two. The existence
of this tree uses D>=2. For D=1 the shell has two disconnected components;
no single-mean internal-energy Poincare estimate can hold there.
R=1,r=0,t=1,L=3 is included, with no empty slabs or rounded intersections.
Translations by integer vectors preserve everything. No torus identification
or arbitrary off-center hole is included in this statement.

3. Derived uniform gluing on thick shells
Recall the proven201B bound, valid for any finite connected tree of sets:
 e_union(f)^2 <= [1+2 Delta_T Q_T] sum_i e_Si(f)^2,
 Q_T=sum_i |S_i| sum_(edges on root-to-i path) 1/|overlap_edge|.
For the explicit tree above there are J-2 vertices at depth one and one at
depth two. Since every edge has overlap theta and every slab has volume v,
 Q_T=J v/theta=J L/t, and Delta_T<=J-1.
Thus, for EVERY f on A,
 e_A(f)^2 <= G(D,R,r) sum_i e_Si(f)^2,
 G(D,R,r)=1+2 J(J-1)L/t.                              (3.1)
No field-dependent tree, cover, mean-selection or geometric premise was
introduced. If t>=eta L with a fixed eta>0, the actual derived constant is
 G<=1+2J(J-1)/eta.
In particular for R=2m,r=m,m>=1, L/t=4+1/m<=5, hence
 G<=G_D:=1+10J(J-1).                                 (3.2)
This verifies a sufficient geometry for these concentric thick lattice
shells; it does not repair every ring cover mentioned in a continuum paper.

4. Actual internal-energy estimate, no extension hypothesis
For any integer box B with coordinate lengths at most L, we have
 e_B(f)^2 <= L^2 E_B(f),                              (4.1)
where E_B is its unweighted internal-edge energy.
For completeness: on an interval of length ell,
 sum_i |g_i-mean g|^2=(1/ell)sum_(i<j)|g_i-g_j|^2.
Cauchy-Schwarz along the interval bounds each pair by
 (j-i)sum_(k=i)^(j-1)|g_(k+1)-g_k|^2.
For a fixed edge k, at most k(ell-k)<=ell^2/4 pairs cross it,
and j-i<=ell. Its coefficient after division by ell is at most
ell^2/4<=L^2. For ell=1 both sides of the variance bound vanish.
For a product box, variance tensorization follows inductively from
 Var(f)=E[Var(f|all but first coordinate)]
       +Var(E[f|all but first coordinate]).
Apply the induction hypothesis to the averaged function in the second
term; Jensen bounds each of its directional differences by the averaged
squared difference of f. Apply the interval estimate to the first term.
Multiplying by |B| gives (4.1), with ordinary counting energy and no extra
dimension or volume factor. This argument also proves the statement for
shorter coordinate lengths and vector components separately.

Every slab is a box with lengths t,L,...,L. Every edge internal to a slab
is internal to A. A fixed internal edge of A belongs to at most J slabs.
Consequently sum_i E_Si(f)<=J E_A(f), and (3.1),(4.1) give
 e_A(f)^2 <= J L^2 [1+2J(J-1)L/t] E_A(f).              (4.2)
For R=2m,r=m this is at most 25 J G_D m^2 E_A(f), since
L=4m+1<=5m. All bounds are for every f using the same cover.
If the specified positive-start coefficient a(x)>=alpha>0 on A, then
E_a,A>=alpha E_A term by term, so (4.2) holds with E_A replaced by
E_a,A/alpha. The critical moment condition ALONE is not proved to supply
such an alpha on selected shells; no hypothesis is claimed eliminated by
that conditional weighted observation.

5. A precise obstruction to extending THIS geometric criterion to thin shells
Take r=R-1, hence t=1, and let R tend through positive integers to infinity.
For this same J-slab cover every nonempty pairwise intersection has measure
theta and every slab volume is v. In ANY rooted spanning tree of its
intersection graph, every nonroot vertex has path length at least one.
Therefore
 Q_T=(L/t)sum_i depth_T(i)>=(J-1)L/t.                 (5.1)
No choice of root or tree gives a dimension-only Q_T bound for this cover.
All slab volumes are equal, so ordering by nonincreasing volume cannot fix
the small overlap fraction t/L. The conjecture that this particular cover
always satisfies the proposed uniform rooted-overlap criterion is refuted
by the exact count (5.1), independently of a numerical search.

THIS IS NOT a field counterexample to a thin-shell Poincare inequality,
nor a lower bound on its optimal constant, nor a refutation of another
cover or of unrestricted rectangle partitions. A divergent sufficient
constant does not prove such impossibility. The three-cube bridge201B
cannot be embedded by merely renaming these comparable-volume slabs.

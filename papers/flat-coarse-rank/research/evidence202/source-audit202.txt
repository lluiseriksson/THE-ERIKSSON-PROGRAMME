Revision202 correctly replaces it by the exact binary count: for H>1 every marked vertex has a=1, contributes mu^p/N to the actual moment, and hence m mu^p/N<=M. This gives m<=MN/mu^p. No distribution or separation assumption is introduced.
For completeness, the stronger base-grid constant does follow from the earlier algorithm. Write x=sqrt(tau/c). In the nonsingleton-algorithm branch tau>=c/4, x>=1/2. If the cap does not choose n, ell=floor(2x)>=x. Thus ceil(n/ell)<=1+n sqrt(c/tau). This is at most (1+sqrt c) max(1,n/sqrt tau). If ell=n the grid has one cell and obeys the same bound. Raising to D gives B_c max(1,N/tau^p), B_c=(1+sqrt c)^D. No old factor 4 max(1,sqrt c) is needed. Isolating one marked vertex adds at most 2D leaves, so J<=B_c max(1,N/H^p)+2Dm.
Because 1<=mu<=H, the two terms are bounded by (B_c+2DM) max(1,N/mu^p). Finally 1+mu<=2mu implies max(1,N/mu^p)<=2^p max(1,N/(1+mu)^p). This is precisely the displayed count, with no missing power of N, n, D or component multiplicity. It is a sufficient upper bound, not an exact cell count or minimum-rank statement.

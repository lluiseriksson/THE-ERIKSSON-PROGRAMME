1. Coefficient and moment algebra — definitions and section 1
For fixed integer D>=3 and n>=3, N=n^D and p=D/2 give H^p=(n^2)^(D/2)=N. The arithmetic mean is exactly H-(H-1)/N. It lies between H/2 and H, so the profile has 1<=a<=2mu and mu>=1. Strict positivity is maintained at the weak vertex.
The inverse moment factors exactly as
 M_actual=(mu/H)^p(2-1/N)
         =[1-(1-1/H)/N]^p(2-1/N).
Both factors imply M_actual<2. As n tends to infinity with D fixed, both mu/H and 1-1/N tend to one, so M_actual tends to 2. Consequently every fixed M0<2 is eventually exceeded. This is an objection to the stronger wording in the assignment, not to the source: the source explicitly and consistently takes M=2. It is already enough to falsify an extension assertion demanded for every admissible profile with a uniform constant C(D,2).
The target scale satisfies N/(1+mu)^p<=N/(H/2)^p=2^p, and therefore max(1,N/(1+mu)^p)<=2^p. No growing moment or growing scale is hidden in this counterexample. The source makes no assertion for every prescribed M between one and two, and this audit does not silently replace its coefficient family to obtain such an assertion.

2. Internal two-point energy — section 2
R={0,e_1} is a valid product rectangle with D-1 singleton sides. With the stated periodic positive-start convention and n>=3, the only edge having both endpoints in R is (0,1). The edge starting at e_1 in direction 1 ends at 2e_1, which is outside R even for n=3. The closing edge from -e_1 to 0 starts outside R. Other directions leave the rectangle. Thus the internal energy contains coefficient one exactly once; it is not the symmetrized sum of weights at both endpoints.
For values 1,-1, counting squared norm is 2 and internal energy is 4. More generally every real mean-zero local field has values t,-t, so the sharp positive Rayleigh quotient is 2. The same holds over complex scalars. This depends on the source's internal-edge convention. At n=2 a second opposite positive start would be internal and the conclusion would change; n=2 is explicitly excluded and cannot be added without revising the example.

3. Lower bound for every exact extension — section 3
The path 0 -> -e_2 -> e_1-e_2 -> e_1 has three distinct positive-start edge labels: (-e_2,2), (-e_2,1), and (e_1-e_2,2). The first is traversed backwards. Both starting vertices are nonzero for n>=3, and all three weights are H. These labels are distinct on the n=3 torus as well; there is no double counting or mistaken assignment of the weak coefficient at zero.
Telescoping fixes the sum of the three oriented increments to the endpoint difference, of magnitude two. Cauchy–Schwarz therefore bounds their squared increments below by 4/3. They are a subset of the actual global energy, proving Ea(v)>=4H/3 for every extension v, irrespective of its other values. The weak direct edge itself also contributes 4, so the stronger bound Ea(v)>=4+4H/3 is available; omitting it merely weakens a valid lower bound and is not an error.
Dividing by the local energy 4 forces C(D,2)>=H/3. Since H=n^2 is unbounded at fixed D and fixed admissibility bound 2, no such uniform finite extension constant exists. This is independent of linearity or of how the extension is selected. Global constants do not affect the endpoint-difference bound; adding one to an exact extension would normally change its exact trace, but even allowing such shifts cannot reduce this energy obstruction. The proof is stronger than failure of zero extension.

4. Global gap and all directed-edge multiplicities — section 4
There are precisely D weak positive starts at zero. For the bypass associated with direction i, all retained edge starts are nonzero, as above with e_2 replaced by e_sigma(i). The inverse dictionary for a fixed positive edge (y,k) is complete: a first segment requires source y+e_k and direction sigma^-1(k); a middle segment requires source y+e_sigma(k) and direction k; a last segment requires source y-e_sigma^-1(k)+e_k and direction sigma^-1(k). Each type fixes a unique path.
For a single cycle sigma through all D>=3 directions, k, sigma(k), and sigma^-1(k) are distinct. Relative to y, the three candidate positions are pairwise different modulo n for n>=3. The last has a nonzero sigma^-1(k) coordinate absent from the first two; the first two differ in distinct coordinates. The single weak source zero can therefore realize at most one type for that edge. This bounds uses rather than merely the number of distinct paths. It proves Ew<=3Es and E0<=4Es by three-step Cauchy–Schwarz. Since Ea=Ew+H Es, Ea>=H Es>=H E0/4 is valid. No assumption about a generated comparison operator replaces this argument.
The standard counting-energy Fourier eigenvalues are 4 sum_i sin^2(pi k_i/n). For every nonconstant mode the minimum is 4 sin^2(pi/n), and sin(pi/n)>=2/n proves the lower bound 16/n^2. Multiplication by H/4 with H=n^2 gives Ea(v)>=4||v-mean v||^2. Complex fields also satisfy the same calculation; the stated real-vector extension follows componentwise.
For c=3, orthogonal decomposition into the global mean and its complement yields 3||v||^2<=Ea(v)+3N|mean v|^2. Thus one global spatial cell suffices while the displayed local gap is 2<3. For vector fields that cell carries one mean per component, not one scalar measurement for all components together. The global gap is a lower bound, not a claim that its sharp value is exactly four.

5. What is and is not refuted
The exact scalar extension assertion in the source is false with fixed M=2, without requiring a linear extension operator. A local low Neumann gap on this rectangle is not automatically a global low-energy mode. This is an obstruction to a specific transfer route; it neither refutes the actual rectangular partition question nor supplies many independent defects for a rank lower bound. In fact the same example has a successful global mean observation.
No local or global coexact extension obstruction is established here: a scalar counterexample is sufficient only for the scalar auxiliary assertion under test. Adding boundary energy, paying a capacity correction, changing local data, weakening exact trace, or allowing contrast-dependent constants changes the assertion and is not excluded by this proof. No claim of weakening a positive theorem's hypotheses is made. The result is a modest, useful negative test for a proposed mechanism, not an endpoint solution or a new general extension principle. It belongs with the existing dossier.

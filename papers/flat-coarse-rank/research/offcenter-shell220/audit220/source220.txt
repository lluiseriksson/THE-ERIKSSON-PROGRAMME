1. Geometry, actual coefficients and exact original data

Fix integers D>=3 and s>=2. Set d=D-1, P=D/2 and

 L=s^D, Q=4L+1, X={0,...,4L}^D, I={1,...,2L}^D,
 Omega=X\I, N=Q^D-(2L)^D.

There is one nonwrapping off-center cubical hole. Its side is comparable to
the outer side, but its lower clearance is one lattice step. Define the plate

 F={x in X:x_1=0, 1<=x_j<=2L for j=2,...,D}, w=|F|=(2L)^d.

Let actual starting-site coefficients on Omega be

 a(x)=A=s^(2d) on F, a(x)=H=Q^2 elsewhere.

In particular 1<=A<H. Their exact arithmetic mean and inverse mass are

 (1) mu=H-w(H-A)/N,
     S=sum_Omega a^(-P)=2^d+(N-w)/Q^D.

Indeed A^P=s^(dD)=L^d, so w/A^P=2^d, while H^P=Q^D.
Also N> (4^D-2^D)L^D, giving

 w/N<=1/[2(2^D-1)L]<=1/14.

Consequently mu>=H(1-w/N)>=13H/14>=H/2>=1, and
1<=a<=H<=2mu. The original normalized critical moment obeys

 (2) 1<=M_a=(mu^P/N) S
       <=(1+2^d)/(1-2^(-D))<2(1+2^d)=:M_D.

The lower bound is Jensen for t^(-P) and mean a=mu. The upper uses mu<=H,
S<1+2^d and N/Q^D=1-(2L/Q)^D>1-2^(-D). No local moment, mean or rare-set
density is an independent input. All are computed from this actual profile.

For real f on Omega use counting variance and actual nonwrapping energy

 V_Omega(f)=sum_Omega|f-mean_Omega f|^2,
 E_a,Omega(f)=sum_{x,x+e_j in Omega}a(x)|f(x+e_j)-f(x)|^2.

Each positive-axis edge is counted once at its ORIGINAL starting site. There
are no periodic identifications, reflected coefficients, endpoint averages,
separately optimized extensions or discarded intercell edges in this form.

2. An actual slow plate mode, with every boundary edge accounted for

For t=1,...,2L set h(t)=min(t-1,2L-t). Thus h(1)=h(2L)=0 and

 (3) sum h=L(L-1),
     T:=sum h^2=(L-1)L(2L-1)/3,
     sum_{t=1}^{2L-1}|h(t+1)-h(t)|^2=2L-2.

The first two identities sum the two copies of0,...,L-1. The last counts
two runs of L-1 unit increments and the single central zero increment.
Since L>=2, T>0. Put

 f(0,x_2,...,x_D)=product_{j=2}^D h(x_j) on F,
 f=0 on Omega\F.

The only nonzero original edge differences in Omega are tangential internal
plate edges. The possible normal incoming edge to x_1=0 starts outside X,
so is absent. The possible normal outgoing edge enters I, so is absent.
At each tangential boundary x_j=1 or2L the plate field is ZERO. Thus edges
joining that boundary to a strong outside-plate site have zero difference,
including all actual strong incoming starts. This is why no strong edge is
being silently reassigned weight A. No interface within Omega is dropped.

Product counting now gives exactly

 (4) ||f||_2^2=T^d,
     sum_Omega f=[L(L-1)]^d,
     E_a,Omega(f)=A d(2L-2) T^(d-1).

Cauchy on its w-site support gives
V_Omega(f)>= (1-w/N)||f||_2^2>=||f||_2^2/2. Hence

 (5) E_a,Omega(f)/||f||_2^2=6d A/[L(2L-1)]<=6d/s^2,
     V_Omega(f)/E_a,Omega(f)>=s^2/(12d).

Subtracting mean_Omega f gives a counting-mean-zero witness with unchanged
energy and variance, if required. This is an ACTUAL annular low-energy
family; it is not only a failed sufficient partition certificate.

3. The centered critical-moment formula cannot extend uniformly

The thickness-uniform centered-shell estimate205 has the form

 V<=U_D Q^2 (mean_Omega a^(-D/2))^(2/D) E_a,Omega.

For the present family its multiplier, without U_D, is bounded by

 (6) Q^2(S/N)^(2/D)
      <=[(1+2^d)/(1-2^(-D))]^(2/D).

This follows by canceling Q^2 against (Q^D)^(2/D). It is dimension-only,
while(5) diverges with s. Therefore no dimension-only U_D makes this
off-center version true, even though the actual mean/envelope and original
normalized critical moment have the fixed bounds(1)-(2), and the inner/outer
side ratio stays comparable to1/2. Centeredness cannot be removed with NO
replacement quantitative geometric condition. This does not prove that exact
centering is the weakest possible condition; clearance hypotheses may suffice.

4. Matching upper order from an actual unweighted cover

We also derive an upper bound of the same s^2 order, not a supplied annular
gap. Let J=2D. Cover Omega by the actual rectangles

 S_{i,-}: x_i=0, others in{0,...,4L};
 S_{i,+}: x_i in{2L+1,...,4L}, others in{0,...,4L}.

Their volumes are v_-=Q^d and v_+=2L Q^d. Every annular vertex belongs to
at least one slab. Root a tree at S_{1,+}; attach every slab with normal
different from1 to the root, and attach S_{1,-} to S_{2,+}. Every path has
at most two edges, each joining distinct normals. Every parent is a fat
plus slab, and a descendant volume is at most every volume on its root path.

Write E_i^0 for each slab's unweighted internal energy and Z=sum_i E_i^0.
The proved product-interval inequality gives V_{S_i}<=Q^2 E_i^0/4.
For completeness its interval step is pairwise variance plus path Cauchy:
an ell-site interval has V<=ell(ell-1)sum|dg|^2/4. Commuting coordinate
averages tensorize this to the largest-side bound on every rectangle.
Singleton normal coordinates have no energy or variance in that direction.
This dependency is proved in revision217/partition.tex, not assumed here.

We give the needed mean transport explicitly. In a Q-site interval B and a
nonempty subinterval T_0, Jensen and pair paths give

 |mean_B g-mean_T0 g|^2
 <=(Q|T_0|)^(-1)sum_{x in B,y in T_0}|g(x)-g(y)|^2
 <=Q sum_{e in B}|dg(e)|^2.

Averaging this over the identical fibers of a slab S_i, in a free full
coordinate j, gives the actual directional inequality

 |mean_Si f-mean_{Si intersect Sj} f|^2
 <=Q^2 E_i^0/v_i,

when the other slab has normal j!=i. Its normal restriction has width1 or2L;
no inverse overlap-width factor occurs. Applying the same inequality from
the other slab and using the identical intersection mean yields

 (7) |m_i-m_parent|^2<=2Q^2(E_i^0/v_i+E_parent^0/v_parent).

For a root path of length at most two, multiply by the descendant's volume,
use its volume monotonicity along that path, and use (a+b)^2<=2(a^2+b^2).
Each intermediate slab energy occurs at most twice, so

 v_i|m_i-m_root|^2<=8Q^2 Z.

The root's term is zero. The cover and exact variance decomposition about
each slab mean therefore imply, for every real g,

 V_Omega(g)<=sum_i[V_Si(g)+v_i|m_i-m_root|^2]
            <=(1/4+8J)Q^2 Z
            <=J(1/4+8J)Q^2 E_Omega^0(g).

The last step counts each actual internal edge at most J times. Since a>=A
and Q<=5L, this proves

 (8) V_Omega(g)<=B_D s^2 E_a,Omega(g),
     B_D=25J(1+8J), J=2D.

For D=3, B_3=7350. The loose integer constant replaces1/4 by1 safely.
Together(5) and(8) prove that the best Poincare constant of this ACTUAL
weighted annular graph is Theta_D(s^2); equivalently its first nonconstant
finite Neumann energy quotient is Theta_D(s^(-2)). These are finite scalar
graph notions, not a physical spectral gap. Neither optimal constants nor

5. Restoring the hole: the original full-domain distinction

Fill I with coefficient H and extend the SAME plate field by zero there.
Then the exact full NONWRAPPING cube energy is

 (9) E_a,X(f)=E_a,Omega(f)+A||f||_2^2.

Each newly present edge with nonzero difference is the normal edge from
x_1=0 into x_1=1, at tangential coordinates in{1,...,2L}^d. Its ORIGINAL
START is on the weak plate and its coefficient is A, NOT H. All other newly
present differences are zero. Thus the actual full energy of this witness
has quotient at least A>=1. It is not a full-domain counterexample.

Indeed the whole cube has inverse critical mass

 S_X=2^d+(Q^D-w)/Q^D<1+2^d.

The complete counting cube proof in source2c881a5e8f31cad5643a49cb205fe8eb3156955d,
revision217/sobolev.tex, gives V_X<=K_D^2(D S_X)^(2/D) E_a,X, with
K_D<=8sqrt(D). To check the latter bound, its exact constants are
K_D=2alpha D^(1/q')/[D(1-2^(-1/D))], alpha=2(D-1)/D,
q'=2D/(D-2). Bernoulli (1-1/(2D))^D>=1/2 gives the denominator>=1/2,
and alpha<=2,D^(1/q')<=sqrt(D) finish the bound. Hence

 V_X<=64D^2(1+2^d) E_a,X.

The actual one-rectangle normalized mean q_X=Q^(-D/2)sum_X f therefore gives

gives the probability-normalized convention. Adding periodic wrap edges

annular theorem. The filled cube also has its own actual mean and bounded

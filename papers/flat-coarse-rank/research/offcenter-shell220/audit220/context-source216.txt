Analytical supplement to shell205, not a separate paper. 2026-11-09.

0. Question, inventory and boundary
The sole main question remains the unrestricted critical rectangular
partition question recorded in Section27 of the existing dossier. This
note tests its local shell mechanism; it opens no second main question.
205 already proves the shell bound with inverse moment D/2 and uniform
thickness constant. We do not re-count that theorem or its proof.
The distinct result here lowers the local moment to (D-1)/2, paying a
derived, necessary thickness factor. No selection/count theorem follows.
The falsifier for the proposed local improvement is a shell/field/positive
starting-site weight violating (1). A second falsifier tests whether its
thickness factor can be deleted. We settle that second test negatively.
Abandon this mechanism on a valid counterexample; do not modify a judge
after failure. Main neutral-ancestor/boundary-additivity routes stay stopped.

1. Statement and conventions
Let D>=3, d=D-1, p=d/2, q=2d/(d+2), J=2D. In counting measure put
 A=([-R,R] intersect Z)^D minus ([-r,r] intersect Z)^D,
 R>=1, 0<=r<R integers, t=R-r, L=2R+1, N=|A|.
For a real field f and arbitrary positive a:A->(0,infinity), define
 V_A(f)=sum_A |f-mean_A(f)|^2,
 E_a,A(f)=sum_(x,x+e_i in A) a(x)|f(x+e_i)-f(x)|^2.
Edges are internal, positive-axis oriented, counted ONCE. Coefficients
are the ORIGINAL starting-site coefficients. No outside energy or weights.
Then, with a finite explicit dimension-only C_D,

 V_A(f) <= C_D (sum_A a^(-p))^(1/p) E_a,A(f)                 (1)
        <= C_D J^(2/d) L^2 t^(2/d)
                       [mean_A a^(-p)]^(1/p) E_a,A(f).

The first line is the stronger volume form. Both hold for every t>=1,
including t=1 and r=0. They are for the nonwrapping concentric integer
shell only. Translation changes nothing; off-center/multiple holes and
wrapping identifications are not covered.
For any parameter mu>0, an ACTUAL LOCAL bound
 mean_A (mu/a)^p <= M_A
therefore gives V_A <= C_D J^(2/d) L^2 t^(2/d) M_A^(1/p) E_a,A/mu.
This is not inferred from an ambient moment by relabeling it as local.

Constants: c_d=d(1-2^(-1/d)). For d=2 take K_2=2. For d>=3 put
 alpha_d=2(d-1)/d, qstar=2d/(d-2),
 K_d=2 alpha_d d^(1/qstar)/c_d.
Set B_d=max(K_d^2,1), S_D=J(B_d+16), C_D=S_D D^(2/d).
In particular S_3=120, C_3=360 and the normalized second-line coefficient
is 2160. These constants are upper bounds, not claimed optimal.

2. Existing cube lemma and the d=2 endpoint
For a d-dimensional L-site cube Q and counting norms,
 V_Q(g) <= K_d^2 (sum_(internal Q edges)|dg|^q)^(2/q).       (2)
For d>=3 this is exactly the already-proved cube theorem in
 papers/flat-coarse-rank/integrated-revision/revision202/sobolev.tex,
commit 8262c72573387d44d82359636f65ec635449a131, with its dimension
variable replaced by d. Its finite entropy/projection, relative EDGE
isoperimetry, coarea/median and power-Holder proof is the dependency,
not a new premise or new result here. Only its cube theorem is used;
no aspect-ratio reflection or weighted extension is required.

For d=2, that same finite projection argument gives
 |boundary_E U| >= (2-sqrt(2)) |U|^(1/2) >= (1/2)|U|^(1/2)
when |U|<=|Q|/2. The second inequality follows from sqrt(2)<=3/2.
For nonnegative v supported on at most half Q, finite coarea and the
triangle inequality in l2 give ||v||_2 <= 2 sum_edges |dv|.
Split g at a counting median into v_+ and v_-. Each support is at most
half Q and edgewise |dv_+|+|dv_-|=|dg|. Thus
 V_Q(g) <= ||g-median(g)||_2^2
 <=4[(sum|dv_+|)^2+(sum|dv_-|)^2] <=4(sum|dg|)^2.
This proves (2) with q=1,K_2=2 without importing a false qstar=infinity
power argument. For an L=1 cube the variance is zero.

3. Slab lemma: one short direction, d long directions
Let B=Q_L^d times {1,...,t}, with 1<=t<=L. Write m_z for the mean
of f in its z-th d-cube layer, T for total q-energy in long directions,
and U for total q-energy in the short direction. The exact orthogonal
variance decomposition is
 V_B(f)=sum_z V_Q(f_z) + L^d sum_z |m_z-mean_z(m)|^2.
By (2) and 2/q>=1, its first term is <=K_d^2 T^(2/q).
For any t-site sequence m, all point differences are at most
 t^(1-1/q)(sum|dm|^q)^(1/q). Jensen at its arithmetic mean implies
 sum_z |m_z-mean(m)|^2 <= t^(3-2/q)(sum|dm|^q)^(2/q).
For t=1 this term is zero. Jensen across each L^d-point layer gives
 sum|dm|^q <= L^(-d) U.
Consequently the second term is bounded by
 L^(d-2d/q) t^(3-2/q) U^(2/q)
 = L^(-2) t^(2-2/d) U^(2/q) <= U^(2/q),
because t<=L and d>=2. Hence
 V_B(f) <= B_d (T+U)^(2/q).                              (3)
Every slab below has precisely this geometry. All edges used in (3)
belong to the slab. A zero short-direction energy for t=1 is allowed.

4. Shell mean transport, independently of overlap-error gluing
Use the J slabs S_(i,+) with x_i in[r+1,R] and S_(i,-) with
x_i in[-R,-r-1], all other coordinates in[-R,R]. They cover A and
have v=t L^d vertices. Distinct normals intersect in t^2 L^(d-1)
points. Root at (1,+), attach the J-2 other-normal slabs to it, then
attach (1,-) to (2,+). Every root path has at most two edges, all
joining distinct normals. This is valid also at t=1,R=1.

For an L-site interval I, a nonempty subinterval T0 and any g,
 |mean_I(g)-mean_T0(g)|^q <= L^(q-1) sum_I |dg|^q.
Indeed average the point differences over I times T0, apply Jensen and
the path Holder inequality of at most L-1 edges. The factor |T0|
cancels in that average; no inverse-overlap-volume factor survives.
For B=I times F, Jensen over the SAME fibers gives
 |mean_B(f)-mean_(T0 times F)(f)|
 <= L^(1-1/q)|F|^(-1/q) ||directional df||_q.
For each side of a tree-edge slab intersection |F|=t L^(d-1).
Thus, defining G=(sum_(edges internal A)|df|^q)^(1/q),
 H=L^(1-d/q)t^(-1/q)=L^(-d/2)t^(-1/q),
each slab mean differs from the intersection mean by at most H G.
Each tree-edge mean difference is at most 2HG, and each slab mean
differs from the root mean by at most 4HG. Importantly,
 v H^2 = t^(1-2/q)=t^(-2/d)<=1.                         (4)

5. Unweighted shell inequality, then original weights
Use the root mean as a candidate constant, then the slab cover and
the EXACT decomposition about each slab mean (no extra factor two):
 V_A(f) <=sum_i [V_Si(f)+v|mean_Si(f)-mean_root(f)|^2]
 <=J B_d G^2 +16J vH^2 G^2 <= S_D G^2.                 (5)
Each individual slab energy is <= the full shell energy. This avoids
assuming disjoint slabs, adding independent optimal extensions, or a
uniform best-constant overlap gluing theorem, all of which have separate
scope restrictions/counterexamples in the earlier dossier.

For each original retained edge b_e=a(start(e)), Holder gives
 G^2 <= (sum_e b_e|df|^2) (sum_e b_e^(-p))^(1/p),
since q/(2-q)=p. At most D edges start at any site, so
 sum_e b_e^(-p) <= D sum_A a^(-p).
Combine with (5) to prove the first line of (1).
The cover gives N<=JtL^d, which proves its second line. All selections
and constants are independent of f and a. Finite real vector fields
follow coordinatewise with the same coefficient factor; no assertion
about physical coexact subspaces or other observations follows.

6. Sharp power losses: an actual starting-site counterexample
These examples show two different obstructions to stronger versions.
Fix any p0>0, the same shell, and x0=(R,R,0,...,0). All D vertices
x0-e_i are in A and distinct. Let W={x0,x0-e_1,...,x0-e_D}, h=D+1,
 epsilon=(h/N)^(1/p0), a=epsilon on W and a=1 elsewhere.
Here N>=3^D-1>=2h, so 0<epsilon<1. Directly,
 mean_A a^(-p0)=2-h/N<2.
For f=indicator_{x0}, V_A=(N-1)/N>=1/2. There are exactly 2D-2
retained incident edges at x0: D incoming and D-2 outgoing. Every
one has starting coefficient epsilon. Thus
 E_a,A(f)=(2D-2)epsilon,
 V_A/E_a,A >= [4(D-1)]^(-1) (N/h)^(1/p0).              (6)
The weakening of incoming STARTING SITES is essential; weakening x0
alone would leave order-one incoming energy and would not prove (6).

The exact volume N=L^D-(L-2t)^D satisfies
 2t L^d <= N <= 2Dt L^d.
The lower bound uses the two disjoint coordinate-1 slabs, and the upper
bound the J-slab cover. At p0=d/2, (6) therefore has order
 L^2 t^(2/d), with dimension-only upper/lower factors. Since its local
p0-moment stays below two, the t^(2/d) power in the second line of (1)
cannot be reduced uniformly over L,t and positive site weights.
For any proposed smaller power beta<2/d, take R->infinity,r=0, so
t=R->infinity: the ratio of the necessary factor to L^2 t^beta diverges.
For t=1 fixed and p0<d/2, N>=2L^d and (6)/L^2 grows as
 L^(d/p0-2). Thus the exponent p=d/2 is the lowest possible for a
uniform L^2 times normalized p-moment bound even on thickness-one shells.
This is sharpness of powers, not of the constants above.

These counterexamples can obey the actual mean comparability convention:
put a_tilde=a/epsilon, mu=mean_A a_tilde. Then
 1<=a_tilde<=1/epsilon<=2mu,
 mean_A (mu/a_tilde)^p0=(mean_A a)^p0 mean_A a^(-p0)<2.
Their indicator energy is exactly 2D-2. However, for p0=d/2 their
normalized D/2 moment grows unbounded with N. They are NOT a counterexample
to the original GLOBAL D/2-moment rectangular-partition question, and
the shell is not itself a rectangular partition. No finite-penalty
observation conclusion is claimed.

a classical sharpness mechanism. This is a stronger local input in the
same existing shell dossier, not a paper made from a small corollary.

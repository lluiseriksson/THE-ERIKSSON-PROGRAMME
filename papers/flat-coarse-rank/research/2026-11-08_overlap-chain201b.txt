QUESTION, HYPOTHESES AND MECHANISM
A possible multilevel route passes through covers of rings by overlapping
cubes. Does a fixed relative overlap with the smaller adjacent cube, together
with a fixed number of cubes, give a uniform L2 gluing constant? No. Three
cubes suffice to falsify that general rule, in both counting and Lebesgue
measure. We also derive an explicit valid bound using actual overlap volumes
on a rooted tree. Its scale loss has the right order on the counterexample.
This does not refute a special annular-domain estimate, a more restrictive
geometric cover, a complete approximation theorem, or the main rectangle target.

1. THE PRECISE GENERIC CLAIM TO TEST
In a measure space let S_i have finite positive measure v_i, and put
  e(f;S)=inf_(z in R) ||f-z||_(L2(S)).
The norm is unnormalized. For f square integrable on the finite union, its
best constant on S is m_S=v_S^(-1)integral_S f, and
  e(f;S)^2=integral_S|f-m_S|^2.
The tested assertion is the existence of a finite C depending only on the
number J of sets, a dimension d, and fixed rho>0, such that
  e(f;union_i S_i)<=C sum_i e(f;S_i),                  (1.1)
whenever neighboring sets satisfy
  measure(S_i intersect S_(i+1))>=rho min(v_i,v_(i+1)). (1.2)
No bound on ratios of set volumes or accumulated-union overlap is assumed.
The next examples have J=3 and actual adjacent overlap ratio1/2, so they
satisfy(1.2) even with the fixed strict margin rho=1/4. They also satisfy
that condition for every pair with positive intersection, not just neighbors.

2. EXACT FINITE COUNTING COUNTEREXAMPLE
Fix an integer d>=2 and an integer L>=2. On the integer lattice define
  S1={0,...,L-1}^d,
  S3={L,...,2L-1} x {0,...,L-1}^(d-1),
  S2={L-1,L} x {0,1}^(d-1).
All three sets are actual cubes. Their counting volumes are L^d,2^d,L^d.
S2 is contained in S1 union S3 and each of its two overlaps has volume
2^(d-1), exactly half its own volume. S1,S3 are disjoint. Thus all required
relative overlap tests hold with fixed rho=1/4, independently of L.
Let f=0 on S1 and f=1 on S3. Then
  e(f;S1)=e(f;S3)=0,
  e(f;S2)^2=2^d/4,
  e(f;S1 union S2 union S3)^2=2L^d/4=L^d/2.
The last two equalities use the minimizing mean1/2 on equally sized halves.
Their exact ratio is
  e(f;union)/sum_i e(f;S_i)=sqrt(2L^d/2^d),           (2.1)
which is unbounded as L grows. No numerical experiment is required. In
particular a fixed number of overlaps, even each occupying half the smaller
cube, does not yield a dimension-only gluing constant. This assertion is
about scalar L2 approximation by one constant, not an energy or physical gap.

3. CONTINUUM COUNTEREXAMPLE AND A SMOOTH VERSION
For 0<t<=1/4 take halfopen cubes in R^d,
  S1=[0,1)^d,
  S3=[1,2) x [0,1)^(d-1),
  S2=[1-t,1+t) x [0,2t)^(d-1).
Write v=(2t)^d. The union is S1 union S3, of Lebesgue measure2. The small
cube has volume v<=1, and both overlaps have measure v/2. The same step
function has exact errors
  e(f;S1)=e(f;S3)=0, e(f;S2)=sqrt(v)/2,
  e(f;union)=1/sqrt(2).
Thus the ratio is sqrt(2/v), tending to infinity as t decreases.
All cubes may be simultaneously translated and rescaled into a unit cube;
the relative overlaps and error ratio are unchanged.

Additional smoothness alone does not rescue this generic claim. Choose a
smooth eta:R->[0,1] with eta(u)=0 for u<=-1 and eta(u)=1 for u>=1. Such
an eta is obtained by integrating a nonnegative smooth bump supported in
(-1,1), and normalizing its integral. Put delta=t^(2d) and
  f_delta(x)=eta((x_1-1)/delta).
It can be multiplied by a fixed compactly supported smooth cutoff equal to1
on a neighborhood of the three cubes; hence it is admissible even when a
globally smooth compactly supported test is wanted. On S1 compare with0
and on S3 with1: the transition has thickness at most delta on each side,
so
  e(f_delta;S1)<=sqrt(delta), e(f_delta;S3)<=sqrt(delta).
On S2 compare with1/2, obtaining e(f_delta;S2)<=sqrt(v)/2.
On the union its L2 distance to the step function is <=sqrt(2delta).
Distance to the closed subspace of constants is1-Lipschitz in L2, so
  e(f_delta;union)>=1/sqrt(2)-sqrt(2delta).
For t<=1/4,d>=2, delta<=1/256, and this lower bound is >=1/(2sqrt(2)).
The sum of the three local errors is <=2sqrt(delta)+sqrt(v)/2, which tends
to zero. This proves failure of(1.1) even for smooth f. Derivatives may grow
with t; the false generic claim had no derivative term or bound to absorb it.

4. A CORRECTED ROOTED-OVERLAP BOUND, DERIVED
Let S_1,...,S_J be any finite positive-measure sets, with f in L2 of their
union. Choose a connected graph of actual positive-measure overlaps, and a
spanning tree T with root r. Let v_i=measure(S_i),
  theta_(ij)=measure(S_i intersect S_j)>0 for tree edges,
  Delta_T=max_i degree_T(i),
  R_i=sum_(edges e on path r to i) theta_e^(-1), R_r=0,
  Q_T=sum_i v_i R_i.
This Q_T is dimensionless and uses ACTUAL set/overlap volumes. For J=1 set
Delta_T=0,Q_T=0. Then the universally valid estimate is
  e(f;union_i S_i)^2 <=(1+2 Delta_T Q_T) sum_i e(f;S_i)^2.  (4.1)
No uniform bound on Q_T is asserted, and (4.1) is not presented as removing
the missing local geometric estimate for a particular cover.

Proof. Let m_i be the average of f on S_i and e_i=e(f;S_i). On a tree edge,
restriction to the overlap and the L2 triangle inequality give
  sqrt(theta_(ij))|m_i-m_j|<=e_i+e_j,
  theta_(ij)|m_i-m_j|^2<=2(e_i^2+e_j^2).
Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz along a tree path yields
  |m_i-m_r|^2<=R_i sum_(e on path) theta_e |D_e m|^2
             <=2 R_i sum_(e in T)(e_(head(e))^2+e_(tail(e))^2)
             <=2 R_i Delta_T sum_j e_j^2.
The first inequality is trivial at the root because both sides vanish.
For any f in L2 of the union, use the candidate constant m_r. Counting a
point once is bounded by counting it with the cover multiplicity. On each
set, orthogonality to its average gives the exact Pythagorean identity.
Consequently
  e(f;union)^2<=integral_union |f-m_r|^2
     <=sum_i integral_(S_i)|f-m_r|^2
     =sum_i[e_i^2+v_i|m_i-m_r|^2]
     <=(1+2 Delta_T Q_T)sum_i e_i^2.
This proves(4.1) for either counting or Lebesgue measure. The elementary
path/graph mechanism is classical; no new general gluing principle is claimed.

On the three-set chain with outer volumes V and small bridge volume v,
each overlap has volume v/2. Taking r=1 gives Delta_T=2,
  R_2=2/v, R_3=4/v, Q_T=2+4V/v.
Our bound is1+4Q_T=9+16V/v. The exact step example has squared error ratio
2V/v against the sum of local squared errors. Thus deterioration of order
V/v in the squared inequality, or sqrt(V/v) in the norm inequality, cannot
in general be eliminated merely because adjacent relative overlaps are fixed.
The constant9+16V/v is sufficient, not claimed optimal.

5. WHY THE NAIVE CHAIN INDUCTION FAILS
From a fixed relative overlap of S_N and S_(N+1), one cannot infer the same
relative overlap between union_(i<=N) S_i and S_(N+1). In our example,
measure(S2 intersect S3)=v/2 is half the smaller set S2, but
measure((S1 union S2) intersect S3)=v/2 while both accumulated-union volume
and S3 volume are of order V. The min-volume denominator changes when sets
are accumulated. The new relative overlap tends to zero as v/V does.
A proof that equates these two minima without an additional condition has
not proved a uniform constant. A geometric ordering, volume comparability,
a suitable conductance bound or a new direct domain estimate might repair a
specific application, but must be derived there. Do not replace it by a
structure field called a gluing certificate and call the hypothesis eliminated.

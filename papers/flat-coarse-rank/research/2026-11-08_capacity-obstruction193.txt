A. EXACT TRACE REDUCTION, WITH NO UNPROVED EXTENSION PREMISE
Let a finite connected undirected graph have positive conductances c_e.
Use E(v)=sum_e c_e(v_i-v_j)^2=v^T L v, each edge once. A positive-start
torus at n>=3 defines exactly such a graph by assigning its edge conductance
from the positive start; the energy matrix is symmetric even though the
coefficient convention names a start. Fix nonempty proper R and B=V\R.
Write L in R,B blocks, and L_int for the Laplacian of edges internal to R.
Let D_cut be the diagonal matrix of sums of conductances from R to B, so
L_RR=L_int+D_cut.

L_BB is positive definite: if z^T L_BB z=0, extend z by zero on R.
Its global energy vanishes; connectedness forces a global constant, hence
zero since R is nonempty. Thus for any trace u on R there is a unique
minimizer h=-L_BB^{-1}L_BR u on B. Completing the square gives
 E(u,z)=u^T Lambda u+(z-h)^T L_BB(z-h),
 Lambda=L_RR-L_RB L_BB^{-1}L_BR.
In particular the exact optimal extension energy is
 min_{v|_R=u} E(v)=E_int(u)+u^T Q_R u,
 Q_R=D_cut-L_RB L_BB^{-1}L_BR.
The correction is derived, not postulated. It is positive semidefinite:
E(u,z)-E_int(u) is the sum of all cut-edge and B-internal edge energies,
each nonnegative; minimizing in z proves u^T Q_R u>=0.
Also Q_R 1_R=0: the constant trace has a constant energy-zero extension,
or equivalently L_BR 1_R=-L_BB 1_B and L_RB 1_B=-D_cut 1_R.
Consequently Q_R is a genuine boundary energy form, though it depends on
the entire eliminated graph through L_BB^{-1}. Naming this matrix does not
give the missing quantitative moment bound and does not remove a hypothesis
from the main theorem. For a rectangle, the exact identity alone does not
preserve the desired locality of a coarse partition construction.

B. EXACT TWO-POINT CAPACITY AND AN ALL-SIZE NEAR-ONE FAMILY
Fix D>=3, n>=3, N=n^D, p=D/2, X=(Z/nZ)^D. Set
 a(0)=1, a(x)=H=n for x!=0, mu=H-(H-1)/N.
Use exactly the unit192 positive-start energy, counting norms and actual
rectangle R={0,e_1}. Its internal energy is (u(0)-u(e_1))^2, and its
mean-zero Neumann gap remains exactly2. Since H/2<=mu<H,
 1<=a<=2mu.
The normalized inverse moment satisfies
 M_actual=mu^p/N+(1-1/N)(mu/H)^p
          < n^{-p}+1.
The first term is <H^p/N=n^{-p}; the second is <1. Conversely convexity
of t -> t^{-p} gives N^{-1}sum a^{-p} >= mu^{-p}, hence M_actual>=1.
It follows that M_actual tends to1 as n tends to infinity. For each fixed
M0>1, all sufficiently large n satisfy M_actual<=M0, while H=n diverges.
This is not an assertion at M0=1: equality in the strict convexity inequality
would force all coefficients equal, which this profile is not.

For two retained vertices, Lambda is symmetric positive semidefinite and
annihilates constants. Thus it has form g[[1,-1],[-1,1]] for a scalar g>0,
and the minimum extension energy is g(u(0)-u(e_1))^2. This defines the
two-terminal effective conductance with the stated no-factor-of-two energy.
The weak direct edge contributes (u(0)-u(e_1))^2. The three-edge strong
bypass 0 -> -e_2 -> e_1-e_2 -> e_1, with positive starts nonzero, contributes
at least (H/3)(u(0)-u(e_1))^2 by Cauchy--Schwarz. It is disjoint from the
direct edge, so g>=1+H/3. For an upper bound choose v(0)=u(0), v(x)=u(e_1)
at all other sites. Only edges incident to0 contribute. They have D outgoing
weights1 and D incoming weightsH, all distinct for n>=3. Hence
 1+H/3 <= g <= D(H+1).
For this trace Q_R=(g-1)[[1,-1],[-1,1]], with boundary correction coefficient
at least H/3. Therefore neither exact optimal extension nor its boundary
correction can be bounded by C(D,M0) times internal Neumann energy, for ANY
fixed M0>1. The ratio diverges at least n/3 although the moment tends to1.
This removes the possible rescue of that particular auxiliary lemma by
assuming the moment is arbitrarily close to its homogeneous minimum.

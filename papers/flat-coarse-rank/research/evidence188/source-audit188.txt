The median halves have at most half support, and their q-energy sums are bounded by the original energy. Squaring and adding is justified since 2/q>=1.
The endpoint-repeating reflection onto an arbitrary rectangle retains its volume/aspect-ratio factor. With s=2L+1 and r=s^D/V, the variance multiplicity is at least 2^-D r and the edge multiplicity at most 3^D r. The residual r^(2/q-1)=s^2/V^(2/D), with s<=3L, yields exactly A_D=K_D^2 2^D 3^(2D/q+2). Singleton coordinate sides are allowed; the all-singleton case has zero variance. The candidate copies this constant faithfully. It does not need a separate unproved weighted theorem.

2. Balanced base tree, section 2, including odd sizes and mixed singleton sides.
The starting node has equal side lengths. If a parent has side lengths m or m-1, bisection of each nonsingleton coordinate preserves spread at most one. For even m=2a and m>=4, possible lengths are a-1,a; for odd m=2a+1 they are a,a+1. For m=2, pre-existing length-one sides remain one and length-two sides split into ones. These cases handle the exception that a length-one coordinate is not actually bisected. There are no empty children.
At least one parent coordinate has length m, so each child has a side of length at least floor(m/2). This proves the lower bound for the child maximum even when some other sides are shorter. The upper bound is ceil(m/2). For every integer m>=2, floor(m/2)>=m/3 and ceil(m/2)<=2m/3, including m=2 and the extremal odd case m=3. The tree is finite, and every m>=2 node has all base side lengths >=m/2. Strict splitting and acceptance at equality are consistent with the non-strict final energy inequality.

5. Terminal internal charging, section 4.
Writing w=m^-d, a split means m^d b>theta, hence w<theta^-1 b; the inequality direction is correct. Terminal internal nodes exist when the tree has any internal node, and they are pairwise disjoint: a terminal internal node has no internal child, so cannot have an internal descendant. Select one such descendant for each internal node. Nodes assigned to a fixed terminal J lie on its ancestor path, at distinct distances. From m_child<=2m_parent/3, w_parent<=rho w_child with rho=(2/3)^d<1. Each assigned sum is therefore <=w(J)/(1-rho). Summing over J and using disjointness bounds the sum by theta^-1/(1-rho) times the base inverse mass. Overlap of all internal nodes is not incorrectly treated as disjoint. This is the step where d>=1 is essential.

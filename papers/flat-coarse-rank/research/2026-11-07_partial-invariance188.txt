PARTIAL COORDINATE INVARIANCE REMOVES ALL BUT ONE LAMINATION DIRECTION
Research unit188, analytical candidate; not a separate paper or AIRR deposit.

Statement. Let D>=3,1<=k<D,d=D-k,p=D/2,n>=2,c>0. On the unit-spacing counting torus X=(Z/nZ)^D take ANY positive a(x)=alpha(x1,...,xk). Define Ea(u)=sum_{x,j}a(x)|u(x+ej)-u(x)|². There exists a disjoint axis-aligned rectangular partition P, independent ofu, such that
 c||u||² <= Ea(u)+c||CPu||²,
where CPu=(sqrt(|R|)mean_Ru)_R, and
 #P <= 2^d + F_{D,k}(c C_D)^p sum_X a^-p,
 C_D=4 A_D D^(2/D),
 F_{D,k}=2^D 3^d/[1-(2/3)^d].
Here A_D is the explicit finite unweighted rectangle Sobolev constant from the complete proof in revision187/sobolev.tex:
 q=2D/(D+2), beta=2(D-1)/D, K_D=2D beta sqrt(2D),
 A_D=K_D² 2^D 3^(2D/q+2).
The claim holds for every real/complex scalar field and coordinatewise for any fixed h-component field, hence also on every actual coexact subspace. Output dimension is h#P over the selected scalar field; double complex dimension for a real count. K=c is explicit; no prescribed abstract gap/partition hypothesis is carried.
Under mu=mean(a)>=1 and mean((mu/a)^p)<=M it follows that
 h#P <= h[2^d+F_{D,k}(c C_D)^p 2^p M] max(1,n^D/(1+mu)^p).
The upper bound a<=2mu and lower bound a>=1 are unnecessary for the unconditional sum bound. In particular D3,k2 covers arbitrary two-coordinate profiles, rather than the one-coordinate-only class of185. At least one exactly invariant coordinate remains a hypothesis. Constants are deliberately nonoptimal.

1. Local inequality, actual edges and provenance.
For a finite cut D-rectangle R with longest side L, volume V and kappa=L²/V^(2/D), the explicit finite unweighted estimate is
 Var_R u <= A_D kappa (sum_internal_edges |du|^q)^(2/q).
A complete finite proof, including projection/relative isoperimetry, median/coarea/power and endpoint-repeating reflection with every multiplicity, is pinned in parent revision187/sobolev.tex, equations rectangle-unweighted-derived and rectangle-weighted-derived. Its scalar-start consequence is
 Var_R u <= A_D D^(1/p) kappa (sum_R a^-p)^(1/p) Ea,R(u).                 (1)
Indeed Holder with exponents2/q and2/(2-q) gives q/(2-q)=p; each original starting vertex supplies at mostD internal edges. This uses original positive-start coefficients only, no reflected coefficient, enlarged-neighborhood energy, physical projection or an assumed local spectral gap. All periodic closing/cross-cell edges may be discarded as nonnegative, including n2 opposite starts. Singleton rectangles have zero variance. The unweighted ingredient is classical; its finite proof is not new in188.

2. Base tree and aspect ratios, all odd/even sizes.
The base domain is {0,...,n-1}^k. For a nodeQ let m(Q) be its maximum integer side length and b(Q)=sum_Q alpha^-p. Start with the full base cube. Set theta=(c C_D)^-p. Split a node exactly if m>=2 and m^d b(Q)>theta. Accept equality; never split a base singleton. A split bisects EVERY non-singleton coordinate into consecutive floor/ceil halves; coordinates already singleton stay unchanged. Children partition the actual base lattice, and there are at most2^k children.
Every node's side lengths differ by at most1. This follows by induction: bisecting lengths m or m-1 produces only floor((m-1)/2),ceil((m-1)/2),floor(m/2),ceil(m/2); the largest and smallest differ by at most1. Ifm2, splitting all length2 sides gives singleton children. In general every child has maximum m' satisfying
 m/3 <= floor(m/2) <= m' <= ceil(m/2) <= 2m/3.                           (2)
The left floor bound and right ceil bound hold for integersm>=2, includingm2. Thus the tree terminates. For m>=2 every base side is >=m/2; m1 means all base sides singleton.
For each leafQ, partition EACH of the d invariant coordinates into r=ceil(n/m) consecutive intervals with balanced lengths floor(n/r),ceil(n/r). Every such length is <=m and >=m/2. For r1 this is immediate; for r>=2, n>=(r-1)m+1, so floor(n/r)>=floor((m+1)/2)>=m/2. Products R=Q times these intervals are disjoint and coverX exactly. Ifm>=2 allD side lengths lie between m/2 andm, so kappa<=4. Ifm1 allfullcells are singletons.

3. Local energy bound and actual mean observations.
Because a is exactly independent of the lastd coordinates, for each fullcellR overQ
 sum_R a^-p = |Z| b(Q) <= m^d b(Q).                                    (3)
For a nonsingleton leaf, (1), kappa<=4 and the acceptance criterion give
 c Var_R u <= c C_D (m^d b(Q))^(1/p) Ea,R(u) <= Ea,R(u).
For a singleton leaf the variance vanishes without imposing an impossible mass acceptance condition. Sum over the disjoint rectangles and discard cross-cell/closing energies. Orthogonal mean decomposition ||u||²=sum_R Var_Ru+sum_R |R||mean_Ru|² gives the stated actual inequality withK=c, simultaneously for allu. All field coordinates sum with the SAME constant. The partition is constructed solely from the actual positive coefficients.

4. Internal-node charging without a logarithm.
Write w(Q)=m(Q)^-d and rho=(2/3)^d<1. Every split satisfies
 w(Q)<theta^-1 b(Q).                                                    (4)
LetT be internal nodes with no internal children. These nodes are pairwise disjoint in the base lattice. For each internal node choose one terminal internal descendant. Nodes assigned to a fixed terminalJ lie on its ancestor path at distinct depths. From(2), ancestor weights decay by at least rho per depth, so
 sum_internal w(Q) <= [1/(1-rho)] sum_{J inT}w(J)
                   <= theta^-1/[1-rho] sum_base alpha^-p.                (5)
The subtree assignment is used only to prove a bound, not to assume local gap or rank. Terminal nodes exist because the tree is finite. Disjointness ofT, not disjointness of ALL internal nodes, is what justifies the final mass charge.
Each split has at most2^k children and (2) implies each childw<=3^d w(parent). Exact telescoping of leaf weights gives
 sum_leaves w <= n^-d+2^k 3^d sum_internal w.                             (6)
A base leaf produces exactly ceil(n/m)^d <=(2n/m)^d full rectangles. Multiply(6) by(2n)^d, substitute(5), and use n^d sum_base alpha^-p=sum_X a^-p. This yields exactly the constants in the statement. If the root is accepted there are no internal nodes and the argument still gives #P=1<=2^d. Base singleton leaves are already included in telescoping; no uncharged exceptional mass is discarded.

5. Critical moment and scope.
The original moment condition gives sum_X a^-p <= M n^D/mu^p <= 2^p M n^D/(1+mu)^p whenmu>=1. Adding the root constant proves the target-order bound. This is an upper-order claim, not a sharp per-profile lower bound. For k=D, d0 and rho1 make(5) divergent; no assertion for fully variable coefficients follows. Invariance in one or more coordinates is actual mathematical input. The method derives local gaps and a partition from coefficients; it does not put the conclusion into a structure. This eliminates the restriction to dependence on just ONE coordinate in185, by proving the wider k<D class, but does not eliminate all coordinate-invariance assumptions.

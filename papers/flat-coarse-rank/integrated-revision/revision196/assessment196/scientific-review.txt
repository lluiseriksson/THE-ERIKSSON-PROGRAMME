SCIENTIFIC SUMMARY
The paper has three distinct useful layers. First, actual unit-spacing lattice Fourier and coexact packets prove necessary dimension budgets, including tangent statements for nonlinear observations and regular nonlinear constraint sectors. Second, finite inverse-form comparison and established Cwikel machinery give sufficient global spectral observations under the critical inverse moment D/2, with compact coexact weak-island tests showing that every lower positive inverse exponent fails as a universal replacement. Third, actual arithmetic means on rectangles are constructed for coefficient gaps, supplied cubes, laminates and, most substantially, arbitrary dependence on any k<D coordinates. Numerous examples carefully separate a successful observation from the cost or failure of a particular certificate. The final boundary examples correctly concern extension and separately eliminated energies, not the unrestricted partition count.

The central positive construction in Section 35 is a credible theorem with a complete analytic route. Its invariant-coordinate factor is doing essential work. The result is stronger than a supplied local-gap certificate: the local comparison is derived from a finite Sobolev inequality, and the partition is constructed from coefficient masses. The tree charge avoids treating all internal nodes as disjoint. The restrictions k<D and exact invariance are indispensable to the proof as written. I found no legitimate basis for removing them.

DETAILED ADVERSARIAL EXAMINATION

1. Sections 2-6, pages 3-7: flat normalization, real dimension and refinement.
I checked the unit-spacing convention, unordered plaquette count and the placement of complex scalars in two real internal coordinates. The real low-band dimension is genuinely 2(2R+1)^d; it is not a mistaken doubling of scalar real sine/cosine modes. Fourier orthogonality must apply to differences as well as to masses; the text supplies this step. Theorem 5.1 uses the actual joint map (C,V), so a nonsymmetric repair causes no problem once the common kernel vector is chosen. The strict threshold c>b_R is necessary and is retained. The explicit large-L argument and order of quantifiers in Theorem 6.1 are adequate. Rescaling a fixed-dimensional linear observation cannot defeat rank-nullity. The older block-map witness is a valid narrower calculation; the text does not claim its single-line repair condition proves full-space coercivity.

2. Sections 7-8, pages 7-10: nonlinear observations and exact sectors.
The shrinking-ray argument uses upper quadratic control of the penalty, not merely differentiability of its factors. The supplied norm-power examples correctly show why this distinction matters. For regular constraints, surjectivity produces an actual implicit-function curve, and the quadratic energy expansion controls departure from the low subspace. A bound only on the linear subspace for an arbitrary C1 energy would not suffice; the manuscript gives an explicit counterexample rather than silently making that inference. Proposition 8.2 fixes the lattice first and only then shrinks the curve; it does not require unjustified uniform implicit-function radii. Singular constraint maps remain outside the theorem. No physical gauge-domain identification follows from these abstract regular-sector conclusions.

3. Sections 9-11, pages 10-17: weighted traces and density.
The directional packet stores quadratures internally, so period-two and other real-frequency degeneracies do not invalidate its dimension. Trace positivity is used exactly where required. Passing to positive parts gives an upper energy envelope for signed weights; ordinary signed means do not substitute for it. The zero-mean signed-weight example explicitly cancels a local negative contribution with observed differences and is nonvacuous. The square-cycle cancellation is an instance of a classical signed-Laplacian mechanism and should receive no general-principle novelty credit. The full-volume packet is coexact, occupies only two field components and normalizes using a nonzero frequency coordinate. The envelope need only dominate on that packet. I checked the full integer-tail floor inequalities, the trace average and its small-frequency coefficient. Their density bounds are necessary lower bounds, not claimed optimal ranks or unrestricted nonlinear theorems.

4. Sections 12-14, pages 17-22: Schur compatibility and provenance.
The singular-Schur range condition is the right condition for boundedness below on each observation fiber. Kernel nonnegativity alone is not sufficient at a degenerate endpoint. The explicit two-coordinate example verifies the point. The programme consumer discussion is a source-level specialization, not an independently checked identification with every constrained RG fluctuation form. A physical n- or block-side squared rescaling changes the obstruction threshold and cannot be ignored. The manuscript explicitly preserves this limitation. The many historic compilation and source claims were not independently verified here. Their presence in the PDF is evidence disclosure, not a replacement for proof checking or grounds to increase the score.

5. Sections 16-18, pages 22-26: critical global spectral theorem.
I traced the finite counting normalization in Lemma 17.2. The density integral gives I_p=(p-1)^2/[p(p+1)], and the weak-frequency tail carries the factor V exactly where required. The pseudoinverse kernel is treated separately. The actual feasible-flow comparison L_a^{-1} <= sum T_i* T_i is a legitimate upper bound without coefficient commutation. The spectral-count inequality for a sum uses min-max and intersection of low-energy complements, not simultaneous diagonalization. The scalar constant mode and h real field copies must be added; the stated construction does so. The real eigenbasis does not inherit a spurious factor two from complex Fourier calculations. I checked the compact-curl weak-box mechanism in Theorem 18.1: field and gradient-start supports are both needed, separation controls cross terms, and the coefficient normalization preserves the exact arithmetic mean. The lower-exponent counterexample therefore addresses the original local differentiable-observation data, not only a scalar surrogate. The exponent claim is appropriately restricted to this family of universal scalar moment assumptions.

6. Sections 19-23, pages 26-39: spatial geometry and local estimates.
The translated full dyadic-tree lower bound does not concern arbitrary rectangular cuts. Its successful complementary-rectangle construction correctly prevents overgeneralization. The scalar weighted rectangle estimate retains aspect ratio. A thin ramp is a valid falsifier of a geometry-free local inverse-mass bound, but not a falsifier of every global partition. Fixed K=c requires a full residual variance comparison, not merely kernel coercivity; Sections 20.2 and 23.6 use the correct distinction. The threshold algorithm is deliberately weaker than the conjectured general result; its tau=sqrt(mu) choice is present in TeX despite extraction losing the radical.

The Section 22 finite proof is internally consistent: entropy gives the projection inequality; internal edge isoperimetry gives the layer-set estimate; the power and median decomposition yield the q-to-2 inequality; reflection is completed before weighted Holder, retaining all aspect-ratio multiplicities. Singleton sides cause no division by zero. Section 23's proper-intersection qualification matters. The corner bound does not imply one object per terminal rectangle. The subsequent slab cover and refinement handle multiple objects, and the half-open assignment plus noninteger cube facets gives actual integer coverage. I found no remaining use of the false one-object implication. These are classical mechanisms adapted to supplied cubes; the cubes and coefficient lower bounds are not derived from the critical moment.

7. Sections 24-28, pages 39-56: exact penalties, matrix coefficients and coexact transfer.
The four-cycle embedding uses an actual divergence-zero component profile and proves the kernel premise on all ambient fields. The coefficient 1138/35 is consistent with the displayed 2-by-2 residual inverse and completing-square identity. At the sharp target, nonzero coupling to a degenerate kernel direction gives a genuine failure for every finite quadratic penalty. Small rescaling transfers it to local upper-quadratic penalties.

For Theorem 28.1 I checked Frank's primary operator-valued Theorem 3.1 directly. With q=D, Fourier L1-to-Linfinity norm V^(-1/2), padded rectangular multiplier and weak-frequency count (3/4)^D V, the stated alpha_D has the correct cancellation of volume. This is a classical theorem applied correctly, not a new matrix Cwikel theorem. The capped-channel bound follows from a positive finite-rank replacement and min-max; rotated channels cannot be counted by a scalar graph-component formula. The resistance threshold and coupled inertia test retain off-diagonal interactions, correctly defeating the individual-to-joint shortcut. The scalar prescribed-mean exact optimum includes the constant-mode floor. Its pole and the restricted coexact leading coefficient are analytically plausible and correctly distinguish leading asymptotics from equality of full ambient and restricted optima. The coexact witness potentials explicitly establish membership; mere divergence zero would not alone establish coexactness on a torus. Finite diagnostic assertions for these older units were not replayed by me.

8. Section 29, pages 57-63: locality counterexamples and useful subclasses.
The isospectral checkerboard example is explicitly a nonlocal positive graph, so it cannot refute nearest-neighbour coefficient sufficiency. Its projection argument genuinely needs coordinate geometry. Its displayed K_min is obtained on the mean-zero space, an important scope that should be repeated beside the formula (see R2 below). The point-supported obstruction uses an actual coexact correction and a different coefficient scale from the earlier high-gap family; the manuscript correctly keeps these regimes apart. The fixed-gap rectangular construction isolates arbitrary weak vertices and controls all fields. The minimum-certificate obstruction defeats a specified certificate, not the successful one-mean observation on the same separated profiles. The distinction between path and retained-cycle gaps is explicit. None of these examples supplies a general lower bound for rectangular observation rank.

9. Sections 30-34, pages 64-70: laminate construction and Green-certificate costs.
The local laminate estimate uses the complete coefficient sequence, including the last starting vertex for transverse averaging. Arithmetic or representative-fibre substitution would be invalid. The tree charge has positive decay exponent d=D-1 and handles odd lengths and singletons. At the critical inverse mass it produces the stated upper order, without asserting optimality for every individual profile.

The Green-moment phase diagram is a certificate-cost result. Its subcritical lower bound uses the maximum eigenvalue and applies to all rectangle shapes. At the endpoint, frequency blocks yield a logarithmic lower bound; large-volume cells force enough minimum side length to make that bound applicable. The meshes furnish matching upper orders. The weak counting test can accept the root while the strong trace rejects it. This is a useful exact distinction, but weak/strong spectral ideals are established theory. The finite c=16 example keeps both oriented period-two edge occurrences when a full coordinate is retained. The representative-fibre coexact test has norm squared 4 and energy 28, below target 30; it is a counterexample to the sampled-fibre mechanism, not to the moment theorem.

10. Section 35, pages 71-73: partial coordinate invariance.
I checked this central result separately rather than accepting the embedded audit. In the balanced base tree, simultaneous floor/ceiling bisection preserves side lengths differing by at most one. The longest child side lies between m/3 and 2m/3, including the m=2 and m=3 cases. Balanced invariant intervals have lengths in [m/2,m], so the extruded cells have kappa<=4. Exact invariance gives sum_R a^(-p)=|Z|b(Q), which is the essential coefficient identity. Acceptance yields c Var_R<=E_a,R, and singleton base leaves extrude only to singleton full cells.

For counting, internal nodes are assigned to terminal internal descendants; only the latter are disjoint. Along an ancestor chain m^(-d) decays geometrically with rho=(2/3)^d. Leaf-weight telescoping then pays for the actual number ceil(n/m)^d of invariant-coordinate cells. Since n^d sum_base alpha^(-p)=sum_X a^(-p), the displayed critical count has the right normalization. The accepted root and oversized coefficients do not violate the additive 2^d bound. At k=D the argument really loses geometric summability. I found no blocking mathematical gap in this proof, but no formal all-size check was made and no approximate-invariance statement follows.

11. Section 36, pages 73-76: weaker detour separation and parity boundary.
The three candidate sources for a strong edge are pairwise distance one when D>=3, using distinct cyclic directions. This proves congestion at most one under distance-two weak-site exclusion. The D=2 list instead permits multiplicity two and justifies only the stated sufficient factor six. Period-three wraps do not break the source distinctions. Nearest-neighbour independence allows the parity construction. Its paired layers have strong-start energy zero and weak-start cyclic differences; the explicitly integrated plaquette potential establishes actual coexactness. The real dimension m-1 and the upper quotient 2D epsilon are consistent. Its inverse moment diverges for the contrast regime, so it does not defeat fixed-M rectangular sufficiency. No finite replay for this section was performed here or assumed from the paper.

12. Section 37, pages 76-80: boundary transfer.
The grounded block is invertible by connectedness and a nonempty retained set. Subtracting the fixed internal energy before minimization proves Q_R>=0 and Q_R 1=0. The two-site weak internal edge plus strong three-edge bypass gives an exact-extension cost at least 4+4H/3. H=n makes the normalized critical moment approach one while the extension/internal ratio diverges; M=1 itself is correctly excluded. H=n^2 is a different, uniformly coercive family, not a hidden substitute for the near-one example.

For Theorem 37.3, the transverse point-difference estimate is dimension-uniform in size only for d>=3. The two weak planes, one strong hole and the five-part comparison use disjoint energy sets within each individual comparison. Different retained pairs reuse those sets, which is precisely why separately minimized corrections need not sum with a uniform constant. The test field has equal positive and negative layers, exact energy 4(2n^d-1+H), and the lower correction sum grows by a factor proportional to H. The critical moment remains below 3 and tends to 3. The original target count is of order n^d, so this is not a partition-count counterexample. No D=3 aggregate claim or coexact conclusion is supported by this proof.

ADVERSE FINDINGS AND REQUESTED MINOR REVISIONS
R1. Novelty remains provisional and the closest adaptive-approximation comparison should be strengthened. The paper's own classical attribution is sound, but a fresh targeted search found Birman-Solomyak's 1967 partition-function construction (Section 2) especially relevant to the coefficient-mass tree arguments. The paper cites Binev-DeVore and weighted interpolation, but not this older direct partition-function background. Add a concise comparison identifying the finite extruded weighted construction, exact-invariance hypothesis, simultaneous control for all fields, and observation normalization that are and are not contained in older approximation results. This review does not establish that Theorem 35.1 is already known, nor that it is first. A priority claim stronger than the current qualified language is unsupported.

R2. Repeat the mean-zero scope beside K_min in Section 29.1, page 58. The derivation is on e-perp. For the full counting space, constants additionally require K>=c, so the full-space minimum is max{c, the displayed expression}. The context currently supplies e-perp, making this a clarification rather than a refutation of the local-geometry obstruction. At tau=1, for example, the expression is c-alpha, which visibly cannot control the constant mode at target c. Labeling the formula prevents a reader from exporting the wrong constant.

R3. Define 'normalized linear observation' explicitly where the penalty lower bound is used in Section 28.3, page 49. The step requires ||C||<=1, or a corresponding explicit normalization factor. Without it, replacing C by tC and K by K/t^2 preserves the repaired form and defeats an unqualified numerical lower bound on K. Normalized spectral coordinates and normalized disjoint means satisfy the intended contraction condition, so this does not affect the principal existence or rank theorems.

R4. The manuscript is substantially harder to audit than its mathematical content requires. Repeated preparation histories, old source-unit labels, pending-permission statements and near-duplicate scope disclaimers interrupt proof flow. In particular the 'frozen reviewed PDF' wording in Sections 22.6 and 23.6 should be clearly labeled as historical source text or rewritten for the changed integrated candidate. Add one compact theorem/dependency map linking Section 35 to 22 and distinguishing analytic, formal and diagnostic evidence; move repeated operational history into one provenance appendix. This is an editorial weakness, not proof of a mathematical gap.

R5. Reproducibility remains partial. The read diagnostic archives have matching hashes and selected finite outputs, but source identity, replay provenance and mathematical correctness are different claims. No complete dependency-closure rebuild or independent all-size replay was done here. Sections 36-37 remain analytic, with no new replay assumed. A stronger reproducibility score would require an outsider to follow one stable entrypoint to the exact source and evidence closure. The limitation is presently disclosed and is retained in this report; it is not represented as resolved by hashes.

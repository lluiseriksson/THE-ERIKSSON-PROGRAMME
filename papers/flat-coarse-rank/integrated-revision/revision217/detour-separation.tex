\section{A weaker detour exclusion and an actual coexact boundary}
\label{sec:detour190}
This section refines the sufficient geometry in (G4), without claiming a

\subsection{Distance two suffices in dimension at least three}
Let $X=(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^D$, $n\ge3$, and let $W\subset X$ be the weak
set. Distinct points in $W$ have periodic maximum-coordinate distance at
least two. Write $\mathcal E_{\rm wk}$ and $\mathcal E_{\rm str}$ for the
unweighted edge-square sums with starts in $W$ and $X\setminus W$.
Suppose $a\ge H>0$ outside $W$ and $a\ge0$ on $W$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:detour190}
For $D\ge3$ the actual energies satisfy
\[
 \mathcal E_{\rm wk}\le3\mathcal E_{\rm str},\qquad
 \mathcal E_0\le4\mathcal E_{\rm str},\qquad
 \mathcal E_a\ge(H/4)\mathcal E_0.
\]
For $D=2$ the same construction gives the sufficient, not asserted sharp,
bounds $\mathcal E_{\rm wk}\le6\mathcal E_{\rm str}$ and
$\mathcal E_a\ge(H/7)\mathcal E_0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\sigma$ be a single cycle through all $D$ directions. For a weak
positive edge $x\to x+e_i$, use
\[
 x\longrightarrow x-e_{\sigma(i)}
 \longrightarrow x+e_i-e_{\sigma(i)}\longrightarrow x+e_i.
\]
Both positive-start vertices $x-e_{\sigma(i)}$ and
$x+e_i-e_{\sigma(i)}$ are at periodic distance one from $x$, so are strong.
The first step is reversed: its coefficient belongs to
$x-e_{\sigma(i)}$, not $x$. Individual path edges are distinct for $n\ge3$.

For a fixed positive edge $(y,k)$ the complete list of possible weak
sources, with the corresponding direction $i$, is
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 y+e_k & \sigma^{-1}(k)\\
 y+e_{\sigma(k)} & k\\
 y-e_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}+e_k & \sigma^{-1}(k).
 \end{array}
\]
Each row specifies at most one actual use. In $D\ge3$, the directions
$k,\sigma(k),\sigma^{-1}(k)$ are distinct. Every pair of these candidate
sources differs by a nonzero vector with coordinates in $\{-1,0,1\}$,
including their periodic interpretation at $n=3$. Thus all pairwise
distances are one, and at most one candidate is weak. Every strong edge
is used at most once. Three-step Cauchy--Schwarz gives the factor three.
For $D=2$, the first candidate is incompatible with both other candidates;
the latter two may coexist, so use multiplicity is at most two. This gives
the factor six. Add $\mathcal E_{\rm str}$ and use
$\mathcal E_a\ge H\mathcal E_{\rm str}$.
\end{proof}
The relaxation from distance three in (G4) is genuine: on $n=4$,
$W=\{0,2e_1\}$ satisfies distance two but not distance three. At $n=3$
both conditions force at most one weak vertex; the stated bounds still
hold. The two-dimensional factor six is not proved optimal.

The torus gap $4\sin^2(\pi/n)\ge16/n^2$ shows that, for $D\ge3$ and
$4H/n^2\ge c$, one spatial-cell mean supplies the desired estimate with
$K=c$. For a field with $h$ real components this observation has $h$ real
outputs, not one scalar output. In $D=2$ the sufficient condition is
$16H/(7n^2)\ge c$. These comparisons apply componentwise and therefore on
actual coexact restrictions of the component-gradient form.

\subsection{Independence between neighbours is insufficient}
Take even $n=2m\ge4$ and $D\ge2$. Set
$s(x)=\sum_jx_j\pmod n\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ and $W=\{s\text{ even}\}$.
No two weak vertices are nearest neighbours, including across periodic
wraps. Put $a=\varepsilon>0$ on $W$ and $a=H>0$ elsewhere. For real
$z\in\mathbb R^m$ with $\sum_rz_r=0$, define
\[
 f_z(x)=z_{j(s(x))},\qquad
 j(s)=\lceil s/2\rceil\pmod m.
\]
Odd layer $2r-1$ is paired with even layer $2r$, including $n-1,0$.
Every layer has $n^{D-1}$ vertices. Strong positive starts stay in a pair,
whereas weak starts move from $z_r$ to $z_{r+1}$. Hence
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal E_{\rm str}(f_z)&=0,&
 \mathcal E_{\rm wk}(f_z)&=Dn^{D-1}\sum_r|z_{r+1}-z_r|^2,\\
 \|f_z\|^2&=2n^{D-1}\sum_r|z_r|^2,&
 \operatorname{mean}_X f_z&=0.
\end{align*}
The cyclic sums retain both positive starts when $m=2$. Since the cyclic
difference energy is at most $4\sum_r|z_r|^2$,
\[
 \frac{\mathcal E_a(f_z)}{\|f_z\|^2}
 =\frac{D\varepsilon}{2}
   \frac{\sum_r|z_{r+1}-z_r|^2}{\sum_r|z_r|^2}
 \le2D\varepsilon.                                      \tag{D190}
\]
For nonzero mean-zero $z$, the weak energy is positive. Thus a finite
comparison $\mathcal E_0\le C\mathcal E_{\rm str}$ cannot follow merely
from nearest-neighbour independence. Nor can
$\mathcal E_a\ge\kappa H\mathcal E_0$ hold uniformly in
$H/\varepsilon$ for fixed $\kappa>0$.

There is an actual coexact preimage. Regard $f_z$ as a function of $s$.
Its cyclic sum is zero. Put $\psi(0)=0$ and
$\psi(s)=\sum_{t=1}^sf_z(t)$ for $1\le s<n$. Then
$\psi(s)-\psi(s-1)=f_z(s)$, including the closing edge at $s=0$.
Set $B_{12}(x)=\psi(s(x))$, with all other antisymmetric components zero.
The same codifferential convention used earlier gives
\begin{align*}
 A_1(x)&=B_{12}(x)-B_{12}(x-e_2)=f_z(x),\\
 A_2(x)&=-B_{12}(x)+B_{12}(x-e_1)=-f_z(x).
\end{align*}
and $A_i=0$ for $i\ge3$. This exhibits coexactness; backward divergence
also cancels directly. Both the squared norm and the weighted
component-gradient energy are twice their scalar counterparts. The map
from real mean-zero $z$ to $A$ is injective, so its real dimension is
$m-1$. Its quotient obeys (D190). One fixed internal coordinate suffices.
This does not identify an arbitrary variable-coefficient physical Hodge
energy with the component-gradient form used here.

The real cosine $z_r=\cos(2\pi r/m)$ is nonzero and mean zero for every
$m\ge2$, and has quotient exactly
$2D\varepsilon\sin^2(\pi/m)=2D\varepsilon\sin^2(2\pi/n)$.
More generally every vector of the exhibited real coexact space has
quotient at most $2D\varepsilon$. Consequently if $c>2D\varepsilon$,
real-linear observation $C$ and real-linear repair $R$ with
\[
 \dim_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{range}C+\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb R}R<m-1
\]
cannot supply the target inequality on that space, for any penalty with
$\Phi(0)=0$. Rank-nullity for $(C,R)$ supplies a nonzero common-kernel
vector; both the penalty and repair term vanish there. A complex-linear
variant complexifies $z$ and counts complex dimensions consistently.
Real-linear observations on complex fields require real dimensions.

\subsection{Moment boundary and evidence context}
The two parity classes have equal size, so
\[
 \mu=(H+\varepsilon)/2,\qquad
 \operatorname{mean}\bigl((\mu/a)^{D/2}\bigr)
 =\tfrac12(\mu/\varepsilon)^{D/2}+\tfrac12(\mu/H)^{D/2}.
\]
For fixed $\varepsilon$ this is unbounded as $H\to\infty$. Even
$\varepsilon=1$, which meets $1\le a\le2\mu$, supplies no fixed moment
constant $M$. The example therefore refutes the specified comparison
route, not fixed-$M$ rectangular sufficiency or Section~\ref{sec:partial188}.

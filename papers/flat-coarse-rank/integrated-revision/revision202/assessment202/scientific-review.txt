SCIENTIFIC SUMMARY
The manuscript separates three questions that are easy to conflate: necessary observation dimension, existence of a global spectral observation, and existence of actual rectangle means with an acceptable penalty. The Fourier/tangent budget is a necessary condition, never advertised as sufficient. Singular Schur compatibility prevents weak kernel coercivity from being mistaken for finite-penalty sufficiency. The critical scalar and matrix inverse-moment arguments provide global coordinates rather than local cells. The later sections supply actual local constructions for specified subclasses and show why several attractive certificates cannot be promoted to universal partition theorems.

The strongest spatial statement in this reading is Theorem 35.1: coefficients depending arbitrarily on k<D coordinates admit actual rectangular normalized means, K=c, and an upper count controlled by the critical inverse mass. Its proof derives its local estimate from the finite Sobolev argument, extrudes balanced base leaves through invariant directions, and charges ancestors using d=D-k>0. This is a restricted all-size analytical theorem, not a theorem inferred from its 648 finite fixtures. The binary result in Proposition 38.2 is correctly presented as a corollary of Section 21's existing threshold construction, with a related earlier inclusion in Theorem 29.3. It is not an additional invention of a new partition mechanism.

The boundary-transfer results are genuinely distinct from those constructions. Exact extension can cost much more than internal energy even when the critical moment tends to one. In D>=4, separately minimized complements do not support a uniform sum bound; the discrepancy persists in a growing-dimensional trace space, so bounded-rank repairs of that particular comparison fail. These statements do not refute selectable rectangular observations. The same binary profiles admit the stated positive construction.

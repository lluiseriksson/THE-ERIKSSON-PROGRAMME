2. PRECISE ACCEPTED STATEMENT

t_i=kappa*i modn, E_i={t_i,t_i-1}x{0,n-1}. Let S_K be the maximum over
cyclic slices i of the minimum over bijections E_i to E_(i+K) of the maximum

4. QUADRATIC-INTEGER ESTIMATE

For any integer K>=1 choose a nearest integer ell to Kalpha and put r=ell/K.
Then |r-alpha|<=1/(2K). The polynomial x^2+x-1 has no rational root: for a
monic integer quadratic the only rational-root candidates are+/-1, neither
of which vanishes. Hence ell^2+ell*K-K^2 is a nonzero integer, and its
absolute value is at least1. Factoring with alpha'=-(1+sqrt(5))/2 yields
 |r-alpha|*|r-alpha'|>=1/K^2.
The triangle inequality gives |r-alpha'|<=sqrt(5)+1/(2K)<3; the final strict
bound holds even for K=1 since sqrt(5)<5/2. Division therefore gives
 |r-alpha|>1/(3K^2), or dist(Kalpha,Z)>1/(3K).
This is valid for all positive K, not just convergent denominators, and uses
no floating-point approximations or empirical constants.

5. ROUNDING AND BOTTLENECK CHECK

Distance to a closed subset of a metric space is1-Lipschitz. Applying this
to nZ and using e=kappa-alpha*n, |e|<2, gives
 d_K=dist(Kkappa,nZ)>=n*dist(Kalpha,Z)-K|e|>n/(3K)-2K.
The right side may be negative for large K; the inequality remains valid
there and the proof correctly uses a different estimate in that regime.

Fix any slice and any target corner whose x2 coordinate is t_i+Kkappa.
The two possible old x2 coordinates are t_i and t_i-1. Their periodic
distances to the target are respectively d_K and dist(Kkappa+1,nZ), the
latter at least d_K-1 by the circle metric triangle inequality. Every old
corner is therefore at transverse l1 distance at least d_K-1 from this
target. Every bijection must supply a preimage for that target. Consequently
the maximal distance of every bijection is at least d_K-1. Taking the finite
minimum over bijections and maximum over i preserves this lower bound:
 S_K>=d_K-1>n/(3K)-2K-1.
Arbitrary permutations of x3 endpoints cannot improve this necessary x2
distance; no preferred or translation matching was assumed. Translation

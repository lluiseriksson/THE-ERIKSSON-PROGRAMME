2. ACTUAL PROJECTION SYMBOL, INCLUDING HARMONIC REMOVAL

Use unitary characters e_k(x)=n^-3/2 exp(2pi i k.x/n).
Put g_j(k)=exp(2pi i k_j/n)-1 and |g|^2=sum_j|g_j|^2.
At k!=0 the actual curl-adjoint image is g(k)^perp in C^3. Indeed the
curl symbol is (g wedge v)_jl=g_j v_l-g_l v_j. For v with g* v=0,
 A_jl=(g_j v_l-g_l v_j)/|g|^2
is a true2-form preimage: its adjoint component is
 (|g|^2 v_j-g_j sum_l conjugate(g_l)v_l)/|g|^2=v_j.
Conversely g* times the adjoint is zero by antisymmetry. Thus the actual
orthogonal projection at nonzero k is P_g=I-gg*/|g|^2. At k=0 it is
P_0=0, because the actual adjoint symbol is zero; constants are excluded.
This derives the image instead of replacing it with a larger kernel.
Symbols at conjugate frequencies conjugate, so real fields are preserved.

3. AN ELEMENTARY TWO-VECTOR ESTIMATE, PAID DIRECTLY

For nonzero g,h in C^3, A,B in C^3 define the tuple
 R=(g_j A-h_j B)_(j=1,2,3), with sum-of-component squared norm.
Let e=g/|g|,f=h/|h| and s^2=1-|e*f|^2.
The rank-one projection difference has norm
 ||P_g-P_h||=s.
To verify, adjust f by a unit scalar so e*f=alpha>=0, then for s>0
write f=alpha e+s v with v unit and perpendicular to e. On span(e,v),
 (ee*-ff*)=[[s^2,-alpha s],[-alpha s,-s^2]],
whose square is s^2 I; it vanishes on the perpendicular complement.
If s=0 the projections coincide. This proves the identity over C as well.

Least squares in A, or completing its square component by component, gives
 ||R||^2=|g|^2||A-(g*h)/|g|^2 B||^2
          +[|h|^2-|g*h|^2/|g|^2]||B||^2
         >=|h|^2 s^2||B||^2.                            (LS)
Here g*h=sum_j conjugate(g_j)h_j, and its scalar multiplies each spatial
component of B. This is a derived residual bound, not a conditioning input.

The projected residual is exactly
 R'=(g_j P_g A-h_j P_h B)_j
    =(P_g R_j)_j+(h_j(P_g-P_h)B)_j.
The first tuple has norm<=||R||; the second has norm
 |h| ||(P_g-P_h)B||<=|h|s||B||<=||R|| by(LS).
Triangle inequality yields ||R'||<=2||R||, hence squared<=4||R||^2.
This argument also covers g,h parallel without division by s.
Replacing B by -B gives the identical bound for g_j A+h_j B.

If g=0,h!=0, with the ACTUAL projection P_0=0, both conclusions reduce
to ||h_j P_h B||_j<=||h_j B||_j, and similarly if h=0,g!=0.
The case both zero is trivial. Thus harmonic removal causes no exception.

4. EXACT ORIGINAL SOURCE PARITY PAIRS AND NORMALIZATION

Let eta=(n/2,n/2,n/2). Partition all frequencies into unordered pairs
{k,k+eta}, choosing one representative per pair. They are distinct for
even n>=2. In a fixed original channel write A=uhat(k),B=uhat(k+eta),
g=g(k),h=g(k+eta), so h_j=-exp(2pi i k_j/n)-1.
At a source x of sum parity epsilon=(-1)^(x1+x2+x3), the forward
gradient contribution of this pair is
 e_k(x)(g_j A+epsilon h_j B).
On the even source sublattice the two characters agree; on odd they
are negatives. Distinct unordered pairs are orthogonal on EACH parity
sublattice: sum_(epsilon fixed) e_k conjugate(e_l) is nonzero only
if k-l is0 or eta. Each parity has n^3/2 sites, and the unitary
character magnitude squared is1/n^3. Consequently, EXACTLY,
 sum_(x odd,j)|D_j u(x)|^2=(1/2)sum_pairs||(g_j A-h_j B)_j||^2,
 sum_(x even,j)|D_j u(x)|^2=(1/2)sum_pairs||(g_j A+h_j B)_j||^2.
No missing volume, pair-count, spatial or channel factor is suppressed.

Applying the derived residual estimate to each tuple after projection,

The reversed channel simply swaps the two parity identities, so the same

A. Actual finite definitions and cross cancellation

Let X=(Z/nZ)^D, with counting inner product, V a real Euclidean space, and A=(A_i(x)) an actual V-valued one-cochain. All translations are periodic. Define
  D_i^+ f(x)=f(x+e_i)-f(x),
  D_i^- f(x)=f(x)-f(x-e_i),
  curl_ij A(x)=D_i^+ A_j(x)-D_j^+ A_i(x), i<j,
  div^- A(x)=sum_i D_i^- A_i(x),
  H_0(A)=sum_{i<j,x} ||curl_ij A(x)||^2
          +sum_x ||div^- A(x)||^2.
There is exactly one curl term per unordered pair, no hidden factor1/2.
For w(i,x,l), define the actual componentwise weighted repair
  R_w(A)=sum_{i,x,l} w(i,x,l)||D_i^+ A_l(x)||^2.
In operator language this is <A,G*WG A>, with G the stacked actual forward gradient; its displayed sum is its definition on paper, not a supplied comparison certificate.

Periodic reindexing proves, for every actual f,h,
  sum_x <D_i^+ f(x),h(x)> = -sum_x <f(x),D_i^- h(x)>.
Forward/backward shifts commute coordinatewise, including i=j. Applying the displayed summation twice and real symmetry gives
  sum_x <D_i^+ f,D_j^+ g>
    =sum_x <D_j^- f,D_i^- g>.
Also sum_x ||D_i^- f||^2=sum_x ||D_i^+ f||^2 by translation.
For f=A_j,g=A_i the cross term of curl_ij therefore equals the corresponding divergence cross term. Expanding curl squares supplies all off-diagonal gradient norms and the negative cross terms. Expanding divergence supplies the diagonal norms and the opposite cross terms. They cancel pair by pair, proving on ALL cochains
  H_0(A)=sum_{i,x,l}||D_i^+ A_l(x)||^2.
No divergence constraint, lift ansatz or coefficient assumption enters this identity.

For the actual n=4 example, alpha=(1,6,4,1) and w(i,x,l)=alpha[x_1]-1. Consequently
  H(A):=H_0(A)+R_w(A)
       =sum_{i,x,l} alpha[x_1]||D_i^+ A_l(x)||^2.
This is obtained by adding repair to the UNWEIGHTED identity. It does not replace curl/divergence by coefficient-weighted curls or move a varying weight through summation by parts. No such weighted Hodge identity is assumed.

B. Exact scalar strict-margin threshold

Use exactly the previous four-cycle energy and complete two-block normalized observation:
  E(u)=(u_1-u_0)^2+6(u_2-u_1)^2
       +4(u_3-u_2)^2+(u_0-u_3)^2,

Every u in R^4 has the UNIQUE decomposition
  u=m(1,1,1,1)+(x,-x,y,-y)+(delta/2,delta/2,-delta/2,-delta/2),
where m=sum_t u_t/4, x=(u_0-u_1)/2, y=(u_2-u_3)/2,
and delta=(u_0+u_1-u_2-u_3)/2.
Thus
  |u|^2=4m^2+2x^2+2y^2+delta^2,
  O(u)=4m^2+delta^2,
  E(u)=11x^2+14xy+23y^2+7delta^2-10delta(x+y).
Define X=x-(25/2)delta, Y=y+(75/14)delta. Direct expansion gives the universal identity, for EVERY real K,m,x,y,delta:
  E(u)+K O(u)-(19/5)|u|^2
   =4(K-19/5)m^2
     +(17/5)(X+(35/17)Y)^2

The constant1138/35 is strictly greater than19/5. All coefficients on the right are nonnegative when K>=1138/35, so (S) proves sufficiency on ALL u.

This proves the threshold, rather than a loose sufficient bound or a finite sample test.

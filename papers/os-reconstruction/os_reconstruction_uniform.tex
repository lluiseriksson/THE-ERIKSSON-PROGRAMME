\begin{abstract}
For the spatial $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (Ising-slice) system in the Dobrushin
window $2\tanh|\beta| + 2\tanh|\gamma| \le \alpha < 1$, we machine-check
in Lean~4 an end-to-end chain: (i)~the Osterwalder--Schrader (site-form)
reconstruction of the transfer operator is unitarily conjugate --- by the
explicit $\sqrt{w}$ boundary dressing --- to the symmetrised Dobrushin
kernel; (ii)~the \emph{unnormalised Gibbs sums themselves} are exact
matrix elements of the reconstructed operator's powers,
$\;\mathrm{gibbsPathSum}\,w\,\beta\,N\,A\,B = \lambda^N
\ip{\hat T^N Q A}{Q B}$ and the partition function is the same shape at
the dressed constant --- identities, not bounds; and (iii)~there is
\emph{one} mass $m>0$ such that for \emph{every} spatial extent $L$ the
projected operator norm is at most $e^{-m}$ and every \emph{mixed}
connected correlator of the reconstructed operator obeys
$\bigl|\ip{u}{\hat T^{\,n} v} - \ip{\Omega}{u}\ip{\Omega}{v}\bigr| \le
\lVert u\rVert\,\lVert v\rVert\,(e^{-m})^n$, the zero-time case
included --- constants per-observable, rate alone uniform in the volume.
All quantifiers live in the Lean statements; the prose here copies the
kernel's cut.  To situate the claim in the 2026 landscape of
machine-checked constructive field theory: the abstract construction
(reflection-positive data $\to$ physical Hilbert space $\to$
self-adjoint contractive transfer operator) has been machine-checked,
sorry-free, in the Harvard formalization programme, \emph{with the
spectral gap carried as an undischarged hypothesis}
(\lean{GappedTransfer}); the Osterwalder--Schrader axioms have been
machine-checked for the free field; and measure-side Dobrushin
clustering has been machine-checked for lattice Yang--Mills.  What we
add, and what to our knowledge no machine-checked artefact yet
contains, is the combination \{concrete interacting model, gap
\emph{proved} rather than hypothesised, uniform in the spatial volume\}
on the reconstructed operator, \emph{identified exactly against the raw
Gibbs sums} --- i.e.\ a concrete discharge of exactly the hypothesis
shape the abstract frameworks carry.  We do not claim Wightman
reconstruction, a continuum limit, $SU(N)$, or any progress on the Clay
problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and landscape}\label{sec:intro}

The Osterwalder--Schrader route builds a physical theory out of
Euclidean data: a reflection-positive form gives a Hilbert space, and
composition with the time shift gives a self-adjoint transfer operator.
For a lattice slice system the construction is elementary --- the
content is never the construction but the \emph{spectrum}: a spectral
gap at every fixed spatial extent $L$ is classical (Perron--Frobenius;
in this programme it is the earlier
\lean{coupled\_gap\_all\_eigenvalues}), and it degenerates as
$L \to \infty$ unless something uniform survives.  What a
thermodynamic limit can actually consume is \emph{one} $m > 0$ valid
for every $L$ at once.

That uniform input exists in this repository: the Dobrushin corollary
\lean{dobrushin\_ising\_uniform\_gap} produces, inside the window
$2\tanh|\beta| + 2\tanh|\gamma| \le \alpha < 1$, a single $m>0$
bounding the projected transfer operator of the tilted kernel at every
extent.  This paper's contribution is the \emph{identification}, in
two stages.  Stage one (Section~\ref{sec:unitary}): the operator that
bound governs \emph{is} --- unitarily, by the explicit $\sqrt{w}$
boundary dressing, with no spectral-transport theory --- the transfer
operator forced on the reconstructed (site-form) theory.  Stage two
(Section~\ref{sec:measure}): the \emph{unnormalised Gibbs sums
themselves} are exact matrix elements of that operator's powers ---
$\lambda^N$ times an inner product, an identity valid at every real
$\beta$ and every positive weight, with the window entering only when
the gap is invoked.  The theorems are deliberately cheap: the same
witnesses as D-6, no new estimate.  Cheapness is the honesty of the
claim --- the uniformity is real because it is D-6's, and the
reconstruction adds commuting squares, machine-checked.

\section{The dressing unitary}\label{sec:unitary}
All statements of this and the following sections live in one module
(\lean{YangMills/OS/OSReconstructionUniform.lean}, sha256
\lean{d9919e256ae59ac9\ldots27bac149}, $20{,}428$ bytes, ten-pass
ladder on the plane, final \lean{ELAB\_EXIT 0}).  Write
$Qu := \sqrt{w}\,u$ for the boundary dressing of a real Euclidean
vector (\lean{qEmbed}; it coincides with the measure side's
\lean{dress}, complexified --- \lean{qEmbed\_eq\_dress} is
definitional); the site form weighs by $1/w$.

\begin{theorem}[A1, \lean{siteForm\_qEmbed}]
For $w > 0$ and real $u, v$:
$\mathrm{siteForm}\,w\,(Qu)\,(Qv) = \sum_\sigma u_\sigma v_\sigma$.
Per site the cancellation is elementary:
$\sqrt{w}u \cdot \sqrt{w}v \cdot (1/w) = uv$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[A2, \lean{transferOp\_qEmbed}]
$T(Qu) = Q\big(\mathrm{act}\,(\mathrm{symWeighted}\,w\,\beta)\,u\big)$
pointwise: the forced transfer operator on a dressed vector is the
dressing of the symmetrised kernel's action.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[A3, \lean{transferOp\_iterate\_qEmbed}]
$T^{[n]}(Qu) = Q\big((\mathrm{act}\,S)^{[n]} u\big)$ --- the shape the
in-tree measure bridge \lean{gibbsPathSum\_eq\_iterate} consumes.
\end{theorem}

Together with A1, the dressing is a unitary between the Euclidean
$\ell^2$ pairing and the site form at every finite extent (the site
form with $w>0$ is definite, so no quotient intervenes --- this is the
elementary, finite-volume case of the OS construction, and we say so
plainly).

$2\tanh|\beta|+2\tanh|\gamma| \le \alpha$.  There is $m>0$ such that

\end{theorem}
The proof is an \lean{obtain} on \lean{dobrushin\_ising\_uniform\_gap}

which is precisely the point.  The uniformity is real because it is

\section{Exact identification against the Gibbs sums}
\label{sec:measure}
The bridge from the raw measure to the operator is a chain of
\emph{identities} --- no bound appears until the gap is invoked, and
none of the statements below needs the window: they hold at every real
$\beta$, every $\lambda \ne 0$, every positive weight.

\begin{lemma}[B0b, \lean{act\_symWeighted\_eq\_smul\_act\_tilt}]
$\mathrm{act}\,S\,u = \lambda\cdot \mathrm{act}\,(\mathrm{tilt})\,u$
for $\lambda \ne 0$, and iterating
(\lean{act\_symWeighted\_iterate\_eq\_smul\_tilt}, via the scalar
transport lemmas \lean{act\_smul\_fun},
\lean{act\_iterate\_smul\_fun}):
$(\mathrm{act}\,S)^{[N]} u = \lambda^N \cdot
(\mathrm{act}\,\mathrm{tilt})^{[N]} u$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[B0a, \lean{act\_iterate\_eq\_opOf\_pow}]
$(\mathrm{act}\,M)^{[n]} u = (\mathrm{opOf}\,M)^n(\mathrm{toLp}\,u)$
pointwise, for any kernel matrix $M$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[B1, \lean{gibbsPathSum\_eq\_inner\_pow}]
For $w > 0$, $\beta \in \mathbb{R}$, $\lambda \ne 0$, $N \in
\mathbb{N}$ and real observables $A, B$:
\[
\mathrm{gibbsPathSum}\,w\,\beta\,N\,A\,B
  \;=\; \lambda^N\,
  \bigl\langle (\mathrm{opOf}\,(\mathrm{tiltKernel}\,w\,\beta\,
  \lambda))^N\, (Q A),\; Q B \bigr\rangle ,
\]
an exact identity: the unnormalised Gibbs two-point sum \emph{is}
$\lambda^N$ times a matrix element of the reconstructed operator's
powers, taken between dressed observables.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[B2, \lean{gibbsPartition\_eq\_inner\_pow}]
Under the same hypotheses, the partition function is the same shape at
the dressed constant:
$\;\mathrm{gibbsPartition}\,w\,\beta\,N = \lambda^N
\ip{\hat T^N (Q\mathbf 1)}{Q\mathbf 1}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Dividing B1 by B2 cancels $\lambda^N$, so the \emph{normalised}
two-point function equals
$\ip{\hat T^N QA}{QB}/\ip{\hat T^N Q\mathbf 1}{Q\mathbf 1}$ --- plain
arithmetic from the two identities, recorded here as prose.  The
quantitative six-term \emph{connected} bound for this quotient
(division-safe, with an explicit floor for the denominator) is the one
named open step of the campaign; see Section~\ref{sec:honesty}.  We
print the boundary between identity and estimate exactly where the
kernel puts it.
\end{remark}

\section{Mixed clustering on a generic Hilbert space}
\label{sec:mixed}
The clustering machinery is stated for an arbitrary real Hilbert
space $H$ (a \lean{section} with
\lean{[NormedAddCommGroup H] [InnerProductSpace \(\mathbb{R}\) H]
[CompleteSpace H]}), so the three lemmas below are reusable against
any transfer operator packaged as a \lean{VacuumTransfer} --- in
particular they are exactly the consumer shape of the
\lean{GappedTransfer} hypothesis of the abstract frameworks in
Section~\ref{sec:prior}.

\begin{lemma}[\lean{norm\_sub\_inner\_smul\_le}]
For $\lVert\Omega\rVert = 1$:
$\lVert v - \ip{\Omega}{v}\,\Omega\rVert \le \lVert v\rVert$
(Pythagoras; removing the vacuum component does not grow the norm).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[\lean{pow\_apply\_norm\_le}]
$\lVert S^n v\rVert \le r^n \lVert v\rVert$ whenever
$\lVert S\rVert \le r$, $r \ge 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{mixed\_connCorr\_bound}]
Let $T$ be a \lean{VacuumTransfer} with vacuum $\Omega$, and suppose
$\lVert \mathrm{projectedTransfer}\,T\,\Omega\rVert \le r$ with
$r \ge 0$.  Then for all $u, v \in H$ and all $n \in \mathbb{N}$
\emph{including $n = 0$}:
\[
\bigl|\ip{u}{T^n v} - \ip{\Omega}{u}\,\ip{\Omega}{v}\bigr|
  \;\le\; \lVert u\rVert\,\lVert v\rVert\, r^n .
\]
The $n = 0$ case is the Pythagoras lemma; $n \ge 1$ goes through the
in-tree \lean{projected\_pow\_succ}.
\end{theorem}

This strictly extends the lane's earlier diagonal clustering
(\lean{os\_reconstruction\_uniform\_clustering}, $u = v$, constant
$\lVert v\rVert^2$), which remains in the module: two \emph{different}
observables, and the time-zero correlator is bounded too --- the
zero-time case is where a naive geometric-series argument is silent,
and it is carried by the projection geometry instead.

\section{The endpoint}\label{sec:endpoint}
\begin{theorem}[\lean{os\_reconstruction\_measure\_uniform}]
Let $\beta, \gamma \in \mathbb{R}$ and $0 < \alpha < 1$ with
$2\tanh|\beta|+2\tanh|\gamma| \le \alpha$.  There is \emph{one}
$m > 0$ such that for \emph{every} spatial extent $L$ there are
$\lambda > 0$ and $\Omega > 0$ with:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] the fixed-point equation for
$\mathrm{tiltKernel}\,(\mathrm{sliceW}\,\gamma\,L)\,\beta\,\lambda$
at $\Omega$;
\item[(ii)] \emph{exact identification}: for every $N$ and all real
observables $A, B$,
$\;\mathrm{gibbsPathSum}\,(\mathrm{sliceW}\,\gamma\,L)\,\beta\,N\,A\,B
= \lambda^N \ip{\hat T^N (QA)}{QB}$ with
$\hat T = \mathrm{opOf}\,(\mathrm{tiltKernel}\ldots)$;
\item[(iii)] \emph{volume-uniform mixed decay}: for all
$u, v$ in the slice Hilbert space and all $n \ge 0$,
$\;\bigl|\ip{u}{\hat T^{\,n} v} -
\ip{\mathrm{vacOf}\,\Omega}{u}\,\ip{\mathrm{vacOf}\,\Omega}{v}\bigr|
\le \lVert u\rVert\,\lVert v\rVert\,(e^{-m})^n$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}
The witnesses of (i)--(iii) are \lean{dobrushin\_ising\_uniform\_gap}'s,
consumed verbatim; the packaging is \lean{vacuumTransfer\_opOf} +
\lean{tiltKernel\_symm}; (ii) is B1 at $w = \mathrm{sliceW}\,\gamma\,L$
(whose positivity is the in-tree \lean{sliceW\_pos}); (iii) is
Theorem~\lean{mixed\_connCorr\_bound} at $r = e^{-m}$.  One theorem,
three clauses, no new constant: the mass that clause (iii) exhibits is
the mass D-6 proved, now measured against the raw Gibbs sums through
the exact identity of clause (ii).

\section{What is not claimed}\label{sec:honesty}
No Wightman axioms, no OS$\to$Wightman analytic continuation, no
continuum limit, no $SU(N)$, no statement about the Clay problem.  The
window is the Dobrushin window; outside it the programme's own
kill-test (repository, Addendum 610) shows the standard-cone Birkhoff
route is projectively blind to the site weight, which is why no claim
beyond the window appears here.  The model is the $\mathbb{Z}_2$
Ising slice; ``reconstruction'' means the elementary finite-volume
site-form construction (definite form, no quotient), and the theorems
are about its \emph{spectrum and its exact relation to the Gibbs
sums}, not about the construction being difficult.  One step of the
campaign charter remains open and is named rather than blurred: the
division-safe \emph{normalised} six-term connected bound --- the
quantitative decay of
$\mathrm{gibbsPathSum}/Z - $ (product of one-point quotients), with an
explicit denominator floor.  Its ingredients are exactly B1, B2 and
Theorem~\lean{mixed\_connCorr\_bound}; nothing in
Sections~\ref{sec:unitary}--\ref{sec:endpoint} depends on it, and the
endpoint theorem stands on its own.

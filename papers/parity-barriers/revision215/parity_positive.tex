\begin{abstract}
On the Boolean cube, the strictly positive laws with density
$1+t\prod_{i=1}^n x_i$, $|t|<1$, have identical proper coordinate
marginals. We compute their complete single-site heat-bath spectrum
for arbitrary positive site rates. If $R$ is the sum of the rates and
$m$ their minimum, the gap is
$[R-\sqrt{(R-2m)^2+4t^2m(R-m)}]/2$.
Its inverse is the exact optimal variance tensorization constant.
This constant diverges as $|t|\to1$ at fixed dimension and rates,
although all proper marginal data stay fixed. Thus no finite-valued
function of those data bounds the constant over all strictly positive
laws. This quantitative obstruction requires neither a hard parity
constraint nor a comparison functional vanishing at the product law.
We also state the older exact-zero certificate argument with a literal
input map and distinguish genuine full-conditional coefficients from
marginal proxies. The construction is classical and the spectral
calculation is elementary; priority of the particular formula is not
established. No fixed-local, spatial-decay, entropy-constant or physical
mass-gap theorem is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{The law and the meaning of marginal input}
Let $n\ge2$, $\Omega=\{-1,1\}^n$, and let $u$ be its uniform product
law. Write $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$,
$\chi_S(x)=\prod_{i\in S}x_i$ and $S^c=[n]\setminus S$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
 \mu_t(x)=2^{-n}(1+t\chi_{[n]}(x)),\qquad -1<t<1.
\end{equation}
Normalization follows from $\E_u\chi_{[n]}=0$, and every configuration
has strictly positive probability. Integrating over any omitted
coordinate removes the perturbation, so every proper marginal equals
the corresponding uniform product marginal. This is the classical
parity mechanism behind limited-order independence; no novelty is
claimed for it~\cite{AGM}.

For a law $\mu$, define the literal input tuple
\[
 M_r(\mu)=(\mu_S:S\subseteq[n],\ |S|\le r),
\]
where coordinate labels are retained. Saying that a functional of
$(\mu,f)$ has this marginal input means an actual factorization
$B(\mu,f)=\Psi(M_r(\mu),f)$, with the same fixed observable $f$ when
laws are compared. No conditional quantity is admitted merely because
it conditions on few variables. In fact, from~\eqref{eq:law},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional}
 \mu_t(X_i=s\mid x_j,\ j\ne i)
 =\frac{1+t s\chi_{[n]\setminus\{i\}}(x)}2.
\end{equation}
These conditionals vary with $t$ while $M_{n-1}(\mu_t)=M_{n-1}(u)$.

\section{Complete spectrum with arbitrary site rates}
Fix rates $\rho_i>0$, independently of $t$, and put
$R=\sum_i\rho_i$, $m=\min_i\rho_i$.
Let $P_i f=\E_{\mu_t}[f\mid x_j,\ j\ne i]$ and use the positive
operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generator}
 H=\sum_i\rho_i(I-P_i).
\end{equation}
Thus the Markov generator is $-H$. Each site is refreshed at rate
$\rho_i$; we do not divide by $R$. The usual full-conditional
heat-bath and variance tensorization conventions are recorded, for
unit rates, in~\cite[eqs.~(1.3), (1.5), (1.6)]{CMT}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:spectrum}
The constant function has eigenvalue zero and
$\chi_{[n]}-t$ has eigenvalue $R$. For each unordered pair
$\{S,S^c\}$ with $S$ nonempty and proper, put
$a=\sum_{i\in S}\rho_i$ and $b=R-a$. The invariant space
$\operatorname{span}(\chi_S,\chi_{S^c})$ has eigenvalues
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roots}
 \lambda_\pm(a)=\frac{R\pm\sqrt{(a-b)^2+4t^2ab}}2.
\end{equation}
These spaces and the two distinguished modes exhaust the spectrum,
with repetitions counted by blocks. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 \gap_\rho(t)=\frac{R-\sqrt{(R-2m)^2+4t^2m(R-m)}}2>0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:conditional} gives $P_i\chi_S=\chi_S$ when
$i\notin S$ and $P_i\chi_S=t\chi_{S^c}$ when $i\in S$.
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:action}
 H\chi_S=a\chi_S-ta\chi_{S^c}.
\end{equation}
For the empty set and full set this proves the distinguished modes.
For a proper nonempty pair, the coefficient matrix in the displayed
ordered basis is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
 B(a)=\begin{pmatrix}a&-tb\\-ta&b\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Its trace is $R$ and determinant is $(1-t^2)ab$, giving
\eqref{eq:roots}; both roots are positive. The off-diagonal rates
must not be interchanged when $a\ne b$.

For completeness and orthogonality, character multiplication gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
 \E_{\mu_t}[\chi_S\chi_T]
 =\one_{S=T}+t\one_{S=T^c}.
\end{equation}
Different complement pairs are orthogonal in $L^2(\mu_t)$.
Each pair has positive definite Gram matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&t\\t&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The $2^n$ characters form a basis, consisting of the empty/full pair
and $2^{n-1}-1$ proper pairs. Each $P_i$ is an orthogonal projection,
so $H$ is self-adjoint; these blocks account for all eigenvalues.

For every proper nonempty $S$, both $a$ and $R-a$ are at least $m$.
Hence $a(R-a)\ge m(R-m)$, with equality for a singleton of minimum
rate. The lower root
$[R-\sqrt{R^2-4(1-t^2)a(R-a)}]/2$ increases with $a(R-a)$.
It is minimized at that singleton, is at most $R/2$, and is no
larger than any upper root or the separate eigenvalue $R$.
This proves~\eqref{eq:gap} and its strict positivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:optimal}
The optimal constant in
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance}
 \Var_{\mu_t}(f)\le C_\rho(t)
 \sum_i\rho_i\E_{\mu_t}[\Var_{\mu_t}(f\mid x_j,\ j\ne i)]
\end{equation}
for all real $f$ is $C_\rho(t)=1/\gap_\rho(t)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Orthogonal projection gives the exact Dirichlet form identity
\[
 \langle f,Hf\rangle_{\mu_t}=
 \sum_i\rho_i\E_{\mu_t}[\Var_{\mu_t}(f\mid x_j,\ j\ne i)].
\]
The finite-dimensional spectral decomposition on the mean-zero space
therefore proves the inequality and its sharpness. More explicitly,
for $t\ne0$, a minimum-rate singleton $S$ and
$\lambda=\gap_\rho(t)$, an extremizer is
\[
 f=\chi_S+\frac{m-\lambda}{t(R-m)}\chi_{S^c}.
\]
Substitution in~\eqref{eq:block} proves the eigenvector equation.
Its mean is zero and variance is positive by~\eqref{eq:gram}.
At $t=0$, $\chi_S$ alone is an extremizer.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Limits and normalization]\label{rem:limits}
Rationalizing~\eqref{eq:gap} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stable}
 \gap_\rho(t)=
 \frac{2(1-t^2)m(R-m)}{R+\sqrt{(R-2m)^2+4t^2m(R-m)}}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $(1-t^2)C_\rho(t)\to R/[m(R-m)]$ as $|t|\to1$,
at fixed dimension and rates. For unit rates the gap is
$[n-\sqrt{(n-2)^2+4(n-1)t^2}]/2$; it equals $1-|t|$ at $n=2$
and equals $1$ at $t=0$. At fixed $|t|<1$ it tends to $1-t^2$
as $n\to\infty$. The random-scan transition operator
$P=R^{-1}\sum_i\rho_iP_i$ has gap $\gap_\rho(t)/R$.
None of these limit statements is inferred from finite diagnostics.
\end{remark}

\section{A quantitative obstruction without exact-zero certification}
For an arbitrary strictly positive law $\mu$ on the cube, write
$C_\rho(\mu)$ for the optimal constant in~\eqref{eq:variance} with
its actual conditionals.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:information}
Fix $n\ge2$ and the positive site rates. There is no finite-valued
function $F$ of all proper coordinate marginals such that
\[
 C_\rho(\mu)\le F(M_{n-1}(\mu))
\]
for every strictly positive law $\mu$ on the cube. Equivalently, no
strictly positive gap lower bound depending only on this tuple can
hold on that whole class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All $\mu_t$ in~\eqref{eq:law} have the same tuple $M_{n-1}$.
A finite $F$ at this common input is a fixed number, while
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum} and~\eqref{eq:stable} show
$C_\rho(\mu_t)\to\infty$ as $|t|\to1$. Every member of this sequence
is strictly positive. The positive-gap formulation is the reciprocal
of the same contradiction.
\end{proof}

Taking $n=r+1$ proves the obstruction for each fixed marginal order
$r\ge1$. Neither a product-law right-hand side equal to zero nor
a law with zero-probability configurations is used in this theorem.
This does not exclude certificates on a class with a specified
uniform probability lower bound, bounded interaction parameters or
other substantive structural information. Here the ratio of largest
to smallest configuration probability is $(1+|t|)/(1-|t|)$, which
is unbounded along the obstructing sequence. Also
$\mu_t$ is proportional to $\exp(\operatorname{atanh}(t)\chi_{[n]})$:
the interaction is global, not a fixed local pair potential.

\section{The restricted exact-zero lemma and its scope}
The earlier elementary certificate argument remains useful if its
input and zero condition are stated literally.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:zero}
Let $B(\mu,f)=\Psi(M_r(\mu),f)$ and fix a single observable $f$.
If laws $\mu,\nu$ have identical $M_r$, if $B(\nu,f)=0$, and if
$D(\mu,f)>0$, then $D(\mu,f)\le cB(\mu,f)$ fails for every finite
$c$. A claimed measure-class application must establish all these
conditions on actual formulas.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Factorization gives $B(\mu,f)=B(\nu,f)=0$, contradicting the
asserted positive left-hand side.
\end{proof}
For example, at unit rates, take $f=X_n$ and
$D(\mu,f)=\Var_\mu(f)-\sum_i\E_\mu\Var_\mu(f\mid\mathrm{rest})$.
Under $u$ this defect is zero. Under $\mu_t$, $\Var(f)=1$,
only the $i=n$ conditional term is nonzero, and that term is
$1-t^2$ by~\eqref{eq:conditional}. Thus $D(\mu_t,f)=t^2$.
The exact-zero lemma still assumes its displayed certification
condition; Theorem~\ref{thm:information} is a different quantitative
statement that does not assume it.

True full-conditional coefficients do not have the marginal-input
factorization above. For the usual total-variation influence, flipping
$x_j$ changes the conditional Bernoulli bias at site $i\ne j$,
giving $c_{ij}=|t|$ and row sum $(n-1)|t|$. These values vary while
all proper marginals stay fixed. No unspecified Dobrushin-to-entropy
constant is invoked. The entropy theorem in~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{CMT}
has its own ergodicity and conditional-coefficient hypotheses;
it is not contradicted by the present construction.

For $n\ge3$, $\Cov_{\mu_t}(X_i,X_j)=0$ when $i\ne j$.
Ordinary pair-coordinate covariance therefore cannot separate these
laws. The specified block observables $X_n$ and $\prod_{i<n}X_i$
have covariance $t$, but no spatial-decay conclusion follows without
a geometry and allowed support dependence of constants.
Classical Efron--Stein bounds under independence are not refuted.
The previous claimed application to four audited inequalities is
withdrawn here: no four-formula bridge establishing factorization,
certification and separation has been supplied.

\section{Evidence, comparison and limits}
The complete proof is analytical. A finite exact rational diagnostic
constructs the actual conditional resampling matrix, rather than
assuming its Fourier block. It checks its action on all characters,
detailed balance, Gram self-adjointness, block dimension counts,
trace, determinant and the minimum subset product. The cases cover
$n=2,\ldots,6$, $t=0,3/5,-9/10$, and both unit and unequal rational
site rates: thirty cases and $32736$ state-character identities in
each mode. On an owned Colab Linux CPU runtime, normal and optimized
Python each exited zero and produced byte-identical complete reports.
These finite cases check implementation consistency; they do not
replace the all-$n$ proof. No new Lean/Lake proof or formal replay
of the spectral theorem is claimed.

Immutable proof, diagnostic, reports and source bindings are available
at~\cite{Artifacts}; the scientific diagnostic source is
\texttt{3b5160dc7b7cbaea8a797fa36f0babf0fe9b8b91}.
The historical Lean character-sum artifact belongs to the older note;
it is not a formalization of the new spectrum or optimal constant.

The parity family and limited-marginal independence are classical.
The calculation is an explicit specialization for a global interaction.
A targeted primary-source search did not establish priority; absence
of a located match is not novelty evidence. No entropy constant,
fixed-local Gibbs, spatial-decay, gauge-field, continuum or physical
mass-gap theorem is claimed.

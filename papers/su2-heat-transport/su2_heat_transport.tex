\begin{abstract}
We give a machine-checked spectral-coordinate theorem for the concrete
$\SU$ heat operator on normalized Haar probability.  The coefficient object
is the algebraic direct sum $\mathcal A=\mathbb N\to_0\mathbb R$, so one
statement covers every finite real character expansion without a fixed mode
cutoff.  Its lift $\Phi:\mathcal A\to\{f:\SU\to\mathbb C\}$ is injective;
Haar pairing recovers every coefficient and gives the exact finite-support
Euclidean pairing.  For $t>0$, actual convolution by the infinite heat kernel
intertwines $\Phi$ with the diagonal Casimir step
\[
 (D_ta)_n=\exp\!\left(-\frac{t\,n(n+2)}4\right)a_n,
 \qquad H_t\Phi=\Phi D_t.
\]
This upgrades the preceding two-mode bridge to all algebraic character modes.
The previously formalized two-state Dobrushin transfer remains an exact
quotient of the $\{0,1\}$ sector: its states contain genuine bundled elements
of $\SU$, its finite lift is bijective, and its actual operator intertwines
with $\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{-3t/4})$.  The infinite-kernel identity follows
from finite character orthogonality and dominated convergence, not from an
eigenvalue hypothesis.  The result is an exact invariant algebraic sector
plus a finite conjugacy on its first two modes, not a Peter--Weyl completeness
theorem for completed $L^2(\SU)$ and not a claim that $\SU$ is finite.  Every
load-bearing identity is checked in Lean 4 against pinned revisions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and contribution}

For a compact gauge group, heat-kernel convolution is the natural transfer
mechanism behind the exact solution of two-dimensional Yang--Mills theory.
At the formal level, however, three distinctions matter.

First, an abstract transfer theorem is not yet an inhabited gauge-theoretic
endpoint.  Second, equality of one scalar decay rate is not an operator map.
Third, a numerical
certificate for a matrix identity is not a Lean witness inhabiting $\SU$.
The purpose of the present construction is to close precisely this interface
by a commuting diagram.

The contribution is the following chain, checked without a project-local
axiom and without assuming any of its conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item finite heat-kernel partial sums act diagonally on every character already
  present in the sum;
\item dominated convergence upgrades this to the actual infinite heat kernel;
\item the Casimir ordering $3\le n(n+2)$ for $n\ge1$ identifies $\chi_1$ as the
  sharp slowest non-vacuum mode;
\item evaluation at the identity proves $\chi_1\ne0$ exactly;
\item all finite-support real character expansions are embedded through one
  exact Haar-isometric lift, and coefficient extraction proves injectivity;
\item the actual infinite-kernel heat operator intertwines this lift with the
  diagonal Casimir step on $\mathbb N\to_0\mathbb R$;
\item the first two coefficient modes map bijectively to the full finite transfer
  space, with the fundamental coefficient mapping to the sign vector; and
\item both operators commute with the same diagonal step, yielding an actual
  restriction and conjugacy theorem in Lean, while the earlier nonzero projected
  gap endpoint remains available as a corollary.
\end{enumerate}

The novelty is deliberately narrower than the exact area-law results already
available in the satellite.  We do not rename those results as new.  Instead,
we add the missing spectral map needed by the parent transport endpoint.

\section{The concrete heat operator}

Let $\Haar$ be normalized Haar probability on $\SU$.  For functions $f,h$ the
satellite convolution convention is
\[
  (f*h)(g)=\int_{\SU} f(x)h(x^{-1}g)\,d\Haar(x).
\]
Write $\chi_n$ for the real-valued Chebyshev character of highest-weight label
$n$, regarded as a complex function, and set
\[
  a_n(t)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{t\,n(n+2)}4\right).
\]
The class heat kernel is the convergent series
\[
  K_t(g)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)a_n(t)\chi_n(g).
\]
The factor $n+1$ is the representation dimension, while $n(n+2)/4$ is the
$\SU$ quadratic Casimir in the satellite's normalization.

The input orthogonality statement is already stronger than a scalar inner
product identity.  It gives the convolution formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:charconv}
  (\chi_n*\chi_m)(g)=
  \begin{cases}
    \chi_n(g)/(n+1),&n=m,\\
    0,&n\ne m.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This normalization cancels the dimension in $K_t$ exactly.

\subsection{Finite partial sums}

Let $K_t^{<N}=\sum_{n<N}(n+1)a_n(t)\chi_n$.  The first new Lean theorem is the
finite identity below.

\begin{theorem}[finite character eigenidentity]\label{thm:finite}
For $m<N$, $t\in\mathbb R$, and $g\in\SU$,
\[
  (K_t^{<N}*\chi_m)(g)=a_m(t)\chi_m(g).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finite integrability follows from continuity on the compact group.  Linearity
of the Haar integral turns convolution with the partial sum into a finite sum
of integrals.  Equation~\eqref{eq:charconv} kills every term except $n=m$;
the remaining factor $(m+1)$ cancels its reciprocal.  The Lean proof expands
the integrand before invoking the satellite theorem, so no desired
eigenidentity is passed as a premise.
\end{proof}

The formal statement is:
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem heatKernelPartial_character_eigen_of_lt
    (N : Nat) (t : Real) (m : Nat) (hm : m < N) (g : SU2) :
  su2Convolution (su2HeatKernelPartial N t)
      (su2CharacterChebyshev m) g =
    su2ClassHeatWeight t m * su2CharacterChebyshev m g
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Passage to the infinite kernel}

The infinite theorem requires more than rewriting a formal series.  For fixed
$m$ and $g$, define
\[
  F_N(x)=K_t^{<N}(x)\chi_m(x^{-1}g),\qquad
  F(x)=K_t(x)\chi_m(x^{-1}g).
\]
The satellite supplies pointwise convergence $K_t^{<N}(x)\to K_t(x)$ for
$t>0$ and a summable heat-kernel majorant.  It also supplies
$|\chi_m(y)|\le m+1$.  Thus
\[
  |F_N(x)|\le
  \left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}M_t(n)\right)(m+1),
\]
a constant integrable against the probability measure $\Haar$.  Dominated
convergence passes the limit through the Haar integral.  For all $N\ge m+1$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite} makes that integral the same constant; uniqueness of
limits completes the proof.

\begin{theorem}[infinite heat-mode identity]\label{thm:infinite}
If $t>0$, then for every $m\in\mathbb N$ and $g\in\SU$,
\[
  (K_t*\chi_m)(g)=
  \exp\!\left(-\frac{t\,m(m+2)}4\right)\chi_m(g).
\]
\end{theorem}

The crucial point is quantification over the actual bundled compact group and
the actual infinite kernel.  No finite carrier appears in
Theorem~\ref{thm:infinite}.

\section{Sharp fundamental mode}

Define $r_n(t)=\exp(-t n(n+2)/4)$.  The vacuum mode is $r_0(t)=1$.  For
$n\ge1$ the elementary inequality
\[
  3\le n(n+2)
\]
and monotonicity of the real exponential give
\[
  r_n(t)\le r_1(t)=e^{-3t/4}\qquad(t\ge0).
\]
For $t>0$, $0<r_1(t)<1$.  Consequently the fundamental representation is the
sharp slowest mode among all non-vacuum character labels.  ``Sharp'' here
means that equality is attained at $n=1$, not that a Peter--Weyl completeness
theorem for the completed class-function Hilbert space has been formalized.

Non-vacuity is exact.  The satellite proves $\chi_n(1)=n+1$; hence
$\chi_1(1)=2$, and function extensionality shows $\chi_1$ cannot be the zero
function.  The specialization of Theorem~\ref{thm:infinite} is therefore a
genuine eigenmode:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fundamental}
  (K_t*\chi_1)(g)=e^{-3t/4}\chi_1(g),\qquad \chi_1\ne0.
\end{equation}

\section{The exact algebraic all-mode intertwining}\label{sec:intertwining}

Let $\mathcal A=\mathbb N\to_0\mathbb R$ be the algebraic direct sum: an
element $a$ is a real sequence with finite support, but no global cutoff is
fixed.  Define
\begin{align*}
  (\Phi a)(g)&=\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}(a)}a_n\chi_n(g),\\
  (D_ta)_n&=r_n(t)a_n,
  &r_n(t)&=\exp\!\left(-\frac{t n(n+2)}4\right).
\end{align*}
Since $D_t$ cannot enlarge support, it is an endomorphism of $\mathcal A$.
The point of using a single finitely supported object, instead of a theorem
for each chosen $N$, is uniformity: every finite character sector and every
finite linear combination is an instance of the same commuting diagram.

\begin{theorem}[exact algebraic all-mode intertwining]\label{thm:allmodes}
For every $t>0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item coefficient extraction is exact,
\[
 \int_{\SU}(\Phi a)(g)\chi_m(g)\,d\Haar(g)=a_m
 \qquad(m\in\mathbb N);
\]
\item $\Phi$ is injective and preserves the finite-support Haar pairing,
\[
 \int_{\SU}(\Phi a)(g)(\Phi b)(g)\,d\Haar(g)
   =\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}(b)}a_nb_n;
\]
\item actual normalized-Haar heat convolution obeys
\[
 H_t\Phi=\Phi D_t.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

No linear-independence or eigenvalue conclusion is passed as a hypothesis.
Coefficient extraction expands a finite support sum, moves that sum through
the Haar integral using proved integrability, and invokes the concrete
orthogonality theorem.  Injectivity follows by extracting every coefficient.
For the commuting identity, the proof again moves only a finite sum through
the integral, then uses Theorem~\ref{thm:infinite} term by term.  Thus the
actual infinite heat kernel participates in the proof of every coordinate.

\subsection{The exact two-mode finite quotient}

Restrict $\mathcal A$ to coefficients supported on $\{0,1\}$ and identify
them with $E=\mathbb R^2$.  The continuous lift becomes
$a_0\chi_0+a_1\chi_1$, and $D_t$ becomes
$\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{-3t/4})$.  The finite lift is
\[
  (\Phi_2a)(x)=a_0+a_1\sigma(x),
  \qquad \sigma(0)=1,\quad\sigma(1)=-1.
\]
The preceding formal module proves that $\Phi_2$ is bijective onto the full
two-state Euclidean transfer space and that the actual finite operator obeys
$W_t\Phi_2=\Phi_2D_t$.  Therefore the induced map from the $\{0,1\}$
continuous sector to the finite space is a genuine operator conjugacy.  This
is the minimum restriction that connects Theorem~\ref{thm:allmodes} to the
published parent transport endpoint.

Non-vacuity has two independent exact checks.  The all-mode theorem recovers
every coefficient by Haar integration.  On the finite quotient, evaluation
at the bundled phase $p=\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)$ and at the identity recovers
$a_0$ and $a_1$, while $\Phi_2(0,1)$ is the exact nonzero sign vector used in
the projected-transfer witness.

\section{Finite \texorpdfstring{$\SU$}{SU(2)}-inhabited transport witness}

The finite endpoint is a quotient/interface object, not an approximation in
which group axioms are discarded.  Its two labels carry two explicit matrices:
the identity and a noncentral diagonal phase with determinant one.  Both are
bundled as elements of $\SU$, and their inequality is proved exactly.

For a rate $0<r<1$, the transition kernel is
\[
  W_r=\frac12
  \begin{pmatrix}1+r&1-r\\1-r&1+r\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its rows sum to one, it is symmetric and strictly positive, and
\[
  W_r^k=W_{r^k}.
\]
For any observable $f$ on the two carrier points, the band covariance is the
identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cov}
  \operatorname{Cov}_k(f,f)=
  \left(\frac{f(0)-f(1)}2\right)^2r^k.
\end{equation}
Thus the decay premise consumed by the Dobrushin theorem is derived, not
assumed.  The sign observable $(1,-1)$ is orthogonal to the normalized constant
vacuum, and the projected transfer sends its first component to $r$.  Since
$r>0$, the projected operator is not zero.

The frozen witness instantiates $r$ by
\[
  \operatorname{heatRate}(t)=e^{-3t/4}.
\]
The new bridge proves this is definitionally the same real number as $r_1(t)$
in the continuous heat theorem.  It then combines
Equation~\eqref{eq:fundamental} with the existing endpoint:

\begin{theorem}[continuous-to-finite transport bridge]\label{thm:bridge}
For $t>0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $K_t*\chi_1=\operatorname{heatRate}(t)\chi_1$ pointwise on $\SU$;
\item $\chi_1\ne0$;
\item there exists $m>0$ for which the norm of the vacuum-projected finite
transfer operator is at most $e^{-m}$; and
\item that projected operator is nonzero.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:bridge} is retained because it is the published parent gap
endpoint.  Theorem~\ref{thm:allmodes} is stronger on the continuous side: it
simultaneously identifies every finite character expansion with an exact
diagonal coordinate vector.  The finite matrix remains the quotient of the
$\{0,1\}$ sector only.  It is not identified with the operator on completed
$L^2(\SU)$, and no two-state approximation of the compact group is claimed.

\section{Scope, limitations, and interpretation}

The results support the following exact statement: the infinite Haar heat
operator has an invariant algebraic sector containing every finite real
character expansion; Haar pairing supplies exact spectral coordinates there;
the heat operator is diagonal in those coordinates; the $\{0,1\}$ restriction
is conjugate to the entire genuine $\SU$-inhabited two-state transfer operator;
and the same finite witness fires the parent projected-gap endpoint
non-vacuously.

They do not establish any of the following:
\begin{itemize}
\item four-dimensional continuum Yang--Mills or the Clay mass-gap problem;
\item a thermodynamic or continuum limit of the finite witness;
\item Peter--Weyl completeness or density of the algebraic character span in
the completed class-function Hilbert space;
\item the spectrum on non-class matrix coefficients, or an operator-norm
computation on the completed full Haar $L^2$ space;
\item a new proof of the satellite's Migdal recursion, semigroup law, or exact
finite-cellulation area law; or
\item that two carrier points exhaust, discretize, or approximate the compact
group.
\end{itemize}

The right interpretation is an exact locally finite invariant restriction:
each vector uses finitely many modes, but the formal coefficient object has no
fixed dimension.  It replaces a previously two-mode continuous endpoint by an
injective all-mode lift and an exact diagonal intertwining.  The finite parent
still consumes only the first two coordinates.  Hilbert completion and
Peter--Weyl density remain separate work.

\section{Relation to the literature}

The use of heat kernels and character expansions in two-dimensional
Yang--Mills goes back to Migdal's recursion equations~\cite{Migdal1975}.
Driver constructed the Yang--Mills measure and related it to the heat equation
on compact groups~\cite{Driver1989}; rigorous compact-surface developments
include Sengupta~\cite{Sengupta1997} and L\'evy~\cite{Levy2003}.  The present
paper does not reprove those broad analytic constructions.  It contributes a
small, exact, inspectable theorem interface inside Lean.

The formalization builds on Mathlib~\cite{Mathlib2020} and on the pinned
two-dimensional satellite~\cite{Lean2dYM}.  The repository citation is a
commit permalink: it identifies the precise source used by Lake rather than a
moving branch.

\section{Conclusion}

The continuous endpoint now covers every algebraic character mode in one
Lean object.  It is an actual Haar integral on bundled $\SU$; the infinite
kernel is handled by dominated convergence; coefficient extraction proves
non-vacuity; and the lift preserves the exact finite-support Haar pairing.
The heat operator commutes with the full Casimir diagonal on this algebraic
sector.  The finite side still consists of distinct genuine holonomies; its
two-coordinate lift is bijective, its covariance identity is exact, and its
projected transfer is nonzero.  Thus $e^{-3t/4}$ is the fundamental entry of a
proved all-mode diagonalization and, on the first two modes, an exact
Lean-checked finite conjugacy.

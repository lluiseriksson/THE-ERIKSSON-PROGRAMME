10. Scope objection S1: no disjoint basis is not separation from351

The statement that this two-dimensional code has no basis of disjoint nonzero supports is TRUE. Each pair of its three nonzero words intersects on exactly one q-block. This verifies the assigned non-disjoint-basis check.

However, the further sentence that this supplies a class 'not reducible to the disjoint-block span used in351' must be qualified. Let a=chi_A,b=chi_B,c=chi_C. Then for EVERY positiveH in the proposed example,
 h(x)=H(chi_(A+B)(x),chi_(B+C)(x))=H(ab,bc)=H_tilde(a,b,c),
 where H_tilde(a,b,c)=H(ab,bc)>0.
The three q-blocksA,B,C are disjoint and equal-sized. Forq>=2 this is exactly351 with n_351=3,m_351=q and no residual coordinates; moreover3rho^q->0. Thus351 already covers every potential and its gap/moment-limit conclusion in this example. Coupling or arbitrary amplitudes ofH do not escape351 because that theorem permits arbitrary positive macro functions, including ones that ignore some independent information.

Equivalently, the two-dimensional code is not itself a disjoint-generator code, but it is contained in span{A,B,C}, a three-dimensional disjoint-generator code. Equality with such a span and containment in one are different properties. Absence of a disjoint basis INSIDE C does not prove absence of an allowed larger disjoint representation ofh.

This does not refute inequalities(1),(2), exact matching, the general-code proof or nonvacuity. It also does not prove that all eligible general codes reduce to351.

3. Direct existence proof and entropy rounding

For a fixed nonzero row a in F_2^n, a dot v is a fair bit when v is a uniform column. Distinct columns of the initially sampled G are independent. Therefore the L bits of aG are independent and fair, even though vectors aG for different a need not be independent. The union bound over a requires no such latter independence.

For every integer i with 0<=i<dL, the function d^i(1-d)^(L-i) decreases as the real index i increases, because its multiplicative ratio is d/(1-d)<1. It is therefore at least d^(dL)(1-d)^((1-d)L)=2^(-L H_2(d)). Multiplying each binomial coefficient by this bound, summing only the selected indices and bounding the resulting binomial probability sum by 1 gives
 sum_(0<=i<dL) binom(L,i) <= 2^(L H_2(d)).
The argument also holds with i<=dL and does not assume dL is integral. The bad event uses the strict inequality weight<dL, so its complement gives integer weights at least ceil(dL), including the case dL is itself an integer. There is no lost unit in the stated distance or matching order.

The resulting union probability is at most (2^n-1)2^(-L) times this volume, hence at most 2^(n-L(1-H_2(d))) <= 2^(-L(1-H_2(d)-R)). Its exponent has a strictly positive decay coefficient by hypothesis. A nonzero vector a in the kernel of G would have zero image weight, and 0<dL, so the same good event implies full row rank. No separate rank assumption or unaccounted rank-loss probability remains. Its image C consequently has exactly dimension n and M=2^n distinct codewords; its nonzero minimum distance is at least ceil(dL).

Each individual column is zero with probability 2^(-n); each fixed pair is equal with the same probability by independence of those two columns. Summing over L columns and L(L-1)/2 pairs gives exactly the displayed upper bound. Correlations among the bad events cause no problem. Since floor(RL)>RL-1, this bound is at most 2[L+L(L-1)/2]2^(-RL), which tends to zero for every fixed R>0. Its sum with the first error probability tends to zero and is therefore less than 1 for every sufficiently large integer L. Simultaneous avoidance of both classes of events is proved, rather than separately asserting two properties possibly attained by different matrices.

Nonzero, distinct columns are compatible with L<=2^n-1 eventually because n grows linearly; in fact the simultaneous positive-probability argument already forces this feasibility for its eventual regime. No finite small-L case is silently asserted. Choosing a successful matrix for each large L gives the claimed all-large-length family. This proof is self-contained and uses no executed random search.

5. Exact marginals and structural separation

The C algebra is closed under products and reciprocals of positive functions, and P_rho preserves it. Thus both normalized densities pi=hPh/E(hPh) and r=(h/Ph)/E(h/Ph) belong to that algebra. They have uniform mean 1. All nonconstant supported characters have degree at least D, so every labelled joint marginal on at most D-1 original coordinates is uniform. Fourier inversion, not merely singleton balancing, gives this conclusion. Since D>=ceil(dL), matching degree at least ceil(dL)-1 is valid and eventually exceeds any prescribed constant times sqrt(L).

For each nonzero column v, a linear functional a detects it; hence its coordinate is active in some codeword aG. For two distinct columns v,w, a functional detecting v+w separates the two coordinates in aG. This uses the same full code constructed above and is unchanged by changing its generator basis.

For n>=1, M-1>0. With 0<tau<1, the coefficients of the specified h0 are exactly 1 on the constant character and tau/(M-1)>0 on every nonzero c in C, and zero elsewhere. Distinct codewords give distinct Walsh characters. Triangle inequality gives 1-tau<=h0<=1+tau, so h0 is strictly positive and its Fourier support is exactly C, not merely contained in C.

Any function of disjoint full-block parities has Fourier support inside the span B of those whole-block indicator words, regardless of its macro dimension or whether its chosen representation is minimal. Thus a representation of this h0 would force C subset B. Every word of B is constant within each block, contradicting the proven separation of any two coordinate positions if a block has size at least 2. These facts rule out every allowed representation in 351, including larger macro algebras, coordinate permutations and changes of the C basis. 6. Actual normalization, floor and cap witnesses

The amplitude tau in section 5 can be chosen once for fixed b in (0,1) and finite s>1, independently of L. Let q=min(1/b,2^(1/s)); then q>1. Since A(tau)=((1+tau)/(1-tau))^2 tends to 1 as tau decreases to zero, there exists 0<tau<1 with A<=q. An explicit admissible choice may be obtained by taking tau no larger than (sqrt(q)-1)/(sqrt(q)+1), with a strict decrease if desired. This is an analytical existence check, not a numerical calculation. The excluded endpoints b=1 and unbounded s would require separate care; the source excludes them. The critical s=(1+rho)/(1-rho) is finite and greater than 1 for each fixed permitted rho.

P_rho is a positive Markov averaging operator, so the pointwise interval for h0 also bounds P_rho h0. Both are positive, and their product lies in [(1-tau)^2,(1+tau)^2]. Averaging gives the same bounds for Z=E(h0 P_rho h0), in particular Z>0. Setting h=h0/sqrt(Z) gives exactly E(h P_rho h)=1 and
 pi=h P_rho h=(h0 P_rho h0)/Z.
Dividing the extreme product bounds yields 1/A<=pi<=A. Since A<=1/b and b>0, pi>=b. Since uniform mu is a probability measure, E pi^s<=A^s<=2. These are the actual normalized stationary density, energy and moment, not an unnormalized surrogate. Multiplying by the positive scalar 1/sqrt(Z) preserves exact Fourier support, strict positivity and K_h, so strict separation, labelled matching and the spectral estimate hold for the same witnesses.

This establishes nonemptiness of an admissible subfamily for all sufficiently large L, with the requested fixed floor and critical cap. It does not convert the cap into a hypothesis needed for the spectral theorem, nor claim the exact cap E r^s<=2 for arbitrary positive h. The source correctly makes no such general transfer. The general stationary-gap question remains open; these witnesses have gaps tending to 1-rho>0 and therefore cannot supply gap collapse.

1. DEFINITIONS AND THE QUESTION

Let mu be uniform probability on {-1,1}^L, L>=1. Let 0<rho<1 and let P=P_rho
be Boolean product noise: P chi_S=rho^|S| chi_S. In particular, P has positive
entries, preserves mu and constants, is self-adjoint, and is positive semidefinite.
Write Q=P_sqrt(rho), so Q^2=P. All unqualified norms and expectations use mu.
Take h>0 with E hPh=||Qh||_2^2=1. Set

 g=Ph,  pi=hg,  u=h/g,  N=Eu,  r=u/N.
 s=(1+rho)/(1-rho),  B=2^(1/s).

Assume pi>=b>0 and E pi^s<=2. Necessarily 0<b<=1 because E pi=1.
Both pi and r are probability densities. The original question also assumes
E pi chi_S=0 for 1<=|S|<=k, k=floor(C sqrt L), fixed C>0 (in the meaningful
range k<=L; for asymptotic statements this holds eventually). Exact matching
always refers to pi, NEVER to r.

Accepted SOURCE349A refutes transfer of the numerical cap2 at exponent s for
one fixed admissible rho and floor, but does NOT refute a larger uniform bound.
This unit asks whether a finite K(rho,b,C) bounds E r^s. It does not settle that

3. EXTERNAL HYPERCONTRACTIVE INPUTS AND CONVENTIONS

For f on the uniform Boolean cube and 1<p<=q<infinity, ordinary scalar Boolean
hypercontractivity says
 ||P_theta f||_q<=||f||_p when theta^2<=(p-1)/(q-1).

For strictly positive f and finite beta<=alpha<1, reverse scalar Boolean
hypercontractivity says
 ||P_theta f||_beta>=||f||_alpha
 when theta^2<=(1-alpha)/(1-beta).

For a negative exponent a, ||f||_a means (E f^a)^(1/a), a positive generalized
mean, NOT a norm. In particular ||f||_(-a)=1/||f^(-1)||_a for a>0.
Every h and g here is strictly positive on a finite cube, so negative means
are defined before any dimension-independent estimate is asserted.

The reverse theorem is Borell's classical Boolean inequality, as explicitly
stated in Mossel--Oleszkiewicz--Sen, On reverse hypercontractivity,
arXiv:1108.1210v5, equation(1.2), and in Cubitt--Kastoryano--Montanaro--Temme,
Quantum reverse hypercontractivity, scalar Theorem3. Their Theorem1 also states
ordinary scalar hypercontractivity in exactly the noise convention above.
No quantum inequality is used. No general-product weaker time constant is
substituted. With theta=exp(-time), the displayed reverse parameter condition
is time >= (1/2) log[(1-beta)/(1-alpha)].
The raw returned primary-source excerpts and a precise reading-scope record
are retained. The entire original Borell paper was NOT read in this unit.

4. NORMALIZATION AND A NONLINEAR FLOOR IDENTITY

Let a=Eh=Eg. The constant Fourier component of Qh and ||Qh||_2=1 imply a<=1.
From hg>=b we get h>=b/g. Jensen under mu gives

 a>=b E(1/g)>=b/a, hence a^2>=b.

Under pi dmu, E_pi(1/g)=Eh=a and E_pi(1/g^2)=E(h/g)=N. Cauchy--Schwarz
therefore gives N>=a^2>=b. This is a lower bound; it does not reverse the
separate accepted PSD inequality N<=1. The latter is not needed below.

Apply the positivity-preserving operator P to h>=b/g:

 g>=b P(1/g).

Pointwise Jensen for each probability transition row of P gives
P(1/g)>=1/(Pg). Hence

 g Pg>=b, equivalently g^(-1)<=b^(-1) Pg.                  (4.1)

This is a derived consequence of the actual hPh floor. It is not an assumption
on an unrelated pair of functions and does not say g>=a fixed constant.

5. DIMENSION-FREE INVERSE MOMENTS

First, Pg=P_(rho^2)h=P_(rho^(3/2))(Qh). Ordinary hypercontractivity from L2
to L^(D0), with (rho^(3/2))^2=1/(D0-1), gives ||Pg||_D0<=1.
Equation(4.1) yields ||g^(-1)||_D0<=b^(-1).

For the stronger starting exponent, g=P_sqrt(rho)(Qh), and
rho=1/(R-1). Ordinary hypercontractivity gives ||g||_R<=1.
The floor gives h^(-1)<=g/b, hence

 ||h^(-1)||_R<=b^(-1), or ||h||_(-R)>=b.                 (5.1)

Use reverse hypercontractivity with alpha=-R, beta=-D and theta=rho.
Since D=(1+R)/rho^2-1, the noise condition is an equality,
rho^2=(1+R)/(1+D). Also D>R because rho<1. Thus beta<alpha<0, all hypotheses
hold, and

 ||g||_(-D)=||Ph||_(-D)>=||h||_(-R)>=b.

Taking reciprocals, ||g^(-1)||_D<=b^(-1), with the inequality in the required
direction. Note D-D0=2(rho^(-2)-1)>0. This is a genuine improvement over the
direct Jensen bound. No cap E pi^s or stationary matching has entered.

6. THE BOOTSTRAP, ITS CONSTANTS AND ITS FIXED POINT

The following particular iteration was suggested by the parent during this
unit and is shared intellectual input, not an independent discovery here.
The fabrication agent supplies the derivation and the ceiling analysis.

Suppose q>s/(s-1) and ||g^(-1)||_q<=A. Set p=sq/(s+q)>1.
Since h=pi/g, ordinary Holder gives

 ||h||_p <= ||pi||_s ||g^(-1)||_q <= B A.

Now Pg=P_(rho^2)h. Ordinary hypercontractivity from p to F(q) applies:
rho^4=(p-1)/(F(q)-1), and F(q)>p because rho<1 and p>1.
Using (4.1) once more gives

 ||g^(-1)||_(F(q)) <= b^(-1)||Pg||_(F(q))
                   <= (B/b) A.                         (6.1)

Thus iteration is valid whenever the current q is in that range.

Here is a complete analysis showing that range persists and identifying the
ceiling. For q>0, direct algebra gives

 F(q)-q =
 [-rho^4 q^2+(s-1)(1-rho^4)q-s(1-rho^4)]/[rho^4(s+q)].

The discriminant of the numerator as a quadratic is
(s-1)^2(1-rho^4)^2-4rho^4 s(1-rho^4)
=4rho^2 s(1-rho^4).
Since s(1-rho^4)=(1+rho)^2(1+rho^2), its roots are exactly q_minus and q_plus
in section2. Both roots are positive and distinct. Therefore F(q)>q
strictly between the roots. Further,
F'(q)=s^2/[rho^4(s+q)^2]>0, so F is increasing.

The starting D=(1+2rho-rho^3)/rho^3 is strictly between the roots. To check
without numerical evaluation, multiply differences by rho^3>0:

 rho^3(D-q_minus)
 =rho-rho^2-2rho^3+(1+rho)sqrt(1+rho^2)
 >=(1+2rho)(1-rho^2)>0;

 rho^3(q_plus-D)
 =-rho+rho^2+2rho^3+(1+rho)sqrt(1+rho^2)
 >=1+rho^2+2rho^3>0.

Also D>R=2s/(s-1)>s/(s-1), as already observed. Inductively q_n is strictly
increasing and q_n<q_plus: monotonicity of F implies F(q_n)<F(q_plus)=q_plus.
Thus each use of (6.1) has p>1. Its increasing bounded limit is a fixed point
of continuous F and is greater than q_minus, hence equals q_plus.
Starting from A_0=b^(-1), (6.1) proves
||g^(-1)||_(q_n)<=A_n=b^(-1)(B/b)^n.

For every fixed q<q_plus, some iterate has q_n>=q (or q<=D already), so norm
monotonicity supplies a dimension-free bound. There is NO claim that the
displayed constants stay bounded as n->infinity: B/b>1, so A_n diverges.
Even a hypothetical finite bound at q_plus would not make q_plus infinite.
This is a precise finite-exponent obstruction to this particular route.

7. ACTUAL WEIGHTING MOMENTS AND STRONG CONVERGENCE

The exact identity r=pi/(N g^2), together with N>=b, gives by Holder

because 1/t_n=1/s+2/q_n. No weighting moment or marginal assumption was used
to prove the bound. In moment form E r^(t_n)<=K_n^(t_n).
For n=0 this reads
 E r^(t_0) <= 2^(t_0/s) b^(-3t_0).
The strict inequality t_0>1 is equivalent to D>2s/(s-1)=R.
Because q_n strictly increases to finite q_plus, t_n strictly increases to
t_plus<s. These inequalities hold for every fixed 0<rho<1, with no
noise-range exception and no uniformity in rho approaching an endpoint.

8. EXACT REMAINING BLOCKAGE AND ABANDONMENT

The requested critical bound would require control of the mixed moment
N^(-s) E[pi^s g^(-2s)]. The proved pi control is exactly L^s. Separate Holder
at a smaller target exponent t needs inverse exponent 2ts/(s-t); at t=s that
expression is infinite. Sections5-6 give finite inverse exponents with
explicit constants. They do not control this mixed critical moment.
The finite ceiling q_plus makes this failure explicit for the proposed map F.

This does NOT prove that a dimension-uniform critical K is impossible. It
does NOT produce a divergent-moment family or a collapsing Doob spectral gap.
It does NOT prove a uniform positive pointwise g floor; SOURCE344A's failure
of uniform convergence g->1 is compatible with what is proved here.
SOURCE349A's exact-cap2 counterexample is also compatible: strong convergence
strictly below s does not force either cap2 or a uniform bound at s.

Bounded attempts in this unit:
1. Direct floor identity plus ordinary hypercontractivity: successful inverse
   exponent D0, but endpoint Holder requires infinitely more.
2. Negative-exponent reverse hypercontractivity: successful larger D and an
   actual r exponent t0>1; still t0<s.
3. Parent's nonlinear Holder/ordinary-noise bootstrap: successful improvement
   through every finite iterate, but rigorously finite fixed point q_plus and
   growing constants. This route is abandoned as an endpoint proof at this
   point. No endless exponent optimization is proposed.

The remaining single question is unchanged. A next substantive step would
need joint control of pi^s on the small-g region (rather than separate
moments), or an actual admissible h-family concentrating that joint quantity.
Such a control has NOT been established, and should not be named a removed

1. Statement and scope
Fix 0<rho<1. Let C be a nonzero binary linear subspace of F_2^L with
dimension n and minimum nonzero Hamming weight D>=2. Put M=2^n and
delta=sum_{c in C, c!=0} rho^|c|. Assume delta<1. Take any strictly
positive real function h whose Walsh support lies in C. Equivalently h
is an arbitrary positive function H of n independent parity coordinates;
their generating supports may overlap. No uniform amplitude or floor
bound is imposed. All values are finite at every finite dimension.

For uniform probability mu on the cube and Boolean noise P=P_rho, set
g=Ph, Z=E h g, K(x,y)=P(x,y)h(y)/g(x), pi=hg/Z,
N=E h/g, r_h=(h/g)/N. pi and r_h are densities relative to mu.
Let lambda2 be the second largest eigenvalue of the actual FULL chain K.
Define eta=M rho^(D/2), alpha=(1-delta)/(1+delta),
B=(rho+eta)/(1-delta), and epsilon=(1-rho^2)delta/[rho(1-delta)].
Then the candidate conclusions are

 max(0,rho-epsilon) <= lambda2 <= min(1,max(1-alpha,B));       (1)
 alpha^2 <= r_h/pi <= alpha^-2 pointwise.                     (2)

Both densities have exact uniform labelled marginals through degree D-1.
For every fixed s>0 their s-moments consequently satisfy the two-sided
comparison alpha^(2s) E pi^s <= E r_h^s <= alpha^(-2s) E pi^s.

For any sequence of such codes at fixed rho with eta->0, delta->0 and
the full-chain gap 1-lambda2 tends to 1-rho, uniformly over all positive
H. No stationary cap is needed for this spectral assertion. An absolute
critical moment bound additionally requires the stated cap E pi^s<=2;
under that cap E r_h^s<=2 alpha^(-2s), not necessarily <=2 exactly.

This extends the algebra beyond disjoint blocks, at the cost of an
explicit rate/distance condition. It does not cover arbitrary exact
marginal matching, all codes, all noise regimes, or block-majority
potentials. It is not an unconditional general stationary-gap theorem.
The disjoint-block estimate351 can be much sharper than this coarse
distance estimate; neither should be represented as containing the
other's full parameter range.

2. Macro algebra and its genuine kernel
Choose a basis c_1,...,c_n of C. Let z_i(x)=chi_{c_i}(x). The map to
z in {-1,1}^n is surjective with equal-size fibers. For I subset[n],
write c(I)=sum_{i in I} c_i and chi_I(z)=product_{i in I}z_i.
The functions in the algebra have expansions in these M characters.
The noise restricted to it has kernel, relative to macro uniform law,

 b0(z,w)=sum_I rho^|c(I)| chi_I(z)chi_I(w).

This is a stochastic nonnegative kernel because it is the actual
conditional action of P on the quotient. Independently its explicit
series gives 1-delta<=b0<=1+delta. If a=E H>0, then
a(1-delta)<=G(z)=(b0 H)(z)<=a(1+delta).
Hence Pi(z)=H(z)G(z)/Z is the macro stationary density, and pi is its
pullback. The quotient transition matrix is K0(z,w)=2^-n b0(z,w)H(w)/G(z).
It minorizes alpha nu(w), nu(w)=2^-n H(w)/a. Subtracting that common
row leaves (1-alpha) times a stochastic matrix. For a nonconstant real
eigenfunction F, oscillation therefore gives |lambda|<=1-alpha.
nu need not equal Pi, and no incorrect nu-mean-zero condition is used.

3. All sectors, including the modes missed by the quotient
For each coset t+C in F_2^L/C, let V_t consist of chi_t(x)F(z(x)).
The cosets partition the Walsh basis. Each space has dimension M,
and there are 2^(L-n) spaces, so their direct sum is the full space.
They are mutually orthogonal for the stationary inner product too:
Pi F J remains in the C algebra, whereas chi_(t+v) belongs to a
different coset when t+C!=v+C, making its uniform integral zero.
Multiplication by h and 1/g preserves each sector, as does diagonal
Walsh noise. Thus K leaves every V_t invariant. Its action there is

 K_t F(z)= E_w b_t(z,w) H(w)F(w)/G(z),
 b_t(z,w)=sum_I rho^|t+c(I)| chi_I(z)chi_I(w).                 (3)

This follows by checking F=chi_I: the full Walsh label is t+c(I),
whose multiplier under P is precisely rho^|t+c(I)|. The signed
kernel b_t is not assumed stochastic. Formula(3) describes every
within-fiber mode and all remaining coordinates, with no dropped
eigenvectors. Changing the representative only changes the macro
coordinate expression, not the invariant space.

4. Distance bounds for every nontrivial coset
Choose t of minimum weight d in a nontrivial coset. Then d>=1.
Distinct words of the coset differ by a nonzero codeword, so their
Hamming distance is at least D. If d<=D/2, every other word v in
t+C has |v|>=D-d>=D/2, by the triangle inequality. Consequently

 sum_{v in t+C}rho^|v| <= rho^d+(M-1)rho^(D/2) <= rho+eta.

If d>D/2, every word has weight at least d, so the same sum is at
most M rho^(D/2)=eta<=rho+eta. Equality d=D/2 causes no problem.
Fractional D/2 is harmless since rho^x decreases for real x.
By triangle inequality in(3), |b_t|<=rho+eta<=B b0.
Thus |K_tF|<=B K0|F|, and ||K_t||infinity<=B. Every sector
eigenvalue has modulus at most B, irrespective of H's amplitudes.

K is reversible under pi because its conductance is proportional
to h(x)P(x,y)h(y), symmetric for uniform mu. Moreover conjugation
by multiplication with sqrt(hg) gives
M_sqrt(h/g) P M_sqrt(h/g), a positive definite symmetric matrix.
All eigenvalues are therefore positive. Strict positivity of P
and h gives a simple top eigenvalue1 and all eigenvalues <=1.
The quotient and nontrivial-sector bounds combine, using the full
decomposition, to give the upper inequality in(1), even if B>=1.

5. Lower spectral bound: actual stationary Rayleigh quotient
Fix any coordinate i. Since D>=2, e_i is outside C. f=chi_{e_i}
has stationary mean0 and norm1. Using t=e_i in(3), subtract rho b0.
For a codeword not containing i the coefficient difference is zero;
for one containing i it is (1-rho^2)rho^(|c|-1). Thus

 |b_(e_i)-rho b0| <= (1-rho^2)delta/rho <= epsilon b0.

Averaging H/G gives |K_(e_i)1-rho|<=epsilon since K01=1.
Hence <f,Kf>_pi=E_Pi K_(e_i)1>=rho-epsilon. Variational
characterization on constants-perpendicular gives lambda2 at
least that value, and positivity gives the max with0 in(1).
No claim that f is itself an exact eigenvector is required.

6. Densities, moments and exact matching
The finite-dimensional C algebra is all functions of z, hence is
closed under products and inverses of positive functions. Both
pi and r_h therefore belong to it and have uniform mean1. Their
nonconstant Walsh support has degree at least D; Fourier inversion
gives each labelled joint marginal through degree D-1 exactly
uniform, not merely singleton balancing.

Under Pi, E_Pi G^-2=N/Z. Since a(1-delta)<=G<=a(1+delta),
the product (N/Z)G^2 lies between alpha^2 and alpha^-2.
Its reciprocal is r_h/pi and has the same pair of bounds. Raising
positive pointwise quantities to s>0 and integrating proves(2)
and the moment comparison, without an energy-one normalization.
Only the additional stationary cap gives an absolute moment bound.

7. Uniform limit, nonempty example and limitations
delta<=(M-1)rho^D<=eta rho^(D/2)<=eta. If eta->0 then
alpha->1, B->rho, epsilon->0. The squeeze in(1) proves the
uniform gap limit. A useful sufficient condition is
n log2 -(D/2)|logrho| -> -infinity. No existence theorem for
arbitrary code rates is assumed by stating this implication.

A concrete genuinely overlapping example uses L=3q, q>=1,
and the two generators which are indicators of blocks A union B
and B union C, where A,B,C are disjoint q-sets. The three
nonzero codewords all have weight2q, so n=2,D=2q,
delta=3rho^(2q) and eta=4rho^q. For large q all hypotheses
hold. H may be any positive coupled function of these two
overlapping parity observables, with amplitudes varying arbitrarily.
Their span has no basis consisting of disjoint nonzero supports:
every two distinct nonzero words overlap on exactly q coordinates.
This supplies a nonempty overlapping code algebra, but NOT a potential
class outside351: with a=chi_A,b=chi_B,c=chi_C, H(ab,bc) is a positive
function of three disjoint q-block parities, which351 already permits.
The separate353A audit caught this scope distinction. Absence of a
nonzero disjoint basis inside C does not exclude containment in a larger
disjoint-block algebra. The original353A source and adverse report are
retained unchanged. See the new explicit separating family in section9.
No statement of arbitrary-code or general stationary matching
closure follows. The condition eta->0 is derived from concrete
code size and distance, not a renamed spectral hypothesis.

9. A genuine separating family: the explicit binary simplex code
This section is new in353B and awaits its own separate audit. The code
construction is classical; no novelty of the code is claimed. All facts
needed here are proved explicitly rather than imported by name.

Let j>=2 and index L=2^j-1 coordinates by the nonzero vectors v in F_2^j.
For each a in F_2^j let c_a be the binary word with coordinate a dot v.
Then C={c_a} is a linear subspace of dimension j: evaluation at the unit
vectors distinguishes a. Each a!=0 has dot product1 on exactly2^(j-1)
vectors in F_2^j, and the omitted zero vector never contributes1.
Thus all its nonzero codewords have weight D=2^(j-1), and M=2^j.
In particular delta=(2^j-1)rho^(2^(j-1)) and
eta=2^j rho^(2^(j-2)), which both tend to0 for each fixed0<rho<1.
The theorem therefore applies for all sufficiently large j, uniformly
in every positive H on this j-dimensional parity quotient. Both
stationary and weighting marginals match through D-1, of linear order
in L and therefore at least any fixed square-root degree eventually.

To prove a strict potential-class separation rather than just a basis
statement, choose h=1+epsilon sum_(c in C,c!=0) chi_c with
0<epsilon<1/(M-1). Triangle inequality gives h>0 everywhere and
EVERY codeword in C has nonzero Fourier coefficient (including0).
Suppose this h could be represented as an arbitrary function of
parities of disjoint coordinate blocks of a common size m>=2, with
any leftover residual coordinates ignored, as in351. Its Fourier
support would then lie in the span B of those full block indicators.
Since its actual support is all C, necessarily C is a subspace of B.
Every word in B has equal entries at two coordinates belonging to the
same block. But any two distinct coordinates v,w in our construction
are separated by some a, because v+w!=0 admits a dot(v+w)=1.
Consequently c_a(v)!=c_a(w). No block can contain two coordinates
if C is contained in B. Also no coordinate can be residual: for each
nonzero v there exists a with a dot v=1, whereas every word in B is
zero on residual coordinates. This contradicts m>=2. Relabelling
coordinates does not alter either separation property.

Thus there exist strictly positive code potentials covered by353B
which have NO representation by the disjoint equal-size blocks of351,
even after enlarging the macro dimension. Allowing singleton blocks
would represent them but fails351's m>=2 domain and long-block regime.
This is the missing class-separation witness; the earlier three-block
example is retained only as an overlapping illustration.
The spectral theorem still concerns a restricted family of actual
Boolean Doob kernels, not every stationary-matching density. No new
unconditional general gap conclusion or physical interpretation follows.

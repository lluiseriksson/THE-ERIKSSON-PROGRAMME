3. Normalization, model and scope checks

The paper consistently works on the uniform Boolean cube with 0<rho<1, positive h, g=P_rho h and energy Z=E[hg]. After energy normalization Z=1, pi=hg is the stationary density relative to uniform measure, u=h/g, N=E[u], r=u/N and Q=P_sqrt(rho). These are different densities and operators; replacing r by pi is precisely an issue the paper investigates.

The actual transition kernel K(x,y)=P_rho(x,y)h(y)/g(x) is stochastic. Detailed balance follows because pi(x)K(x,y)=h(x)P_rho(x,y)h(y) is symmetric. Strict positivity gives a simple Perron eigenvalue 1. Conjugating by sqrt(pi) yields M_sqrt(u) P_rho M_sqrt(u). Product noise is positive definite for 0<rho<1, so K has nonnegative real spectrum. With X=M_sqrt(u)Q, XX* is that conjugate and X*X=T=Q M_u Q has the same eigenvalues. This validates the use of T for the actual Doob spectrum, rather than merely a generic weighted comparison operator.

The stationary assumptions are pi>=b, E[pi^s]<=2 with s=(1+rho)/(1-rho), and labelled-coordinate Fourier matching through k (usually floor(C sqrt(L)), clipped by L). Feasibility already forces b<=1. Constants and asymptotic claims hold for fixed parameters, not uniformly as rho approaches an endpoint. The paper does not exchange the original noise for noise in arbitrarily changed binary coordinates. It does not assert a physical mass gap or a solution of a Millennium problem.

4. Section 2: exact numerical-cap failure and restricted safeguards

The two-variable potential H takes values 29/16, 1/4, 1/8, 29/16, has mean one, and has expansion 1+c(x-y)+dxy with c=1/32 and d=13/16. With q=c(x-y), v=E[q^2]=1/512 and w=d^2=169/256, the smoothed quotient is 1+theta q+theta^2 dxy and its energy is 1+theta v+theta^2 w. The first derivatives of normalized stationary and weighting densities at theta=0 are opposite: H(q-v) and -H(q-v). This comparison keeps the normalizing factors; omitting them would give a different sign test.

For t>1 the coefficient C_t=E[H^t q]-vE[H^t] has the displayed numerator 31(1/4)^t-33(1/8)^t-2(29/16)^t, divided by 2048. At t=1 its numerator vanishes. The derivative of the first two terms is log(2)8^(-t)(99-62*2^t), strictly negative for t>=1, while the subtracted last term increases. Thus C_t<0 for t>1. The stated second and third moments put the unique crossing t_* between 2 and 3. Strict convexity of t -> E[H^t], together with E[H log H]>0, justifies uniqueness beyond t=1. The parameter rho_*=(t_*-1)/(t_*+1) consequently lies between 1/3 and 1/2.

Embedding the two quotient variables as disjoint block parities makes theta=rho_*^m small and gives original-coordinate matching through m-1. Taking m=floor(L/2) eventually exceeds the required sqrt(L) cutoff. Continuity and strict derivative signs yield E[pi^s]<2<E[r^s] for all sufficiently large L, while the fixed positive floor is retained; filling finitely many earlier dimensions with h=1 is legitimate. Both moments tend to 2, so this disproves the exact numerical cap, not every finite critical bound. The separate block theorem shows this family's gap remains asymptotically unweighted. No collapsing-gap example has been smuggled into this result.

The two-level safeguard is valid under energy normalization. Writing a=Eh gives a<=1 and, from the floor and Jensen/Cauchy–Schwarz, a^2>=b and N>=a^2. If the lower level m occupies mass q, integration of the floor on that set gives m>=bq. Since M(1-q)<=a<=1 and g>=b/M on the upper level (and >=b/m>=b/M on the lower level), g>=b(1-q) everywhere. Together with g>=m>=bq, this gives g>=b/2. Substituting into r=pi/(Ng^2) yields the stated factor 4/b^3. This argument genuinely uses two levels; it is not a proof of a uniform minimum for arbitrary h.

For product potentials the one-bit stationary and weighting amplitudes have denominators 1+rho t^2 and 1-rho t^2 respectively. Their absolute values compare in the required direction because the difference reduces to a positive multiple of rho(1-t^2). The even convex moment function is increasing in the absolute amplitude, so the one-bit comparison tensorizes. The conclusion excludes exact-cap failure within that restricted product family, not throughout the original class.

5. Section 3: inverse moments, bootstrap and matching transfer

The floor yields h>=b/g. Applying P and Jensen gives g>=bP(1/g)>=b/(Pg), hence g Pg>=b and g^(-1)<=Pg/b. The directions matter: this is an upper bound on the reciprocal. Under energy normalization ||Qh||_2=1. Ordinary hypercontractivity gives ||g||_R<=1 for R=1+rho^(-1) and ||Pg||_(1+rho^(-3))<=1. Therefore the initial reciprocal bounds and constants are dimension-free.

The stronger reverse step is admissible, including its negative exponents. From 1/h<=g/b one obtains ||h||_(-R)=1/||1/h||_R>=b. Put p=-R and q=-D_* with D_*=(1+R)/rho^2-1. Then q<p<1 and rho^2=(1-p)/(1-q), exactly the Boolean reverse-hypercontractive boundary. Thus ||g||_(-D_*)>=b, equivalent to ||1/g||_(D_*)<=1/b. Raising or inverting a negative-power expression has not reversed this conclusion incorrectly. Strict positivity makes all these finite-dimensional negative moments legitimate. The relevant primary statement was checked directly in Mossel–Oleszkiewicz–Sen, equations (1.1)–(1.2): https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.1210v5 .

Given ||1/g||_q<=A and q>s/(s-1), Hölder gives ||h||_p<=BA for p=sq/(s+q)>1, B=2^(1/s). Since Pg=P_(rho^2)h, ordinary hypercontractivity maps p to F(q)=1+(p-1)/rho^4. Reusing the floor gives the next bound (B/b)A. This verifies both the fourth power of rho and the multiplicative constant. The displayed quadratic numerator of F(q)-q has the claimed roots, and D_* lies strictly between them. Since F is increasing and positive there, the iterates increase to q_+, while their constants A_n=b^(-1)(B/b)^n are not uniformly controlled as n grows. The paper explicitly refrains from taking a bounded endpoint limit.

The relation 1/t_n=1/s+2/q_n gives r=pi/(Ng^2) its stated K_n bound using N>=b. The inequalities 1<t_n<t_+<s follow from the reciprocal exponent comparison. These are finite, fixed-index statements. A limit of exponents alone would not establish a critical moment bound, and the manuscript does not make that inference.

Lemma 3.2 supplies its own matching argument instead of assuming a spectral result. With a=Eh and v=||Qh-a||_2^2=1-a^2, the high-frequency part of g has L^(p') norm at most eta_p^(k+1)sqrt(v). If p'>2 the factorization uses P_(1/sqrt(p'-1)) from L2 to L^(p') and a commuting noise factor with parameter eta_p<1; if p'<=2 norm monotonicity suffices. No unproved dimension-free norm for a sharp Fourier projection on Lp is used.

Exact matching removes the low polynomial from E[pi g], leaving E[pi g]<=a+e sqrt(v). Cauchy–Schwarz with the positive measure h dmu gives 1<=(Eh)E[hg^2]=aE[pi g], hence v<=a^2 e^2 and a^2>=(1+e^2)^(-1). The Perron representation of T gives N=<1,T1><=1; under pi, Cauchy–Schwarz gives a^2<=N. The affinity E[sqrt(r pi)]=a/sqrt(N)>=a then bounds the Hellinger distance, and multiplying the sum and difference of square roots gives ||r-pi||_1<=2e. Total variation is half that L1 distance. Interpolation with the fixed t_n bound yields the stated subcritical convergence, not critical convergence.

6. Sections 4–6: full-chain code and block rigidity

For a binary linear code C of distance D>=2, its character algebra is closed under products and positive inversion. The quotient noise density b_0 is between 1-delta and 1+delta. For arbitrary positive quotient potential H, its average a gives a(1-delta)<=G<=a(1+delta), so the quotient Doob kernel minorizes alpha=(1-delta)/(1+delta) times the density H/a. This bounds nonconstant quotient eigenvalues without an amplitude bound.

The proof does not confuse quotient spectrum with full-chain spectrum. Every coset t+C yields an invariant sector chi_t F(z); sectors are orthogonal in the stationary measure because pi lies in the C algebra. They span the entire cube. Their signed noise kernels are controlled by the quotient kernel. A minimum-weight representative of weight d>=1 either has d<=D/2, forcing every other word in its coset to have weight at least D-d>=D/2, or all words have weight>D/2. Therefore the signed kernel has absolute mass at most rho+M rho^(D/2). Dividing by 1-delta gives B_C. This controls every nontrivial sector, uniformly over H.

For the lower bound a singleton lies outside C because D>=2. Comparing its sector kernel to rho b_0 produces coefficients zero for codewords not containing that coordinate and (1-rho^2)rho^(|c|-1) otherwise. The stated epsilon_gap follows, and the singleton character is stationary-mean-zero with norm one. Its Rayleigh quotient gives the lower bound for lambda_2. Positivity of the actual spectrum validates the clipping at zero. The ratio r/pi is the quotient function G^(-2) divided by its stationary average, giving alpha^2<=r/pi<=alpha^(-2). Hence eta->0 controls both the full gap and moment transfer on this class. No stationary cap is needed for the spectral bound; the cap is used only for the absolute moment estimate.

The disjoint-block proposition keeps its sharper n rho^m->0 regime. An interior block gives the factor (rho+rho^(m-1))/(1-rho^m). The proof treats separately the case where this factor is at least one, using the global Markov bound instead of the false inequality beta^j<=beta. Residual-coordinate sectors are included. The singleton lower estimate and logarithmic bound for alpha_b give the claimed asymptotic gap. The paper correctly distinguishes structural inclusion from quantitative regime inclusion: the coarser general-code condition does not subsume every direct-block parameter sequence.

The simplex construction proves a real representation separation for explicitly full-support potentials. Pair separation of original coordinates forces any putative parity block to be a singleton, while nonzero columns exclude unused coordinates. Thus it cannot be represented in a larger disjoint-block algebra with block size>=2, not merely in a chosen disjoint basis. This repairs the historically weaker and false basis argument recorded in the paper. It is an existence assertion about the displayed full-support potentials, not about every member of their code algebra. Constants and other special potentials remain representable. Arbitrary invertible binary coordinate changes are correctly excluded as replacements for permutations because they do not preserve the product-noise weights.

The positive-rate construction is a valid analytic existence proof, not an executed random experiment. The union bound over nonzero messages controls low weight; a second union bound excludes zero and repeated generator columns. Both errors decay for a fixed positive rate below the stated entropy and spectral thresholds. Excluding weight<dL gives distance>=ceil(dL), including nonintegral dL. The same event also excludes a nonzero kernel vector. Consequently one obtains full rank, distance and distinct nonzero columns simultaneously for every sufficiently large length. The spectral error eta_L<=exp(-a_*L) and delta_L<=eta_L give the asserted exponential full-gap estimate.

The positive full-support witness with coefficients tau/(M_L-1) remains between 1-tau and 1+tau. A single small tau independent of L makes the normalized stationary density satisfy both prescribed b<1 and the finite moment cap. Support and kernel are unchanged by energy normalization. Thus floor, cap, matching, strict representation separation and full-gap bound all concern the same constructed potential. The endpoint b=1 is excluded from this witness clause, as required. Efficient construction and nestedness are not claimed.

The general symmetry framework and coding ingredients are classical. The checked sources support that attribution, not priority of the specific Doob estimate: Boyd et al., introduction/symmetry framework, https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/symmetry.pdf ; Guruswami, entropy-volume and random-linear-code statements, https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~venkatg/teaching/codingtheory/notes/notes2.pdf ; Wootters, simplex-code exercise, https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs250/cs250.1254/Homework/HW1/hw1.pdf . The manuscript includes the needed elementary proofs rather than treating the coding construction itself as novel.

7. Section 7: fixed finite crossing and whole-operator window

The new finite-crossing step is substantive. With p_0=1+rho, the inequality t_n>p_0 is equivalent to q_n>q_c=2(1+rho)/rho. Direct substitution gives F(q_c)-q_c=(1-rho^2)(3-rho^2)/(rho^3(2-rho))>0. Since the numerator of F(q)-q is a negative-leading quadratic, q_c lies between its roots. The increasing sequence q_n converges to q_+, so it crosses q_c after finitely many steps. Fixing that index before sending dimension or matching degree to infinity avoids an illicit uniform-in-n assertion. The resulting t remains strictly below s.

The passage from r to u is correctly normalized: u=N(r-pi)+(N-1)pi+pi, with N<=1 and 1-N<=e^2. Interpolation at p=(p_0+t)/2 produces the displayed Delta_k bound. Because alpha is between zero and one and eta_0<1, the e^2 term can be absorbed into the slower eta_0^(alpha(k+1)) bound with coefficient B^3.

For low-degree polynomials, the L2-to-Lr estimate uses an algebraic degree multiplier followed by genuine noise. When the intermediate multiplier exceeds one it is explicitly not called a Markov kernel. The factor max(1,sqrt(rho(r-1)))^d is correct. This is the same classical low-degree method in O'Donnell, Theorem 9.21 and its proof, printed page 262: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.10386 . It is not a new hypercontractivity theorem.

The Hölder exponents are admissible. Since p_0<p<t<s, 1/r_1=1/p_0-1/p is positive and strictly smaller than rho/(1+rho); hence r_1>1+1/rho>2 and A_1>1. Also p_0<z<min(s,2), so 1/r_2=1/z-1/s gives r_2>1+1/rho and A_2>1, while beta=sqrt(rho/(z-1)) is strictly between zero and one. No endpoint z=2 or p=p_0 is silently used.

For F in the entire low-degree subspace, (T-P)F splits as Q[(u-pi)QF]+Q[(pi-1)QF]. The first term is controlled by Q:L^(1+rho)->L2 and Hölder at p,r_1. The second has no Fourier degrees below k+1-d: multiplying a degree>k character by a degree<=d character cannot lower its symmetric-difference weight below that threshold. Factoring Q=P_beta P_sqrt(z-1), using hypercontractivity on the second factor and high-degree L2 damping on the first, gives the second term in epsilon_(k,d). The bound holds simultaneously for every unit F in the low-degree subspace. There is no sum over columns and no binomial or square-root-dimension loss. Self-adjointness supplies the row estimate.

Choosing d=floor(c k) with c below all three strict thresholds makes both decay exponents positive. The floor only improves the upper estimates. The statement requires k->infinity and fixed rho,b and chosen exponents; it is not uniform at the boundary of those inequalities.

The Perron vector of T is w=Qh, not the constant vector. The paper controls ||w-1||_2 by e and uses orthogonality to w to bound the constant component of any non-Perron mode. Inverting lambda-P on the nonconstant low subspace has norm at most 1/zeta when lambda>=rho+zeta. Thus the projected eigenvector mass is at most e^2+(epsilon/zeta)^2. This requires a fixed positive margin and does not apply uniformly to eigenvalues approaching rho at an unspecified rate.

For 1<=d<=min(k,L-1), compare T to diag(P_low,H_d). The low diagonal error is at most epsilon; the self-adjoint off-diagonal error has norm at most epsilon. Their sum is therefore bounded by 2epsilon. Since H_d is a positive compression of T, ||H_d||<=1. The comparison operator has largest eigenvalue 1 and second eigenvalue max(rho,||H_d||), including the possible case ||H_d||=1. Min–max then proves the displayed lambda_2 estimate. This is an actual high-block reduction; claiming a high-block bound from it would be circular. The paper does not do so.

8. Section 8: actual-mode localization and the tail obstruction

For an actual real eigenfunction Kf=lambda f, stationary normalization E[pi f]=0 and E[pi f^2]=1 are essential. The equation P(hf)=lambda g f gives psi=Q(hf)/sqrt(lambda), ||psi||_2=1 and <psi,Qh>=0. Applying Q once more yields Qpsi=sqrt(lambda)g f, so u|Qpsi|^2=lambda pi f^2 pointwise. This is stronger and more specific than an arbitrary spectral-coordinate statement; the conjugation factors are correct.

Splitting psi at degree d gives ||Qpsi||_2^2<=delta_k+rho^(d+1). On {u<=M}, integrate the exact identity to obtain the stated upper bound on stationary squared mass. The probability estimate follows from the uniform t-moment bound on u, then Hölder with ||pi||_s<=B. In particular, the exponent is t/s', not t or t/s, and the coefficient is B K_t^(t/s').

For M=exp(theta d), both strict constraints on theta are needed: theta<log(1/rho) controls the high-frequency damping term, and theta<2gamma_0/c controls the squared discrepancy. Since d=floor(c k), these ensure the complement's squared mass tends to zero. The stationary probability of {u>M} decays exponentially in d; with k of order sqrt(L), this is an exponential-in-sqrt(L) scale. The quantifiers are conditional on existence of a stationary-mean-zero mode above rho+zeta at fixed zeta. They do not assert such modes exist or are impossible.

The higher L^ell consequence correctly uses Hölder on the stationary probability space: squared mass on U is at most ||f||_ell^2 pi(U)^(1-2/ell). Combining the lower mass and upper probability bounds yields the stated exponential lower bound for every fixed finite ell>2. There is no independently proved upper bound on these moments, so this divergence is not a contradiction and does not close the gap problem.

The final tail-space example has unit L2 norm, all Fourier degrees at least m=d_L+1, and an exactly factorized smoothed Lq norm. For fixed finite q>1+1/rho, the coefficient of epsilon^2 in log(kappa) is positive, so a sufficiently small fixed epsilon makes kappa>1. Since d_L=o(L), the parity damping contributes o(L) to the logarithm while the product contributes asymptotically L log(kappa). Therefore no dimension-uniform L2-to-Lq bound can hold on that entire tail subspace. This is an elementary tensor sharpness obstruction, not an actual Doob-family counterexample.

The boundary with prior tail literature is handled correctly. Heilman–Mossel–Oleszkiewicz's displayed heat-smoothing statements compare the same Lp norm before and after smoothing; Volberg's cited setting is likewise a same-exponent Banach-valued tail setting. Neither supplies the cross-exponent estimate refuted here. Checked primary sources: https://www.stevenheilman.org/~heilman/papers/hmo.pdf and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.00472v4 . The inequality 2t'>2s'=1+1/rho for t<s identifies exactly why the naive Hölder repair using only ||u||_t asks for an unavailable cross-exponent bound. It does not invalidate the low-degree polynomial estimate used earlier.

9. Originality, significance and objections

No global priority claim is established by this review. I checked the material classical inputs and compared their scope with the manuscript. I did not perform an exhaustive literature survey of weighted product-noise kernels, hypercontractive Doob transforms, coding-based Markov decompositions or related perturbation theory. The cited general frameworks do not by themselves show that the exact results here were previously published, but absence of such a match in bounded reading is not proof of originality.

Specific novelty candidates worth further comparison are: the normalized exact-cap counterexample; the floor-derived reciprocal bootstrap with its explicit fixed-point ceiling and fixed finite crossing; amplitude-uniform control of every code coset for actual Doob kernels; and the low-to-all window plus fixed-margin actual-mode localization. The random-code existence mechanism, ordinary/reverse hypercontractivity, sector decomposition in general, min–max perturbation, and tensor obstruction are classical tools or straightforward applications and should not be promoted individually as frontier discoveries.

Material mathematical objections remaining: NONE identified in this reading. This means no concrete invalidating flaw was found; it is not certainty of correctness. The shared-context review and lack of formal verification are material limitations on confidence, not hidden proof repairs.

Nonblocking weaknesses and suggestions, all retained:
(a) The manuscript is dense and symbol-heavy. R is an exponent while R_d is a projection; d is used locally for a code distance fraction, a coset representative weight and a spectral cutoff, and u is locally an upper block-density parameter before becoming h/g. The scopes can be recovered, but a short notation table would help readers.
(b) Much of the contribution combines classical estimates. Broader primary-literature comparison would strengthen the significance/originality discussion; it should not be replaced by a stronger unsupported novelty adjective.
(c) Constants deteriorate near rho=0 or rho=1, and the finite bootstrap index may be large. The current fixed-parameter statements are sound, but no uniform endpoint or practical finite-size claim should be inferred.
(d) The unresolved high block is a real remaining obstacle. The result localizes a possible failure mechanism and excludes specific structured classes; it does not establish a uniform gap for the original full class.
(e) The exact-cap example only crosses the constant 2 and has a noncollapsing gap. Its role should remain diagnostic rather than being advertised as evidence for a general collapsing family.
(f) Full computational provenance exists for PDF production, not for a numerical or formal mathematical verification. Reproducible typesetting is not reproducible proof checking.
(g) Two small overfull-line warnings remain in the production log. They are visually harmless in the supplied pages; correcting them would be an editorial change to a new artifact, not a reason to rescore this one.

Potential mathematical errors considered and not sustained: reversed negative-power inequalities; forgetting N in u/r transfer; applying a Markov argument to an expanding degree multiplier; using quotient eigenvalues as the full spectrum; multiplying block bounds incorrectly when beta_b>=1; losing the constant Perron direction in the high-block comparison; extending fixed-margin localization to all modes; and interpreting a generic tail example as an admissible Doob counterexample. The PDF explicitly addresses or correctly avoids each of these. No further numerical computation was used to reach these findings.

2. Sections1-2: premise ranges and the finite exponent crossing

The source354 theorem gives exactly the stated normalized-density bounds and the finite F-iteration. The interpolation exponent is equivalent to the source's formula:
  (1/p-1/t_n)/(1-1/t_n)=(t_n-p)/(p(t_n-1)).
For 1<p<t_n it lies strictly between zero and one. The cited matching lemma requires its stationary moment exponent to exceed 1+rho; the candidate uses s=(1+rho)/(1-rho)>1+rho, so that requirement is satisfied. Its eta is strictly below one.

Independently checking the crossing, put p=1+rho. Then s-p=rho s and
  sq_n/(q_n+2s)>p iff q_n>2sp/(s-p)=2(1+rho)/rho=q_c.
At q_c,
  s q_c/(s+q_c)=2(1+rho)/(2-rho),
so its difference from one is 3rho/(2-rho). Consequently
  F(q_c)-q_c
  =1+3/[rho^3(2-rho)]-2(1+rho)/rho
  =[rho^4-4rho^2+3]/[rho^3(2-rho)]
  =(1-rho^2)(3-rho^2)/[rho^3(2-rho)]>0.
The denominator is positive throughout 0<rho<1. The known concave quadratic numerator for F(q)-q therefore places q_c strictly between its two roots. Since q_n increases to the upper root, some finite n has q_n>q_c. The choice depends only on rho, not on dimension or h, and is fixed before any asymptotic limit. This is stronger than merely selecting a dimension-dependent n with growing norm constants.

For the u-to-pi estimate, u=Nr gives
  u-pi=N(r-pi)+(N-1)pi.
The source inequalities imply 0<=1-N<=1-a^2<=e^2. Probability-space norm monotonicity gives ||pi||_{1+rho}<=||pi||_s<=B. Triangle inequality and N<=1 yield exactly
  ||u-pi||_{1+rho}<=(2e)^alpha(K_n+B)^{1-alpha}+B e^2=D_L.
The constants depend only on rho,b and the fixed n. Since alpha<1, the e^2 contribution decays faster in k than the leading e^alpha term. No pointwise bound on u or g is assumed here.

The stationary floor is genuinely used through source354's finite moment bound K_n. This is allowed by the candidate's original setting. Source341's floor-free theorem alone would not justify this step.

3. Section3: actual Doob spectrum and normalization

K is a stochastic kernel because P(h)/g=1. Reversibility for pi dmu follows from symmetry of P and pi(x)K(x,y)=h(x)P(x,y)h(y), with the uniform reference weights understood. The map f -> sqrt(pi) f is unitary from L2(pi dmu) to L2(mu). Direct cancellation gives its conjugated kernel
  sqrt(h(x)/g(x)) P(x,y) sqrt(h(y)/g(y)),
which is A=M_sqrt(u) P M_sqrt(u), exactly as claimed.

With X=M_sqrt(u)Q, A=XX* and T=X*X. They are finite square matrices with the same eigenvalues and multiplicities. In fact all are nonsingular because h,g are positive and every Walsh eigenvalue of Q is positive. Thus no nonzero-eigenvalue-only qualification is silently used. A and T are positive definite, hence positive semidefinite as stated. K's strictly positive entries give a simple Perron eigenvalue one; stochasticity bounds its spectral radius by one. Therefore the self-adjoint positive T has norm one.

The proposed Perron vector satisfies
  Tw=Q M_u Q(Qh)=Q(u Ph)=Qh=w,
  ||w||_2^2=E hPh=1.
It is positive and thus is the normalized Perron vector. No extraneous factor N is present. The candidate uses T=Q M_u Q for the actual K spectrum, whereas source341's weighting-normalized Q M_r Q differs by 1/N. This distinction is handled correctly.

4. Section4: the full image and cross block

For a Walsh character of degree j=|S|,
  (T-P)chi_S=rho^{j/2} Q[(u-1)chi_S].
The split into u-pi and pi-1 is exact. Ordinary Boolean hypercontractivity maps Q=P_sqrt(rho) from L^{1+rho} to L2 with norm at most one, since its noise parameter squared equals rho=(1+rho-1)/(2-1). Multiplication by chi_S preserves every Lp norm because |chi_S|=1. The first term is therefore bounded by D_L.

Exact matching through k, including the constant normalization E pi=1, means pi-1 has Walsh coefficients only at indices R with |R|>=k+1. The product rule chi_R chi_S=chi_(R triangle S) implies
  |R triangle S|>=|R|-|S|>=k+1-j.
Thus f_S=(pi-1)chi_S really has the required high-degree support. This is a support assertion, not an assumed Lp bound for a projection.

Set z=min(s,2). For all fixed 0<rho<1, z>1+rho. Taking t=sqrt(z-1) and eta=sqrt(rho/(z-1)) gives 0<t<=1, 0<eta<1 and eta t=sqrt(rho). The definition agrees with the earlier eta in both regimes: s>=2 gives eta=sqrt(rho), while s<2 gives eta=sqrt(rho/(s-1)). P_t maps L^z contractively to L2 by hypercontractivity; at z=2 it is simply the identity. As a Walsh multiplier it preserves high-degree support. P_eta on that support has L2 norm at most eta^{k+1-j}. Also ||f_S||_z=||pi-1||_z<=B+1. These facts establish (4.1) for every j<=d<=k, including j=0, without a 2d<=k condition.

For f in V_d^0, expand f=sum c_S chi_S. Orthonormality gives sum|c_S|^2=||f||_2^2. Triangle inequality followed by Cauchy--Schwarz in the finite coefficient sum gives
  ||(T-P)f||_2 <= ||f||_2 [sum_{1<=|S|<=d}||(T-P)chi_S||_2^2]^(1/2).
Each column is bounded by D_L+(B+1)eta^{k+1-d}, after dropping rho^{j/2}<=1. This proves (4.2) with the stated sqrt(M_d), not M_d. Self-adjointness gives ((T-P)J_d)*=J_d(T-P), proving (4.3).

These are full-image operator estimates. In particular Pi_>d T J_d=Pi_>d(T-P)J_d, since P preserves degree, so the low-to-high block obeys the same bound. The constant column is separately controlled by (4.1) at S empty. Combining it with (4.2) also controls the full domain Pi_<=d if desired. This is not merely another compression estimate.

5. Section5: growing window and escape

Under k_L/sqrt(L)->C>0, k_L->infinity and log L=o(k_L). For fixed allowed c>0, d_L=floor(c k_L/log(L+1)) eventually satisfies 1<=d_L<=k_L and d_L=o(k_L). The elementary count
  M_d<=d L^d
implies log M_d<=log d+d log L<=c k_L+o(k_L). This bound is adequate; sharper binomial entropy estimates are unnecessary.

D_L=O(eta^{alpha(k_L+1)}) with fixed constants. Multiplying by sqrt(M_d), the logarithmic exponents for the two terms of E_Ld are precisely
  [c/2-alpha log(1/eta)]k_L+o(k_L)
and
  [c/2-log(1/eta)]k_L+o(k_L).
The first coefficient is negative by the imposed strict c bound, and the second is negative because alpha<1. Thus E_(L,d_L) tends to zero exponentially in k_L. The condition is nonempty because alpha>0 and eta<1. No endpoint c equality or uniformity as rho approaches zero/one is claimed.

For a normalized T-eigenvector psi with eigenvalue lambda>=rho+zeta, commuting J_d and P gives
  (lambda I-P)J_d psi=J_d(T-P)psi.
The Walsh eigenvalues of P on the nonconstant V_d^0 are at most rho. Therefore the inverse on that subspace has norm at most 1/zeta, and (4.3) gives ||J_d psi||_2<=E_Ld/zeta. This is exactly where a compression-only estimate would have been insufficient: the right side takes the FULL psi as input.

For the constant component, psi perpendicular to w gives
  |<psi,1>|=|<psi,1-w>|<=||1-w||_2.
Since E w=a and both 1 and w have unit norm, the latter squared is 2(1-a). The bound a>=1/sqrt(1+e^2) and the elementary scalar inequality 2(1-(1+x)^(-1/2))<=x yield |<psi,1>|<=e. Constants are orthogonal to V_d^0, so adding squared norms gives (5.1) with no missing cross term or factor two. When zeta>1-rho the eigenvalue condition is empty, which is harmless. For a collapsing actual gap, lambda_2 tends to one and zeta=(1-rho)/2 applies eventually.

The representation warning is essential and correct. For a real normalized mean-zero K-eigenfunction f, P(hf)=lambda gf. Thus
  ||Q(hf)||_2^2=E hf P(hf)=lambda E pi f^2=lambda,
  T Q(hf)=Q u P(hf)=lambda Q(hf),
  <Q(hf),Qh>=E hf Ph=E pi f=0.
Dividing by sqrt(lambda)>0 gives the normalized non-Perron T eigenvector. For complex eigenfunctions the same proof uses conjugate inner products and |f|^2. The theorem constrains Walsh degrees of Q(hf)/sqrt(lambda), not of f; no unsupported raw-function escape conclusion is made.

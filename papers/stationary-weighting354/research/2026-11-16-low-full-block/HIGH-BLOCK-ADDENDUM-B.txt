Section4's column bound also holds for the empty character. Thus ||(T-P)R_d||<=eps_d and its adjoint obeys the same bound. Define the block diagonal operator
 T_d^diag=R_d P R_d + Z_d T Z_d.
Then
 ||T-T_d^diag||<=2eps_d,                                      (B.1)
 |lambda_2(K)-max{rho,||H_d||}|<=2eps_d.                       (B.2)
All eigenvalues are ordered with multiplicity; K and T have the same eigenvalues. The norm of H_d is its top eigenvalue because H_d is positive semidefinite.

Proof of (B.1): in the R_d/Z_d decomposition, T-T_d^diag has blocks [A B; B* 0]. A=R_d(T-P)R_d and B=R_d T Z_d=R_d(T-P)Z_d have norms at most eps_d. The off-diagonal matrix [0 B; B* 0] has norm ||B||. The triangle inequality therefore gives2eps_d. No norm of the uncontrolled high block is estimated by this step.
Proof of (B.2): ||T||=1, so H_d<=I. The low block R_d P R_d has largest eigenvalue1 and second eigenvalue rho (since d>=1 and L>=2). Hence the second eigenvalue of T_d^diag is exactly max{rho,||H_d||}, including if ||H_d||=1. For finite self-adjoint matrices the ordered-eigenvalue perturbation bound |lambda_j(A)-lambda_j(B)|<=||A-B|| follows immediately from the min-max variational principle; apply it with j=2 and use the exact spectral identity from A section3.

A concrete limitation of the currently available estimates
For a high-degree unit vector f, the usual Holder step is
 <f,Tf>=E u (Qf)^2 <=||u||_t ||Qf||_(2t')^2.
Every finite bootstrap exponent available in A obeys t=t_n<s. Thus 2t'>2s'=1+1/rho. Ordinary hypercontractivity for Q=P_sqrt(rho) from L2 into L^(2t') would require rho<=1/(2t'-1), which fails strictly. Consequently the direct Holder/hypercontractive route cannot turn this proved subcritical bound into a uniform high-block estimate. This identifies a failed admissibility condition for that route, not a counterexample to a different method or to the target theorem.

LOW-TO-FULL OPERATOR CONTROL FROM STATIONARY MATCHING
Frozen analytic candidate A, 16 November 2026. Internal grouped research; not a new paper or publication.

Context and scope
The single active question is stationary-doob-gap. This note combines the already published stationary-to-weighting subcritical transfer with an ordinary Boolean hypercontractive map into L2. Unlike the compression-only estimate in source341 section6, it bounds the entire image of a low-degree subspace, including its cross block. It proves quantitative escape of non-Perron eigenvectors above the Boolean noise edge in a precisely stated energy representation. It does NOT prove a uniform actual gap or the critical weighting moment. No new hypothesis is assumed to obtain the block bound. Global originality is not established. This candidate is authored by the involved parent Codex role; backend/version/effort are not independently attested. No numerical, symbolic, Lean or oracle execution was used.

1. Setting and previously proved inputs
Uniform probability mu on {-1,1}^L. Fix 0<rho<1 and 0<b<=1, P=P_rho, Q=P_sqrt(rho). Let h>0, g=Ph, E hPh=1, pi=hg>=b, s=(1+rho)/(1-rho), and E pi^s<=2. Assume pi has exact uniform labelled marginals through integer degree k. Write a=E h, v=1-a^2, u=h/g, N=E u, r=u/N, B=2^(1/s), and
 eta=[rho max{1,1/(s-1)}]^(1/2)<1, e=B eta^(k+1).
Walsh characters are chi_S, with P chi_S=rho^|S| chi_S. Every norm/projection below is in L2(mu) unless otherwise specified.

The published paper354 (Stationary-to-weighting moment transfer and spectral rigidity for Boolean Doob kernels, AIRR ARR-2026-4T35VQ6MZB8F99F4 v1, source8e452190b8f7d3f7382ba2e6c05d0f14fa9c3dc5, PDF SHA256 fbd2b9784d11740cef74c85f870dff27e4b97158210388cf3ae355eb4ffcbcf3) proves:
 a^2 >=1/(1+e^2), a^2<=N<=1, ||r-pi||_1<=2e,
 and, for the finite iteration below, ||r||_{t_n}<=K_n.
Specifically q_0=2rho^-2+rho^-3-1,
 F(q)=1+(s q/(s+q)-1)/rho^4, q_(n+1)=F(q_n),
 q_n increases to q_+=(1+rho)rho^-3[1+rho^2+sqrt(1+rho^2)],
 t_n=s q_n/(q_n+2s), K_n=B b^-3 (B/b)^(2n).
For 1<p<t_n, interpolation yields
 ||r-pi||_p <=(2e)^alpha (K_n+B)^(1-alpha),
 alpha=(1/p-1/t_n)/(1-1/t_n) in(0,1).
Here t_n<s. The candidate relies on these proved inputs, not on the later stronger G-iteration. The stationary floor remains a genuine assumption.

2. A finite iteration crosses the exponent needed for a full column
Put p=1+rho. Then s-p=rho s>0 and
 t_n>p iff q_n>q_c:=2(1+rho)/rho.
Direct substitution gives
 F(q_c)-q_c=(1-rho^2)(3-rho^2)/[rho^3(2-rho)]>0.
The numerator of F(q)-q is a concave quadratic with its two real roots q_-<q_+. Thus q_c lies strictly between these roots, in particular q_c<q_+. Since q_n increases to q_+, there is a finite n depending only on rho with t_n>1+rho. Fix such n ONCE, before L tends to infinity, and use its alpha and K_n throughout.
Define
 D_L=(2e)^alpha (K_n+B)^(1-alpha)+B e^2.
Then ||u-pi||_(1+rho)<=D_L.
Proof: u-pi=N(r-pi)+(N-1)pi, N<=1, 1-N<=v<=e^2, and ||pi||_(1+rho)<=B. In particular D_L decays exponentially in k, with constants depending only on rho,b and the chosen finite n.

3. The operator representing the actual Doob spectrum
Let T=Q M_u Q. It is positive semidefinite and self-adjoint, has norm1, and its unit Perron vector is w=Qh: T w=Q[u Ph]=Qh and ||w||_2^2=E hPh=1.
To verify that its spectrum is the ACTUAL Doob spectrum, define
 Kf=P(hf)/g on L2(pi dmu),
 A=M_sqrt(u) P M_sqrt(u), X=M_sqrt(u) Q.
Conjugating K by f -> sqrt(pi)f gives A. Also A=XX* and T=X*X, so their eigenvalues coincide, with multiplicities (all operators are finite square matrices). Positivity of K gives a simple Perron eigenvalue1. No replacement weighting kernel is being substituted for K.

4. Entire column estimate, not only compression
For 0<=|S|<=d<=k,
 ||(T-P)chi_S||_2 <=rho^(|S|/2) [D_L+(B+1)eta^(k+1-|S|)].       (4.1)
Proof: exactly
 (T-P)chi_S=rho^(|S|/2) Q[(u-pi)chi_S+(pi-1)chi_S].
Ordinary hypercontractivity gives Q:L^(1+rho)->L2 contractively; multiplying by a character does not change this norm. This bounds the first term by D_L.
For the second, f_S=(pi-1)chi_S has Walsh support strictly above degree k-|S|: multiplication by chi_S sends an index R to R symmetric-difference S, and |R|>k implies |R triangle S|>=k+1-|S|. Put z=min{s,2}>1+rho. Factor Q=P_eta P_t with t=sqrt(z-1), eta=sqrt(rho/(z-1)); this equals the eta in section1. P_t:L^z->L2 is a contraction (identity when z=2). Its image still has the same high-degree support. The P_eta multiplier on that support is at most eta^(k+1-|S|). Finally ||f_S||_z=||pi-1||_z<=B+1. This proves (4.1) without assuming boundedness of a Walsh projection on L^z.

Let V_d^0 be the span of all nonempty characters of degree<=d, and let J_d be its orthogonal projection. For 1<=d<=k put
 M_d=sum_{j=1}^d binomial(L,j),
 E_Ld=sqrt(M_d)[D_L+(B+1)eta^(k+1-d)].
Then
 ||(T-P)J_d||_(2->2)<=E_Ld,                                    (4.2)
 ||J_d(T-P)||_(2->2)<=E_Ld.                                    (4.3)
Indeed sum the column estimates with Cauchy--Schwarz and use rho^(|S|/2)<=1; (4.3) is the adjoint identity. These are full column/row operator bounds. No 2d<=k condition is required. In particular the low-to-high block is bounded by E_Ld, a conclusion absent from source341's compression-only argument.

5. Growing degree window and spectral escape
Assume k=k_L and k_L/sqrt(L)->C>0. Fix any constant
 0<c<2 alpha log(1/eta), d_L=floor(c k_L/log(L+1)).
For sufficiently large L, 1<=d_L<=k_L, and E_(L,d_L)->0 exponentially in k_L. For detail,
 log M_d <=log d+d log L<=c k_L+o(k_L), d_L=o(k_L),
 D_L=O(eta^(alpha(k_L+1)))
 with constants fixed independently of L. The two contributions to E_Ld are respectively
 O(exp[(c/2-alpha log(1/eta))k_L+o(k_L)]) and
 O(exp[(c/2-log(1/eta))k_L+o(k_L)]),
 both tending0 because alpha<1.

Fix zeta>0. If T psi=lambda psi, ||psi||_2=1, psi orthogonal to w, and lambda>=rho+zeta, then
 ||J_d psi||_2<=E_Ld/zeta,
 ||Pi_<=d psi||_2^2<= e^2+(E_Ld/zeta)^2.                       (5.1)
For the nonconstant part, commute P with J_d and write
 (lambda I-P)J_d psi=J_d(T-P)psi.
All eigenvalues of P on V_d^0 are at most rho, so inversion has norm at most1/zeta. For the constant part, <psi,w>=0 implies
 |<psi,1>|<=||1-w||_2=sqrt(2(1-a))<=e,
 using a>=1/sqrt(1+e^2) and 2(1-1/sqrt(1+x))<=x for x>=0.
This proves (5.1), including for the growing d_L above. Thus any non-Perron modes bounded a fixed distance above rho escape that entire growing low-degree window in this representation. If the actual gap collapses, its second eigenvalue tends1, and one can take zeta=(1-rho)/2 eventually.

Representation warning: for an actual normalized mean-zero K-eigenfunction f of eigenvalue lambda>0, the corresponding normalized T-eigenvector is
 psi=Q(hf)/sqrt(lambda).
Indeed ||Q(hf)||_2^2=E hf P(hf)=lambda E pi f^2=lambda, and TQ(hf)=lambda Q(hf); its inner product with Qh vanishes because E pi f=0. The theorem concerns Walsh degrees of this energy-represented psi, NOT automatically those of the raw function f.

6. What is resolved and what remains
The specific missing low-to-full block control left open by source341 is now derived from the original hypotheses via a finite published bootstrap. It is not inserted as an extra assumption, and no new critical-moment bound is claimed. The previous statement that source341 alone did not justify eigenvector escape remains correct; the additional L^(1+rho) bridge supplies what was missing.
The uncontrolled block is J_>d T J_>d. Its norm may in principle approach1. Vanishing cross blocks and escape of modes into that block do not bound its top non-Perron eigenvalue. Consequently the general stationary dimension-uniform gap and the critical weighting moment remain open.

2. DEFINITIONS AND CONSTANTS

For 0<rho<1, Boolean noise P=P_rho is symmetric, strictly entrywise positive, and has Walsh eigenvalues rho^j for j=0,...,L. It preserves uniform mu and has gap 1-rho. With positive h and g=Ph, the actual K(x,y)=P(x,y)h(y)/g(x) is stochastic and strictly positive. Normalization E hPh=1 makes nu(x)=mu(x)h(x)g(x) a probability distribution. Detailed balance gives
nu(x)K(x,y)=mu(x)P(x,y)h(x)h(y),
which is the edge law used throughout the proof.

Set s=(1+rho)/(1-rho)>1, s'=s/(s-1)>1 and B=2^(1/s)>1. For fixed 0<b<1 all displayed constants are finite and positive:
kappa=4B/b^2,
eta=(1-2^(-1/s))/(1+kappa),
t0=min(2^(-s),(eta^2/(4kappa))^s), epsilon0=b*t0,
gamma_bal=b^2(1-rho)*epsilon0^(s'-1)/(4*2^(s')*B^(2s')),
Phi0=min(eta/2,gamma_bal),
G=min(b^2(1-rho)/16,Phi0^2/4).

Since kappa>4 and 2^(-1/s)>1/2, the numerator of eta is in (0,1/2), and 0<eta<1/2. Also t0<=2^(-s)<1/2 and 0<b<1 imply 0<epsilon0<1/2. No dependence on L, level ratio, or shape of the level set is hidden in these constants. They may deteriorate when rho approaches an excluded endpoint or b approaches zero; no parameter-uniform lower bound across those limits is claimed.

For a constant positive h, normalization forces h=1 and K=P. Its gap 1-rho dominates G. Thus the subsequent proof may assume two distinct attained values, with both level sets nonempty. The finite-dimensional meaning of gap is 1 minus the largest nonconstant eigenvalue. K is also spectrally positive, as verified below, so no separate negative-eigenvalue obstruction is left unresolved.

3. DERIVED LOWER POTENTIAL AND LEVEL-RATIO/MASS CONTROL

Let h=M on A of mass alpha in (0,1), and h=m on its nonempty complement, with 0<m<M. Put a=E h. Since Q=P_sqrt(rho) preserves constants,
1=||Qh||_2^2>=(E Qh)^2=a^2,
and hence a<=1. No false claim that ||h||_2<=1 is used.

On Bset the floor reads m*g>=b. Integrating only on this set gives
m E(g1_Bset)>=b(1-alpha).
As E(g1_Bset)<=E g=a<=1, it follows that m>=b(1-alpha). Independently, g<=M everywhere, so the floor at any Bset point implies mM>=b. Since alpha*M<=E h<=1, 1/M>=alpha. Therefore
m>=b/M>=b*alpha.
Together these yield m>=b max(alpha,1-alpha)>=b/2. All divisions are by positive numbers and both inequality directions are correct. The use of a single constant low value on the entire complement is explicit and essential; this is not a lower bound proved here for arbitrary multilevel potentials.

Because g>=m, the high-level stationary density satisfies pi=Mg>=Mm on A. Its cap gives (Mm)^s alpha<=2, or Mm*alpha^(1/s)<=B. Dividing by m^2 and using the derived m>=b/2 yields
(M/m)*alpha^(1/s)<=B/m^2<=4B/b^2=kappa.
Thus the high-level ratio is allowed to diverge only with compensating small mass. The proof does not assume a uniform bound on M/m or infer one when alpha tends to zero.

4. RESTRICTION BOUNDS FROM ORDINARY HYPERCONTRACTIVITY

The hypercontractive use is Q=P_sqrt(rho): L^(1+rho)(mu) -> L^2(mu), exactly at the ordinary Boolean admissibility boundary. For a function supported on U, Holder gives
||f||_(1+rho)<=mu(U)^[1/(1+rho)-1/2]||f||_2
              =mu(U)^(1/(2s))||f||_2.
This direction is appropriate because 1+rho<2. For f and g supported on U and V,
|<f,Pg>|=|<Qf,Qg>|
 <=[mu(U)mu(V)]^(1/(2s))||f||_2||g||_2.
Taking the bilinear supremum establishes the operator norm bound in I3. For U=V it is mu(U)^(1/s). Empty sets correspond to zero operators; no division by their masses is required.

The square-root factorization P=Q^2 and support estimate concern the unweighted reference measure mu. They are not asserted directly for the weighted K. The later multiplier bounds correctly transfer them to its conjugated blocks.

5. CONJUGACY AND THE ACTUAL HIGH-LEVEL KILLED OPERATOR

The map T:f -> sqrt(pi)f is an isometry from L2(nu) to L2(mu). A direct entry calculation gives
T K T^(-1)=M_sqrt(h/g) P M_sqrt(h/g)=S.
Indeed the coefficient equals sqrt(h(x)/g(x))*P(x,y)*sqrt(h(y)/g(y)). Both T and T^(-1) are positive diagonal maps and commute with restriction to any subset. Thus the killed restrictions are unitarily conjugate in the appropriate restricted Hilbert spaces, not merely related by an uncontrolled similarity. P is positive definite and the multipliers are invertible, so S is positive definite; K is therefore positive definite in L2(nu), self-adjoint, and a Markov contraction.

On A put r=m/M and R=diag(P1_A)|A. The exact two-level identity is
g/M=r+(1-r)P1_A.
Consequently D=rI+(1-r)R is strictly positive and S_AA=D^(-1/2)P_A D^(-1/2).

For the restricted reference operator P_A, symmetry gives
<f,(R-P_A)f>=(1/2)sum_(x,y in A)mu(x)P(x,y)(f(x)-f(y))^2>=0.
There is no omitted boundary term because R is the internal row sum. Diagonal terms contribute zero. Hence P_A<=R. The independent I3 bound and positivity give P_A<=qI, q=alpha^(1/s) in (0,1).

Let Hden=r+(1-r)q. The proposed interpolation coefficient theta=r/Hden is in (0,1), since Hden>r and Hden>0. With lambda=q/Hden,
theta*q=lambda*r,
1-theta=lambda*(1-r).
A convex combination of the two valid Loewner inequalities therefore yields
P_A<=theta*qI+(1-theta)R=lambda D.
No commutativity between P_A and R is required for this convex combination or its congruence by D^(-1/2). Thus 0<=S_AA<=lambda I and ||S_AA||<=lambda<1. Algebra then gives
1-lambda=r(1-q)/(r+(1-r)q)
        =(1-q)/(1+(M/m-1)q).
This verifies I4 without a free or dimension-dependent potential parameter being dropped incorrectly.

If alpha<=1/2, then 1-q>=1-2^(-1/s), while
1+(M/m-1)q<=1+(M/m)q<=1+kappa.
Both numerator and denominator are positive. Their ratio is therefore at least eta. Every compression to U subset A remains PSD with norm at most 1-eta by the Rayleigh variational bound. This supplies an actual proved killed estimate, rather than inserting it as a premise. Crucially, it retains g=Ph in D; the surrogate H has no such representation.

6. SMALL CUTS MEETING EITHER OR BOTH LEVELS

Assume alpha<=1/2 and let 0<nu(C)<=epsilon0, with U=C intersect A and V=C intersect Bset. The floor implies mu(V)<=nu(C)/b<=t0. On Bset, g>=m implies u=m/g<=1; on A, u=M/g<=M/m.

The low block is M_sqrt(u) P_VV M_sqrt(u), with multiplier norm at most one. Therefore its norm is at most ||P_VV||<=mu(V)^(1/s)<=t0^(1/s)<=1/2. The entrywise-positive quadratic-form explanation in the candidate is also valid, including for signed f after replacing it by |f|. Positivity of the block ensures its operator norm is controlled by this upper quadratic bound.

For the cross block, direct multiplication and I3 give
||S_UV||<=sqrt(M/m)[mu(U)mu(V)]^(1/(2s))
 <=sqrt((M/m)alpha^(1/s))*mu(V)^(1/(2s))
 <=sqrt(kappa)*t0^(1/(2s))<=eta/2.
The last step uses t0<=(eta^2/(4kappa))^s, not merely its other bound. This is where the cap compensates for arbitrarily large M/m. No smallness assumption on mu(U) beyond U subset A is needed.

In the orthogonal decomposition into U and V, the block-diagonal part has norm at most max(1-eta,1/2)=1-eta. For any operator T, the self-adjoint off-diagonal block with T and T* has norm ||T||, since its square has diagonal blocks TT* and T*T. Hence the cross contribution is eta/2, not an unaccounted eta or dimension factor. The triangle inequality proves ||S_CC||<=1-eta/2. The same argument applies if one block is empty.

For the vector v_C=sqrt(pi)1_C, ||v_C||_mu^2=nu(C). Conjugacy and restriction imply
<1_C,K1_C>_nu=<v_C,S_CC v_C>_mu<=(1-eta/2)nu(C).
Using the exact stationary edge law gives
F_h(C)=<1_C,(I-K)1_C>_nu>=eta*nu(C)/2.
The definition of F_h(C) uses only the outward orientation; the two orientations in the Dirichlet form with its factor 1/2 yield exactly that flow. There is no factor-of-two error in I8.

7. REMAINING CUTS AND COMPLETE CONDUCTANCE COVERAGE

The pointwise lower bound h>=m implies h(x)h(y)>=m^2 on every reference edge. Therefore D_K(f)>=m^2 D_P(f) for all real f, using the actual normalized edge law. For an indicator, the unweighted P gap gives
F_h(C)>=m^2(1-rho)mu(C)mu(C^c).

The cap gives ||pi||_s<=B and hence nu(E)<=B mu(E)^(1/s') for every E. This direction of Holder supplies lower, not upper, bounds on reference masses when stationary masses are known. If epsilon0<=nu(C)<=1/2, then
mu(C)>=(nu(C)/B)^(s'),
mu(C^c)>=((1-nu(C))/B)^(s')>=(1/(2B))^(s').
Combining with m^2>=b^2/4 and dividing by positive nu(C) gives
F_h(C)/nu(C)>=b^2(1-rho)*nu(C)^(s'-1)/(4*2^(s')*B^(2s'))
 >=gamma_bal,
because s'-1>0 and nu(C)>=epsilon0. The exponent, denominator, and inequality directions match I9 exactly.

The two ranges (0,epsilon0] and [epsilon0,1/2] cover every cut relevant to conductance, with harmless overlap at the threshold. This includes arbitrary cuts straddling the two levels, not only A, its complement, or sets contained within a single level. Thus Phi(K)>=min(eta/2,gamma_bal)=Phi0 for alpha<=1/2. It is this all-cut statement that makes the result a full-gap theorem.

8. LAZY CHEEGER CONSTANT AND THE LARGE-HIGH-SET CASE

The classical lazy reversible Cheeger theorem cited in the candidate states that the gap is at least conductance squared divided by two. For Kbar=(I+K)/2, reversibility and stationarity persist, all holding probabilities are at least 1/2, every off-diagonal flow is halved, and the nontrivial eigenvalues are (1+lambda_i(K))/2. Therefore
Phi(Kbar)=Phi(K)/2, gap(Kbar)=gap(K)/2.
Applying the cited theorem gives
gap(K)/2>=Phi(K)^2/8,
or gap(K)>=Phi(K)^2/4>=Phi0^2/4.
The factor 1/4 is conservative but correct. No unproved nonlazy version is needed. Spectral positivity already established for K means this controls the full nonconstant spectrum as well as the usual Poincare gap.

If alpha>1/2, the separate comparison is valid: M alpha<=1 implies M<2, and m>=b/2. As g<=M and h<=M, pi<=M^2. For any f, choosing its mu-mean as a test centering value in the weighted variance gives
Var_nu(f)<=E_nu[(f-E_mu f)^2]<=M^2 Var_mu(f).
The edge comparison gives D_K(f)>=m^2(1-rho)Var_mu(f). Combining yields the Rayleigh lower bound
gap(K)>=(m/M)^2(1-rho)>=b^2(1-rho)/16.
This argument controls every nonconstant f, not just indicators. Alpha=1/2 belongs to the preceding branch. Constant potentials were handled separately. These cases exhaust all admissible h and every L>=1, proving the displayed minimum G.

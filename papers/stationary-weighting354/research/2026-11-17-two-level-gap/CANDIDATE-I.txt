1. Statement and exact scope

Let mu be uniform on {-1,1}^L, L>=1; fix 0<rho<1 and 0<b<1.
Let P=P_rho and let h>0 take at most two values. Normalize E_mu hPh=1, put g=Ph, pi=hg, nu=pi mu, and define the ACTUAL kernel
K(x,y)=P(x,y)h(y)/g(x).
Assume pi>=b and E_mu pi^s<=2, where
s=(1+rho)/(1-rho), s'=s/(s-1), B=2^(1/s).
No exact marginal matching is assumed.

Claim I: there is an explicit G(rho,b)>0, independent of L, the two values of h, and the geometry or mass of their level sets, such that gap(K)>=G(rho,b).

One deliberately conservative constant is as follows:
kappa=4B/b^2;
eta=(1-2^(-1/s))/(1+kappa);
t0=min{2^(-s), [eta^2/(4kappa)]^s};
epsilon0=b*t0;
gamma_bal=[b^2(1-rho)/(4*2^(s')*B^(2s'))]*epsilon0^(s'-1);
Phi0=min{eta/2,gamma_bal};
G(rho,b)=min{b^2(1-rho)/16, Phi0^2/4}.

All are strictly positive for fixed rho,b. Since s>1, kappa>4 and 2^(-1/s)>1/2, we have 0<eta<1/2 and 0<epsilon0<1/2. No optimality is claimed.

This is a full finite-chain spectral-gap theorem for the RESTRICTED two-level class, not merely quotient, bounded-observable, or killed-set control. The arbitrary-positive-potential question remains open. Two-level structure is an explicit restriction, not a proved replacement for general h. No hypothesis is eliminated from the unrestricted question. The theorem needs no matching within its stated restricted class.

2. A derived lower bound on the potential

The constant potential normalizes to h=1 and gives K=P, with gap1-rho, so assume two distinct values m<M.
Let A={h=M}, alpha=mu(A) in(0,1), and let its complement be denoted Bset.
Write a=E h=E g. Boolean positivity gives
1=<h,Ph>=||P_sqrt(rho)h||_2^2 >=(E h)^2,
so a<=1.

Integrate the floor on Bset, where h=m:
m E(g 1_Bset)>=b(1-alpha).
Since E(g 1_Bset)<=E g=a<=1, this gives m>=b(1-alpha).
Also g<=M everywhere and the floor at any point of Bset gives mM>=b.
Since M alpha<=E h<=1, 1/M>=alpha, hence m>=b/M>=b alpha.
Therefore
m>=b max(alpha,1-alpha)>=b/2.                    (I1)

This is stronger than the previously recorded g>=b/2 observation for this class, and is derived from normalization, floor and two-level structure alone.
Because g>=m, on A one has pi=M g>=Mm. The cap yields
(Mm)^s alpha<=E pi^s<=2,
hence
(M/m)*alpha^(1/s)<=B/m^2<=4B/b^2=kappa.          (I2)
No bound on M/m itself is assumed; alpha may tend to zero.

3. Hypercontractive restriction bounds

All operators in this section act on L2(mu). For Q=P_sqrt(rho), ordinary Boolean hypercontractivity gives
||Qf||_2<=||f||_(1+rho).
For f supported on U,
||f||_(1+rho)<=mu(U)^(1/(1+rho)-1/2)||f||_2
                 =mu(U)^(1/(2s))||f||_2.
Consequently, for any U,V and f,g supported there,
|<f,Pg>|=|<Qf,Qg>|
 <=[mu(U)mu(V)]^(1/(2s))||f||_2||g||_2.
In particular
||1_U P1_V||_(2->2)<=[mu(U)mu(V)]^(1/(2s)),
and ||1_U P1_U||<=mu(U)^(1/s).                 (I3)

4. Actual killed operator on the high level

Set u=h/g and S=M_sqrt(u) P M_sqrt(u).
The isometry f->sqrt(pi)f from L2(nu) to L2(mu) conjugates K to S.
It commutes with restriction to coordinate sets, so killed compressions of K and S have identical spectra and norms. P and S are positive definite, and K is reversible and Markov.

Write P_A=1_A P1_A restricted to A and R=diag(P1_A)|A.
Put r=m/M in(0,1), q=alpha^(1/s).
The internal Dirichlet form gives P_A<=R in Loewner order:
<f,(R-P_A)f>=(1/2) sum_(x,y in A)mu(x)P(x,y)(f(x)-f(y))^2>=0.
By (I3), P_A<=q I.
The actual two-level relation is
g/M = r+(1-r)P1_A on A.
Thus S_AA=D^(-1/2)P_A D^(-1/2), D=rI+(1-r)R.

Convexly combine P_A<=q I and P_A<=R.
With lambda=q/[r+(1-r)q] and theta=r/[r+(1-r)q],
theta*q=lambda*r and 1-theta=lambda*(1-r).
Therefore P_A<=theta*q I+(1-theta)R=lambda D.
It follows that
||S_AA||<= q/[r+(1-r)q],
1-||S_AA|| >= (1-q)/[1+(M/m-1)q].             (I4)

If alpha<=1/2, (I2) and q<=2^(-1/s) give
||S_AA||<=1-eta.                              (I5)
Every compression S_UU with U subset A satisfies the same bound.
This estimate uses the actual denominator Ph. It was not available for the surrogate slow-vertex kernel H, which has no Doob potential.

5. Small stationary cuts, including cuts meeting BOTH levels

Continue in the case alpha<=1/2. For any cut C with 0<nu(C)<=epsilon0, write U=C cap A and V=C cap Bset.
The floor gives mu(V)<=nu(C)/b<=t0.
On Bset, u=m/g<=1; everywhere on A, u=M/g<=M/m.

Using (I3), the low-block compression satisfies
||S_VV||<=mu(V)^(1/s)<=t0^(1/s)<=1/2.
For completeness, entrywise positivity and u<=1 imply
<f,S_VV f><=<|f|,P_VV|f|><=mu(V)^(1/s)||f||_2^2;
S_VV is PSD, so this bounds its operator norm.

The cross block obeys
||S_UV||<=sqrt(M/m)[mu(U)mu(V)]^(1/(2s))
         <=sqrt(kappa)*mu(V)^(1/(2s))
         <=eta/2,                            (I6)
where (I2) and the definition of t0 are used.
Its transpose has the same norm. An off-diagonal self-adjoint block matrix with entries T,T* has norm||T||. Combining (I5)-(I6), the diagonal-block maximum is at most1-eta (because eta<1/2), hence
||S_CC||<=1-eta/2.                            (I7)
Empty U or V causes no difficulty: the corresponding block is zero.

For a stationary cut flow
F_h(C)=sum_(x in C,y outside C)mu(x)P(x,y)h(x)h(y),
the actual Dirichlet form gives
F_h(C)=<1_C,(I-K)1_C>_nu.
Restriction and the conjugacy above imply
F_h(C)>= (eta/2)nu(C) for 0<nu(C)<=epsilon0.   (I8)

6. Remaining cuts from the floor and moment cap

For all real f, h>=m and positive edge weights give
D_K(f)>=m^2 D_P(f).
For indicators, P's gap yields
F_h(C)>=m^2(1-rho)mu(C)mu(C^c).

The stationary cap and Holder give nu(E)<=B mu(E)^(1/s') for every E.
If epsilon0<=nu(C)<=1/2, then
mu(C)>=(nu(C)/B)^(s'),
mu(C^c)>=(1/(2B))^(s').
Using (I1) and dividing the cut-flow bound by nu(C),
F_h(C)/nu(C)
 >=[b^2(1-rho)/(4*2^(s')*B^(2s'))]*nu(C)^(s'-1)
 >=gamma_bal.                               (I9)

Equations (I8)-(I9) prove the UNIFORM conductance estimate
Phi(K):=inf_(0<nu(C)<=1/2) F_h(C)/nu(C) >=Phi0.
Thus ALL cuts are covered, not merely the high-level set or cuts contained in it.

Apply the classical lazy-chain Cheeger inequality to Kbar=(I+K)/2.
This chain is lazy and reversible, Phi(Kbar)=Phi(K)/2 and gap(Kbar)=gap(K)/2.
The bound gap(Kbar)>=Phi(Kbar)^2/2 yields
gap(K)>=Phi(K)^2/4>=Phi0^2/4.                 (I10)
Using lazification here intentionally sacrifices a factor to match exactly the cited lazy theorem's assumptions. No unproved nonlazy extension is required.

7. The other level-set size case

If alpha>1/2, M alpha<=1 implies M<2, whereas m>=b/2.
Here pi=hg<=M^2. For every f, centering by E_mu f gives
Var_nu(f)<=M^2 Var_mu(f),
while D_K(f)>=m^2(1-rho)Var_mu(f).
Therefore
gap(K)>=(m/M)^2(1-rho)>=b^2(1-rho)/16.
Together with (I10), this proves the stated G in every case and every L.
For constant h, gap1-rho also dominates G.

8. Interpretation, falsifier and relation to H

The original arbitrary-potential stationary-gap problem is not solved.
The potential is restricted to at most two values, while their ratio and the geometry of their level sets are unrestricted and may vary with dimension.
The cap proves (M/m)alpha^(1/s)<=kappa; it does not prove a uniform pointwise ratio.
The high-level killed bound, the small mixed-cut argument and the remaining-cut comparison collectively prove a full gap for this class.
This excludes all collapsing-gap mechanisms based on a single constant-height plateau above a constant background, subject to the original floor/cap, without matching.

H had proved that abstract E/F/G bounds and low-window noise agreement alone cannot close a full gap. The present proof instead retains g=m+(M-m)P1_A pointwise and the actual edge h(x)h(y), which H cannot satisfy.
Neither old abstract convergence nor an assumed killed estimate is substituted for those identities.
The general case with multiple or continuously varying positive levels remains open.

A falsifier is a finite two-level positive h meeting the exact normalized floor/cap for which the displayed G bound fails, or a gap in the Loewner interpolation, mixed-cut estimate, normalization or Cheeger application.

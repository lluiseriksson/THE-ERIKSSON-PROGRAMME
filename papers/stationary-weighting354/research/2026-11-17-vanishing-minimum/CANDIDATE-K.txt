1. Statement and exact scope
Fix rho in (0,1), b in (0,1), and any finite s>1. For each sufficiently large integer L there is a strictly positive three-valued function h_L on {-1,1}^L such that, with uniform probability mu, Boolean noise P=P_rho, and pi_L=h_L P h_L,
  E_mu h_L P h_L=1,
  pi_L >= b pointwise,
  ||pi_L-1||_{L^s(mu)} -> 0,
  E_mu pi_L^s -> 1,
  ||h_L-1||_{L^{2s}(mu)} -> 0,
  min h_L -> 0, and (P h_L)(x_0) -> infinity.
Here x_0=(1,...,1). In particular E pi_L^s <= 2 eventually, including at the critical s=(1+rho)/(1-rho). Any finitely many missing dimensions can be filled with h_L=1; the three-valued assertion is only for sufficiently large L.
These are actual positive-potential noise Doob kernels
  K_L(x,y)=P(x,y) h_L(y)/(P h_L)(x),
with stationary probability nu_L=pi_L mu. There is NO assertion of exact stationary marginal matching or of a collapsing spectral gap.

Consequently the original stationary floor and critical cap ALONE do not imply a dimension-uniform positive minimum of the energy-normalized potential. This refutes precisely the bridge omitting exact matching. It does not refute a bridge using exact matching, the original full-gap conjecture, or theorem J's sufficient condition.

2. A rare set seen with high probability from one point
Let p=(1-rho)/2 and q=(p+1/2)/2. Then 0<p<q<1/2.
Let D(x_0,y) be Hamming distance and define
  A_L={y:1 <= D(x_0,y) <= q L},
  alpha_L=mu(A_L), delta_L=2^{-L},
  r_L=P(x_0,A_L), u_L=P(x_0,x_0)=(1-p)^L.
For large L, A_L is nonempty and its complement has points other than x_0, so 0<alpha_L<1.
Under uniform mu, D has Binomial(L,1/2) distribution. Its variance L/4 and Chebyshev's inequality give
  alpha_L <= 1/[4L(1/2-q)^2] -> 0.
Under P(x_0,.), D has Binomial(L,p) distribution. Thus
  1-r_L <= u_L + p(1-p)/[L(q-p)^2] -> 0.
Integer rounding causes no issue: A_L uses the real threshold qL, and its complement consists of D=0 or D>qL. Finally, for every y,
  P(y,x_0)=p^{D(y,x_0)}(1-p)^{L-D(y,x_0)} <= u_L,
since p<1/2. No concentration theorem stronger than Chebyshev is needed.

3. Three levels and exact pointwise balance
Suppress L in the following formulas. Put
  M=alpha^{-1/(4s)} > 1, beta=(1+b)/2.
Define t as the unique root in (0,1) of
  t [u t + M r + 1-u-r] = beta.                 (K1)
The expression on the left is continuous and strictly increasing for t>=0: its linear coefficient M r+1-u-r is positive for large L, and all its summands are nonnegative. Its value at 0 is 0 and at 1 is 1+(M-1)r>1>beta. Hence the claimed unique t exists. Also
  0<t <= beta/(M r) -> 0.
Let H=t at x_0, H=M on A_L, and H=1 elsewhere. Then
  H(x_0)(PH)(x_0)=beta                         (K2)
exactly. Set Z=E H PH and h=H/sqrt(Z). Thus E hPh=1 and pi=HPH/Z exactly.

4. Normalization tends to one
The pointwise bounds 1-1_{x_0} <= H <= 1+M 1_A and positivity of P imply
  1-2 delta+delta u <= Z
    <= 1+2M alpha+M^2 <1_A,P1_A>
    <= 1+2M alpha+M^2 alpha.
The last inequality uses 0<=P1_A<=1. Since s>1,
  M alpha=alpha^{1-1/(4s)} -> 0,
  M^2 alpha=alpha^{1-1/(2s)} -> 0.
Therefore Z -> 1, with Z>0 always.

5. Uniform stationary floor
For every y != x_0, H(y)>=1 and
  (PH)(y) >= P(1-1_{x_0})(y)
           =1-P(y,x_0) >=1-u.
It follows that H(y)(PH)(y)>=1-u outside x_0, whereas (K2) holds at x_0. Hence
  pi >= min(beta,1-u)/Z.
As beta>b and Z->1, u->0, this is at least b in all sufficiently large dimensions.

6. Strong near-uniformity of the stationary density
Let R=HPH. From H<=1+M1_A and positivity of P,
  R <= 1+M1_A+M P1_A+M^2 1_A P1_A.
Markov contraction on L^s(mu) follows from Jensen and invariance of mu, giving ||P1_A||_s<=alpha^{1/s}. Also ||1_A P1_A||_s<=alpha^{1/s}. Therefore
  ||(R-1)_+||_s <= 2M alpha^{1/s}+M^2 alpha^{1/s}
     =2 alpha^{3/(4s)}+alpha^{1/(2s)} -> 0.     (K3)
The lower bound in Section 5 gives, outside x_0, R>=1-u; at x_0, R=beta. Consequently
  ||(1-R)_+||_s <= u+(1-beta) delta^{1/s} -> 0. (K4)
By (K3)-(K4), ||R-1||_s ->0. Since Z->1,
  ||pi-1||_s <= Z^{-1}||R-1||_s+|Z^{-1}-1| ->0.
It follows that ||pi||_s->1, and E pi^s=||pi||_s^s->1. This proves a cap strictly stronger asymptotically than the fixed bound 2, without any claim of exact equality of marginal probabilities.

7. Potential closeness, vanishing minimum and high denominator
Because H differs from 1 only on A and at x_0,
  ||H-1||_{2s}^{2s}
    =alpha(M-1)^{2s}+delta(1-t)^{2s}
    <=alpha M^{2s}+delta
    =alpha^{1/2}+delta ->0.
Normalization Z->1 yields ||h-1||_{2s}->0 by the triangle inequality. Nevertheless, min h=t/sqrt(Z)->0, whereas from (K1)
  (Ph)(x_0)=beta/[t sqrt(Z)] -> infinity.
In particular h(x_0)(Ph)(x_0)=beta/Z->beta, fully consistent with the stationary floor. Small L can be filled with h=1 without changing any limit or eventual three-level property.

8. Actual reversibility and the exact matching boundary
P has strictly positive entries and P1=1, so K is strictly positive and stochastic. Directly,
  nu(x)K(x,y)=mu(x)P(x,y)h(x)h(y)
            =nu(y)K(y,x),
using reversibility of P with respect to uniform mu. Thus no abstract surrogate chain is being substituted.
For any bounded test f with |f|<=1,
  |E_nu f-E_mu f| <= ||pi-1||_1 <= ||pi-1||_s ->0.
This bound is uniform over such f, hence also over all marginal events. It proves APPROXIMATE matching, not the exact labelled matching required by the original question. No argument here upgrades an asymptotic error to zero. No spectral-gap estimate is inferred from min h->0: theorem J supplies necessity of vanishing minima for gap collapse, not its sufficiency.

9. Sources, novelty, verification and review context

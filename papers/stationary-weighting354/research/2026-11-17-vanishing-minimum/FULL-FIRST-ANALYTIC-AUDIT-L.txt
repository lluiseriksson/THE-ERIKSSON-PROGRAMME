3. FINITE SYMMETRIC SCALING: EXISTENCE, COERCIVITY, UNIQUENESS

Each exponential of a linear functional is convex. In particular the diagonal terms have coefficients mu(i)P_ii/2>0 and exponent 2v_i. Their Hessian is diagonal with entries 2 mu(i)P_ii exp(2v_i)>0. The remaining terms have positive semidefinite Hessians. Therefore the total Hessian is positive definite at every finite v, and F_w is strictly convex. This argument does not incorrectly assume positive semidefiniteness of the matrix P; entry positivity, including its diagonal, suffices in this section.

For m=max_i v_i and d=min_i mu(i)P_ii/2>0, the diagonal term at a maximizing index gives at least d exp(2m). Since sum a_i=1 and v_i<=m, the linear part is at least -m. Thus F_w(v)>=d exp(2m)-m. To control coordinates tending to minus infinity while another coordinate stays bounded above, use that all v_j<=M on a sublevel. For any i,

4. Critical hypercontractive sandwich, M1

Work in L2(mu) for uniform probability on the cube, using its weighted inner product. P_rho and Q=P_sqrt(rho) are real self-adjoint, and their Walsh eigenvalues show Q^2=P_rho. With s as stated,
s'=s/(s-1)=(1+rho)/(2rho),
2s'=(1+rho)/rho=1+1/rho,
2s'-1=1/rho.
Thus the 2-to-2s' hypercontractive noise threshold is exactly sqrt((2-1)/(2s'-1))=sqrt(rho). Equality at the threshold is allowed. As 0<rho<1, the output exponent is finite and greater than 2.

For arbitrary real f,g,v, self-adjointness gives
<f,Q M_v Qg>=E_mu[v(Qf)(Qg)].
The three Holder reciprocals add to one:
1/s+1/(2s')+1/(2s')=1.
Taking absolute values, applying Holder and the two hypercontractive bounds yields
|<f,Q M_v Qg>|<=||v||_s ||Qf||_(2s') ||Qg||_(2s')
                    <=||v||_s ||f||_2 ||g||_2.
The bilinear supremum over unit f,g is the operator norm, so M1 follows. The sign of v is immaterial. There is no missing dimension factor: every norm uses the same probability measure.

I retrieved the primary reference Ryan O'Donnell, Analysis of Boolean Functions, May 2021 arXiv edition, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.10386, section 10.1, printed page 284 (PDF page index 283). Its Hypercontractivity Theorem gives the stated exponent/noise condition, including equality. This source supports the classical input; the application to the present ratio is demonstrated above. This is not a new hypercontractivity theorem or an inference from stationary moments alone.

5. Actual-chain spectral identity and eigenvalue perturbation, M2–M3

Let H>0, G=PH>0, Z=E_mu HG>0 and U=H/G. The actual kernel K_H(x,y)=P(x,y)H(y)/G(x) is stochastic, strictly positive and reversible under density HG/Z with respect to mu. The unitary change from L2((HG/Z)mu) to L2(mu) is multiplication by sqrt(HG/Z). Its conjugated kernel is
sqrt(H(x)/G(x)) P(x,y) sqrt(H(y)/G(y)),
so its self-adjoint representative is exactly S=M_sqrt(U) P M_sqrt(U). The normalization factor Z cancels. This computation uses the actual stationary law, not a surrogate law.

Set X=M_sqrt(U)Q. Its adjoint is Q M_sqrt(U), giving S=XX* and T=X*X=Q M_U Q. Multiplication by sqrt(U) is invertible since all entries are positive; Q is invertible since its finitely many Walsh eigenvalues rho^(|S|/2) are strictly positive. Therefore X is invertible and X^(-1)SX=X*X=T. The matrices share the full spectrum with multiplicity. No bound on the condition number of X is required: perturbation is applied to the self-adjoint T and P, not to an ill-conditioned similarity transform of a perturbation.

T-P=Q M_(U-1) Q, and M1 proves ||T-P||<=||U-1||_s. The min-max characterization then gives the ordered-eigenvalue estimate for every index. In particular, order eigenvalues decreasingly. The actual stochastic irreducible chain has top eigenvalue 1, and positivity of S makes every eigenvalue positive. The noise chain has second eigenvalue rho for L>=1, even though its multiplicity is L. Consequently
|[1-lambda_2(K_H)]-(1-rho)|<=||U-1||_s.
This is the usual reversible Poincare gap; the absolute-spectrum gap coincides here because there are no negative eigenvalues. Multiplicity at rho does not defeat min-max, and changing stationary mean-zero subspaces never enters the comparison. M2 and M3 are correct.

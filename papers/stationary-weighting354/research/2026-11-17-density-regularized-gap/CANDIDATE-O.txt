1. QUESTION, RESULT AND SCOPE
The single active question remains the original stationary-doob-gap problem.
N proved that smoothing the potential can lose exact stationary matching.
Here we smooth the stationary density and reconstruct its actual potential.
We establish the regularized side of the pending spectral comparison: a
sufficiently strong fixed noise smoothing gives full actual spectral
convergence to Boolean noise for EVERY input satisfying the original floor,
critical cap and matching, with no code assumption or potential minimum.
This does not compare the regularized gap back to the original gap. The
regularization changes the chain, and the general original question stays
open. A simple admissible parity family below also disproves the premise
that fixed density smoothing is uniformly a small L^s perturbation.
Group this result with N and the existing L/M manuscript, not a new paper.

2. STATEMENT WITH EXPLICIT FINITE CONSTANTS
Fix 0<rho<1 and 0<b<1. Write P=P_rho, s=(1+rho)/(1-rho), B=2^(1/s),
and use uniform probability mu on {-1,1}^L. For L>=1 and integer 0<=k<=L,
let pi be a positive mean-one density such that
  pi>=b, E pi^s<=2, E pi chi_S=0 for 1<=|S|<=k.               (O1)
In particular pi may be the actual density hPh of any energy-normalized
positive potential from the original question. The conclusion does not
depend on choosing a special input family.

Define constants depending only on rho:
  q0=2rho^(-2)+rho^(-3)-1 >2, Qstar=8s,
  sigma=max{4s, 2Qstar^2/[(1-rho^4)(q0-1)]},
  F_sigma(q)=1+[sigma*q/(sigma+q)-1]/rho^4.
Starting at q0, iterate q_(n+1)=F_sigma(q_n) only until the first n=n_*
with q_n>=Qstar, taking n_*=0 if q0>=Qstar. Section4 proves n_* finite;
it is fixed before L,k,pi or the regularization parameter are chosen.
Set
  A=b^(-1)(B/b)^(n_*), K=b^(-1) B A^2,
  theta_in=min{1,sqrt(s-1)}, theta_out=min{1,1/sqrt(s-1)},
  tau0=(1/2)min{theta_in*theta_out,sqrt((s-1)/(sigma-1))}>0,
  gamma=1/(2s-1), e_k=B rho^((k+1)/2),
  E_k=(2e_k)^gamma (K+B)^(1-gamma)
                   +(B+1)2^(-(k+1))+e_k^2.                (O2)

THEOREM O. For every pi in(O1) and every 0<=tau<=tau0, put w=P_tau pi.
There is a unique positive u with uPu=w, hence E uPu=1. The reconstructed
chain K_u(x,y)=P(x,y)u(y)/(Pu)(x) retains every assumption(O1), with
stationary density w. Its ordered eigenvalues satisfy
  |lambda_j(K_u)-lambda_j(P)| <= E_k,  1<=j<=2^L.           (O3)
In particular
  |gap(K_u)-(1-rho)| <= E_k.                               (O4)
E_k tends to zero exponentially in k, with constants depending only on
rho,b, uniformly in L,pi and tau in the specified interval. For the original
k_L=min{L,floor(C sqrt(L))}, C>0, this is exponential in sqrt(L).
No normalized lower bound for u or Pu is assumed or needed.

3. ACTUAL RECONSTRUCTION AND MOMENT GAIN
Stochasticity and invariance give E w=1 and w>=b. Jensen gives E w^s<=2.
The noise multiplier preserves every zero low-degree coefficient, so w
has the same exact labelled marginals. The positive symmetric scaling
lemma inversestationary in the preserved grouped source supplies a unique
positive u with uPu=w. Its proof uses a strictly convex coercive functional
in log u; thus reconstruction is a theorem, not an extra existence premise.
Since the kernel is strictly positive and symmetric, that lemma applies
in every finite dimension. We do not use its dimension-dependent inverse
norm estimate in the proof of O.

Ordinary Boolean hypercontractivity gives
  ||w||_sigma=||P_tau pi||_sigma <=||pi||_s<=B,              (O5)
because tau^2<=(s-1)/(sigma-1). The stronger stationary moment is DERIVED
from the original s cap and the specified regularization, not assumed.
Its bound is ||w||_sigma<=B, not the false claim E w^sigma<=2.

4. FINITE INVERSE-MOMENT BOOTSTRAP ABOVE THE CRITICAL REQUIREMENT
Write g=Pu and Q=P_sqrt(rho). Energy normalization gives ||Qu||_2=1.
Ordinary hypercontractivity yields ||g||_R<=1, R=1+rho^(-1), since
g=Q(Qu). From ug=w>=b, u^(-1)<=g/b. In negative-mean notation this says
||u||_(-R)>=b. Reverse Boolean hypercontractivity with input exponent -R,
output exponent -q0 and noise rho applies at
  rho^2=(1+R)/(1+q0),
and gives ||g^(-1)||_(q0)<=b^(-1). All negative powers are finite since
u and g are strictly positive on the finite cube.

The same stationary floor also gives, by positivity and rowwise Jensen,
  g>=b P(1/g)>=b/(Pg), hence 1/g<=Pg/b.                    (O6)
If ||g^(-1)||_q<=A_q and q>sigma/(sigma-1), Holder applied to u=w/g
gives ||u||_p<=B A_q, where p=sigma*q/(sigma+q)>1. Since Pg=P_(rho^2)u,
ordinary hypercontractivity gives
  ||g^(-1)||_(F_sigma(q)) <=(B/b) A_q.                     (O7)
This rederives the bootstrap for the new exponent sigma; it does not reuse
the old fixed-point formula at the original critical exponent s.

It remains to prove that the chosen finite iteration reaches Qstar.
If q0>=Qstar there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, whenever q0<=q<Qstar,
  F_sigma(q)-q
    =[(1-rho^4)(q-1)-q^2/(sigma+q)]/rho^4
    >=(1-rho^4)(q0-1)/(2rho^4)=:d>0,                     (O8)
using sigma>=2Qstar^2/[(1-rho^4)(q0-1)]. All such q exceed2, whereas
sigma>=4s>4 implies sigma/(sigma-1)<4/3. Thus every step before the
first crossing meets the p>1 condition in(O7). If no crossing occurred,
q_n>=q0+n d would eventually exceed Qstar, a contradiction. In fact
n_*<=ceil((Qstar-q0)/d) when q0<Qstar. At the crossing the last valid
step has already established the required inverse bound; no continuation
or monotonicity assertion after crossing is needed. Therefore
  ||g^(-1)||_(q_n*) <= A, q_n*>=8s.

Let U=u/g=w/g^2 and N=E U, r=U/N. Since sigma>=4s and q_n*>=8s,
Holder and norm monotonicity give
  ||U||_(2s) <= ||w||_(4s) ||g^(-1)||_(8s)^2 <= B A^2.
The actual-density identities give N>=b (recalled in Section5), hence
  ||r||_(2s)<=K, while ||w||_(2s)<=B.                       (O9)
The result is a bound above the critical sandwich exponent, obtained in
finitely many steps uniformly in dimension. Constants need not be small.

5. MATCHING RIGIDITY AND CONTROL OF THE ACTUAL RATIO
The preserved source lemma rigidity, with p=sigma>1+rho and ||w||_sigma<=B,
gives, for a=E u and the same N,r,
  a^2>=(1+e_k^2)^(-1), a^2<=N<=1,
  ||r-w||_1<=2e_k.                                       (O10)
Here sigma>2 makes the lemma's matching-decay factor exactly sqrt(rho).
For clarity, its essential argument is reproduced rather than replacing
the hypotheses by an unproved closeness assertion.

Put v=1-a^2=||Qu-a||_2^2. Since sigma'=sigma/(sigma-1)<2, the high-degree
part of g=Q(Qu) has L^(sigma') norm at most rho^((k+1)/2)sqrt(v).
Matching gives E w Pi_(<=k)g=a, so E wg<=a+e_k sqrt(v). Cauchy--Schwarz
under u dmu gives 1=(E ug)^2<=a E ug^2=a E wg. Thus v<=a e_k sqrt(v),
and v<=a^2 e_k^2, including the case v=0. This yields the a^2 bound.

Also a<=1. The floor and Jensen imply
a=E u>=b E(1/g)>=b/a, so a^2>=b. Cauchy--Schwarz under w dmu gives
N=E_w(1/g^2)>=E_w(1/g)^2=a^2>=b. For the upper bound N<=1, the actual
reversible conjugate S=M_sqrt(U) P M_sqrt(U) has Perron eigenvalue1
and nonnegative spectrum. With X=M_sqrt(U) Q, S=XX* and T=X*X=Q M_U Q
are similar, because X is invertible. Thus ||T||_(2->2)=1 and testing
at the unit constant gives N<=1. Finally
  E sqrt(rw)=a/sqrt(N)>=a,
  ||sqrt(r)-sqrt(w)||_2^2=2(1-a/sqrt(N))<=2(1-a)<=e_k^2.
Cauchy--Schwarz after multiplication by sqrt(r)+sqrt(w) gives the last
bound in(O10). This is a statement about the actual u, not two arbitrary
close densities.

Interpolating(O10) and(O9), with gamma=1/(2s-1), yields
  ||r-w||_s <= (2e_k)^gamma(K+B)^(1-gamma).                 (O11)
We also need w close to1; matching alone without smoothing would not give
that. Factor
  P_tau=P_(theta_out) P_beta P_(theta_in),
  beta=tau/(theta_in theta_out)<=1/2.
The input map is a contraction from L^s to L^2: this is hypercontractivity
if s<2, and norm monotonicity with the identity map if s>=2. The output
map contracts L^2 to L^s by hypercontractivity if s>2 and by norm
monotonicity with the identity map if s<=2. The Fourier support of pi-1
is above degree k, and remains so under all three noise maps. The middle
map therefore contracts its L^2 norm by beta^(k+1), giving
  ||w-1||_s <=(B+1)2^(-(k+1)).                            (O12)
At tau=0, the high-degree part is annihilated and the same bound holds.

Since U=Nr, the identity U-1=N(r-w)+N(w-1)+(N-1), together with N<=1
and 1-N<=e_k^2/(1+e_k^2)<=e_k^2, proves
  ||U-1||_s<=E_k.                                        (O13)

6. FULL ACTUAL SPECTRUM, NOT ONLY A LOW-DEGREE SECTOR
Let s'=s/(s-1); then 2s'=1+rho^(-1). Hypercontractivity for Q maps
L^2 to L^(2s') with norm at most1. Holder therefore gives, for every
real v and f,l with L^2 norms1,
  |E v (Qf)(Ql)|<=||v||_s.
Consequently ||Q M_v Q||_(2->2)<=||v||_s, the classical critical
sandwich bound already used in M. Apply it with v=U-1 to get
  ||T-P||_(2->2)<=E_k.
T and the actual reversible conjugate S have the same full spectrum,
as shown above. Both T and P are self-adjoint on L^2(mu); the finite
min--max principle compares each ordered eigenvalue by this operator norm.
This proves(O3); lambda_2(P)=rho for L>=1 proves(O4).
All constants in E_k depend only on fixed rho,b. Its three terms decay
exponentially in k. No numerical oracle, eigenvalue computation or assumed
uniform condition number of the similarity X is used.

7. FIXED DENSITY SMOOTHING IS NOT UNIFORMLY A SMALL PERTURBATION
Fix any 0<=tau<1 and let
  epsilon=min{(1-b)/2,(2^(1/s)-1)/2}>0.
For sufficiently large L with k_L<L, take pi_L=1+epsilon chi_[L].
It has mean1, floor> b, moment E pi_L^s<2 and exact matching through k_L.
The inverse-stationary lemma realizes it as h_L P h_L with positive h_L
and energy1, so this is an actual input family. Use h=1 in remaining
finitely many dimensions. Orthogonality and |chi_[L]|=1 give exactly
  ||pi_L-P_tau pi_L||_s=epsilon(1-tau^L) -> epsilon>0.     (O14)
Thus exact growing matching does not turn fixed smoothing into a uniformly
small L^s perturbation of the original density. This refutes that simple
transfer premise, not a possible actual spectral comparison: the parity
family itself lies in an already controlled code class. O establishes the
regularized spectrum, not the original spectrum via an invalid norm limit.

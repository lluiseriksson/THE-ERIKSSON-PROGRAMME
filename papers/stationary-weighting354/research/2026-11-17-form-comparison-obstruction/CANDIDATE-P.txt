1. BOUNDED QUESTION AND RESULT
The active question is still stationary-doob-gap under the original fixed
floor, critical moment cap and exact growing matching. O controls the chain
obtained by smoothing the stationary density and positively reconstructing
its potential. Can one transfer that bound by dimension-uniform comparison
of the stationary variances and Dirichlet forms separately?

This note gives a fully admissible actual family for which BOTH
  Var_pi(f)/Var_w(f) and E_h(f,f)/E_u(f,f)
diverge on the same indicator, where w=P_tau pi and uPu=w. Both actual
full-chain gaps nevertheless tend to 1-rho, and both Rayleigh quotients
on that indicator tend to 1. Thus common scale factors can cancel in a
Rayleigh quotient even when separate upper comparisons fail. In particular
the usual proposed bridge Var_pi <= V Var_w for all f cannot follow from
the original hypotheses, even after restricting to chains with good gaps.
This does not refute a direct Rayleigh-quotient or gap comparison.

2. STATEMENT
Fix 0<rho<1, 0<b<1, C>0, s=(1+rho)/(1-rho), B=2^(1/s), and
any fixed 0<=tau<1. Put k_L=min{L,floor(C sqrt(L))}.
There exist positive energy-normalized potentials h_L and u_L on the
L-cube, with actual stationary densities pi_L=h_L P_rho h_L and
w_L=u_L P_rho u_L=P_tau pi_L, both satisfying
  density>=b, E density^s<=2,
  E density chi_S=0 for 1<=|S|<=k_L.                         (P1)
There are events A_L, with f_L=1_A_L, such that, as L tends to infinity,
  Var_pi_L(f_L)/Var_w_L(f_L) ~ t_L -> infinity,
  E_h_L(f_L,f_L)/E_u_L(f_L,f_L) ~ t_L -> infinity,           (P2)
  E_h_L(f_L,f_L)/Var_pi_L(f_L) -> 1,
  E_u_L(f_L,f_L)/Var_w_L(f_L) -> 1,                          (P3)
  gap(K_h_L) -> 1-rho, gap(K_u_L) -> 1-rho.                 (P4)
Here t_L=c 2^(n_L/s), n_L~sqrt(L)/C, for an explicit fixed c>0 below.
All limits concern sufficiently large dimensions; constant potentials fill
the finitely many earlier dimensions. No scalar comparison constant
depending only on fixed rho,b,C,tau can upper-bound either ratio in(P2).
In addition
  ||w_L-1||_infinity ->0, ||pi_L-w_L||_s ->c>0,              (P5)
  E pi_L^s ->1+c^s,
and the family pi_L^s is not uniformly integrable.                  (P6)
The last properties explain the critical-mass concentration, not gap collapse.

The Dirichlet forms have their respective actual stationary measures:
  E_h(f,f)=<f,(I-K_h)f>_(pi mu)
          =(1/2) sum_(x,y) mu(x)P_rho(x,y)h(x)h(y)(f(x)-f(y))^2,
where P_rho(x,y) is the transition probability and E hP_rho h=1.
Variance is centered separately under each density. No common invariant
measure is silently assigned to these different kernels.

3. EXPLICIT STATIONARY DENSITIES AND MATCHING
For large L put d_L=k_L+1 and n_L=floor(L/d_L). Use n_L disjoint blocks
of d_L coordinates, with the remaining coordinates unused. Let z_i be the
parity of block i and let A_L be the event z_1=...=z_n=1. Uniform measure
of A_L is p_L=2^(-n_L). Choose
  c=min{(1-b)/2,(B-1)/4}>0,
  t_L=c p_L^(-1/s),
  pi_L=1+t_L(1_A_L-p_L).                                  (P7)
In this proof subscripts L may be omitted. Since d=C sqrt(L)+O(1),
n=sqrt(L)/C+O(1), d,n tend to infinity. In particular d<=L eventually.
Mean one is exact. Outside A the density is 1-tp, and
  tp=c p^(1-1/s) <=c, so pi>=1-c>b.
Minkowski gives
  ||pi||_s <=1+t||1_A-p||_s
          <=1+t(p^(1/s)+p)
          =1+c(1+p^(1-1/s)) <=1+2c <B.                    (P8)
Thus E pi^s<2, with strict slack independent of dimension.
The Fourier expansion 1_A=2^(-n) product_i(1+z_i) contains only unions
of whole blocks. Every nonconstant such index has weight at least d=k+1.
Subtracting p removes the constant from the perturbation. This proves
all the labelled matching conditions through k, not merely on the quotient.
The inverse-stationary lemma supplies a unique positive h with hPh=pi;
mean one supplies its exact energy normalization. This is a proved actual
realization rather than independent arbitrary choices of density and kernel.

4. NOISE SMOOTHING AND ACTUAL QUOTIENT POTENTIALS
Product noise with parameter tau acts on each block parity by tau^d,
independently across blocks. With epsilon_tau=tau^d,
  P_tau 1_A(z)=p product_i(1+epsilon_tau z_i),
  w(z)=1+tp[product_i(1+epsilon_tau z_i)-1].                 (P9)
Put e_tau=(1+epsilon_tau)^n-1. For 0<=epsilon_tau<=1,
the maximum absolute deviation of that product from1 is e_tau:
(1+epsilon_tau)^n+(1-epsilon_tau)^n>=2 proves the lower endpoint
deviation is no larger. Hence
  ||w-1||_infinity<=tp e_tau
      <=c p^(1-1/s)[exp(n tau^d)-1] ->0.                   (P10)
Indeed n tau^d tends to zero for each fixed tau<1, including tau=0.
No assertion uniform as tau tends to1 with L is made. By positivity,
Jensen, mean preservation and Fourier multipliers, w also satisfies(P1).
Apply the same inverse lemma to obtain a unique positive u with uPu=w.

Both reconstructed potentials belong to the same disjoint-block Fourier
algebra. Here is the needed justification. Translate the cube by any sign
vector whose parity in every chosen block is1; residual coordinates may
be translated arbitrarily. The densities pi and w are invariant under this
subgroup, and P commutes with every such translation. Translating h or u
therefore gives another positive solution to the same inverse equation.
Uniqueness makes h and u invariant. The subgroup's orbits are precisely
the fibers of (z_1,...,z_n); consequently h=H(z), u=J(z). No unproved
Fourier-algebra preservation of nonlinear reconstruction is assumed.

5. QUOTIENT BOUNDS AND RETURN PROBABILITIES
Write theta=rho^d, ell=(1-theta)^n, v=(1+theta)^n.
The quotient transition probability from z to z' is
  p b_rho(z,z'), b_rho(z,z')=product_i(1+theta z_i z'_i).
Thus ell<=b_rho<=v and ell,v tend to1 since n rho^d tends to0.
For either energy-normalized quotient potential H, let G=P H and
a=E H (quotient expectations are uniform). Positivity gives
  a ell<=G(z)<=a v.
Since E HG=1,
  1/v<=a^2<=1/ell,
  ell^2/v<=G(z)^2<=v^2/ell.                               (P11)
These bounds are uniform in H, even though its maximum can diverge.
Let q be its actual stationary mass of the quotient atom z_+=(1,...,1):
q=p H(z_+)G(z_+). The probability of returning to the event A from any
point of A is exactly the quotient self-transition
  r_A=p v H(z_+)/G(z_+)=v q/G(z_+)^2
      <=(v^2/ell^2)q.                                    (P12)
It is an event return probability, not the single ambient-point holding
probability. This distinction retains transitions inside the entire fiber.

For h and u respectively, the event masses are explicitly
  q_pi=p[1+t(1-p)],
  q_w =p[1+tp((1+tau^d)^n-1)].                            (P13)
Consequently q_pi~tp=c p^(1-1/s)->0, q_w~p->0, and
  q_pi/q_w ~t ->infinity.                                (P14)
By(P12), both return probabilities r_pi,r_w tend to0.
For f=1_A, exact stationarity and reversibility yield
  Var_pi(f)=q_pi(1-q_pi), E_h(f,f)=q_pi(1-r_pi),
  Var_w(f)=q_w(1-q_w),   E_u(f,f)=q_w(1-r_w).              (P15)
Combining(P14)--(P15) proves both divergence statements(P2) and both
normalized limits(P3). This verifies the claimed failure using the actual
forms; no pointwise edge-density failure is substituted for a form failure.

6. THE FULL GAPS ARE NONCOLLAPSING
The existing disjoint-block full-chain proposition blocktheorem applies
to every positive quotient potential, with no height, floor or moment
assumption needed for its spectral conclusion. Its explicit bounds are
  max{0,rho-epsilon_gap}<=lambda_2(K)
    <=min{1,max{1-alpha,beta}},
  alpha=((1-rho^d)/(1+rho^d))^n,
  beta=(rho+rho^(d-1))/(1-rho^d),
  epsilon_gap=(1-rho^2)rho^(d-1)/(1-rho^d).                (P16)
Here d>=2 eventually, d->infinity and n rho^d->0. Thus alpha->1,
beta->rho and epsilon_gap->0, uniformly over positive quotient values.
We proved that both actual potentials have exactly that quotient form,
so(P16) proves(P4) for both original and reconstructed chains. The
proposition controls every invariant coset of the full cube, including
residual-coordinate sectors; a quotient gap alone would not suffice.
Its proof and bounds are retained in the exact dependency manuscript.
The constant fillings in finitely many small dimensions change no limit.

7. CRITICAL-MASS CONCENTRATION AND PRECISE FAILED BRIDGE
The L^s norm of t1_A is exactly c, whereas
  ||t P_tau 1_A||_s <=tp(1+tau^d)^n ->0.
The triangle and reverse triangle inequalities applied to
pi-w=t(1_A-P_tau1_A) prove the second part of(P5). Also
  E pi^s=(1-p)(1-tp)^s+p[1+t(1-p)]^s ->1+c^s,              (P17)
since t->infinity, tp->0 and p t^s=c^s. For any fixed finite R,
the value pi^s on A eventually exceeds R, and
  E[pi^s 1_{pi^s>R}] >=p[1+t(1-p)]^s ->c^s.
The tail expectations therefore cannot tend to0 uniformly as R->infinity;
the critical powers are not uniformly integrable. This claim is formulated
as a family of distributions, so no common underlying cube is required.

A proposed sufficient transfer often seeks constants V,D independent of L:
  Var_pi(f)<=V Var_w(f), E_u(f,f)<=D E_h(f,f) for all f.
The first of these estimates is disproved by(P2), regardless of whether
the second might hold. Two-sided norm/form equivalence also fails because
the upper estimate E_h<=D' E_u fails. We do NOT claim to refute the lower
form bound E_h>=d' E_u. Nor do we refute an estimate comparing the two
Rayleigh quotients directly; on our chosen test the divergent factors
cancel and both quotients tend to1. The family gives no gap collapse and
does not settle the general original-chain gap question.

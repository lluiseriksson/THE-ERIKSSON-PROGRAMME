Both block size and block count tend to infinity. In particular n>=1 and d>=2 for all sufficiently large L. The unused residue L-nd lies between zero and d-1, so the construction covers every sufficiently large integer dimension, not just a subsequence divisible by d. Constants implicit in these asymptotics may depend on fixed C; no claim uniform as C tends to zero is made.

5. Density smoothing, tau=0 and actual quotient invariance

Product noise acts independently on the disjoint block parities, with effective parameter tau^d. Therefore
  P_tau 1_A(z)=p product_i(1+tau^d z_i),
  w=1+tp[product_i(1+tau^d z_i)-1].
This is the actual ambient noise action, including unused coordinates. For epsilon=tau^d in [0,1], the product lies between (1-epsilon)^n and (1+epsilon)^n. The sum of these endpoint values is at least two by the nonnegative even terms in their binomial expansions. Thus its largest absolute deviation from one is the upper deviation e_tau=(1+epsilon)^n-1, attained at all-plus parities. The inequality (1+epsilon)^n<=exp(n epsilon) gives P10.

For each fixed 0<tau<1, exponential decay in d~C sqrt(L) dominates n~sqrt(L)/C, so n tau^d tends to zero. At tau=0, epsilon and e_tau are identically zero for d>=1; w is exactly one. Hence ||w-1||_infinity tends to zero throughout the claimed fixed-tau range. Nothing here is uniform for a dimension-dependent tau tending to one. Positivity, invariant mean, Jensen and Walsh multipliers preserve the floor, cap and exact matching for w. The second inverse uPu=w is therefore an actual positive energy-normalized potential too.

The proof that h and u belong to the block-parity algebra is essential and valid. Let the translation subgroup consist of sign vectors with parity one on each chosen block, with arbitrary residual signs. Its orbits are precisely the fibers of the vector of block parities. Both input densities are invariant under this subgroup, and Boolean noise commutes with every translation. Translating a positive solution of hPh=pi produces another positive solution of the same equation; uniqueness makes the solution invariant. The same reasoning applies to u. Consequently h=H(z), u=J(z) and both ignore residual coordinates. This establishes nonlinear reconstruction's algebra preservation in this case; it is not asserted without proof for arbitrary Fourier truncation.

The inverse lemma applies because P_rho is strictly positive, symmetric and stochastic on the finite cube. Its strictly convex log-potential functional has positive diagonal exponential Hessian contributions. Its sublevel sets are bounded by exponential growth when the maximum coordinate grows and the positive linear density weights when any coordinate tends to minus infinity. Thus the uniqueness used in the symmetry argument is genuine, not an additional premise. The dimension-dependent stability part of that lemma is not used.

6. Quotient normalization and the event-return probability

For the rho kernel, theta=rho^d and the quotient transition from z to z' is p b_rho(z,z'), with b_rho=product_i(1+theta z_i z'_i). Writing ell=(1-theta)^n and v=(1+theta)^n gives ell<=b_rho<=v. Both tend to one because n rho^d tends to zero; ell>0 for each finite admissible dimension.

For either quotient potential H, uniform quotient mean a=E H and G=P H satisfy a ell<=G<=a v. Energy normalization gives a^2 ell<=1<=a^2 v, equivalently 1/v<=a^2<=1/ell. Squaring the pointwise bounds then gives exactly ell^2/v<=G^2<=v^2/ell. This estimate is uniform over all positive H, so a diverging spike in H cannot invalidate it.

The event A is a whole fiber over the quotient atom z_+, not an individual cube vertex. Summing actual transitions from any x in A over every y in A gives
  K_H(x,A)=p v H(z_+)/G(z_+).
The sum is independent of x within that fiber. Its actual stationary event mass is q=p H(z_+)G(z_+), so
  r_A=v q/G(z_+)^2 <=(v^2/ell^2)q.
The coefficient in the candidate is correct: the lower bound for G^2 is ell^2/v, producing the second power of v. This calculation includes all internal-fiber transitions and all residual-coordinate transitions. Replacing it by the ambient single-point holding probability would be wrong, but the source does not do that.

Detailed balance of the actual h-kernel follows from pi(x)K_h(x,y)=h(x)P(x,y)h(y) in energy normalization. The displayed Dirichlet form has factor one half and the uniform mu weight in the correct places, with P denoting transition probabilities. It is exactly the form for the stationary measure pi mu; the u-kernel uses w mu. No common invariant measure is assumed.

7. The two divergent ratios and cancellation

At the all-plus quotient atom, the densities give exactly
  q_pi=p[1+t(1-p)],
  q_w=p[1+tp((1+tau^d)^n-1)].
Since t tends to infinity, p tends to zero and tp tends to zero, q_pi/(tp)=1/t+1-p tends to one. The bracket in q_w tends to one by the smoothing bound, so q_w/p tends to one. Both masses tend to zero and q_pi/q_w divided by t tends to one. The event-return bound applies to each actual potential, with ell,v tending to one, hence both r_pi and r_w tend to zero.

For an indicator, its separately centered variance is q(1-q). Its form is the stationary flow from A to its complement, q(1-r_A), because the return probability is constant over A. Equivalently this follows directly from the actual Dirichlet identity; there is no missing factor two. Thus
  Var_pi(f)/Var_w(f)=(q_pi/q_w)(1-q_pi)/(1-q_w) ~ t,
  E_h(f,f)/E_u(f,f)=(q_pi/q_w)(1-r_pi)/(1-r_w) ~ t.
The separate Rayleigh quotients are (1-r_pi)/(1-q_pi) and (1-r_w)/(1-q_w), both tending to one. Positivity of the finite kernels and a nontrivial quotient event ensure the denominators are positive in every sufficiently large dimension. This is an actual form-level witness, not merely a pointwise mismatch of edge densities. The divergence rate t=c 2^(n/s) and n~sqrt(L)/C is exactly the one stated.

An indicator Rayleigh quotient tending to one is consistent with a full gap tending to 1-rho, which is less than one: the indicator need not be a minimizing test. This distinction is preserved in the candidate.

9. Critical norm, moment limit and uniform integrability

The equality ||t1_A||_s=t p^(1/s)=c is exact. Meanwhile the product formula gives ||tP_tau1_A||_s<=tp(1+tau^d)^n, which tends to zero. Applying both triangle inequalities to pi-w=t(1_A-P_tau1_A) proves ||pi-w||_s tends to c. The earlier infinity estimate proves ||w-1||_infinity tends to zero. This pair is possible because the original densities concentrate a nonzero critical mass on an event whose probability vanishes.

The exact s-moment is (1-p)(1-tp)^s+p[1+t(1-p)]^s. Its first term tends to one. Its second is p t^s[1/t+1-p]^s=c^s[1/t+1-p]^s, tending to c^s. Thus E pi^s tends to 1+c^s. This is compatible with the uniform strict cap established earlier; no equality with the cap two is asserted.

For every fixed finite threshold R, the density power on A eventually exceeds R since t tends to infinity. The tail expectation is then at least p[1+t(1-p)]^s, which tends to c^s>0. Therefore sup_L E[pi_L^s 1_(pi_L^s>R)] cannot tend to zero as R tends to infinity. This is the correct uniform-integrability test for a family of nonnegative distributions on varying finite probability spaces; no common underlying cube is required. The proof does not confuse bounded s moments of pi with uniform integrability of their s-th powers.

10. Which comparison fails and which remains open

The variance upper comparison Var_pi(f)<=V Var_w(f), with V depending only on fixed rho,b,C,tau, fails because the indicated admissible sequence makes the ratio diverge. The separate form upper comparison E_h(f,f)<=D' E_u(f,f) also fails on the same test. Hence two-sided equivalence of the forms cannot be deduced from the original assumptions.

The candidate correctly distinguishes the form direction in a sufficient gap bridge. Combining Var_pi<=V Var_w with E_u<=D E_h and a regularized Poincare bound would give an original bound; P disproves the variance premise regardless of the status of the second premise. The exhibited diverging E_h/E_u ratio does not disprove E_u<=D E_h, equivalently a lower bound E_h>=d' E_u. Nor does it disprove direct Rayleigh-quotient comparison: the divergent scale factors cancel on this witness. The good full gaps explicitly rule out interpreting P as a collapsing-gap example.

# Critical order under logarithmic control

Follow-up to the frozen two-base paper. This note has no model score and is outside the reviewed PDF. The single active question is recovery at p = 15/7 for two disjoint bases, without additional hypotheses.

A partial critical result is available if the critical stationary powers are uniformly integrable and the uniform means of log pi are bounded below. Normalize the strictly positive potential h by E(hPh) = 1 and put g = Ph. From E log g <= log E g <= 0 and pi = h g it follows that E log h >= E log pi. Apply the two-base reverse hypercontractivity inequality for P at input exponent a decreasing to zero. Its output exponent is 1 - 16(1-a), which tends to -15. On each fixed finite cube generalized means are continuous through exponent zero. Consequently ||1/g||_15 <= exp(-E log h), uniformly bounded under the stated log assumption. At p = 15/7 the exact identity 3/5 = 1/p + 2/15 bounds ||U 1_E||_(5/3) by ||pi 1_E||_p ||1/g||_15^2. Here U = pi/g^2 and E is the low-probability exceptional set in the inverse-moment recovery theorem in the new manuscript. Uniform integrability makes the first factor vanish. The matching variance bound controls the complement. The signed comparison still has exponential slack since p > 2. Thus the full ordered spectral error tends to zero, without a general rate under qualitative uniform integrability.

This argument does not imply that a bounded p moment makes the critical powers uniformly integrable or that matching bounds E log pi. Test whether direct localized endpoint estimates work without these two controls. A matched sequence with nonvanishing spectral discrepancy falsifies unconditional recovery. Discontinue the argument through a global inverse L15 bound if it cannot be obtained or replaced at the localized form. No claim about the critical case with only bounded p moment has been made.

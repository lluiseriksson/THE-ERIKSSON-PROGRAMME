\begin{abstract}
For half-correlated Boolean noise, we study how a reversible change of
transition probabilities affects the ordered spectrum when the stationary
coordinate marginals are exactly uniform through increasing order.
At the stationary moment endpoint we prove recovery under an exponential
cutoff on the critical density tail. For every stationary moment order
strictly above three and a fixed positive density floor, we identify a set
of vertices on which local one-step return characterizes full spectral
recovery. Its killed eigenvalue also determines the second eigenvalue up to
exponential accuracy. Interpolating stationary densities in an existing
shell construction gives chains with any prescribed finite number of correct
leading noise bands and a persistent error lower in the spectrum.
Without coordinate matching, the endpoint relaxation time is at most $\exp(C\sqrt{L\log(2L)})$ in dimension $L$. Above the endpoint the spectral gap has a dimension-independent positive lower bound.
For a two-valued stationary density, a bounded cubic moment gives a dimension-independent gap. The entire ordered spectrum varies from ordinary noise by at most $C_b\|\pi-1\|_3$ without matching, and a two-coordinate example attains linear order. We extend both estimates to finitely many stationary levels whose uniform masses have comparable logarithms, with an explicit sufficient ratio bound. The gap also holds with variable heights under separate cubic envelopes on the exceptional sets.
\end{abstract}

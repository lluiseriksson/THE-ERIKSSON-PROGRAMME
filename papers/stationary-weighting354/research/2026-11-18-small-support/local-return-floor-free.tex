\begin{abstract}
For half-correlated Boolean noise we characterize recovery of the full ordered spectrum after a reversible Doob transform by one-step returns inside a single exceptional set. The set consists of vertices where the potential divided by its one-step noise average exceeds an exponential threshold in the order of stationary marginal matching. With a bounded stationary moment of order above three the criterion holds for arbitrary potentials without a positive uniform density floor. On quotients with two disjoint coordinate bases it holds at every moment order above $5/3$, including the critical order $15/7$. At the critical order, matching through degree $k$ yields full recovery when the quotient rank is $o(k^2/\log(k+2))$. More generally the same bound need only hold for the number of directions missed by an additional independent family of columns. A bound on reverse relative entropy removes the logarithmic loss. At this endpoint the exceptional set can be selected using ordinary noise applied to the square root of the stationary density. This proves recovery even for a family with deep holes of superlinear column radius. A local coverage condition gives exponential recovery at arbitrary rank even when all stationary inverse moments diverge. For symmetric scalings of a finite positive reversible kernel, an upper factor of two from marginal to edge relative entropy characterizes nonnegative spectrum. This and a logarithmic event bound certify approximate returns. A matched shell exhibits a false negative for an uncertified one-step approximation. On two-base quotients every exponentially small fraction of the syndromes has exponentially small exact and trial returns. Genuine return tending to one forces full conditional Shannon entropy per syndrome bit. With two identical bases and stationary densities invariant under permutations within a fixed number of blocks of arbitrary sizes, we prove critical recovery at unrestricted rank. We recover all eigenvalues with multiplicity from ordinary noise and the chain killed outside the exceptional set. A family with bounded stationary densities disproves an alternative test by unnormalized Schatten norms of any finite order prescribed from the matching order. The paper also gives sufficient recovery estimates using fractional base packing and code weight enumerators, gap bounds without matching, and examples separating control of the leading bands from control of the whole spectrum.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Model assessments and review context}
Codex assisted with the mathematical derivation, comparison with primary
sources, and source checks in the programme's research chat. This supplement
uses the previously constructed shell as an explicit input to the interpolation
argument. The \href{https://airr.science/papers/ARR-2026-2SH3CGGBHG9Q5BYN/versions/v1/}{first version's record} identifies its assessment. The final sections extend the local return criterion to densities with arbitrarily small minimum and examine the limits of trace estimates. The critical-rank section derives a two-point norm estimate and applies it to high multipliers and affine constraints. The matched-smoothing section uses matching to sharpen the inverse moment and measures the forward cost by the defect of an incomplete third basis. The local coverage argument uses a bounded number of column translations and Hall's theorem. The last section identifies a return set from the stationary density using forward and reverse noise inequalities. The final comparison of approximate edge laws uses marginal residuals, positive semidefiniteness, and the matched-shell construction. The final support estimates use the rank cost of near-endpoint hypercontractivity. The block-exchangeable result uses one-bit entropy duality, tilted binomial layer probabilities and block centering and binomial anti-concentration. Codex participated in these derivations as well. Assessments apply to the exact PDF identified in each version's record.

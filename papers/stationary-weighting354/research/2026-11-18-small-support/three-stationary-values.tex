\section{Three stationary values suffice for exponential trapping}
\label{sec:three-values}
The rarity comparison in (F1) cannot be discarded, even for exact
levels. For every $\kappa>64/9$ and every fixed floor $0<b<1$ there
are a fixed $p>3$, $\sigma>0$ and arbitrarily large dimensions with
exactly three stationary density values such that
\[
 \pi\ge b,\qquad \|\pi-1\|_p\le e^{-\sigma L},\qquad
 1-\lambda_2(K)\le e^{-\sigma L}.
 \tag{T1}
\]
All one-coordinate stationary marginals are uniform. The two exceptional
levels have uniform masses $m_S<m_C$ with $m_S\ge m_C^\kappa$.
Together with (S1), this identifies three as the smallest number of
stationary values allowing loss of the uniform gap under a fixed floor
and bounded cubic moment. The assertions about the number of levels
do not impose higher-order marginal matching.

\paragraph{Ordinary shell probabilities.}
Write $z(x)=L^{-1}\sum x_i$ and $I(t)=((1+t)\log(1+t)
+(1-t)\log(1-t))/2$. For attainable $t>0$,
$\mu\{|z|=t\}=\exp[-LI(t)+O(\log L)]$ and $I(t)=t^2/2+O(t^4)$.
From $z(x)=s$, one half-noise step has magnetization mean $s/2$.
For $|t|,|s|=O(d)$ write $w=t-s/2$. The probability of landing at
the exact attainable $t$ is between
\[
 \exp[-L(2w^2/3+O(d^3))-O(\log L)]
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \exp[-L(2w^2/3-O(d^3))].
 \tag{T2}
\]
For the lower bound the two groups of input signs have output-plus
probabilities $3/4$ and $1/4$. Raise each by $w/2$ (a negative shift
is allowed). Round the first group's output count to an integer and
choose the second to give exactly the prescribed total. Each group
has size comparable to $L$. Stirling's bounds for both binomial terms
give the two relative entropies with $O(\log L)$ error. Each divergence
is $2w^2/3+O(d^3)$ by Taylor expansion. For the upper bound, the
centered log moment generating function per output sign is
$3v^2/8+O(|v|^3)$ for a tilt $v$ of either sign; use $v=4w/3$.
The constants are uniform for the shell locations used below.

\paragraph{Two prescribed exceptional densities.}
Choose rational $u<3/8$ sufficiently close to $3/8$ that
$u^{-2}<\kappa$. Choose $k_0$ strictly between
\[
 \tfrac23(u-1/4)^2
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \min\{\tfrac23(1/2-u)^2,\ u^2/12,\ (u-1/8)^2/2\}.
 \tag{T3}
\]
Such an interval exists: at $3/8$ the first two square-distance
rates coincide, the other two upper bounds are strictly larger,
and moving $u$ to the left separates the first two rates.
Choose a fixed $\omega>0$ small enough that
$\tfrac23(u-1/4+\omega/2)^2<k_0$ and $\omega<1/2-u$.
Choose $e>0$ so small that each of the gaps in (T3), the new gap
after adding $\omega/2$, the number $\omega^2/2$, and
$(7/8)^2/2-1/6$ exceeds $10e$. Take $0<\gamma<\min(e,1/32)$.
Finally take rational $d>0$ sufficiently small that all strict
inequalities below survive the $O(d^3)$ errors in (T2). Take $L$
along multiples making both positive shells attainable. Set
\[
 S=\{|z|=d\},\qquad C=\{|z|=ud\},\qquad
 B=\{\,||z|-d/2|\le\omega d\,\}.
\]
For $x\in S$, $q=P_x(S)$ is independent of $x$. By (T2) and
Hoeffding's bound,
\[
 e^{-(d^2/6+O(d^3))L-O(\log L)}\le q\le2e^{-d^2L/8}.
\]
Prescribe the density itself by
\[
 M_C=e^{k_0 d^2 L},\qquad M_S=e^{-\gamma d^2L}/q,\qquad
 \pi=M_C{\bf1}_C+M_S{\bf1}_S+a{\bf1}_{(S\cup C)^c},
 \quad a=\frac{1-m_C M_C-m_S M_S}{1-m_C-m_S}.
\]
Since $k_0<u^2/12$, $M_Cm_C^{1/3}$ decays exponentially. The
corresponding exponent for $M_Sm_S^{1/3}$ is at most
$-\gamma d^2+O(d^3)$, also strictly negative. Thus $a\to1$ and the
cubic distance decays exponentially. The same strict inequalities
hold with $1/p$ in place of $1/3$ for some fixed $p>3$ near three.
The upper bound on $q$ ensures $M_S>M_C>1$ eventually. Sign reversal
gives exact one-coordinate marginals. The limit of $\log m_S/\log m_C$
is $I(d)/I(ud)$, which tends to $u^{-2}$ as $d\downarrow0$, so (after
fixing sufficiently small $d$) $m_S\ge m_C^\kappa$ eventually.

\paragraph{Recovering the potential from the prescribed law.}
The unique positive solution $hPh=\pi$ follows by strict convexity
and coercivity of the finite symmetric scaling functional in log
coordinates (as in the published shell proof). Permutations and global
sign reversal preserve this solution. In particular $h,g=Ph$ are
constant on each of $S,C$, denoted by subscripts. Put
$\delta_2=\|\pi-1\|_2$. The variance proof in the one-spike section
uses only the stationary second norm of the perturbation and yields
$\|g-1\|_3\le\delta_2+\delta_2^2$. Here
$\delta_2=O(M_C\sqrt{m_C}+M_S\sqrt{m_S})$.
After division by $m_C^{1/3}$ the two exponential rates are bounded
above by $k_0d^2-I(ud)/6$ and
$d^2/6+O(d^3)-I(d)/2+I(ud)/3$, both strictly negative for small $d$.
Therefore $g_C=1+o(1)$ and $h_C=(1+o(1))M_C$.
On $S$ positivity and $g_S\ge qh_S$ imply
$h_S\le\sqrt{M_S/q}\le1/q$.

For $y\in B$, the lower bound (T2) aimed at the appropriately signed
component of $C$ gives
$P_y(C)\ge\exp[-(\tfrac23(u-1/4+\omega/2)^2d^2+O(d^3))L-O(\log L)]$.
Since $g(y)\ge h_C P_y(C)$ and $\pi(y)=a$, for large $L$,
\[
 \sup_B h\le e^{-4ed^2 L}.
 \tag{T4}
\]
This depression in the potential is a consequence of the two
prescribed high stationary values.

\paragraph{Escape from the rarer level.}
For $x\in S$ let $D=P_x(h{\bf1}_{S^c})$. The smallness of $h$ on
$B$ alone would not control atypical transitions. To bound them set
$T=P^2$, $Y=1/g$. The floor $a$ gives $Y\le Pg/a=T h/a$ by Jensen.
Because $h=aY$ off the exceptional levels,
\[
 Y\le TY+a^{-1}(h_C T{\bf1}_C+h_S T{\bf1}_S).
\]
Iterate $n=1+\lceil\log_{16}(1+L)\rceil$ times. The ambient bound
$\|Y\|_9\le1/a$ and hypercontractivity give
$\|T^nY\|_\infty\le2^{L/(1+8\cdot16^n)}/a\le 2/a$.
Bounding separately the landing regions $C$, $B$, and ordinary
background outside $B$, and using positivity in the iterated terms,
gives the explicit estimate
\[
 D\le h_C P_x(C)+\sup_B h+2P_x(B^c)
       +\sum_{i=1}^n\big[h_C P_x^{2i+1}(C)+h_S P_x^{2i+1}(S)\big].
 \tag{T5}
\]
The one-step term for $C$ decays with exponent at least
$(\tfrac23(1/2-u)^2-k_0)d^2-O(d^3)$ by (T2), including the more
distant opposite-sign component. The third term costs at most
$4e^{-\omega^2d^2L/2}$. For every odd step $2i+1\ge3$ the signs are
independent given $x$, with magnetization mean of magnitude at most
$d/8$. Hoeffding bounds the two probabilities in the sum by
$2e^{-(u-1/8)^2d^2L/2}$ and $2e^{-(7/8)^2d^2L/2}$ respectively.
Multiplication by $h_C\sim M_C$ and $h_S\le1/q$ preserves strict
exponential decay. With the chosen slack and $n=O(\log L)$, (T5)
gives $D\le e^{-ed^2 L}$.

Finally $g_S=qh_S+D$ and $g_Sh_S=M_S$ imply
$g_S\ge\sqrt{qM_S}=e^{-\gamma d^2L/2}$. Hence
$K_x(S^c)=D/g_S\le e^{-(e-\gamma/2)d^2L}$ on the whole shell.
The centered shell indicator in the Rayleigh quotient gives
$1-\lambda_2(K)\le K_x(S^c)/(1-M_Sm_S)$, proving (T1).

\paragraph{Exact matching and the number of approximate levels.}
There is a corresponding obstruction with exact matching through a
positive fraction of the coordinates. Let $\pi_0$ be the three-valued
law just constructed. For sufficiently small fixed $\beta>0$, subtract
from $\pi_0$ its nonconstant Walsh coefficients of orders at most
$\lfloor\beta L\rfloor$ to obtain $\pi_1$. Each coefficient is bounded
by $\|\pi_0-1\|_1$, and their number is at most $e^{L H(\beta)}$,
where $H$ is binary entropy in natural units. By (T1) we can choose
$H(\beta)<\sigma/2$. Thus $\pi_1$ is exactly matched, normalized,
and $\|\pi_1-\pi_0\|_\infty\le e^{-\sigma L/2}$. Its floor tends to
one and its supercubic norm distance still tends exponentially to zero.

For completeness, let two normalized densities $v_0,v_1$ have floor
$b_0>0$ and logarithmic distance
$\epsilon=\|\log(v_1/v_0)\|_\infty\le1$. Along their straight-line
interpolation, $\|\dot v_t/v_t\|_\infty\le e^\epsilon-1\le2\epsilon$.
Logarithmic differentiation of $h_tPh_t=v_t$ gives
$(I+K_t)\,\partial_t\log h_t=\dot v_t/v_t$. The operator
$A_t=(I-K_t)/2$ has absolute row sums at most one. Positivity of the
noise quadratic form gives stationary $L^2$ norm at most $1/2$.
Since the smallest stationary mass is at least $b_0 2^{-L}$,
$\|A_t^j\|_{\infty\to\infty}\le\min(1,2^{L/2-j}/\sqrt{b_0})$.
The inverse is $\tfrac12\sum_{j\ge0}A_t^j$, so
$\|\log(h_1/h_0)\|_\infty\le C_{b_0}(L+1)\epsilon$.
In particular every stationary edge weight changes by a factor
between $e^{-2C_{b_0}(L+1)\epsilon}$ and $e^{2C_{b_0}(L+1)\epsilon}$, and every variance, written
as an infimum over constant centerings, changes by a factor between
$e^{-\epsilon}$ and $e^\epsilon$. Taking Dirichlet quotients gives
\[
 \left|\log\frac{\operatorname{gap}(K_1)}
                         {\operatorname{gap}(K_0)}\right|
 \le 2C_{b_0}(L+1)\epsilon+\epsilon. \tag{T6}
\]
Applied to the projected law this preserves the exponential gap
upper bound. The three exact levels refer to the reference law;
the matched law lies exponentially close to those levels.

Conversely, suppose a density is within $o(1)$ in log sup norm of a
normalized two-valued reference with uniform floor and bounded cubic
norm. The one-spike theorem and (T6) imply
$\log(1/\operatorname{gap}(K))=o(L)$; distance $O(1/L)$ gives a
uniform positive gap. Thus an exponential trap with a fixed positive
rate is impossible even at vanishing logarithmic distance from two
such levels, whereas exponentially accurate three-level approximation
already allows it under linear-order matching. The unrestricted
sufficient stationary exponent is unchanged by this construction.

\paragraph{Consequences and comparison.}
For the two-exceptional-level cubic gap problem, (F1) proves
sufficiency for $\kappa<21-8\sqrt5$. Equation (T1) excludes
uniform sufficiency for every $\kappa>64/9$. Neither endpoint is
decided. The \href{https://airr.science/papers/ARR-2026-2B2W1WQ7DF9N5VW7/versions/v1/}{earlier shell}
started with several potential bands and then corrected the stationary
marginals through linear order. Here the stationary law has precisely
three values by construction; the reciprocal estimate (T5) controls
its reconstructed transition rows. Ordinary Hamming sphere noise
estimates are studied, for example, by
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11929}{Kirshner and Samorodnitsky}.
The present argument does not decide the unrestricted matched endpoint.

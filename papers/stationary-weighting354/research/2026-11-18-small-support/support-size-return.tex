\section{Which supports can produce a false positive?}
\label{sec:support-size}
Keep half noise on a quotient with two disjoint column bases, and suppose
$\|\pi\|_{15/7}\le B$. Write $d$ for the number of directions missed by an
independent family of unused columns, as in (M5). With no such family use
$d=r$, the quotient rank. For a nonempty quotient set $E$ write
$L=\log(1/\mu(E))$. Put $V=P\sqrt\pi$, $u=\pi/V$, and let
$\widetilde J=J_u/J_u(\Omega,\Omega)$. Define $M(E)$ as the supremum, over
nonempty $F\subseteq E$, of the larger of
$J_h(F,F)/\nu(F)$ and $\widetilde J(F,F)/\nu(F)$.
Both comparisons use the actual stationary mass. The trial ratio can exceed one.

\paragraph{Support bound.}
There are $c>0$ and $C_B<\infty$ such that, for $L\ge1$,
\[
 \log M(E)\le C_B(1+d^{1/3})-c\,\frac{L^2}{d+L}.             \tag{SS1}
\]
In particular $M(E)\to0$ if $L\to\infty$ and $d=o(L^{3/2})$.
With stationary matching through degree $k$, for all sufficiently large $k$,
\[
 \log M(E)\le C_B\left(1+d\left(\frac{\log(k+2)}k\right)^2\right)
                              -c\,\frac{L^2}{d+L}.             \tag{SS2}
\]
Either estimate may be used. Neither requires an affine description of $E$.
For example, at ranks tending to infinity, any set of at most
$2^{(1-\theta)r}$ syndromes, for fixed $0<\theta<1$, has both returns
exponentially small in $r$, uniformly over its subsets, even without matching.
In particular on the repeated-identity quotient the union of at most
$\exp(o(r))$ translates of Hamming balls of radius $\alpha r$, for fixed
$0<\alpha<1/2$, cannot supply a false positive, regardless of the placement
of the translates or the density inside the balls. Indeed the uniform ball
measure is at most $\exp[-r(\log 2-H(\alpha))]$, with $H$ binary entropy
in natural units. This excludes those constructions also after intersection
with the critical return set $D$.

\paragraph{Mass of the trial and its inverse moment.}
The total trial mass is at least $(\mathbb E u)^2\ge B^{-2}$.
The first inequality uses positive semidefiniteness and the constant mode.
For the second use Jensen under $\nu$ and
$\mathbb E_\nu V\le\|\pi\|_2\|V\|_2\le B$.
For $q=15-\delta$ with small positive $\delta$, the two reverse half-noise
steps from order $\delta/16$ give
$\log\|1/V\|_q\le C_B/\delta$. Indeed $\sqrt\pi\ge1/\sqrt2$ on
uniform mass at least $(2B)^{-2}$ by Cauchy--Schwarz. The corresponding
bound on $1/g$ for $g=Ph$ was proved before (CR1).

For the matched improvement, set $s=\delta/128$ and take the short ambient
noise step $T_s$. The set $G=\{T_s\pi\ge1/2\}$ has complement of measure at
most $4B^2e^{-2sk}$ by matching and Parseval. Rowwise interpolation gives
\[
 T_s\pi\le(T_s\sqrt\pi)^{1-\lambda}(T_s\pi^p)^\lambda,\qquad
 p=15/7,\quad \lambda=\frac{1/2}{p-1/2}.
\]
On $G$, therefore, $T_s\sqrt\pi\ge 2^{-1/(1-\lambda)}
(T_s\pi^p)^{-\lambda/(1-\lambda)}$. For any $a>0$, Jensen conditional
on $G$ and $\mathbb E T_s\pi^p\le B^p$ yield
\[
 \|T_s\sqrt\pi\|_a\ge
 2^{-1/(1-\lambda)}
 (\mu(G)/B^p)^{\lambda/(1-\lambda)}\mu(G)^{1/a}.
\]
The remaining two steps have input order $a=1-(16-\delta)e^{4s}/16$
and output order $-q$, as in (M4). For $\delta=A\log(k+2)/k$ with fixed
$A>128$, one has $a\ge c\delta$, $\mu(G)\to1$ and
$(1-\mu(G))/a\to0$. This proves $\|1/V\|_q\le C_B$ for large $k$.
The same bound for $1/g$ follows from (M4). This argument does not assume
that smoothing $\sqrt\pi$ gives the square root of the smoothed density.

\paragraph{Trading integrability against support size.}
Use either $W=\pi/g^2$ or $W=\pi/V^2$. In the stationary unit vector
$1_F\sqrt{\pi/\nu(F)}$ in $L^2(\mu)$ the quadratic form of
$M_{\sqrt W}P M_{\sqrt W}$ is the corresponding unnormalized edge mass
divided by $\nu(F)$. The trial normalization costs at most $B^2$.
Set $1/v_0=7/15+2/(15-\delta)$. By H\"older,
$\log\|W\|_{v_0}\le C_B/\delta$ in general and $\le C_B$ for the matched
choice above. Choose a further small parameter $z>0$ independently of
$\delta$, and put $1/v=1/v_0+z$. Restriction gives
$\|1_EW\|_v\le e^{-zL}\|W\|_{v_0}$. Since $m=2/(1-1/v)=5+O(\delta+z)$,
(CR3) with the two factors in (M5) gives
\[
 \log M(E)\le 2\log B+\log\|W\|_{v_0}
                    -zL+Cd(\delta+z)^2                       \tag{SS3}
\]
with the maximum over the two indicated weights on the right. Choose $z=bL/(d+L)$ with a sufficiently small fixed $b>0$. Since
$(\delta+z)^2\le2\delta^2+2z^2$, the $z$ terms yield
$-c L^2/(d+L)$. The remaining cost is at most $C_B/\delta+2Cd\delta^2$
without matching; taking $\delta=b_0(1+d)^{-1/3}$ proves (SS1).
Using the matched $\delta=A\log(k+2)/k$ proves (SS2). The latter
forces vanishing if $L^2/(d+L)\to\infty$ and
$d(\log(k+2)/k)^2=o(L^2/(d+L))$.

A third bound replaces both positive error terms by
$C_B+2H(\mu\mid\nu)$. Indeed the endpoint reverse estimate from order
zero to $-15$ bounds $\log\|1/V\|_{15}$ by
$-\mathbb E\log\sqrt\pi=H(\mu\mid\nu)/2$. Likewise
$-\mathbb E\log h=H(\mu\mid\nu)+\mathbb E\log g\le H(\mu\mid\nu)$
since $\mathbb E g=\mathbb E h\le1$ by the constant mode.
Use $\delta=0$ in the weight and support estimates. In particular, bounded
reverse entropy excludes persistent returns as soon as $L\to\infty$
and $d=o(L^2)$, without matching.

\paragraph{A consequence for genuine returns.}
For any sequence of stationary cuts of conditional return at least
$s_0>0$, the conditional Shannon entropy of the syndrome in bits
satisfies $\liminf H_{\rm bits}/r\ge s_0$ as $r\to\infty$.
In particular, return tending to one forces full limiting entropy per bit.
Take the $\lfloor 2^{(1-\theta)r}\rfloor$ largest conditional atoms.
The proof of (SS1) bounds the exact compressed operator norm on this
list by a quantity tending to zero. For the stationary unit vector $f$ of
the cut, the contraction $T=M_{\sqrt{\pi/g^2}}P M_{\sqrt{\pi/g^2}}$
is positive semidefinite. Splitting $T^{1/2}f$ at the list as in (CR2)
shows that the conditional mass off it is at least $s_0-o(1)$.
The same bound holds for a cut splitting syndrome fibers by the lifting
argument after (CR2). Every remaining atom has mass at most the reciprocal
of the list size. Its contribution gives the stated bound after sending
$\theta$ to zero. This consequence uses the exact Markov contraction.

For small supports, improvements on hypercontractivity of ordinary
unweighted cube noise are proved by
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07491}{Polyanskiy and Samorodnitsky}.
The negative-order input here is the Boolean reverse inequality used in
(M4); see also \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.1210}{Mossel, Oleszkiewicz
and Sen}. The two weighted returns and the localization of possible trial
errors are the objects estimated here. The argument leaves open false
positives on larger sets inside $D$.

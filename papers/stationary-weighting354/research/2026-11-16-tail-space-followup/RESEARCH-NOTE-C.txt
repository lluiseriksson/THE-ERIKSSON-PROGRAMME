2. Localization theorem for the actual eigenfunction
Let f be a real K eigenfunction with Kf=lambda f, E pi f=0, E pi f^2=1 and lambda>=rho+zeta. Define
 J_L=delta_L+rho^(d_L+1).
For any M>0 put U_L(M)={x:u(x)>M}. Then
 E[pi f^2 1_(U_L(M)^c)] <= M J_L/lambda,                    (2.1)
 mu(U_L(M)) <= K_t^t M^-t,                                 (2.2)
 Pr_pi(U_L(M)) <= B K_t^(t/s') M^(-t/s'), s'=s/(s-1).       (2.3)
The first assertion concerns the L2(pi) mass of the actual f, not merely a test vector or a proposed cut. The last two concern respectively uniform and stationary probabilities.

Proof. The exact spectral representation from the dependency is
 psi=Q(hf)/sqrt(lambda), ||psi||_2=1, psi perpendicular to Qh,
 T psi=lambda psi.
For completeness, P(hf)=lambda g f gives
 ||Q(hf)||_2^2=E hf P(hf)=lambda E pi f^2=lambda,
 TQ(hf)=Q u P(hf)=lambda Q(hf),
 <Q(hf),Qh>=E pi f=0.
Also Qpsi=P(hf)/sqrt(lambda)=sqrt(lambda)g f. Therefore the following is a POINTWISE identity:
 u |Qpsi|^2=lambda pi f^2.                                  (2.4)
By the Walsh multiplier formula and (1.1),
 ||Qpsi||_2^2=sum_S rho^|S| |psi_hat(S)|^2
 <=delta_L+rho^(d_L+1)=J_L.
On U_L(M)^c, u<=M. Integrate (2.4) over this complement and use the last bound to obtain (2.1). Markov's inequality and ||u||_t<=K_t give (2.2). Holder applied to pi 1_U with ||pi||_s<=B gives Pr_pi(U)<=B mu(U)^(1/s'), proving (2.3). No bound on an unproved critical norm of u is used.

3. Exponential thresholds and eigenfunction intermittency
Fix0<theta<log(1/rho) and M_L=exp(theta d_L). Then
 M_L J_L ->0,
 because delta_L decays exponentially in k_L while d_L=o(k_L), and
 M_L rho^(d_L+1)=rho exp[-(log(1/rho)-theta)d_L]->0.
Consequently (2.1) says that asymptotically all the actual normalized L2(pi) mass of f lies on U_L(M_L), although
 Pr_pi(U_L(M_L)) <= C_t exp[-theta t d_L/s'] ->0,
 C_t=B K_t^(t/s').
The exceptional sets are defined by the actual u=h/Ph, not chosen freely to suit f. If the actual gaps collapse, their second eigenvalues eventually satisfy the above condition with zeta=(1-rho)/2, so every choice of normalized real second eigenfunction obeys the conclusion.

More quantitatively, for every fixed finite p>2, writing
 eta_L=M_L J_L/lambda and assuming eta_L<1, Holder on the stationary probability space gives
 ||f||_(L^p(pi))
 >=sqrt(1-eta_L) [Pr_pi(U_L(M_L))]^(-(1/2-1/p))
 >=sqrt(1-eta_L) C_t^(-(1/2-1/p))
   exp[theta t d_L(1/2-1/p)/s'].                            (3.1)
The first inequality follows from ||f1_U||_2<=Pr_pi(U)^(1/2-1/p)||f||_p and (2.1). The lower bound on its L2 mass also ensures Pr_pi(U)>0, so there is no division by an empty set. This is a necessary divergence of higher stationary moments of such eigenfunctions, not an assumed eigenfunction bound or a proved absence of these modes.

4. High-degree support alone does not repair the missing norm bound
Fix rho and any finite q>1+1/rho. For any sequence of integer cutoffs d_L>=0 with d_L=o(L), there are explicit real functions F_L with ||F_L||_2=1 and all Walsh coefficients through degree d_L zero, but
 ||Q F_L||_q grows exponentially in L.                      (4.1)
This refutes a dimension-uniform L2->Lq bound on that entire tail space. It is a classical tensor-product sharpness construction, not a new general hypercontractivity theorem or an admissible Doob counterexample.

Proof. Choose fixed small0<epsilon<1, to be specified, let m=d_L+1 and ell=L-m (nonnegative for all sufficiently large L), and set
 F_L(x)= [product_(i=1)^m x_i]
          [product_(i=m+1)^L (1+epsilon x_i)]/(1+epsilon^2)^(ell/2).
Independence gives ||F_L||_2=1. Every Walsh monomial in its expansion contains all of the first m coordinates, so every degree is at least m>d_L. Put
 kappa(epsilon)=
 {[(1+sqrt(rho)epsilon)^q+(1-sqrt(rho)epsilon)^q]/2}^(1/q)
 /(1+epsilon^2)^(1/2).
Noise acts independently on coordinates, and all displayed bases are positive. Thus exactly
 ||Q F_L||_q=rho^(m/2) kappa(epsilon)^ell.                   (4.2)
The even analytic expansion at epsilon0 is
 log kappa(epsilon)=[rho(q-1)-1]epsilon^2/2+O(epsilon^4).
Its quadratic coefficient is positive by the fixed q hypothesis. Hence some fixed epsilon in(0,1) has kappa>1. Now
 log ||QF_L||_q
 = L log kappa - m[log kappa+(1/2)log(1/rho)].
Since m/L->0, the ratio of this logarithm to L tends to log kappa>0, proving exponential divergence. This direct proof also shows why the parity factor's damping cannot pay for the product amplification when the removed degree is sublinear.

Apply this with q=2t' for any of the available finite t=t_n<s. Since2s'=1+1/rho, q exceeds the required threshold. The actual proved window d_L=O(sqrt(L)/log L) is sublinear, so passing to that high-degree space does NOT restore the dimension-uniform scalar map needed by the naive Holder step.
This says nothing against a more specific estimate using u=h/Ph, the stationary floor/matching, positivity, or the eigenfunction equation. F_L is not claimed to be an actual eigenvector, and no h,pi satisfying all the target hypotheses is generated by the construction. In particular (4.1) cannot be reported as collapse of an actual Doob gap.

5. Entire operator estimate and support argument

For arbitrary f in the whole low-degree subspace, not merely one character,

(T-P)f=Q[(u-pi)Qf]+Q[(pi-1)Qf].

For the first term Q contracts L^(p0) to L2 because its squared parameter is rho=p0-1. Holder with p,r1 and then the polynomial estimate yield

||Q[(u-pi)Qf]||_2
 <=||u-pi||_p ||Qf||_(r1)
 <=Delta_k A1^d ||f||_2.

No subspace projection in Lp is used. The exponent p strictly above p0 is precisely what makes finite r1 possible; p=p0 would not support this argument with the same finite cost.

Since E pi=1 and all nonempty coefficients through degree k vanish, pi-1 has Fourier support in degrees at least k+1. Qf keeps f's support in degrees at most d. In the product, any resulting index is R symmetric-difference S with |R|>=k+1 and |S|<=d. It has size at least |R|-|S|>=k+1-d. Summation or cancellation may remove such coefficients, but cannot introduce lower degrees. If k=L, pi-1 is identically zero and the assertion remains valid. For k=d=0 it asserts mean-zero support, also valid.

Applying P_sqrt(z-1) preserves support, and applying P_beta afterwards has L2 norm at most beta^(k+1-d) on that support. Therefore

||Q[(pi-1)Qf]||_2
 <=beta^(k+1-d)||(pi-1)Qf||_z

The triangle bound ||pi-1||_s<=B+1 is valid because s>1 and the base measure is a probability. These two estimates apply uniformly to every unit vector in the low-degree subspace. Self-adjointness yields ((T-P)R_d)*=R_d(T-P), hence equality of their operator norms. The proof includes the constant and cross block and requires only d<=k, not 2d<=k.

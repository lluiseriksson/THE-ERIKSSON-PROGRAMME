3. Exact representation and pointwise mass identity

Let f be an actual real K eigenfunction with E pi f=0, E pi f^2=1 and eigenvalue lambda>=rho+zeta>0. The eigen-equation is P(hf)=lambda g f. Set psi=Q(hf)/sqrt(lambda). Self-adjointness and Q^2=P imply

||Q(hf)||_2^2=<hf,P(hf)>=lambda E h g f^2=lambda.

Moreover,
T Q(hf)=Q M_u P(hf)=lambda Q(u g f)=lambda Q(hf),
because u g=h. Orthogonality is
<Q(hf),Qh>=<hf,Ph>=E pi f=0.

These establish the unit normalization and non-Perron condition needed to apply A. Applying Q once more gives Qpsi=P(hf)/sqrt(lambda)=sqrt(lambda)g f at every cube point. Multiplication by u=h/g gives the exact pointwise relation

u |Qpsi|^2=lambda h g f^2=lambda pi f^2.

There is no missing factor N, stationary density normalization, square root, or spectral-conjugacy factor. The identity is about the actual eigenfunction f, not an arbitrary high-degree test vector. For real functions f^2 is correct; a complex formulation would use |f|^2 and conjugate inner products, but real eigenbases suffice here.

4. Localization and probability estimates

Parseval in the uniform Walsh basis gives ||Qpsi||_2^2=sum_S rho^|S| |psi_hat(S)|^2. On the degrees at most d_L the multipliers are at most 1; above d_L they are at most rho^(d_L+1). Since ||psi||_2=1 and its low projection has squared norm at most delta_L,

||Qpsi||_2^2 <= delta_L+rho^(d_L+1)(1-||Pi_<=d_L psi||_2^2)
             <= delta_L+rho^(d_L+1)=J_L.

The simpler upper bound used in C is valid even if delta_L is not small in a particular finite dimension. For M>0 and U={u>M}, its complement has u<=M, including the threshold equality. Integrating the exact identity over that complement yields

E[pi f^2 1_(U^c)] <= (M/lambda)||Qpsi||_2^2 <= M J_L/lambda.

Thus (2.1) is an upper bound on the actual f's stationary squared mass. It does not assert literal support or pointwise vanishing outside U. The other two estimates concern probabilities, which must not be confused with this squared mass:

mu(U) <= M^-t E u^t <= K_t^t M^-t;
Pr_pi(U)=E pi 1_U <= ||pi||_s mu(U)^(1/s')
         <= B K_t^(t/s') M^(-t/s').

Both Holder exponents and the power of K_t are correct. They are valid when the displayed probability upper bound exceeds 1 as well, although then it is weaker than the trivial bound. The positive floor is part of the inherited moment estimate, not an additional factor needed in these two probability inequalities.

5. Growing thresholds and higher stationary moments

Take theta fixed in (0,log(1/rho)) and M_L=exp(theta d_L). Because d_L=o(k_L), multiplying an exponentially decaying delta_L by exp(theta d_L) still gives a quantity tending to zero. The remaining term is exactly

M_L rho^(d_L+1)=rho exp(-(log(1/rho)-theta)d_L),

which tends to zero since d_L tends to infinity and the coefficient in parentheses is positive. Therefore M_L J_L tends to zero. The eigenvalue is bounded below by the fixed rho+zeta, so eta_L=M_L J_L/lambda tends to zero uniformly over the eligible normalized eigenfunctions. The stationary probability bound has fixed constant C_t=B K_t^(t/s') and tends to zero at the stated exp(-theta t d_L/s') rate. All the actual stationary L2 mass tends into this particular high-u set in the precise sense that its complement has mass at most eta_L. The set depends on h and L, but not on a separate choice made to fit the eigenfunction.

For a collapsing actual gap, lambda_2 tends to 1. With fixed rho, eventually lambda_2>=(1+rho)/2=rho+(1-rho)/2. Any real normalized second eigenfunction is stationary-mean-zero because it is orthogonal to the simple Perron eigenfunction in the reversible stationary space. Eigenvalue multiplicity does not matter: the estimates hold for every such normalized vector. The conclusion is conditional on collapse; no collapsing sequence is manufactured.

For fixed finite p>2, write a_p=1/2-1/p>0. Holder on the probability space pi dmu gives

E_pi[|f|^2 1_U] <= (E_pi |f|^p)^(2/p) Pr_pi(U)^(1-2/p).

If eta_L<1, (2.1) makes the left side at least 1-eta_L>0, which also proves Pr_pi(U)>0. Taking square roots and dividing gives

||f||_(Lp(pi)) >= sqrt(1-eta_L) Pr_pi(U)^(-a_p).

The exponent -a_p is negative; substituting an UPPER bound on Pr_pi(U) therefore gives a LOWER bound, as required. Inserting C_t exp(-theta t d_L/s') reproduces (3.1) with the correct sign, constant and growth rate. It is a necessary divergence of these eigenfunctions' higher stationary norms, not a contradiction: no uniform upper bound for those norms has been proved. Finite-dimensional norms exist for each L even though the sequence diverges. No p=infinity assertion is necessary for the stated finite-p theorem.

6. Tail-space obstruction: construction and sharp range

Fix finite q>1+1/rho and integer d_L>=0 with d_L=o(L). All construction claims are for sufficiently large L; this is essential if an arbitrary early cutoff exceeds the dimension. Put m=d_L+1 and ell=L-m>=0. Write F as the product of the first m coordinates times the normalized positive-coordinate product over the remaining ell coordinates. Independence gives E F^2=1, because each remaining factor has second moment 1+epsilon^2, exactly canceled by the squared normalization. Every Walsh term includes the first m coordinates, so the minimum possible degree is m, with no cancellation creating lower degrees. F has no Walsh coefficient through degree d_L.

Under Q=P_sqrt(rho), each parity coordinate contributes sqrt(rho), while each factor 1+epsilon x becomes 1+sqrt(rho)epsilon x. On taking absolute values the parity has modulus 1. Independence of the remaining coordinates therefore yields exactly

||QF||_q=rho^(m/2) kappa(epsilon)^ell,
kappa(epsilon)=(((1+sqrt(rho)epsilon)^q+(1-sqrt(rho)epsilon)^q)/2)^(1/q)
               /(1+epsilon^2)^(1/2).

For 0<epsilon<1 and 0<rho<1 both bases are positive, so this expression and its Taylor expansion are well defined also for noninteger q. To verify the coefficient without symbolic computation, the even binomial expansion gives the averaged numerator before the 1/q power as 1+q(q-1)rho epsilon^2/2+O(epsilon^4). Its logarithm divided by q is (q-1)rho epsilon^2/2+O(epsilon^4). Subtracting (1/2)log(1+epsilon^2)=epsilon^2/2+O(epsilon^4) leaves

log kappa=[rho(q-1)-1]epsilon^2/2+O(epsilon^4).

The fixed strict range makes this coefficient positive, so one sufficiently small fixed epsilon>0 gives log kappa>0. This epsilon may depend on rho and q but not on L. Then

(1/L)log||QF||_q = log kappa-(m/L)(log kappa+(1/2)log(1/rho))
                 -> log kappa>0.

Thus the entire tail space has an exponentially diverging L2-to-Lq operator norm along these sublinear cutoffs. The damping factor from parity consumes only o(L) in the exponent; it cannot cancel the positive linear product contribution. The argument makes no assertion at the equality threshold or for arbitrary linearly growing cutoffs. At the scalar endpoint q=1+1/rho ordinary hypercontractivity instead contracts L2 to Lq, so the strict inequality is indispensable.

For an available finite exponent t=t_n>1 with t<s, conjugate exponents reverse order: t'>s'. Consequently q=2t'>2s'=1+1/rho, and the construction applies. The actual window d_L=O(sqrt(L)/log L) is sublinear. Therefore restricting the input only to that whole high-degree space cannot restore the missing dimension-uniform scalar map in the naive Holder bound. This refutes that method step even allowing a fixed constant larger than 1; it is stronger than merely failure of contractivity.

The vector F is generally sign-changing and is not specified as an eigenvector of T. No corresponding h, g, u or stationary law satisfying the nonlinear relation and all original constraints is constructed. A restricted estimate for actual eigenvectors, or one using the coupling u=h/Ph, remains logically possible. The counterexample to a generic operator estimate must not be promoted to a counterexample to the original Doob-gap conjecture.

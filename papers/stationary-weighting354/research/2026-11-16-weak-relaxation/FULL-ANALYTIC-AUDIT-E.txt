1. HYPOTHESES, NORMALIZATION AND THE ACTUAL KERNEL

The argument is for finite L, uniform probability mu on {-1,1}^L, fixed 0<rho<1, and a strictly positive real potential h. All inverse moments exist for each finite model because h has a positive minimum; their dimension-uniform bounds still require proof and are supplied below. The normalization is E_mu[h P_rho h]=1. Rescaling a positive h to this normalization does not change its Doob kernel or normalized stationary density.

Set g=Ph and pi=hg. Then E_mu pi=1, so nu=pi mu is a probability measure. The pointwise floor pi>=b>0 implies b<=1. The moment cap E_mu pi^s<=2, s=(1+rho)/(1-rho)>1, gives ||pi||_s<=B=2^(1/s). The conjugate exponent is s'=(1+rho)/(2rho)>1. No endpoint rho=0 or rho=1 is included.

The actual row sum is
sum_y K(x,y)=sum_y P(x,y)h(y)/g(x)=1.
Because uniform Boolean noise is reversible,
nu(x)K(x,y)=mu(x)h(x)P(x,y)h(y)
is symmetric in x,y. Hence nu is invariant and K is self-adjoint on L2(nu). Its Dirichlet form is precisely the displayed half-sum with edge multiplier h(x)h(y). This verifies the relevant nonlinear model rather than merely matching a convenient density pair.

The original integrated manuscript's admissibility assumptions are the same stationary floor and critical cap plus exact low-degree stationary matching. Candidate E deliberately uses only the first two after normalization. There is no change from stationary density pi to normalized weighting density r or u hidden in the proof.

2. FIRST HYPERCONTRACTIVE STEP: INVERSE POTENTIAL MOMENT

Let Q=P_sqrt(rho). It is self-adjoint and Q^2=P. Thus
||Qh||_2^2=<h,Ph>_mu=1.

Write R=1+1/rho. The Boolean hypercontractive condition for Q from L2 to L^R is
sqrt(rho)<=sqrt((2-1)/(R-1)).
Since R-1=1/rho, this is equality. Both exponents lie in the ordinary norm range, with finite R>2. Therefore
||g||_R=||Q(Qh)||_R<=||Qh||_2=1.

From h(x)g(x)>=b, with h,g positive, one obtains
1/h(x)<=g(x)/b.
The direction is correct: multiplying by h(x)b>0 gives the original floor. Taking the R norm yields ||h^(-1)||_R<=b^(-1). This inverse moment is a proved consequence of the floor and energy normalization alone. The stationary cap is not used in this step.

3. SECOND HYPERCONTRACTIVE STEP: INVERSE EDGE MOMENT

The base edge measure m(x,y)=mu(x)P(x,y) is a probability measure, including its diagonal mass. Put w(x,y)=h(x)h(y), r=1/rho and v=h^(-r). Then exactly
E_m w^(-r)=<v,Pv>_mu=||Qv||_2^2.

For Q:L^(1+rho)->L2, the admissibility condition is
sqrt(rho)<=sqrt(((1+rho)-1)/(2-1)),
again equality. All exponents are greater than one. Consequently
E_m w^(-r)<=||v||_(1+rho)^2
=||h^(-1)||_[r(1+rho)]^(2r)
<=b^(-2r),
because r(1+rho)=R.

This is not an assertion that h(x) and h(y) are independent under m; they are generally correlated. The <v,Pv> identity and Q factorization handle that dependence exactly. It is also not an inverse moment under nu tensor nu. Using the correct base edge law is essential and the note does so.

4. EDGE HOLDER COMPARISON AND FACTORS OF TWO

For nonconstant real f let O=max f-min f>0 and z=(f(x)-f(y))^2/O^2. Then 0<=z<=1. Define alpha=r/(r+1)=1/(1+rho), so 0<alpha<1 and r(1-alpha)=alpha. Pointwise,
(wz)^alpha (w^(-r)z)^(1-alpha)=z,
including z=0 since both powers are positive.

Holder with conjugate powers 1/alpha and 1/(1-alpha) gives
E_m z <= (E_m wz)^alpha (E_m w^(-r)z)^(1-alpha)
<= (2 D_K(f)/O^2)^alpha b^[-2r(1-alpha)]
= (2 D_K(f)/O^2)^alpha b^(-2alpha).

The last inequality uses z<=1 and the inverse edge moment; it does not reverse a negative-exponent inequality. As D_P=(O^2/2)E_m z,
D_P(f)<=2^(alpha-1)b^(-2alpha)D_K(f)^alpha O^[2(1-alpha)].

The omitted factor 2^(alpha-1) is at most one. Replacing it by one makes the upper bound larger and is valid. This deliberate loss only weakens the final constant; it does not alter the power. The Dirichlet half-factor is accounted for correctly.

5. TRANSFER OF VARIANCE AND CONSTANT AUDIT

On nonconstant Walsh characters, the eigenvalues of P are rho^|S|<=rho, hence
Var_mu(f)<=D_P(f)/(1-rho).

Let m_f=E_mu f. Since nu is a probability,
Var_nu(f)=inf_t E_mu[pi(f-t)^2]<=E_mu[pi(f-m_f)^2].
Holder with s,s' then gives
Var_nu(f)<=B (E_mu|f-m_f|^(2s'))^(1/s').
The uniform mean m_f lies in the range of f, so |f-m_f|<=O. Therefore
E_mu|f-m_f|^(2s')<=O^(2s'-2)Var_mu(f),
and
Var_nu(f)<=B O^(2/s) Var_mu(f)^(1/s').

Combining with the edge comparison and the noise gap gives
Var_nu(f)<=B(1-rho)^(-1/s')b^[-2alpha/s']
             D_K(f)^(alpha/s') O^[2/s+2(1-alpha)/s'].
Since 1/s+1/s'=1, the O exponent is 2(1-alpha/s'). Thus E1 holds with
a=alpha/s'=2rho/(1+rho)^2,
C=B(1-rho)^(-1/s')b^(-2a).

For 0<rho<1, actually 0<a<1/2, a stronger range than the note's sufficient 0<a<1. Thus
p=(1-a)/a=(1+rho^2)/(2rho)>1,
1/p=2rho/(1+rho^2) in (0,1).

Inverting the positive constant,
C^(-1/a)=b^2(1-rho)^[1/(s'a)]2^[-1/(sa)].
Here 1/(s'a)=1/alpha=1+rho and 1/(sa)=(1-rho^2)/(2rho), proving the stated
c=b^2(1-rho)^(1+rho)2^[-(1-rho^2)/(2rho)].
All factors are strictly positive and finite for the stated fixed parameters. No L or hidden number of states enters them. The estimates are not uniform at rho approaching either excluded endpoint or at b approaching zero. Such endpoint uniformity is not claimed.

If O=0, f is constant on the finite cube, both variances and Dirichlet forms vanish, and E1/E2/E3 hold directly without divisions. If D_K=0, strict positivity of the actual kernel also forces f constant. No singular nonconstant zero-energy case is overlooked.

6. WEAK POINCARE CONVERSION E2

For O>0 put y=Var_nu(f)/O^2 and e=D_K(f)/O^2. E1 yields y<=C e^a, equivalently e>=c y^(1/a). Since 1/a=1+p, this is e>=c y^(1+p).

For any epsilon>0, either y<=epsilon, in which case y<=c^(-1)epsilon^(-p)e+epsilon, or y>epsilon. In the latter case y^p>epsilon^p and
e>=c y y^p>=c y epsilon^p,
so y<=c^(-1)epsilon^(-p)e. Adding epsilon only weakens this inequality. Multiplying by O^2 proves E2 with precisely the coefficient stated. No optimization or missing Young-inequality factor is needed for this two-case proof. Arbitrarily large epsilon are permitted; the bound then becomes trivially valid rather than inconsistent.

7. ACTUAL PSD KERNEL AND DISCRETE DECAY E3

The unitary identification U:f->sqrt(pi)f from L2(nu) to L2(mu) satisfies
UKU^(-1)=M_sqrt(h/g) P M_sqrt(h/g).
Indeed the coefficient sqrt(h(x)g(x))h(y)/(g(x)sqrt(h(y)g(y))) equals sqrt(h(x)/g(x))sqrt(h(y)/g(y)). The right-hand operator is positive semidefinite since P is. K is also a stationary Markov contraction by Jensen and invariance, so its spectrum lies in [0,1]. In fact it is positive definite for a finite cube and 0<rho<1, but that stronger fact is unnecessary.

For F=K^n f, invariance gives E_nu KF=E_nu F. Self-adjointness therefore yields
Var_nu(F)-Var_nu(KF)=<F,(I-K^2)F>_nu.
For every spectral value lambda in [0,1], 1-lambda^2>=(1-lambda), so the right side is at least D_K(F). This would require care for a generic reversible kernel with negative spectrum; the candidate proves the needed PSD property for its actual K.

Markov averaging keeps every K^n f in the original range of f, so osc(K^n f)<=O. Applying E1 to F, using the larger denominator O in the oscillation factor, gives
D_K(F)>=c Var_nu(F)^(1+p)/O^(2p).
Thus y_n=Var_nu(K^n f)/O^2 obeys
y_(n+1)<=y_n-c y_n^(1+p).
The use of O rather than the possibly smaller current oscillation is a weakening in the correct direction.

If y_n=0, the observable is nu-almost surely constant and all subsequent variances vanish. If consecutive values are positive, convexity of t^(-p) gives its tangent bound at y_n:
y_(n+1)^(-p)-y_n^(-p)
 >=p(y_n-y_(n+1))y_n^(-p-1)
 >=pc.
Summing until any possible first zero, and treating subsequent zeros directly, proves
Var_nu(K^n f)<=O^2[y_0^(-p)+pcn]^(-1/p)
when y_0>0. This has equality in its initial-value normalization at n=0. Since y_0<=1, replacing y_0^(-p) by 1 enlarges the bound and gives E3. The standard stronger y_0<=1/4 is correct but not needed. There is no continuous-time substitution, missing factor of two in n, or reversed negative power in this argument.

8. INTERPRETATION, LIMITS AND POTENTIAL OVERCLAIMS

The variance bound is uniform in L and in all positive h satisfying the fixed floor/cap, after scaling by the initial oscillation squared. On a finite cube every function is bounded; the substantive uniform assertion concerns families whose oscillation is controlled as dimension varies. It does not compare decay with initial variance alone by a uniform constant.

For a nonconstant eigenfunction with eigenvalue lambda, D_K(f)=(1-lambda)Var_nu(f). E1 consequently yields
1-lambda>=c[Var_nu(f)/osc(f)^2]^p.
The ratio can be small for localized normalized eigenfunctions; no dimension-uniform lower bound for it has been established here. The constant Perron eigenfunction must be excluded from dividing by its zero variance/oscillation. The note's eigenfunction discussion is correctly understood for nonconstant modes. It should be made explicit if converted into a standalone theorem statement.

Saying that these estimates permit a vanishing dimension-dependent gap is a limitation of these estimates, not a construction of an admissible collapsing-gap family. The original stationary-gap question remains open. Likewise E1-E3 do not imply a critical weighting moment or control a killed operator in full L2 norm. Exact matching is absent because this is a weaker conclusion; the gap problem's matching requirement has not been eliminated by proving its own intended conclusion without matching.

The fixed-rate exponent is derived correctly, but no optimality argument is provided. The deliberately discarded factor and the choice of exponents leave room for stronger constants or rates. A quantitative polynomial upper bound need not be the actual asymptotic rate of each finite chain: each such irreducible finite chain also has some exponential relaxation with model-dependent constants. There is no contradiction.

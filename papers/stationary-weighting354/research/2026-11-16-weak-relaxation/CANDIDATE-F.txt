1. Exact assumptions and conclusion
Use E's actual Boolean Doob setup, but fix rho=1/2. Thus s=3, B=2^(1/3).
Assume only h>0, E hP h=1, pi=hPh>=b>0 and E pi^3<=2. No matching.
Let nu=pi mu, K=P(h dot)/(Ph), and O=osc(f) for real f.
Set c_*=b^4 2^(-61/24), A=(7/8)c_*.
Claim F1:
 Var_nu(K^n f) <= O^2 [1+A n]^(-8/7), all n>=0.
Claim F2:
 For a stationary K-chain X_0,X_1,... and any integer N>=1,
 Var[(1/N) sum_{i=0}^{N-1} f(X_i)]
 <= (3+32/c_*) O^2/N.
Neither claim is a dimension-uniform L2 spectral gap or an arbitrary-start bound.
The prefactors depend on fixed b, not on L, h, N, or f beyond O.
For O=0 all claims are immediate.

2. A stronger inverse potential moment, derived rather than assumed
Write g=Ph, Q=P_sqrt(rho), so ||Qh||_2=1.
From h>=b/g, applying P gives g>=b P(1/g).
Rowwise Cauchy-Schwarz/Jensen gives P(1/g)>=1/(Pg).
Therefore g Pg>=b, and 1/g<=Pg/b.
Since Pg=P_(rho^(3/2)) Qh, ordinary hypercontractivity at rho=1/2 gives
 ||Pg||_9<=1:
rho^3=1/8=(2-1)/(9-1).
Consequently ||1/g||_9<=b^(-1).

Using h=pi/g and Holder with 1/(9/4)=1/3+1/9 gives
 ||h||_(9/4)<=B/b.
Ordinary hypercontractivity P_(1/2):L^(9/4)->L^6 is admissible because
(1/2)^2=((9/4)-1)/(6-1)=1/4.
It follows that ||g||_6<=B/b.
The ORIGINAL floor also gives 1/h<=g/b, hence
 ||1/h||_6<=H:=B/b^2.
All these are uniform bounds derived from the original floor, cap and normalization. No new inverse-moment assumption is inserted.

3. Inverse edge moment and energy-variance inequality

4. Discrete relaxation
E proves the actual K is self-adjoint, PSD and Markov, so for F=K^n f
 Var_nu(F)-Var_nu(KF)>=D_K(F)
and osc(K^n f)<=O.
The energy-variance inequality yields y_(n+1)<=y_n-c_* y_n^(1+p_*)
for y_n=Var_nu(K^n f)/O^2.
The same negative-power convexity proof as E gives
 y_n<=[1+p_* c_* n]^(-1/p_*),
which is F1. Zero variance is handled without negative powers by permanence of constants.
No numerical computation or analogy with continuous time is used.

5. Stationary covariances and finite-N sample-mean bound
Center f by its nu mean and let gamma_j=Cov(f(X_0),f(X_j)).
Stationarity and reversibility give gamma_j=<f,K^j f>_nu.
PSD implies gamma_j>=0. For j=2m, gamma_j=Var_nu(K^m f).
For j=2m+1, PSD contraction gives
 gamma_j=<K^m f,K K^m f>_nu<=Var_nu(K^m f).
Therefore
 sum_{j>=1} gamma_j <= Var_nu(f)+2 sum_{m>=1} Var_nu(K^m f).
The decreasing bound in F1 satisfies
 sum_{m>=1}(1+A m)^(-8/7)<=int_0^infinity(1+A x)^(-8/7)dx
 =7/A=8/c_*.
Using Var_nu(f)<=O^2 yields
 sum_{j>=1} gamma_j <= O^2(1+16/c_*).
All terms are nonnegative and the series is finite, so no conditional rearrangement is involved.

For finite N, the exact stationary covariance identity is
 Var[(1/N)sum_{i=0}^{N-1}f(X_i)]
 =(1/N^2)[N gamma_0+2 sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)gamma_j]
 <=(1/N)[gamma_0+2 sum_{j>=1}gamma_j]
 <=(3+32/c_*)O^2/N.
This proves F2. It is a uniform stationary variance/error bound; it does not include burn-in, initialization bias, nonstationary warm starts or a dimension-uniform full-spectrum gap.

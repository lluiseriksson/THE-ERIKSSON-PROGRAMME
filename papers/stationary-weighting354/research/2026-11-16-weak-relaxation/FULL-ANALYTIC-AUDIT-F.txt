1. ACTUAL MODEL AND ASSUMPTIONS

The setting is the same finite Boolean cube with uniform probability mu, strictly positive h and actual noise Doob kernel as E, now at the fixed noise parameter rho=1/2. Thus P=P_(1/2), Q=P_sqrt(1/2), g=Ph, and E_mu[hPh]=1. The pointwise expression pi=hPh means h(x)(Ph)(x), not the scalar expectation. The assumptions are pi>=b>0 and E_mu pi^3<=2. Since E_mu pi=1, necessarily b<=1.

The invariant probability is nu=pi mu. The kernel K(x,y)=P(x,y)h(y)/g(x) is Markov and reversible under nu, because nu(x)K(x,y)=mu(x)P(x,y)h(x)h(y). Its conjugate on L2(mu) is M_sqrt(h/g) P M_sqrt(h/g), which is PSD. Markov contraction bounds its eigenvalues above by one. All subsequent spectral and covariance statements concern this actual K and its actual stationary nu, not a surrogate weighting distribution.

The cap gives B=||pi||_3 bounded above by the chosen constant 2^(1/3); the note uses B=2^(1/3) as a uniform bound, not an assertion of equality for every potential. The normalization and floor are preserved throughout. No matching, additional inverse-moment assumption, killed-norm estimate or pointwise upper bound on h is inserted.

All constants are dimension-free only with fixed b and rho=1/2. There is no assertion for arbitrary rho, b tending to zero, or a changing time-dependent kernel. The result is for finite cubes; no infinite-dimensional limiting process is constructed.

2. STRONGER INVERSE POTENTIAL MOMENT

Since Q^2=P and Q is self-adjoint,
||Qh||_2^2=<h,Ph>_mu=1.

The floor hg>=b implies h>=b/g pointwise. Positivity of P gives
g=Ph>=b P(1/g).
For each probability row P(x,.), convexity of reciprocal (or Cauchy-Schwarz) gives P(1/g)>=1/(Pg). Every denominator is positive. Therefore g(Pg)>=b and
1/g<=Pg/b.
The direction of this inverse inequality is correct.

Noise composition gives Pg=P_rho^2 h=P_(rho^(3/2))Qh. At rho=1/2 the squared noise parameter rho^3 is 1/8. The ordinary Boolean hypercontractivity condition from L2 to L9 is exactly noise-parameter-squared <=(2-1)/(9-1)=1/8. Hence
||Pg||_9<=||Qh||_2=1,
and ||1/g||_9<=b^(-1).

This floor identity and smoothing bound were already present in the grouped manuscript as D_0=1+rho^(-3). F accurately reuses and specializes them rather than claiming a new hypercontractivity theorem.

Now h=pi/g. Holder's reciprocal exponents satisfy
1/(9/4)=4/9=1/3+1/9.
All are ordinary finite norm exponents, so
||h||_(9/4)<=||pi||_3 ||1/g||_9<=B/b.

For P_(1/2):L^(9/4)->L6, the hypercontractive threshold is
(1/2)^2=((9/4)-1)/(6-1)=(5/4)/5=1/4.
It is admissible at equality. Thus ||g||_6<=B/b. Finally the original floor gives h^(-1)<=g/b, so
||h^(-1)||_6<=B/b^2=:H.

Every step is dimension-uniform. The conclusion is genuinely proved from the original floor, cap and energy normalization, not placed in a new structure as an assumption. The cap is used in the h=pi/g Holder step.

3. EDGE MOMENT, VARIANCE TRANSFER AND CONSTANTS

Let m(x,y)=mu(x)P(x,y), a probability measure, and w(x,y)=h(x)h(y). With v=h^(-4),
E_m w^(-4)=<v,Pv>_mu=||Qv||_2^2.
The Q:L^(3/2)->L2 condition is sqrt(rho)<=sqrt(((3/2)-1)/(2-1)), again equality. Therefore
E_m w^(-4)<=||v||_(3/2)^2=||h^(-1)||_6^8<=H^8.
The power eight is correct: squaring the L^(3/2) norm of h^(-4) equals the eighth power of the L6 norm of h^(-1). Independence of edge endpoints is not assumed.

For O=osc(f)>0 set z=(f(x)-f(y))^2/O^2, so 0<=z<=1. With alpha=4/5 and 4(1-alpha)=alpha, Holder gives
E_m z <=(E_m wz)^alpha(E_m w^(-4)z)^(1-alpha)
       <=(2D_K(f)/O^2)^alpha H^(8/5).
As D_P(f)=(O^2/2)E_m z, this yields
D_P(f)<=2^(alpha-1)H^(2alpha)D_K(f)^alpha O^[2(1-alpha)].
The 2^(alpha-1)=2^(-1/5) factor is below one; dropping it weakens the bound in the valid direction.

The unweighted noise gap is 1-rho=1/2, and the conjugate of the stationary moment exponent s=3 is s'=3/2. The same centered variance transfer as E gives
Var_nu(f)<=B O^(2/3) Var_mu(f)^(2/3)
          <=B 2^(2/3) H^(16/15) D_K(f)^(8/15) O^(14/15).
Here the energy exponent is (4/5)(2/3)=8/15, and the oscillation exponent is 2/3+(2/5)(2/3)=14/15. They satisfy the required quadratic homogeneity: 2(8/15)+14/15=2.

Thus a_*=8/15, p_*=(1-a_*)/a_*=7/8, and
C_*=B 2^(2/3)H^(16/15).
Raising to -1/a_*=-15/8 gives
C_*^(-15/8)=B^(-15/8)2^(-5/4)H^(-2)
           =b^4 B^(-31/8)2^(-5/4)
           =b^4 2^(-31/24-30/24)
           =b^4 2^(-61/24)=c_*.
Every displayed exponent and constant in the candidate matches this calculation. c_*>0 for fixed b>0. The deliberate factor loss means the constant is conservative, not optimal.

4. DISCRETE VARIANCE DECAY

For any F, reversibility, invariance and PSD contraction yield
Var_nu(F)-Var_nu(KF)=<F,(I-K^2)F>_nu>=D_K(F),
since 1-lambda^2>=1-lambda for lambda in [0,1]. Also Markov averaging preserves the range of the initial f, so osc(K^n f)<=O.

Apply the energy-variance inequality to F=K^n f and use this fixed initial oscillation as the denominator. For y_n=Var_nu(K^n f)/O^2 one obtains
y_(n+1)<=y_n-c_* y_n^(1+p_*).
The replacement of current oscillation by O weakens the lower bound on energy in the correct direction.

For p_*=7/8>0, t->t^(-p_*) is convex on positive t. Its tangent inequality at y_n shows
y_(n+1)^(-p_*)-y_n^(-p_*)>=p_*c_*
whenever consecutive values are positive. If a variance is zero, all later variances vanish; no inverse power is applied to zero. Telescoping and y_0<=1 give
y_n<=[1+p_*c_*n]^(-1/p_*)
    =[1+(7/8)c_*n]^(-8/7),
which is F1. At n=0 the relaxed right side is O^2 and dominates the initial variance. A sharper initial-value factor is available as in E, but not necessary for F1.

This argument requires p_*>0, not p_*>=1. Therefore crossing from E's previous p>1 to the present p_*=7/8 causes no defect in the discrete recursion. It is exactly why the resulting rate exponent 8/7 is above one. No continuous-time comparison or numerical computation is used.

5. STATIONARY COVARIANCES AND SUMMABILITY

Assume X_0 has distribution nu and (X_i) evolves under this same K. Center f by its nu mean; oscillation and the sample-mean variance are unchanged. Then
gamma_j=Cov(f(X_0),f(X_j))=<f,K^j f>_nu.
PSD gives gamma_j>=0. By self-adjointness,
gamma_(2m)=||K^m f||_nu^2=Var_nu(K^m f).
For odd indices,
gamma_(2m+1)=<K^m f,K K^m f>_nu<=||K^m f||_nu^2.
The upper bound uses contraction and the lower nonnegativity uses PSD.

This even/odd argument is important. A generic Cauchy-Schwarz bound against ||K^j f|| alone would lose half the decay exponent and would not establish summability here. The candidate avoids that loss correctly through reversibility and the exact covariance identity.

The odd term at j=1 is at most Var_nu(f). Pairing all remaining even/odd terms gives
sum_(j>=1)gamma_j<=Var_nu(f)+2 sum_(m>=1)Var_nu(K^m f).
For A=(7/8)c_*>0 the function (1+Ax)^(-8/7) is positive and decreasing. The usual sum-integral comparison in the stated direction gives
sum_(m>=1)(1+Am)^(-8/7)
 <=integral_0^infinity(1+Ax)^(-8/7)dx
 =7/A=8/c_*.
The integral is finite specifically because 8/7>1. Thus
sum_(j>=1)gamma_j<=O^2(1+16/c_*).
All summands are nonnegative and the bound proves convergence, so no conditionally convergent rearrangement is made.

6. FINITE-N SAMPLE-MEAN BOUND

For integer N>=1, stationarity gives exactly
Var[(1/N)sum_(i=0)^(N-1)f(X_i)]
 =[N gamma_0+2 sum_(j=1)^(N-1)(N-j)gamma_j]/N^2.
Since 0<=N-j<=N and gamma_j>=0, this is at most
[gamma_0+2 sum_(j>=1)gamma_j]/N.
Using gamma_0<=O^2 and the preceding sum estimate yields
Var[(1/N)sum f(X_i)]<=(3+32/c_*)O^2/N,
as stated in F2. For N=1 this remains a valid loose bound. The unused stronger variance bound O^2/4 would improve constants but is not required.

Under stationarity the empirical average is unbiased for nu(f), so its mean-square estimation error about nu(f) equals the bounded variance. This does not extend to arbitrary initialization, where bias and changed covariance structure would require additional analysis. Stationarity must mean nu, not the uniform Boolean reference measure mu. The note explicitly imposes stationarity and disclaims arbitrary starts.

7. INTERPRETATION AND LIMITS

F obtains a dimension-uniform integrated covariance/variance coefficient for real observable families with controlled oscillation. On each finite cube all functions are bounded, but oscillations of variance-normalized localized eigenfunctions can grow with dimension. Hence an O^2/N bound does not imply a uniform full-spectrum gap. Neither F nor this audit constructs an admissible gap-collapse example.

The fixed rho=1/2 specialization is essential to the particular exponents and constants proved. The derivation does not state a parameter interval or optimize over available inverse moments. No optimality or frontier-priority conclusion follows from the explicit 8/7 rate. A finite chain can have faster eventual exponential decay with dimension-dependent constants, consistently with this uniform polynomial envelope.

Summable stationary covariances justify the displayed variance estimate and an integrated-covariance bound. A central limit theorem, concentration inequality, burn-in bound or nonstationary mean-square error statement would require its own stated theorem; none is asserted or certified here. The proof does not require matching, but it does not remove matching from the original full-gap problem while preserving that stronger conclusion.

1. Question and scope
The active question remains the stationary Boolean Doob gap. Here we test a different implication of its original floor and critical cap: a dimension-uniform weak Poincare inequality and polynomial variance decay for bounded observables. Exact marginal matching is NOT assumed or needed for this consequence. No uniform spectral gap, actual killed-norm upper bound, endpoint weighting moment, optimal exponent or global originality is claimed.

2. Definitions
Let mu be uniform on {-1,1}^L, 0<rho<1, P=P_rho, Q=P_sqrt(rho).
Let h>0, g=Ph, normalize E_mu hPh=1, set pi=hg.
Thus pi is a density relative to mu. Assume pi>=b>0 and E_mu pi^s<=2,
where s=(1+rho)/(1-rho), s'=s/(s-1)=(1+rho)/(2rho), B=2^(1/s).
Necessarily b<=1. Write nu(x)=mu(x)pi(x).
The ACTUAL kernel K(x,y)=P(x,y)h(y)/g(x) is Markov and reversible for nu.
Define D_K(f)=<f,(I-K)f>_nu
 = (1/2) sum_{x,y} mu(x)P(x,y)h(x)h(y)(f(x)-f(y))^2.
Define D_P analogously with h(x)h(y) omitted. Let O=osc(f)=max f-min f for real f.

Constants:
alpha=1/(1+rho),
a=alpha/s'=2rho/(1+rho)^2 in (0,1),
p=(1-a)/a=(1+rho^2)/(2rho),
C=B (1-rho)^(-1/s') b^(-2a),
c=C^(-1/a)=b^2(1-rho)^(1+rho) 2^(-(1-rho^2)/(2rho)).

3. Claimed inequalities, uniform in L and h under the stated assumptions
(E1) Var_nu(f) <= C D_K(f)^a O^(2(1-a)).
For O=0 both sides are zero.
(E2) For every epsilon>0:
 Var_nu(f) <= c^(-1) epsilon^(-p) D_K(f) + epsilon O^2.
(E3) For all integers n>=0:
 Var_nu(K^n f) <= O^2 [1+p c n]^(-1/p).
The decay exponent is 1/p=2rho/(1+rho^2). The sharper initial-value version,
when Var_nu(f)>0 and O>0, replaces 1 in the brackets by
(Var_nu(f)/O^2)^(-p).

4. Derivation of E1 from the actual nonlinear floor
Because ||Qh||_2^2=E hPh=1 and g=Q(Qh), ordinary Boolean hypercontractivity gives
 ||g||_R<=1, R=1+1/rho.
The pointwise floor hg>=b gives 1/h<=g/b, hence ||1/h||_R<=b^(-1).
This inverse moment is PROVED here from the original floor and normalization.

Use the probability measure m(x,y)=mu(x)P(x,y) and weight w=h(x)h(y).
For r=1/rho, put v=h^(-r). Then
 E_m w^(-r)=<v,Pv>_mu=||Qv||_2^2
 <=||v||_(1+rho)^2=||1/h||_(r(1+rho))^(2r)<=b^(-2r),
because r(1+rho)=R. This second hypercontractive use is
Q:L^(1+rho)(mu)->L^2(mu).

For O>0 put z(x,y)=(f(x)-f(y))^2/O^2, so 0<=z<=1.
Holder, with alpha=r/(r+1)=1/(1+rho), gives
 E_m z
 =E_m[(wz)^alpha (w^(-r)z)^(1-alpha)]
 <=(E_m wz)^alpha (E_m w^(-r)z)^(1-alpha)
 <=(2D_K(f)/O^2)^alpha b^(-2alpha).
Consequently
 D_P(f) <=2^(alpha-1) b^(-2alpha) D_K(f)^alpha O^(2(1-alpha))
 <=b^(-2alpha) D_K(f)^alpha O^(2(1-alpha)).
The dropped factor is <=1; constants are intentionally not optimized.

P's nonconstant Walsh eigenvalues are <=rho, so
 Var_mu(f)<=D_P(f)/(1-rho).
Let m_f=E_mu f. Since |f-m_f|<=O,
 Var_nu(f)<=E_mu pi(f-m_f)^2
 <=B [E_mu |f-m_f|^(2s')]^(1/s')
 <=B O^(2/s) Var_mu(f)^(1/s').
Combining gives E1 exactly, with a=alpha/s' and the stated C.
No matching, critical u=h/g moment, off-model density or killed estimate was inserted.

5. Weak Poincare conversion
Normalize y=Var_nu(f)/O^2 and e=D_K(f)/O^2. E1 is y<=C e^a,
equivalently e>=c y^(1/a).
If y<=epsilon, E2 is immediate. Otherwise
 y<=c^(-1) epsilon^(-p) e since y^(1/a)=y*y^p>=y*epsilon^p.
Multiplication by O^2 proves E2.

6. Discrete-time decay, including positivity justification
Under the isometry f -> sqrt(pi) f from L^2(nu) to L^2(mu),
K is similar to S=M_sqrt(h/g) P M_sqrt(h/g).
P is positive semidefinite, hence K has spectrum in [0,1].
For F=K^n f,
 Var_nu(F)-Var_nu(KF)=<F,(I-K^2)F>_nu>=D_K(F).
Markov averaging gives osc(K^n f)<=O.
Thus y_n=Var_nu(K^n f)/O^2 satisfies
 y_{n+1}<=y_n-c y_n^(1+p).
If y_n=0, all subsequent terms vanish. For positive consecutive terms,
the convexity inequality for t -> t^(-p) gives
 y_{n+1}^(-p)-y_n^(-p)>=p(y_n-y_{n+1})y_n^(-p-1)>=p c.
Summing proves the initial-value version. Since y_0<=1 (indeed <=1/4),
it implies E3. No continuous-time/discrete-time substitution is used.

7. Limits, falsifier and compatibility with the open gap
These estimates permit a dimension-dependent spectral gap tending to zero:
the factor osc(f)^2 is not controlled by its L^2(nu) norm uniformly for rare localized eigenfunctions.
Applying E1 to a normalized eigenfunction gives only
1-lambda >= c [Var_nu(f)/osc(f)^2]^p.
Thus E1-E3 do not remove the original hypotheses of the spectral-gap question.
They provide a dimension-free polynomial rate for a DIFFERENT, explicitly bounded-observable norm.
A falsifier is any finite-dimensional positive h satisfying the exact original normalization, floor and cap for which E1 fails with the specified C, or an error in the inverse-edge moment or discrete-time argument.

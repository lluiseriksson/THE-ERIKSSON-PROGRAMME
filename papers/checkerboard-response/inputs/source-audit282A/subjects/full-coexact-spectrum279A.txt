2. THE ACTUAL IMAGE AT EACH FREQUENCY, INCLUDING ZERO

Use the unitary convention u(x)=n^(-3/2) sum_k uhat(k) exp(2 pi i k.x/n).
Set theta_j=2 pi k_j/n and g_j(k)=exp(i theta_j)-1. Then Delta_j has
symbol g_j. For a skew array T_(j,l)=-T_(l,j), the actual adjoint symbol is
 (d1* T)_l=sum_j conjugate(g_j) T_(j,l).
Its image is contained in g^perp={v:sum_j conjugate(g_j)v_j=0}.
For k!=0, D=|g|^2>0, and every such v has the explicit preimage
 T_(j,l)=(g_j v_l-g_l v_j)/D.
Indeed the displayed adjoint gives v_l-g_l(g*v)/D=v_l. Thus the image is
EXACTLY g^perp, of complex dimension2. At k=0, d1*=0 and its image is
{0}, not a three-dimensional unconstrained constant mode. This also proves
the absence of harmonic constants. For real fields, all these formulas
respect conjugation at -k, so the preimage can be chosen real. Each channel
has real dimension2(n^3-1), equal to its complexification's dimension.

3. ORIGINAL TWO-FREQUENCY FORM, WITH ITS CROSS-TERM SIGN

Write q=(n/2,n/2,n/2) in X. Multiplication by chi shifts k to k+q.
These unordered pairs partition the frequencies, with no fixed pair member.
For one such pair set h=g(k+q), D'=|h|^2, Gamma=g*h. Directly,
 h_j=-exp(i theta_j)-1, D'=12-D,
 Gamma=sum_j (exp(-i theta_j)-1)(-exp(i theta_j)-1)
      =2i sum_j sin(theta_j).
For coefficients x=uhat(k), y=uhat(k+q), the EXACT original channel form is
 A D |x|^2 + A D' |y|^2 + 2 epsilon B Re(Gamma x* y).       (BLOCK)
The expression x*y denotes the Hermitian scalar product. The cross term
comes from summing chi times the product of the two Fourier modes in each
forward difference; there is no extra factor n^3 or 1/2. Thus its ambient
matrix is [ A D I3, epsilon B Gamma I3;
            epsilon B conjugate(Gamma) I3, A D' I3 ].
For k,k+q nonzero its actual domain is g^perp direct-sum h^perp.

4. TWO POLARIZATIONS: THEIR COUPLING IS DERIVED, NOT ASSUMED

Let E,F be any isometries C^2->C^3 with ranges g^perp,h^perp, respectively.
The compressed block has diagonal A D I2,A D' I2 and off-diagonal
epsilon B Gamma E*F. Put rho=|Gamma|/sqrt(D D'), between0 and1 by Cauchy.
The two singular values of E*F are exactly1 and rho:
the two planes have a common unit vector, giving one singular value1,
while both projections are contractions so no singular value exceeds1.
For P_g=I-gg*/D, P_h=I-hh*/D,
 trace(P_g P_h)=3-1-1+|g*h|^2/(D D')=1+rho^2.
This is the sum of the two squared singular values of E*F, giving the
remaining squared singular value rho^2. This argument also covers
coincident planes (rho=1) and orthogonal normals (rho=0).
Taking an ordinary singular-value decomposition of E*F and absorbing the
phase of epsilon Gamma splits the form into two Hermitian 2-by-2 blocks
with diagonals A D,A D' and coupling moduli B|Gamma| and B|Gamma|rho.
Consequently the FOUR eigenvalues for each nonexceptional pair/channel are
 lambda_1,+/- = 6A +/- sqrt(A^2(D-6)^2+B^2|Gamma|^2),       (S1)
 lambda_2,+/- = 6A +/- sqrt(A^2(D-6)^2
                                    +B^2|Gamma|^4/(D D')). (S2)
No preferred transverse basis is needed; scalar Gamma=0 simply kills both
couplings. Repeated roots are retained with their algebraic multiplicities.
Since rho<=1, lambda_1,-<=lambda_2,-<=lambda_2,+<=lambda_1,+.

5. EXCEPTIONAL PAIR, REALITY, AND COMPLETE DIMENSION CHECK

For the pair{0,q}, x at0 is ABSENT. At q, h=(-2,-2,-2), so the sole plane
is h^perp of dimension2, and its two eigenvalues are12A. They are not the
zero roots of an uncompressed scalar pair. Formula(S2)'s denominator is
never used at this pair. Both channels have the same spectrum: changing
epsilon is removed by multiplying the second coefficient by -1.
There are n^3/2-1 nonexceptional pairs and one exceptional pair. The total
per-channel multiplicity is4(n^3/2-1)+2=2(n^3-1), exactly Section2.
The two-channel total is4(n^3-1). No actual modes have been omitted.
Complex conjugation maps a pair to its negative pair and commutes with the
real operator. Real eigenspace dimensions equal those of complexification;
one must combine conjugate pairs, or use the real fixed space of a
self-conjugate pair. This includes k=-k and k+q=-k. These statements follow
by taking real/imaginary parts of a complex eigenvector of a real matrix:
its complex eigenspace is the complexification of its real eigenspace.
They do not assert that a lone nonreal Fourier coefficient is a real field.
All eigenvalues are strictly positive for a>0: Q>=a sum|Delta u|^2 and
an actual field has zero global mean. For n=2 there are still seven
nonzero frequencies per channel; the same pair count applies.

6. THE STRONG-ONLY LIMIT, AND AN EXPLICIT NONDIAGONAL GAP

Set a=0 for this paragraph (H>0); this means the original strong-source
form Q_H, not the full form. Put C0=sum_j cos(theta_j), S0=sum_j sin(theta_j).
Since D-6=-2C0 and Gamma=2iS0, its smallest pair eigenvalue is
 lambda_1,-(Q_H)=H(3-|sum_j exp(i theta_j)|).               (STRONG)
It is achieved in the true coexact space, by Section4, and is zero exactly
when all three unit phases agree, by equality in the triangle inequality.
At such nonexceptional diagonal pairs g and h are parallel, rho=1, so
the zero eigenvalue has multiplicity2 per channel. The exceptional pair
has only the positive eigenvalue6H. Therefore dim ker(Q_H|U)=2(n-2)
over R for both channels together. The paired strong-kernel profile from

If the three frequency residues are not all equal, at least TWO of their
three pairwise differences are nonzero modulo n. Each corresponding
sin^2((theta_j-theta_l)/2) is at least sin^2(pi/n). Expanding gives
 9-|sum_j exp(i theta_j)|^2=4 sum_(j<l)sin^2((theta_j-theta_l)/2).
Divide by3+|sum_j exp(i theta_j)|<=6. Hence every such pair obeys
 lambda_min(Q_H)>= (4H/3)sin^2(pi/n)>=16H/(3n^2).           (TRANSVERSE)

This is a derived elementary bound, not a supplied Poincare assumption.
It sharpens the coarser off-component gap used in267, but that gap's
existence and graph mechanism were already known. No novelty claim is
made for the constant improvement alone.
